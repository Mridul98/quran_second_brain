## Observation / Question
The disconnected letters are rendered by Itani as transliterated letter-names ("Alif, Lam, Meem." / "Kaf, Ha, Ya, Ayn, Saad." / "Noon.") and are **numbered as ayah 1** — yet they carry no semantic translation. They do three things to a reader of an English book: (a) they mark the boundary of what translation can do (some of the text is deliberately non-meaning), (b) they function as structural signatures — the Ha-Meem family (40–46) is recognizable *only* by these letters, and (c) a striking number of letter-openings are immediately followed by Book-claims ("The sending down of the Scripture is from Allah..." — 40:2, 46:2; "By the Book that makes things clear" — 43:2; cf. 42:1–3), while others are followed by oaths ("Qaf. By the Glorious Quran" — 50:1; "Noon. By the pen" — 68:1) or narratives (19, 20, 29, 30). Classical disagreement on their meaning runs from "God knows best" (tawkuf) to letter-numerology; the edition here refuses to gloss them, which is itself an interpretive stance worth remembering when our own notes are tempted to explain them.
