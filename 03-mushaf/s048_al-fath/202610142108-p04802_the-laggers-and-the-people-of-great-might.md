# The laggers' excuses and a people of great might (48:11–17)
**UID:** 202610142108
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 48:11–17 (Ruku 2 of 4 in al-Fath)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[48:11]` – `[48:17]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The desert-Arabs who stayed behind arrive with a rehearsed apology — "our belongings and our families have preoccupied us, so ask forgiveness for us" — and the text catches the mouth-heart gap mid-sentence (11); their evil presumption about the return (12) and their late grab for the gains (15) are audited as attempts "to change the Word of Allah," and the ruku closes by drafting them forward: a future call-up against a people of great might, with exemptions written for the blind, the lame, and the sick (16–17).
* **Placement:** The surah's nifaq-adjacent middle: ruku 1 named the hypocrites' fate (6), ruku 2 shows the paperwork — excuses, jealousy, tongue-vs-heart. The k-nifaq note already treats 48:11–15 as the dossier's Hudaybiyya chapter.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[48:11]`** "The Desert-Arabs who remained behind will say to you, “Our belongings and our families have preoccupied us, so ask forgiveness for us.” They say with their tongues what is not in their hearts. Say, “Who can avail you anything against Allah, if He desires loss for you, or desires gain for you?” In fact, Allah is Informed of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The excuse formula and the diagnosis in one breath: tongue without heart — the same line the k-nifaq note pairs with `[3:167]` ("They say with their mouths what is not in their hearts"); "ask forgiveness for us" weaponizes the intercession of a prophet for people who did not come.
* **`[48:12]`** "But you thought that the Messenger and the believers will never return to their families, and this seemed fine to your hearts; and you harbored evil thoughts, and were uncivilized people."
  * **Gloss:** The thought-crime named: they assumed a permanent no-return; "seemed fine to your hearts" — the heart approves what the tongue later apologizes for; "uncivilized people" is a bold translator rendering; other editions carry "a doomed/wretched people" — classically buhk, "shameless, wretched" — corpus-unique word here (rg "uncivilized"). *Source: translator's term choice (observable in corpus); classical lexicon — verify in print.*
* **`[48:13]`** "He who does not believe in Allah and His Messenger—We have prepared for the disbelievers a Blazing Fire."
  * **Gloss:** The cliff-edge warning verse — short, bracketing, no narrative needed.
* **`[48:14]`** "To Allah belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. He forgives whomever He wills, and He punishes whomever He wills. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The sovereignty refrain's third climb (48:4 forces, 48:7 troops, now kingdom — internal ladder, rg-verified); forgives-punishes "whomever He wills" — the discretion verse, twin in shape to `[5:40]`, `[57:2]`.
* **`[48:15]`** "Those who lagged behind will say when you depart to collect the gains, “Let us follow you.” They want to change the Word of Allah. Say, “You will not follow us; Allah has said so before.” Then they will say, “But you are jealous of us.” In fact, they understand only a little."
  * **Gloss:** Task claim checked: the corpus says "They want to change the Word of Allah" — not "sealing Allah's speech" (user's wording is another edition's for a different verse-family; rg "seal" yields no such line in s048). The greed-moment is the split of the spoils (maghanim); attempting to "change the Word" answers `[6:34]` "There can be no change to Allah’s words"; and their second move is counter-accusation: "But you are jealous of us" — losing the gains, then blaming the winners for it. *Adjudication (task's nifaq question):* the text calls them Desert-Arabs/laggers, not hypocrites; but 9:97 classifies the Desert-Arabs as "the most steeped in disbelief and hypocrisy," and the mouth–heart formula of v.11 is nifaq's signature — so nifaq-adjacent is right if labeled: the surah indicts a behavior-cluster, not a sealed census (contrast `[9:101]` "you do not know them"). *Source: agent inference from the cross-text formula — verify.*
* **`[48:16]`** "Say to the Desert-Arabs who lagged behind, “You will be called against a people of great might; you will fight them, unless they submit. If you obey, Allah will give you a fine reward. But if you turn away, as you turned away before, He will punish you with a painful punishment.”"
  * **Gloss:** The draft-forward: a future call-up ("a people of great might" — the same phrase as `[17:5]`'s punitive servants); the test re-offered with an exit ramp and a painful-punishment rail; classical tables fight over who "a people of great might" are (Persians? Romans? Hawazin? Yemen?) — *Source: transmitted identifications — verify in print; the text itself names none.*
* **`[48:17]`** "There is no blame on the blind, nor any blame on the lame, nor any blame on the sick. Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger— He will admit him into gardens beneath which rivers flow; but whoever turns away— He will punish him with a painful punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The disability exemptions — the triple verbatim-shape twin of `[24:61]` (in its eating-houses context); the ruku's final fork: gardens or "a painful punishment," the same clause closing v.16 — the two outcomes bracket obedience.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **takhalluf (staying behind):** "remained behind" (11), "lagged behind" (15, 16) — Itani renders the root both ways inside one ruku.
  * **Elsewhere:** the 9:83–87 lagging dossier and `[9:90]` "Some of the Desert-Arabs came to make excuses... while those who were untrue... stayed behind" — the excuse-sayings have a whole chapter in At-Tawbah.
* **tabdil (change):** 48:15 "They want to change the Word of Allah... Allah has said so before."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:34]` "There can be no change to Allah’s words" — the direct answer to 48:15's ambition; the sibling crime is twisting, not changing: `[5:13]` "They twist the words out of their context", `[4:46]` "take words out of context... twisting with their tongues", `[3:78]` "twist the Scripture with their tongues" (k-kalam's two scripture-crimes: change vs. twist). Note 48:15's closer "they understand only a little" rhymes with `[4:46]` "they do not believe except a little" — same except-a-little grammar of deficit.
* **maghanim (gains/spoils):** 48:15 "collect the gains," 19–20 "abundant gains" (ruku 3) — spoils vocabulary.
  * **Rendering:** Itani's single word "gains" spans excuse-audited (15) and promise-blessed (19–20) — the same loot, two moral frames.
* **'a'maa, a'raj, marid (blind, lame, sick):** 48:17 exemptions.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[24:61]` same triple with the same "no blame on" formula — the corpus's standard hardship-exemption clause (also cf. the sick-eater route at `[2:196]`).
