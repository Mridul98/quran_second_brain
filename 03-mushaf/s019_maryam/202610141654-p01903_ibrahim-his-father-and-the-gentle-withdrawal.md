# Ibrahim and his father: truthfulness, peace, and a gentle exit (19:41–50)
**UID:** 202610141654
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 19:41–50 (Ruku 3 of 6 in Maryam)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[19:41]` – `[19:50]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The third "And mention in the Scripture" turns from sons to fathers: Ibrahim — "a man of truth, a prophet" — four times addresses "O my father" with knowledge his father never received (19:42–45), is answered with a stoning threat (19:46), and responds with salam, a promise to seek forgiveness, and a clean, sorrowful withdrawal — which Allah repays with Isaac, Jacob, prophethood, and "a noble reputation of truth" (19:47–50).
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 6; the surah's hinge between the two mercy-births (1–40) and the prophets' roll (51–65) — the model of how truth behaves when kin reject it.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[19:41]`** "And mention in the Scripture Abraham. He was a man of truth, a prophet."
  * **Gloss:** The dossier opens with the credential: siddiq ("man of truth") before any argument — the same title Enoch gets at 19:56.
* **`[19:42]`** "He said to his father, “O my father, why do you worship what can neither hear, nor see, nor benefit you in any way?"
  * **Gloss:** The question, not the sermon: idols fail all three senses tests — hear/see/benefit — and "in any way" closes every loophole.
* **`[19:43]`** "O my father, there has come to me knowledge that never came to you. So follow me, and I will guide you along a straight way."
  * **Gloss:** Asymmetry stated without arrogance — knowledge is *received* ("has come to me"); the invitation is "follow me" toward guidance, a straight-way (sirat) idiom matching 1:6 and 19:36.
* **`[19:44]`** "O my father, do not worship the devil. The devil is disobedient to the Most Gracious."
  * **Gloss:** Idolatry re-diagnosed as diabolism — and the disobedience is defined by the mercy-name: 'asiya li-r-Rahman, the rebel *against the Most Gracious*.
* **`[19:45]`** "O my father, I fear that a punishment from the Most Gracious will afflict you, and you become an ally of the devil.”"
  * **Gloss:** The fourth "my father"; the fear is for his father's fate, not for his own safety — da'wa as care.
* **`[19:46]`** "He said, “Are you renouncing my gods, O Abraham? If you do not desist, I will stone you. So leave me alone for a while.”"
  * **Gloss:** The answer: threat of violence ("I will stone you") and exile ("leave me alone") — the task's remembered line "if you do not turn, I will withdraw from you" is NOT here; desistance is demanded under threat.
* **`[19:47]`** "He said, “Peace be upon you. I will ask my Lord to forgive you; He has been Kind to me."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's gentleness-crescendo: salam to a man who threatened stoning, plus istighfar — and hope grounded in Allah's past kindness ("ra'uf/Kind").
* **`[19:48]`** "And I will withdraw from you, and from what you pray to instead of Allah. And I will pray to my Lord, and I hope I will not be disappointed in my prayer to my Lord.”"
  * **Gloss:** Separation with precision: he withdraws from the man *and* from his prayers-to-others, but not from prayer — and "I hope I will not be disappointed" rhymes Zechariah's ledger (19:4).
* **`[19:49]`** "When he withdrew from them, and from what they worship besides Allah, We granted him Isaac and Jacob. And each We made a prophet."
  * **Gloss:** The divine swap: loss of father, gain of sons — "when he withdrew" (i'tazala) answered exactly as Maryam's withdrawal was answered (19:22–25): seclusion → gift.
* **`[19:50]`** "And We gave them freely of Our mercy, and gave them a noble reputation of truth."
  * **Gloss:** "Freely" — unpriced mercy; "a reputation of truth" (lisan sidq) granted — Ibrahim's own 26:84 du'a ("give me a reputation of truth among the others") answered in kind.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`siddiq (man of truth)`** `[19:41]`: truthful before prophetic.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[19:56]` "a man of truth, a prophet" for Enoch — same double credential; `[19:34]` "the Word of truth"; `[33:23]` "men who are true to what they pledged to Allah" — sidq as kept-word, the surah's favorite virtue.
* **`withdraw (i'tizal)`** `[19:48]`: principled exit, not hatred.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[19:16]` Mary "withdrew from her people", `[19:22]` "secluded herself", `[19:49]` "When he withdrew... We granted him" — observable pattern in the English: every withdrawal in the surah is followed by a gift. *Source: agent structural observation on Itani's renderings — verify Arabic roots (tanabada vs. i'tazala differ).*
* **`salām (Peace be upon you)`** `[19:47]`: spoken to the threatening father.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[19:15]`/`[19:33]` the three-day peace of the two miracle-sons; `[19:62]` paradise hears "nothing... but only peace"; `[25:63]` the servants of the Merciful answer the ignorant with "Peace"; `[6:54]` "Peace be upon you, your Lord has prescribed mercy for Himself" — peace as the surah's parting gift.
* **`a noble reputation of truth`** `[19:50]`: lisan sidq — posthumous speech.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:84]` "And give me a reputation of truth among the others" — Ibrahim asking it; `[94:4]` "And raised for you your reputation?" — the same boon promised to the final Prophet; the word "reputation" (`rg -c reputation` in corpus — 3 hits total, all prophetic).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus) — the Ibrahim-father scene is the surah's template for the Prophet's own strained kin-argument (the threat "I will stone you" mirrors Qurayshi intimidation). *Source: standard homiletic reading — verify.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** no transmitted asbab for the ruku as a unit; the father's name (Azar) comes from 6:74 and extra-Quranic genealogical lore, not from Maryam. *Source: silence of the asbab genre; name from 6:74 — observable.*
