# The mercy of your Lord: a hidden call and a name never given (19:1–15)
**UID:** 202610141652
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 19:1–15 (Ruku 1 of 6 in Maryam)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[19:1]` – `[19:15]` (15 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The untranslated letters give way immediately to "a mention of the mercy of your Lord": Zechariah — feeble bones, gray hair, a barren wife, and a never-disappointing prayer-history — is answered with a son God names Himself (19:7), whose dossier is Scripture, tenderness, innocence, parental kindness, and peace across three days (19:12–15).
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 6; the first of the surah's twin miracle-births. It sets the mercy/'abd/peace grammar that ruku 2 (Isa) completes and ruku 6 (19:93, 19:96) closes.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[19:1]`** "Kaf, Ha, Ya, Ayn, Saad."
  * **Gloss:** Transliterated letter-names, numbered as ayah 1, no semantic translation — a mercy-narrative begins on sound beyond translation.
* **`[19:2]`** "A mention of the mercy of your Lord towards His servant Zechariah." / **`[19:3]`** "When he called on his Lord, a call in seclusion."
  * **Gloss:** "A mention" (dhikr) declares what follows to be recited mercy; "His servant" tags the prototype; "a call in seclusion" is the surah's first detail of worship — privacy as sincerity.
* **`[19:4]`** "He said, “My Lord, my bones have become feeble, and my hair is aflame with gray, and never, Lord, have I been disappointed in my prayer to you."
  * **Gloss:** Complaint and ledger in one breath: physical decay stated, then the track record — "never disappointed" — offered as grounds for hope.
* **`[19:5]`** "“And I fear for my dependents after me, and my wife is barren. So grant me, from Yourself, an heir." / **`[19:6]`** "To inherit me, and inherit from the House of Jacob, and make him, my Lord, pleasing.”"
  * **Gloss:** The request is precise: lineage (House of Jacob) and "pleasing" — the heir's acceptance, not his résumé. "From Yourself" keeps the gift unmediated.
* **`[19:7]`** "“O Zechariah, We give you good news of a son, whose name is John, a name We have never given before.”"
  * **Gloss:** The answer arrives with the divine passive ("We give good news... We have never given") — even the name is an act of mercy.
* **`[19:8]`** "He said, “My Lord, how can I have a son, when my wife is barren, and I have become decrepit with old age?”"
  * **Gloss:** Bewilderment, not unbelief — the same "how" Mary will ask verbatim-style at 19:20; the surah is rhyming its two births.
* **`[19:9]`** "He said, “It will be so, your Lord says, ‘it is easy for me, and I created you before, when you were nothing.’”"
  * **Gloss:** The ex-nihilo argument answers the biological objection; "easy for Me" is a thread the surah pulls twice more (19:21, 19:97).
* **`[19:10]`** "He said, “My Lord, give me a sign.” He said, “Your sign is that you will not speak to the people for three nights straight.”"
  * **Gloss:** The sign is discipline, not proof — speech withdrawn; ruku 2 inverts this exactly: Mary is told to vow silence, and an infant speaks instead.
* **`[19:11]`** "And he came out to his people, from the sanctuary, and signaled to them to praise morning and evening."
  * **Gloss:** Mute, he still preaches — worship by gesture; "sanctuary" pairs the scene with 3:39's angels calling him there.
* **`[19:12]`** "“O John, hold on to the Scripture firmly,” and We gave him wisdom in his youth."
  * **Gloss:** The command precedes the capacity; wisdom (hikmah) is "given" — and given young, against every seniority instinct.
* **`[19:13]`** "And tenderness from Us, and innocence. He was devout." / **`[19:14]`** "And kind to his parents; and he was not a disobedient tyrant."
  * **Gloss:** Portrait in mercy-mode: even his tenderness is "from Us"; piety is cashed out as parental kindness, with its opposite ("jabbār 'aṣī" — "disobedient tyrant") named as the negative space.
* **`[19:15]`** "And peace be upon him the day he was born, and the day he dies, and the Day he is raised alive."
  * **Gloss:** Three days of peace bracket a life — birth, death, resurrection all greeted by divine salam; Isa will answer in the first person at 19:33.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Kaf, Ha, Ya, Ayn, Saad`** `[19:1]`: the disconnected letters; Itani transliterates and numbers, refuses to gloss.
  * **Elsewhere:** "Ta, Ha." `[20:1]`, "Noon." `[68:1]`, `[2:1]` — Maryam is one of the letter-openings whose letters precede a *narrative*, not a Book-claim; see Fleeting note (its roster includes 19:1).
* **`rahma (mercy)`** `[19:2]`: a noun — "the mercy of your Lord" is the thing being "mentioned," not an attribute essay.
  * **Rendering:** "mercy" at 19:2; "a mercy from Us" at 19:21; "freely of Our mercy" at 19:50; "out of Our mercy" at 19:53 (`rg -o "mercy" s019_maryam.md` — 4 hits).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:12]` "He has inscribed for Himself mercy" — the same object-language of mercy outside the surah.
* **`John (Yahya)`** `[19:7]`: named by God; "a name We have never given before."
  * **Elsewhere:** the annunciation at `[3:39]`, the grant at `[21:90]`, the lists at `[6:85]` and `[21:85]` — the naming-claim is unique to Maryam.
* **`'abd (servant)`** `[19:2]`: the prophet's title before any miracle.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[19:30]` "I am the servant of Allah" (the crib's first word), `[19:93]` all heaven-and-earth "will come to the Most Merciful as a servant", `[4:172]`, `[72:19]` — the surah's arc opens, hinges, and closes on 'ubudiyyah.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus) — intimate prayer-narratives, no law; fits the surah's mercy-address register.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** "a name We have never given before" is classically glossed as "no one previously bore this name (Yahya)". *Source: classical tafsir reports on 19:7 (Tabari/Ibn Kathir genre) — agent labeling; verify in print.* The heir's link to "the House of Jacob" hints a priestly line; *Source: classical reconstruction — verify; the ayah states no genealogy.*
