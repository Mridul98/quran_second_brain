# Maryam's withdrawal and the first word from the crib (19:16–40)
**UID:** 202610141653
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 19:16–40 (Ruku 2 of 6 in Maryam)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[19:16]` – `[19:40]` (25 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The mercy-grammar crosses from aged barrenness to untouched virginity: Mary withdraws, receives "Our spirit," labors at the palm-trunk, and when her people's accusation demands an answer she only points — the infant speaks, and his first miracle is a *sentence* beginning "I am the servant of Allah" (19:30); the surah then seals the infancy with tanzih ("It is not for Allah to have a child," 19:35) and hinges to eschatology (37–40).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 6 — the longest unit and the densest; it is the core cluster of the Quran's Isa-dossier. Verses 37–40 are the pivot: faction, the Day of Regret, divine inheritance — themes ruku 5–6 complete.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[19:16]`** "And mention in the Scripture Mary, when she withdrew from her people to an eastern location." / **`[19:17]`** "She screened herself away from them, and We sent to her Our spirit, and He appeared to her as an immaculate human."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's signature opener ("And mention in the Scripture") for the second time; withdrawal double-stated (withdrew, screened) — then "Our spirit" arrives in human form: mercy takes bodily shape.
* **`[19:18]`** "She said, “I take refuge from you in the Most Merciful, should you be righteous.”" / **`[19:19]`** "He said, “I am only the messenger of your Lord, to give you the gift of a pure son.”"
  * **Gloss:** Her refuge is named by the mercy-name; his self-limiting formula — "I am only the messenger" — is itself a tawhid lesson before the birth.
* **`[19:20]`** "She said, “How can I have a son, when no man has touched me, and I was never unchaste?”""
  * **Gloss:** Zechariah's "how" (19:8) rhymed; her defense pairs untouched with "never unchaste" — the text protects her reputation in the same breath.
* **`[19:21]`** "He said, “Thus said your Lord, `It is easy for Me, and We will make him a sign for humanity, and a mercy from Us. It is a matter already decided.'“" [sic — backtick in corpus]
  * **Gloss:** "Easy for Me" returns (↔ 19:9); the son is titled "a sign for humanity" and "a mercy from Us"; "a matter already decided" closes the debate before it starts.
* **`[19:22]`** "So she carried him, and secluded herself with him in a remote place."
  * **Gloss:** Conception stated plainly ("she carried him"), then a second withdrawal — seclusion is the surah's staging for mercy.
* **`[19:23]`** "The labor-pains came upon her, by the trunk of a palm-tree. She said, “I wish I had died before this, and been completely forgotten.”"
  * **Gloss:** The surah's rawest human line: pain, shame-fear, the wish for oblivion — honesty the text does not censure.
* **`[19:24]`** "Whereupon he called her from beneath her: “Do not worry; your Lord has placed a stream beneath you." / **`[19:25]`** "And shake the trunk of the palm-tree towards you, and it will drop ripe dates by you.”"
  * **Gloss:** Care answers despair: a stream, fresh dates — food and drink and beauty in one prescription; "do not worry" is the mercy-verb.
* **`[19:26]`** "“So eat, and drink, and be consoled. And if you see any human, say, ‘I have vowed a fast to the Most Gracious, so I will not speak to any human today.'“"
  * **Gloss:** The fast-vow carries silence — the surah's second speech-withdrawal (cf. 19:10); "be consoled" (qurra) answers her grief directly.
* **`[19:27]`** "Then she came to her people, carrying him. They said, “O Mary, you have done something terrible." / **`[19:28]`** "O sister of Aaron, your father was not an evil man, and your mother was not a whore.”"
  * **Gloss:** The accusation lands in lineage-idiom: "sister of Aaron" — an appeal to a priestly house (see §6); shame by family name is the very court she fled.
* **`[19:29]`** "So she pointed to him. They said, “How can we speak to an infant in the crib?”""
  * **Gloss:** Her defense attorney is the accused child; the crowd's question is the setup the whole passage has been building toward.
* **`[19:30]`** "He said, “I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture, and made me a prophet."
  * **Gloss:** The first miracle is a *sentence*, and its first word is 'ubudiyyah — the crib's opening claim is tawhid from the deified's own mouth.
* **`[19:31]`** "And has made me blessed wherever I may be; and has enjoined on me prayer and charity, so long as I live." / **`[19:32]`** "And kind to my mother, and He did not make me a disobedient rebel."
  * **Gloss:** Blessing is portable ("wherever I may be"); religion is enjoined as prayer-zakat-continuity; the Yahya portrait (19:13–14) is mirrored, down to the negated tyrant-rebel.
* **`[19:33]`** "So Peace is upon me the day I was born, and the day I die, and the Day I get resurrected alive.”"
  * **Gloss:** The three-day peace formula now in the first person — the infant quotes the blessing over himself.
* **`[19:34]`** "That is Jesus son of Mary—the Word of truth about which they doubt."
  * **Gloss:** The narrator re-enters: "the Word of truth" (kalimat al-haqq) — the disputed fact stated as the surah's own title for him.
* **`[19:35]`** "It is not for Allah to have a child—glory be to Him. To have anything done, He says to it, “Be,” and it becomes."
  * **Gloss:** The dogmatic exclusion plus the "Be" formula (cf. 2:117; 3:59) — sonship dissolved into creative fiat.
* **`[19:36]`** "“Allah is my Lord and your Lord, so worship Him. That is a straight path.”""
  * **Gloss:** Isa's ministry-creed, verbatim identical to 3:51 — the crib speech's tawhid is his whole message.
* **`[19:37]`** "But the various factions differed among themselves. So woe to those who disbelieve from the scene of a tremendous Day."
  * **Gloss:** The historical reaction ("factions" — shi'u) to the Isa-debate; woe brackets the doubt named in 19:34.
* **`[19:38]`** "Listen to them and watch for them the Day they come to Us. But the wrongdoers today are completely lost." / **`[19:39]`** "And warn them of the Day of Regret, when the matter will be concluded. Yet they are heedless, and they do not believe."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's first explicit eschatological imperative; "the Day of Regret" (yasra-hassara) — unique name in the corpus (`rg "Regret"` — only 19:39).
* **`[19:40]`** "It is We who will inherit the earth and everyone on it, and to Us they will be returned."
  * **Gloss:** The inheritance-theology that 19:77–80 will weaponize — one word (warith) answered by the only true Heir.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Our spirit`** `[19:17]`: the messenger "appeared... as an immaculate human."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[21:91]` "We breathed into her of Our spirit" — same story-compressed pair; `[16:102]` "The Holy Spirit has brought it down"; `[97:4]` "the angels and the Spirit... descend". Whether spirit = Gabriel is classically debated. *Source: classical identification — verify; the corpus only says "Our spirit."*
* **`'abd (servant of Allah)`** `[19:30]`: the crib's first phrase.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:46]` "He will speak to the people from the crib"; `[5:110]` "You spoke to the people from the crib" — the speech prophesied at annunciation is executed here; `[4:172]` "The Messiah does not disdain to be a servant of Allah" — the direct theological gloss of this line.
* **`the Word of truth`** `[19:34]`: the surah's own titulary for Isa amid the doubt.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:45]` "a Word from Him"; `[3:39]` John "confirming a Word from Allah" — the Word-language spans both births of this surah; cf. k-kalam.
* **`the Day of Regret`** `[19:39]`: unique divine-Day epithet in the corpus (`rg "Regret" corpus/` — single hit, 19:39).
  * **Elsewhere:** none — the name is this surah's; its classical gloss (a Day on which the wrongdoer regrets, when "the matter will be concluded") stays inside the ayah's own frame. *Source: epithet-uniqueness verified; gloss beyond the text = classical — verify.*

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus); the Isa-material here is the Quran's most sustained infancy narrative outside Ali Imran, and the "factions differed" of 19:37 reads as addressed to the Meccan debate about angels/sons/divine progeny. *Source: standard reading — verify.*
* **Narrative context:** "O sister of Aaron" is classically glossed as "of the priestly house/named for a pious eponym," not literal siblinghood, since the surah itself mentions Aaron centuries later (19:53). *Source: classical reports (also a transmitted answer of the Prophet to this objection — verify in print).* The Arabic idiom behind "crib" (miḥrab/ji'ḍāʿ) is rendered "crib" here and "sanctuary" at 19:11/3:39 — translator's scene-shifting choice. *Source: observable term-choice.*
