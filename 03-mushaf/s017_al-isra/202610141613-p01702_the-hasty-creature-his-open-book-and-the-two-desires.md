# The hasty creature, his open book, and the two desires (17:11–22)
**UID:** 202610141613
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 17:11–22 (Ruku 2 of 12 in al-Isra’)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[17:11]` – `[17:22]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah turns from salvation-history to the human who receives it: hasty in prayer (11), issued day-and-night clocks (12), already carrying his fate tied to his neck and a scroll he alone will read (13–14), governed by a God who never punishes before sending (15) — then sorted by desire: the fleeting expedited, the Hereafter appreciated (18–21), until the first decree-word opens the moral block (22).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 12; the bridge between the two scriptures (ruku 1) and the decree (ruku 3–4); the personal book here (13–14) and the national roll-call at 17:71 form the surah's ledger bracket.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[17:11]`** The human being prays for evil as he prays for good. The human being is very hasty.
  * **Gloss:** Prayer gone wrong: man calls on evil with the same urgency he calls on good; "very hasty" ('ajul) diagnoses the Meccan impatience motif as a species trait.
* **`[17:12]`** We have made the night and the day two wonders. We erased the wonder of the night, and made the wonder of the day revealing, that you may seek bounty from your Lord, and know the number of years, and the calculation. We have explained all things in detail.
  * **Gloss:** Night and day as two signs-of-sight: the night's erased, the day made revealing — for labor ("seek bounty"), for calendary reckoning; "We have explained all things in detail" makes the clock-book a sibling of the revealed book.
* **`[17:13]`** For every person We have attached his fate to his neck. And on the Day of Resurrection, We will bring out for him a book which he will find spread open.
  * **Gloss:** Every person's fate is attached to his neck; on the Day a book comes out spread open — the neck-image elsewhere carries punishment-yokes; here it carries the self's own record.
* **`[17:14]`** “Read your book; today there will be none but yourself to call you to account.”
  * **Gloss:** The courtroom's single instruction — "Read your book; today there will be none but yourself to call you to account" — the self as its own witness.
* **`[17:15]`** Whoever is guided—is guided for his own good. And whoever goes astray—goes astray to his detriment. No burdened soul carries the burdens of another, nor do We ever punish until We have sent a messenger.
  * **Gloss:** Three axioms in one ayah: guidance and error accrue to the self; no bearer bears another's load; and God's justice is procedurally bound — punishment never precedes a messenger.
* **`[17:16]`** When We decide to destroy a town, We command its affluent ones, they transgress in it, so the word becomes justified against it, and We destroy it completely.
  * **Gloss:** The sociology of ruin: when destruction is decided, the affluent are "commanded" and transgress, the word falls justified, the town is erased — sin as the self-confirming verdict.
* **`[17:17]`** How many generations have We destroyed after Noah? Your Lord is sufficient as Knower and Beholder of the sins of his servants.
  * **Gloss:** Generations since Noah counted; "Your Lord is sufficient as Knower and Beholder of the sins of his servants" — no ledger is needed for Him, and none escapes Him.
* **`[17:18]`** Whoever desires the fleeting life, We expedite for him what We decide to give him, to whomever We desire. Then We consign him to Hell, where he will roast, condemned and defeated.
  * **Gloss:** Whoever wants the fleeting (ajil) is expedited what We decide, for whomever We will — provision can be pure delivery, not commendation; the road ends roasted, condemned, defeated.
* **`[17:19]`** But whoever desires the Hereafter, and pursues it as it should be pursued, while he is a believer; these—their effort will be appreciated.
  * **Gloss:** Whoever wants the Hereafter and pursues it as it should be pursued, while believing — their effort is "appreciated" (mashkura): the shukr root of 17:3 returns as the reward-word.
* **`[17:20]`** To all—these and those—We extend from the gifts of your Lord. The gifts of your Lord are not restricted.
  * **Gloss:** Both crews are fed from the Lord's gifts, and the gifts are "not restricted" — worldly provision is indiscriminate; only the account distinguishes.
* **`[17:21]`** See how We have favored some of them over others; yet the Hereafter is greater in ranks, and greater in favors.
  * **Gloss:** "See how We have favored some over others" — inequality is displayed, then relativized: the Hereafter is greater in ranks and greater in favors.
* **`[17:22]`** Do not set up another god with Allah, lest you become condemned and damned.
  * **Gloss:** The decree's first word and the ring's first bracket: no other god with Allah — else condemned (malam) and damned (sa'aq), the same pair closing at 17:39.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`'ajul` (very hasty)** `[17:11]`:
  * **Rendering:** "The human being is very hasty" — paired with praying for evil as for good.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[21:37]` "The human being was created of haste" — the creation-grammar of the same trait; `[13:6]` "they urge you to hasten evil before good"; `[41:49]` "never tires of praying for good things" — the mirror of 17:11's double prayer.
* **`kitab` (the personal book)** `[17:13–14]`:
  * **Rendering:** "attached his fate to his neck"; "a book which he will find spread open"; "Read your book."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[69:19]` "Here, take my book and read it"; `[84:7]`–`[84:12]` given in the right hand / behind the back; `[81:10]` records made public; punitive necks elsewhere (`[13:5]`, `[36:8]`, `[40:71]`) sharpen 17:13's neutral, personal record.
* **`no punishment until a messenger`** `[17:15]`:
  * **Rendering:** "nor do We ever punish until We have sent a messenger," bound to the non-transferable-burden clause.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:164]`, `[35:18]`, `[53:38]` no soul bears another's burdens; `[16:36]` "To every community We sent a messenger"; `[20:134]` the damned wish "if only You had sent us a messenger" — the rule of 17:15 is that wish's answer.
* **`another god`** `[17:22]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Do not set up another god with Allah, lest you become condemned and damned."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:213]` "else you will be of those tormented"; `[15:96]`; `[50:26]`; and inside this surah the identical idiom closes the decree ring at `[17:39]`.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus); the rush-and-expedite scene (17:11, 18) fits the Meccan punishment-impatience cluster (`[13:6]`, `[22:47]`, `[46:35]`).
* **Note on 17:16's rendering:** Itani prints "We command its affluent ones" for what the tafsir literature reads as "We command its most luxurious, they transgress" (amarna mutrafaha); a variant takes amarna as "We became many [among them]" — *Source: classical variant collection (al-Tabari's reading-list on 17:16) — agent reconstruction; verify in print.* The observable corpus fact is the pairing: command → transgression → justified word → destruction.
