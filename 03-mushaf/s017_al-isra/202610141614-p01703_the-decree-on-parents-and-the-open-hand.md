# The decree on parents and the open hand (17:23–30)
**UID:** 202610141614
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 17:23–30 (Ruku 3 of 12 in al-Isra’)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[17:23]` – `[17:30]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The heart of al-Isra's decree: worship reduced to One, and the first right after God assigned to parents — down to the grammar of a single syllable of disrespect — then the road-people (relative, poor, wayfarer) claimed against the owner's wealth, squandering named as devil-fraternity, and the hand legislated between clenched fist and open palm while provision itself expands and restricts by divine will.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 12; the first half of the decree ring (22–39); the passage where the surah's theology of names (Knower, Observant, Forgiving) meets its economics.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[17:23]`** Your Lord has commanded that you worship none but Him, and that you be good to your parents. If either of them or both of them reach old age with you, do not say to them a word of disrespect, nor scold them, but say to them kind words.
  * **Gloss:** "Your Lord has decreed" (qad) — the decree verb; tawhid first, then ihsan to parents; the scene is domestic old age; the prohibition reaches the smallest sound of contempt ("a word of disrespect") and the rebuke; kind words (qawlan karima) are the commanded minimum.
* **`[17:24]`** And lower to them the wing of humility, out of mercy, and say, “My Lord, have mercy on them, as they raised me when I was a child.”
  * **Gloss:** The posture (the wing of humility lowered, khafid) and the child's prayer for the parents who raised him — mercy asked for them as it was given in childhood.
* **`[17:25]`** Your Lord knows best what is in your minds. If you are righteous—He is Forgiving to the obedient.
  * **Gloss:** God knows what is in the minds; if you are righteous, He is "Forgiving to the obedient" (the one who turns, awwab) — interiority legislated by a Knower, with repentance built into the law.
* **`[17:26]`** And give the relative his rights, and the poor, and the wayfarer, and do not squander wastefully.
  * **Gloss:** Three road-people get their haqq: relative, poor, wayfarer; and wasteful spending (tabdhir) is banned — the same triad as `[30:38]` nearly verbatim.
* **`[17:27]`** The extravagant are brethren of the devils, and the devil is ever ungrateful to his Lord.
  * **Gloss:** The squanderers are the devils' brethren — and the devil "is ever ungrateful (kafur) to his Lord": the gratitude ledger of 17:3/19 returns as the diagnosis of waste.
* **`[17:28]`** But if you turn away from them, seeking mercy from your Lord which you hope for, then say to them words of comfort.
  * **Gloss:** The clause for the unable giver: if you must turn away, hoping for God's mercy you await, still speak "words of comfort" — the law legislates kindness for the poor.
* **`[17:29]`** And do not keep your hand tied to your neck, nor spread it out fully, lest you end up liable and regretful.
  * **Gloss:** Neither a hand tied to the neck (clenched) nor spread all the way open — the two failure modes ending liable and regretful; the idiom answers the Jews' taunt "Allah's hand is tied" at `[5:64]`.
* **`[17:30]`** Your Lord expands the provision for whomever He wills, and restricts it. He is fully Informed, Observant of His servants.
  * **Gloss:** Provision (rizq) expands and restricts for whom He wills — "fully Informed, Observant" — the metaphysical ground making 17:29's moderation rational rather than stoic.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`qad rabbaka` (Your Lord has decreed)** `[17:23]`:
  * **Rendering:** the decree verb introducing parents immediately after God's oneness.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:83]` the covenant formula pairs the same two duties — "Worship none but Allah; and be good to parents"; `[4:36]` identical sequence (partners—parents); `[6:151]` "that you honor your parents" inside the forbidden-list sermon; `[46:15]` "We have enjoined upon man kindness to his parents" — the parent-command is a stable kitabi pair with tawhid.
* **`wing of humility`** `[17:24]`:
  * **Rendering:** "lower to them the wing of humility, out of mercy."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[15:88]` "lower your wing to the believers" and `[26:215]` the same verb for the community of believers — the parental wing is the domestic case of the prophetic gesture (`rg "lower.*wing"` — 3 hits total).
* **`kafur` (ungrateful)** `[17:27]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the devil is ever ungrateful to his Lord" — ingratitude as the devil's epithet.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:83]` "they recognize Allah's blessing, but then deny it, as most of them are ungrateful"; `[25:50]` "most people persist in thanklessness"; `[2:88]` cursed "for their ingratitude" — the k-shukr vocabulary in full charge.
* **`hand tied / spread`** `[17:29]`:
  * **Rendering:** "do not keep your hand tied to your neck, nor spread it out fully."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:64]` "The Jews say, 'Allah's hand is tied.' It is their hands that are tied… His hands are outstretched" — the polemical mirror image; `[25:67]` the believers are "neither wasteful nor stingy, but choose a middle course between that" — the same two-extremes economics.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)

* **Parallel:** `[6:151–152]` — the an-An'am sermon that runs the same decree cluster (no partners → parents → no killing children → no approaching indecency → orphan's property in the best way → full measure): the two surahs teach the same table.
* **Parallel:** `[5:64]` — see Key Terms; 17:29's proverb is also a quiet rebuttal of a tied-hand caricature of God.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus) — the parents-and-provision decree is preached to a tribal audience whose kin-economy it legislates.
* **Tafsir note:** the "word of disrespect" is classically linked to the syllable "uff"; the two-extremes reading of 17:29 (iqtar vs tabdhir) is standard in Tabari/Kathir on 17:29. *Source: classical tafsir idiom — agent labeling; verify in print.*
