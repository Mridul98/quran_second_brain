# The night journey and the two scriptures (17:1–10)
**UID:** 202610141612
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 17:1–10 (Ruku 1 of 12 in al-Isra’)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[17:1]` – `[17:10]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens in tasbih over a transported servant — Sacred Mosque to Farthest Mosque — and pivots at once from shown wonders to given books: the Torah made a guide for Israel, whose double corruption and double restoration is prophesied inside it, then the Quran's rival claim to "guide to what is most upright," closing with the two-audience cut (believers / Hereafter-deniers) that the whole surah will keep re-drawing.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 12; sets the surah's coordinates — vision (1), two scriptures (2, 9), Israel's promise-cycle (4–8), the sorting (10) — every one of which returns later (the open book at 13, the barrier at 45, "the promise of our Lord is fulfilled" at 108).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[17:1]`** Glory to Him who journeyed His servant by night, from the Sacred Mosque, to the Farthest Mosque, whose precincts We have blessed, in order to show him of Our wonders. He is the Listener, the Beholder.
  * **Gloss:** Subhanahu first, then the journey: a servant carried by night between two named mosques, one of them "blessed in its precincts"; the vision's purpose is "to show him of Our wonders," and the ayah closes on "the Listener, the Beholder" — divine perception grounding what was seen.
* **`[17:2]`** And We gave Moses the Scripture, and made it a guide for the Children of Israel: Take none for protector other than Me.
  * **Gloss:** The journey's cargo is a book: Moses receives the Scripture as a guide (hudan) for the Children of Israel, its content one protective command — "Take none for protector other than Me."
* **`[17:3]`** The descendants of those We carried with Noah. He was an appreciative servant.
  * **Gloss:** The addressed people are genealogically marked — descendants of those carried with Noah — and Noah is named "an appreciative servant," planting the gratitude ledger the surah audits (27, 67, 69).
* **`[17:4]`** And We conveyed to the Children of Israel in the Scripture: You will commit evil on earth twice, and you will rise to a great height.
  * **Gloss:** Inside that same Scripture, God conveys to Israel what will happen: evil on earth twice, and a great rising to a height — prophecy as covenant-consequence, not fatalism.
* **`[17:5]`** When the first of the two promises came true, We sent against you servants of Ours, possessing great might, and they ransacked your homes. It was a promise fulfilled.
  * **Gloss:** The first promise comes true: servants of "great might" sent against them, ransacking homes; "It was a promise fulfilled" — history as the Book's word cashing out.
* **`[17:6]`** Then We gave you back your turn against them, and supplied you with wealth and children, and made you more numerous.
  * **Gloss:** The reversal: Israel's turn against the oppressors, backed with wealth, children, and numbers — elevation is also a gift, and neither gift nor defeat is endorsement.
* **`[17:7]`** If you work righteousness, you work righteousness for yourselves; and if you commit evil, you do so against yourselves. Then, when the second promise comes true, they will make your faces filled with sorrow, and enter the Temple as they entered it the first time, and utterly destroy all that falls into their power.
  * **Gloss:** The moral key: righteousness and evil both accrue "to yourselves"; then the second promise — sorrowful faces, a second entry into the Temple "as they entered it the first time," and total devastation of what they seize.
* **`[17:8]`** Perhaps your Lord will have mercy on you. But if you revert, We will revert. We have made Hell a prison for the disbelievers.
  * **Gloss:** An open ending ("Perhaps your Lord will have mercy"), a symmetric threat ("if you revert, We will revert"), and the fixed sentence: Hell made a prison for the disbelievers.
* **`[17:9]`** This Quran guides to what is most upright; and it gives good news to the believers who do good deeds, that they will have a great reward.
  * **Gloss:** This Quran guides to what is most upright (al-latī hiya aqwam) and bears good news of great reward — the guide-office transferred from the Torah (17:2) to the Quran, in one breath.
* **`[17:10]`** And those who do not believe in the Hereafter—We have prepared for them a painful punishment.
  * **Gloss:** The cut: those who do not believe in the Hereafter have a painful punishment prepared — the same pair (9–10) that 17:45 will re-open as perceptual architecture.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`isra'` ("journeyed")** `[17:1]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Glory to Him who journeyed His servant by night" — the causative of asra; God moves the servant.
  * **Elsewhere:** the verb recurs only as ordinary travel: `[22:46]`, `[35:44]`, `[40:82]`, `[47:10]` "Have they not journeyed in/through the land" (`rg "journeyed"` — 5 hits total; the night-journey usage is unique here).
* **`al-Masjid al-Aqsa` (Farthest Mosque)** `[17:1]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the Farthest Mosque, whose precincts We have blessed" — the phrase is unique in the corpus (`rg "Farthest"` — 1 hit).
  * **Elsewhere:** the counter-term "Sacred Mosque" has 14 hits (`[2:144]`, `[5:2]`, `[9:19]`, `[22:25]`, `[48:27]`…); the blessed-ground idiom ↔ `[7:137]` "the land, which We had blessed" and `[28:30]` "the Blessed Spot."
* **`shakur` (appreciative)** `[17:3]`:
  * **Rendering:** "He was an appreciative servant" — Noah named in gratitude terms.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[31:12]` "whoever is appreciative—is appreciative for the benefit of his own soul"; `[3:144]`/`[3:145]` Allah rewards the appreciative; `[34:13]` "O House of David, work with appreciation" — the shukr dossier (k-shukr) that this surah runs against kafur (17:27, 67, 69).
* **`twice`** `[17:4]`:
  * **Rendering:** "You will commit evil on earth twice, and you will rise to a great height."
  * **Elsewhere:** ordinals are rare: `[2:229]` divorce twice, `[9:101]` punished twice, `[40:11]` died twice and made alive twice — 17:4's "twice" is the surah's structural prophecy of Israel.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus). *Source: classical surah-introductions (Ibn Kathir on surah 17) — verify in print.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the Isra's dating sits in the hadith/sirah corpus, not in this text.
