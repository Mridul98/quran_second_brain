# The human-messenger scandal and the withheld treasuries (17:94–100)
**UID:** 202610141622
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 17:94–100 (Ruku 11 of 12 in al-Isra’)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[17:94]` – `[17:100]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The diagnosis of unbelief in one line: nothing prevented belief when guidance came except the offense that God sent a human messenger (94) — answered by the counterfactual: had angels walked the earth in peace, an angel would have been sent (95); then the courtroom's witness is God alone (96), and the sorting verdict — guided or left astray, no protectors, gathered on their faces blind-dumb-deaf, the blaze renewed whenever it abates (97) — with its crime named: blasphemy at the revelations and the bone-yard taunt (98) and its answer: the heavens-and-earth Creator can create the likes of them on an appointed term beyond doubt (99). The unit closes on the economy of mercy: if you held God's mercy-treasuries, you would strangle them for fear of spending — man was created stingy (100).
* **Placement:** Ruku 11 of 12; the messenger-argument ruku between the challenge (88–93) and the Moses-coda (101–104); 94's "what prevented them" re-runs 18:55, and 100 re-opens 26–30's provision-ethic at the level of the soul.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[17:94]`** Nothing prevented the people from believing, when guidance has come to them, except that they said, “Did Allah send a human messenger?”
  * **Gloss:** The only blocker named: "Did Allah send a human messenger?" — guidance accepted, the scandal of its carrier rejected; the same "nothing prevented… when guidance came" formula as 18:55.
* **`[17:95]`** Say, “If there were angels on earth, walking around in peace, We would have sent down to them from heaven an angel messenger.”
  * **Gloss:** The ecological principle of messenger-selection: send angels only to a world of angels walking around in peace — cf. 43:60's "angels as successors" and 6:8's "had We sent an angel, the matter would have been settled."
* **`[17:96]`** Say, “Allah is enough witness between you and me. He is fully aware of His servants, and He sees them well.”
  * **Gloss:** "Allah is enough witness between you and me" — the office under divine surveillance (13:43/29:52 formula), "fully aware of His servants and He sees them well" (basir).
* **`[17:97]`** Whomever Allah guides is the guided one. And whomever He leaves astray—for them you will find no protectors apart from Him. And We will gather them on the Day of Resurrection, on their faces, blind, dumb, and deaf. Their abode is Hell; whenever it abates, We intensify the blaze for them.
  * **Gloss:** The guidance-formula verbatim with 7:178 ("Whomever Allah guides is the guided one"); for the astray no protectors; the gathering: on their faces, blind, dumb, and deaf (2:18's trait-trio), Hell's blaze renewed each time it subsides — cf. 25:34's worse position.
* **`[17:98]`** This is their repayment for having blasphemed against Our revelations, and having said, “Shall we, when we have become bones and fragments, be resurrected as a new creation?”
  * **Gloss:** The sentence's two charges: blaspheming the revelations, and the bones-and-fragments/new-creation taunt quoted word-for-word as at 49 — the sneer now read back into the verdict.
* **`[17:99]`** Do they not consider that Allah, Who created the heavens and the earth, is Able to create the likes of them? He has assigned for them a term, in which there is no doubt. But the wrongdoers persist in denying the truth.
  * **Gloss:** The resurrection-argument from power: the Creator of heavens and earth can create their like; "He has assigned for them a term, in which there is no doubt" (cf. 45:26, 7:34); the wrongdoers still persist in denial (anid again).
* **`[17:100]`** Say, “If you possessed the treasuries of my Lord’s mercy, you would have withheld them for fear of spending.” The human being has always been stingy.
  * **Gloss:** The psychology of the withheld hand: own the treasuries of mercy, and you would clutch them "for fear of spending" — bakhil, the human default; 38:9 asks rhetorically whether anyone holds those treasuries at all.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`human messenger`** `[17:94]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Did Allah send a human messenger?" — the offense stated in the interrogative.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:8]` "Why was an angel not sent down to him"; `[25:7]` the marketplace-walking messenger mocked; `[43:60]` angels-as-successors counterfactual — the bashar-messenger theodicy is the Meccan debate's fixed point.
* **`gathered on their faces`** `[17:97]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We will gather them… on their faces, blind, dumb, and deaf."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:34]` "herded into Hell on their faces—those are in a worse position, and further astray from the way"; `[27:90]` "their faces will be tumbled into the Fire"; deaf-dumb-blind as a trait set: `[2:18]`, `[8:22]`, `[5:71]` — and 17:97 re-combines the 72 blindness with the face-idiom.
* **`term (ajal)`** `[17:99]`:
  * **Rendering:** "a term, in which there is no doubt."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:34]` "For every nation is an appointed time. When their time has come, they cannot delay"; `[45:26]` "He gathers you for the Day of Resurrection, about which there is no doubt"; `[6:2]` "decided a term" — the term is fixed, the doubt is theirs.
* **`treasuries / stingy (khazn, bakhil)`** `[17:100]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the treasuries of my Lord's mercy… you would have withheld them for fear of spending."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[38:9]` "Or do they possess the treasuries of the mercy of your Lord—the Majestic, the Giver of Gifts?" — near-verbatim partner; `[52:37]` "the treasuries of your Lord?"; `[6:50]`/`[11:31]` prophets disclaim treasure-possession; `[4:37]` the stingy who exhort stinginess; `[47:38]` "whoever withholds is withholding against his own soul."

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus); the messenger-counterfactual (95) belongs to the Meccan angel-demand debate (6:8–9, 25:7–8).
* **Reading note:** 17:100's bakhil-line is the surah's third anthropological default after 'ajul (11) and kafur (67) — one portrait in three vocabularies. *Source: agent observation across the ruku set; verify.*
