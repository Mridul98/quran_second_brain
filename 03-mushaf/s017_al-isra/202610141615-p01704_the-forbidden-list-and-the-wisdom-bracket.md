# The forbidden list and the wisdom bracket (17:31–40)
**UID:** 202610141615
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 17:31–40 (Ruku 4 of 12 in al-Isra’)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[17:31]` – `[17:40]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The decree's prohibitions run to the threshold: children, chastity, the sacred soul, the orphan's property, the scale, the faculty of knowledge itself, and the gait — every "do not" closing on the verdict that such evil is hated by your Lord; the ring-word (no other god) shuts the block, and the one shirk named in full — daughters-for-God, sons-for-yourselves — is answered as the "terrible thing" the whole list was protecting against.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 12; the second half of the decree ring (22–39, with 40 as its application); the surah's ethics-table before it turns to the Quran's dividing effect (41).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[17:31]`** And do not kill your children for fear of poverty. We provide for them, and for you. Killing them is a grave sin.
  * **Gloss:** Killing children "for fear of poverty" banned — and answered with provision-first grammar: "We provide for them, and for you"; infanticide named a "grave sin" (kabiran).
* **`[17:32]`** And do not come near adultery. It is immoral, and an evil way.
  * **Gloss:** Not adultery only but its approach banned; labeled fahisha and "an evil way" (sa'a sabila) — the ethics of thresholds.
* **`[17:33]`** And do not kill the soul which Allah has made sacred, except in the course of justice. If someone is killed unjustly, We have given his next of kin certain authority. But he should not be excessive in killing, for he will be supported.
  * **Gloss:** The sacred soul killed only "in the course of justice"; the victim's next of kin given authority (sultan) with a cap on excess in killing — retaliation under divine surveillance ("he will be supported").
* **`[17:34]`** And do not go near the orphan’s property, except with the best of intentions, until he has reached his maturity. And honor your pledge, because the pledge involves responsibility.
  * **Gloss:** The orphan's property approached only in the best way until maturity — and the pledge itself added: "the pledge involves responsibility" (was su'alan), every promise interrogated at the trial.
* **`[17:35]`** And give full measure when you measure, and weigh with accurate scales. That is fair, and the best determination.
  * **Gloss:** Full measure and accurate scales — "fair, and the best determination" — commerce as eschatology (cf. the defrauders' woe at 83:1–3).
* **`[17:36]`** And do not occupy yourself with what you have no knowledge of. The hearing, and the sight, and the brains—all these will be questioned.
  * **Gloss:** The epistemic command: do not pursue what you have no knowledge of; hearing, sight, and the brains (af'idah) — each is questioned. The faculties are summoned as defendants.
* **`[17:37]`** And do not walk proudly on earth. You can neither pierce the earth, nor can you match the mountains in height.
  * **Gloss:** No strutting: you cannot split the earth open nor out-height the mountains — a geometry lesson against pride, Luqman's twin.
* **`[17:38]`** The evil of all these is disliked by your Lord.
  * **Gloss:** The summary verdict: "The evil of all these is disliked by your Lord" — the whole list compressed into one moral taxonomy.
* **`[17:39]`** That is some of the wisdom your Lord has revealed to you. Do not set up with Allah another god, or else you will be thrown in Hell, rebuked and banished.
  * **Gloss:** "That is some of the wisdom your Lord has revealed to you" — the decree is hikma; and the ring-word returns verbatim-in-function: no other god, else Hell, rebuked and banished (22's pair restated).
* **`[17:40]`** Has your Lord favored you with sons, while choosing for Himself daughters from among the angels? You are indeed saying a terrible thing.
  * **Gloss:** The concrete shirk named: sons reserved for yourselves, "daughters from among the angels" assigned to Him — "You are indeed saying a terrible thing," said of the arbitrators' genealogy.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fear of poverty`** `[17:31]`:
  * **Rendering:** "do not kill your children for fear of poverty. We provide for them, and for you."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:151]` "do not kill your children because of poverty—We provide for you and for them" (the order reversed); `[6:140]` "kill their children foolishly, with no basis in knowledge" — the 17:31/6:151 provision-order difference is observable in the corpus; classical commentators see 17:31 starting with the children's provision — *Source: agent observation + widely reported tafsir nuance; verify in print.*
* **`orphan's property / pledge`** `[17:34]`:
  * **Rendering:** "except with the best of intentions, until he has reached his maturity… honor your pledge, because the pledge involves responsibility."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:152]` near-verbatim ("do not come near the property of the orphan, except with the best intentions, until he reaches maturity"); `[5:13]` breaking the pledge → curse; `[23:8]`/`[70:32]` "faithful to their trusts and their pledges" — the covenant vocabulary inside domestic law.
* **`questioned (mas'ul)`** `[17:34,36]`:
  * **Rendering:** the pledge "involves responsibility"; the faculties "will be questioned."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:78]` and `[32:9]` issue the same hearing-sight-brains triad "that you may give thanks" / as divine endowment; `[43:19]` claims "recorded, and they will be questioned" — accountability vocabulary shared with 17:13–15.
* **`daughters`** `[17:40]`:
  * **Rendering:** "choosing for Himself daughters from among the angels?"
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:149]` verbatim twin ("Are the daughters for your Lord, while for them the sons?"); `[43:16]`; `[16:57]` "they attribute to Allah daughters—exalted is He"; `[53:19–23]` al-Lat and al-Uzza named as "nothing but names" — the surah's bracket closes on the shirk it actually confronts.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus); the list targets Meccan practice (female-infanticide attribution, the angelic-daughters claim) as well as universal law.
* **Numerology warning:** the label "decalogue" for 17:22–39 is a modern comparative convenience; the classical lists enumerate sins without a fixed ten-count. *Source: agent inference — verify; do not present "ten commandments" as transmitted.*
