# Nine signs, the fallen scholars, and the closing tasbih (17:101–111)
**UID:** 202610141623
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 17:101–111 (Ruku 12 of 12 in al-Isra’)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[17:101]` – `[17:111]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah closes its own opening covenant-story: Moses given nine clear signs, called bewitched by Pharaoh, answering with "eye openers," resolved to scare them off the land and drowned — and Israel told "Inhabit the land" until the gathering (101–104). Then the Quran's own descent is stated — with truth, in stages, unfolded — and its right audience shown: the given-knowledge falling to their chins, weeping, "it adds to their humility" (105–109). The last two words are worship: call Him Allah or the Most Merciful — whichever, the Best Names — pray neither loud nor silent; and the final hamd denies son, partner, and weakness-ally, commanding constant glorification (110–111).
* **Placement:** Ruku 12 of 12; the ring-closure: Moses/Israel material (2–8) returns (101–104), the promise-cycle (5) ends as the scholars' confession (108), and 17:1's "Glory to Him" answers 17:111's "glorify Him constantly."

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[17:101]`** We gave Moses nine clear signs—ask the Children of Israel. When he went to them, Pharaoh said to him, “I think that you, Moses, are bewitched.”
  * **Gloss:** The count fixed: nine clear signs — the same tally at 27:12 — with the proof-text move "ask the Children of Israel" (2:211 verbatim in method); Pharaoh's diagnosis is the surah's recurring slur: "bewitched" (17:47's accusation spoken by the type).
* **`[17:102]`** He said, “You know that none sent these down except the Lord of the heavens and the earth—eye openers; and I think that you, Pharaoh, are doomed.”
  * **Gloss:** Moses' answer: none sent these but the Lord of heavens and earth — "eye openers" (basa'ir, Itani's hapax rendering) — and he turns the spell: "I think you… doomed."
* **`[17:103]`** He resolved to scare them off the land, but We drowned him, and those with him, altogether.
  * **Gloss:** The intent to scare Israel off the land, and the drowning of Pharaoh and his company "altogether" (cf. 7:136, 26:66) — the 17:76 expulsion-pattern inverted onto its plotter.
* **`[17:104]`** After him, We said to the Children of Israel, “Inhabit the land, and when the promise of the Hereafter arrives, We will bring you all together.”
  * **Gloss:** "Inhabit the land" (7:137's blessed-land promise in one line), and when the promise of the Hereafter arrives, "We will bring you all together" — the mixed gathering, answering 17:71's roll-call.
* **`[17:105]`** With the truth We sent it down, and with the truth it descended. We sent you only as a bearer of good news and a warner.
  * **Gloss:** The descent double-phrase — with truth sent down, with truth descended — the cargo and the trajectory both al-haqq (cf. 3:3, 26:193); the office restated: "only a bearer of good news and a warner" (2:119, 25:56).
* **`[17:106]`** A Quran which We unfolded gradually, that you may recite to the people over time. And We revealed it in stages.
  * **Gloss:** Gradualism as design: unfolded that it may be recited "to the people over time," revealed in stages (ma' — the phrase shared verbatim with 25:32's answer to the instant-revelation demand).
* **`[17:107]`** Say, “Believe in it, or do not believe.” Those who were given knowledge before it, when it is recited to them, they fall to their chins, prostrating.
  * **Gloss:** The dare "believe in it, or do not believe" — and the counter-audience: those given knowledge before it fall to their chins (dhiqan), prostrating (28:52's twin: "Those to whom We gave the Scripture before it believe in it").
* **`[17:108]`** And they say, “Glory to our Lord. The promise of our Lord is fulfilled.”
  * **Gloss:** Their confession: "Glory to our Lord. The promise of our Lord is fulfilled" — 17:5's promise-line, now prayed by the humble.
* **`[17:109]`** And they fall to their chins, weeping, and it adds to their humility.
  * **Gloss:** The second chin-fall, weeping, and the motif reversed: "it adds to their humility" — where 41/60/82 multiplied ruin, the same verb now multiplies khushu' (cf. 19:58's prostrating-and-weeping).
* **`[17:110]`** Say, “Call Him Allah, or call Him the Most Merciful. Whichever name you use, to Him belong the Best Names.” And be neither loud in your prayer, nor silent in it, but follow a course in between.
  * **Gloss:** The Names: call Him Allah or the Most Merciful (ar-Rahman) — "to Him belong the Best Names" (7:180/59:24) — and the prayer's register: neither loud nor silent, "a course in between" (cf. 25:67's middle, 31:19's lowered voice).
* **`[17:111]`** And say, “Praise be to Allah, who has not begotten a son, nor has He a partner in sovereignty, nor has He an ally out of weakness, and glorify Him constantly.”
  * **Gloss:** The closing hamd: Praise be to Allah (1:2's opening) — no begotten son, no partner in sovereignty, no ally out of weakness (cf. 25:2's near-verbatim triple, 72:3, 112:3) — "and glorify Him constantly": the surah ends where 17:1 began, in tasbih.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`nine clear signs`** `[17:101]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We gave Moses nine clear signs—ask the Children of Israel."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[27:12]` "among nine miracles to Pharaoh and his people" — the corpus' only other enumeration; `[2:211]` "Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them" — the ask method paired verbatim.
* **`Best Names`** `[17:110]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Call Him Allah, or call Him the Most Merciful… the Best Names."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:180]` "To Allah belong the Most Beautiful Names, so call Him by them"; `[59:24]`/`[20:8]` "His are the Most Beautiful Names" — one Arabic idiom (al-asma' al-husna), two renderings in one edition ("Best" here, "Most Beautiful" there); the drift is an observable translator's choice (*Source: translator's term choice (observable in corpus)*).
* **`fall to their chins (dhiqan)`** `[17:107,109]`:
  * **Rendering:** prostration named by the chin's landing, twice.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[36:8]` shackles "up to their chins, so they are stiff-necked" — the chin in the corpus is otherwise bound, never bowed; `[19:58]` "they would fall down, prostrating and weeping" — the scholars' posture is the righteous remnant's idiom.
* **`in stages (manzar?)`** `[17:106]`:
  * **Rendering:** "And We revealed it in stages."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:32]` verbatim same phrase; `[97:1]` "We sent it down on the Night of Decree"; `[2:97]` Gabriel "revealed it to your heart by Allah's leave" — the descent vocabulary this surah has been building since 17:2 and 17:9.
