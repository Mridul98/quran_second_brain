# Successful are the believers — the success-list and the road from clay to resurrection (23:1–22)
**UID:** 202610141702
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 23:1–22 (Ruku 1 of 6 in al-Mu’minun)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[23:1]` – `[23:22]` (22 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah issues the verdict first — success belongs to a *qualified* set of believers — then proves the verdict reachable by walking the reader back through his own manufacture (clay→seed→clot→bones→flesh→“another creature”), before listing the sky, the water, the orchards, the Mount Sinai tree, the livestock and the ships as one continuous argument that the We who made you is not heedless.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 6; the whole surah's contract. The opening qualities (2–9) are re-weighed at the closing scales (`[23:102]`), and the creation ladder (12–16) is the template the resurrection-doubters will reject in ruku 3 (35–37) and ruku 5 (82–83).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[23:1]`** "Successful are the believers."
  * **Gloss:** Verdict before conditions — *falāh*, success as outcome; the word brackets the surah (`[23:102]` "those are the successful").
* **`[23:2]`** "Those who are humble in their prayers." **`[23:3]`** "Those who avoid nonsense."
  * **Gloss:** Interior posture (khushū‘ — "humble in" is unique in the corpus here) and exterior pruning (*lahw* → "nonsense"); the same "nonsense" will be the mockers' crime at 23:67.
* **`[23:4]`** "Those who work for charity."
  * **Gloss:** Itani's active phrasing for the zakah-line — wealth in motion, the k-infraq grammar.
* **`[23:5]`** "Those who safeguard their chastity." **`[23:6]`** "Except from their spouses, or their dependents—for then they are free from blame." **`[23:7]`** "But whoever seeks anything beyond that— these are the transgressors."
  * **Gloss:** A boundary drawn with its exception clause and its transgressor clause — the same three-move law as `[70:29]`–`[70:31]`.
* **`[23:8]`** "Those who are faithful to their trusts and pledges." **`[23:9]`** "Those who safeguard their prayers."
  * **Gloss:** *Amānāt* and *‘uhūd* — keeping what is deposited and what is sworn (the k-covenant personal layer); prayer returns as ring-piece, humbled at the start (2), safeguarded at the end (9).
* **`[23:10]`** "These are the inheritors." **`[23:11]`** "Who will inherit Paradise, wherein they will dwell forever."
  * **Gloss:** Inheritance language — cf. `[26:85]` "inheritors of the Garden of Bliss" and `[39:74]` "made us inherit the land… enjoying Paradise".
* **`[23:12]`** "We created man from an extract of clay."
  * **Gloss:** The first-person plural We (majestic style, observable in this translation) takes over; origin is sediment, not pedigree.
* **`[23:13]`** "Then We made him a seed, in a secure repository."
  * **Gloss:** "Secure repository" — this edition's unique rendering of *makīn*; the womb as architecture.
* **`[23:14]`** "Then We developed the seed into a clot. Then We developed the clot into a lump. Then We developed the lump into bones. Then We clothed the bones with flesh. Then We produced it into another creature. Most Blessed is Allah, the Best of Creators."
  * **Gloss:** Five-stage relay then a doxology mid-argument — the only stage-list in the corpus that breaks into praise; "Best of Creators" occurs nowhere else (rg-verified).
* **`[23:15]`** "Then, after that, you will die." **`[23:16]`** "Then, on the Day of Resurrection, you will be resurrected."
  * **Gloss:** The switch from "man" (third person) to "you" lands the argument on the reader: your biography already contains a second creation.
* **`[23:17]`** "We created above you seven pathways, and We are never heedless of the creation."
  * **Gloss:** "Seven pathways" (*sab‘a ṭara’iq*) — unique phrasing; classical readers take it as the seven heavens (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir — verify*); cf. Pharaoh's "pathways of the heavens" at `[40:36]`–`[40:37]`, and the "seven heavens in layers" of `[67:3]`/`[71:15]`. The closing clause — never heedless — is the ruku's theology.
* **`[23:18]`** "And We sent down water from the sky in proper quantity, and settled it in the ground, and We are Able to take it away."
  * **Gloss:** Provision with a revoke-clause attached — gratitude and risk in the same breath (the k-shukr grammar).
* **`[23:19]`** "With it We produce for you gardens of palms and vines, yielding abundant fruit for you to eat." **`[23:20]`** "And a tree springing out of Mount Sinai, producing oil, and seasoning for those who eat."
  * **Gloss:** The Sinai tree is unnamed here; classically identified as the olive (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir — verify*; cf. `[24:35]` "a blessed tree, an olive tree", `[95:1]`–`[95:2]`).
* **`[23:21]`** "And there is a lesson for you in livestock: We give you to drink from what is in their bellies, and you have many benefits in them, and from them you eat." **`[23:22]`** "And on them, and on the ships, you are transported."
  * **Gloss:** From inside the animal to over the sea — the inventory ends in motion; 23:22's closing line is verbatim identical to `[40:80]`.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`falāh`** ("Successful," 23:1): success as survival-of-the-audit.
  * **Rendering:** "Successful are the believers" (23:1); "those are the successful" (23:102); same verdict at `[7:8]` "Those whose weights are heavy—it is they who are the successful."
  * **Elsewhere:** the ring — ruku 6's scales re-decide ruku 1's verdict.
* **`sab‘a ṭara’iq`** ("seven pathways," 23:17):
  * **Rendering:** "pathways," not "heavens" — this edition's unique choice (rg: *pathway* family at `[40:36]`, `[40:37]`, `[45:18]`, `[16:69]`, `[43:10]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:29]` "made them seven heavens"; `[67:3]`/`[71:15]` "seven heavens in layers"; `[23:86]` "Lord of the seven heavens."
* **`nutfah`** ("seed," 23:13): the starter-drop.
  * **Rendering:** "seed" here; the same origin-liquid family is "a small drop" at `[22:5]`, "a sperm-drop" at `[18:37]`, "a drop of fluid" at `[16:4]`, "a seed, then… an embryo" at `[40:67]` — observable term-choice drift (Source: translator's term choice, observable in corpus).
* **`lahw`** ("nonsense," 23:3): idle play that forfeits weight.
  * **Rendering:** "avoid nonsense" (23:3); the mockers "talked nonsense about it" (23:67) — same English word, first and last third of the surah.
  * **Elsewhere:** the Garden "you will hear no nonsense" (`[19:62]`, `[56:25]`, `[88:11]`) — what the believers avoid is literally what Paradise lacks.
