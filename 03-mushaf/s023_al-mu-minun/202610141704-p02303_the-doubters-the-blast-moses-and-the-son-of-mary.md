# The doubters, the Blast, Moses and Aaron, and Mary's son a sign (23:33–50)
**UID:** 202610141704
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 23:33–50 (Ruku 3 of 6 in al-Mu’minun)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[23:33]` – `[23:50]` (18 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The unnamed generation re-performs Noah's rejection script with one fatal addition — a flat metaphysical denial ("dust and bones… Farfetched… There is nothing but our life in this world") — and receives not an Ark but the Blast; the surah then compresses the rest of salvation history into four breaths: other generations, Moses and Aaron against Pharaoh, the Scripture given, and Mary's son and his mother sheltered on high ground as a standing sign.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 6; the negative twin of ruku 2 (same script, no rescue) and the far-side evidence locker for the resurrection argument begun at 23:12–16.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[23:33]`** "But the dignitaries of his people, those who disbelieved and denied the meeting of the Hereafter, and We had indulged them in the present life, said, “This is nothing but a human like you; he eats what you eat, and he drinks what you drink."
  * **Gloss:** The rejection formula, now annotated: denial of the *meeting* (liqā’) and indulgence (*mutrafūn* — "We had indulged them," cf. `[9:69]` "you indulged, as they indulged") pre-color the motive.
* **`[23:34]`** "If you obey a human being like yourselves, then you will be losers." **`[23:35]`** "Does he promise you that when you have died and become dust and bones, you will be brought out?"
  * **Gloss:** The anthropological objection escalates into the eschatological one; **this is the resurrection-doubt refrain's first site** (reprised at 23:82).
* **`[23:36]`** "Farfetched, farfetched is what you are promised." **`[23:37]`** "There is nothing but our life in this world. We die, and we live, and we are not resurrected."
  * **Gloss:** Double "Farfetched" (cf. `[50:3]` "a farfetched return" — this edition's shared word-choice); 23:37's materialism is near-verbatim at `[45:24]` "There is nothing but this our present life; we die and we live…"
* **`[23:38]`** "He is nothing but a man, making up lies about Allah. We have no faith in him.”"
  * **Gloss:** Fifth charge: fabrication — which the surah will turn back on them verbatim at 23:90 ("We have given them the truth, and they are liars").
* **`[23:39]`** "He said, “My Lord, help me, for they have rejected me.”" **`[23:40]`** "He said, “Soon they will be filled with regret.”"
  * **Gloss:** The 23:26 prayer, unaltered — the script repetition is deliberate; the answer's speaker is not named in the line (read most naturally as Allah's reply to the prayer — *Source: agent inference — verify*).
* **`[23:41]`** "Then the Blast struck them, justifiably, and We turned them into scum. So away with the wicked people."
  * **Gloss:** "Justifiably" (*bi-l-ḥaqq*) — the destruction is juridical, not temperamental; "scum" (*ghuṣāʾ*-class word) recurs in the corpus only as Hell's food (`[69:36]`) — imagery of waste-flotsam (Source: agent inference — verify).
* **`[23:42]`** "Then, after them, We raised other generations." **`[23:43]`** "No nation can advance its time, nor can they postpone it."
  * **Gloss:** History keeps moving under fixed appointments — the term-law also stated at `[7:34]`-class verses.
* **`[23:44]`** "Then We sent Our messengers in succession. Every time a messenger came to his community, they called him a liar. So We made them follow one another, and made them history. So away with a people who do not believe."
  * **Gloss:** The whole prophetic past compressed into one conveyor belt of denial and disappearance; "made them history" is this edition's striking idiom — the exact phrase at `[34:19]` (Saba').
* **`[23:45]`** "Then We sent Moses and his brother Aaron, with Our signs and a clear authority." **`[23:46]`** "To Pharaoh and his nobles, but they turned arrogant. They were oppressive people."
  * **Gloss:** Named at last: the Mosaic pair with *sulṭān mubīn* ("clear authority" — the credential idiom, cf. 20:24's commission).
* **`[23:47]`** "They said, “Are we to believe in two mortals like us, and their people are our slaves?”" **`[23:48]`** "So they called them liars, and thus were among those destroyed."
  * **Gloss:** Pharaoh's court adds a class clause to the stock "human like you" charge — denial of the messengers is soaked in social contempt.
* **`[23:49]`** "And We gave Moses the Scripture, that they may be guided."
  * **Gloss:** Even amid destruction the through-line is guidance — Scripture as *hudan*, the k-guidance grammar (cf. `[40:53]` "We gave Moses guidance").
* **`[23:50]`** "And We made Mary’s son and his mother a sign, and We sheltered them on high ground with security and flowing springs."
  * **Gloss:** The cycle's final stamp: Jesus unnamed ("Mary's son"), both as one *āya*, housed on *rabwa* — "high ground with security and flowing springs" pairs with `[21:91]` "made her and her son a sign to the world."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`al-liqā’ al-ākhirah`** ("the meeting of the Hereafter," 23:33):
  * **Rendering:** the deniers' defining symptom; the same "meeting" grammar at `[10:45]` ("Those who denied the meeting with Allah will be the losers"), `[29:5]`, `[13:2]` — trace via `rg "meeting"`.
  * **Elsewhere:** internal: 23:60 "knowing that to their Lord they will return" — the believers' mirror of the denied meeting.
* **`ṣayḥa`** ("the Blast," 23:41):
  * **Rendering:** "the Blast struck them, justifiably."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[15:73]`/`[15:83]` "the Blast struck them" (Lot's people), `[29:40]` "Some were struck by the Blast", `[11:94]` — one weapon in the corpus's destruction inventory.
* **`āya` ("a sign," 23:50):**
  * **Rendering:** Mary's son *and* his mother — jointly the sign, no narrative attached.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[21:91]` (near-twin), `[19:21]` "We will make him a sign for humanity" — the k-isa note's sign-cluster.
* **`mutraf`** ("We had indulged them," 23:33):
  * **Rendering:** indulgence as prior divine action — an ambiguity the surah exploits again at 23:55–56.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:69]` "you indulged, as they indulged"; `[11:116]` "the luxuries they were indulged in."
