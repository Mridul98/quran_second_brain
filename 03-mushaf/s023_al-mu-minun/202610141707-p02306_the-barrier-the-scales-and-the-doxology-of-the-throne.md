# The barrier, the scales, and the doxology of the Throne (23:93–118)
**UID:** 202610141707
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 23:93–118 (Ruku 6 of 6 in al-Mu’minun)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[23:93]` – `[23:118]` (26 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Instead of granting the prophet a preview of the punishment, the surah hands him four prayers — refuge, repulsion of evil by good, and two petitions for forgiveness — then runs the far side in full: death's rejected "send me back," the barrier, the Horn, the scales that re-issue the surah's first verdict, the Fire's dialogue, the years that shrink to a day, and the doxology of the Ruler, the Real — ending on the very sentence the mocked servants used to pray.
* **Placement:** Ruku 6 of 6; the accounting close. 23:102 restates 23:1; 23:105 restates 23:66; 23:109 and 23:118 rhyme the surah shut; 23:117's "The disbelievers will not succeed" is 23:1 in the negative.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[23:93]`** "Say, “My Lord, if You would show me what they are promised." **`[23:94]`** "My Lord, do not place me among the wicked people.”" **`[23:95]`** "We are surely Able to show you what We promise them."
  * **Gloss:** The prophet's two-line petition — sight of the threat, and protection from the threatened company — answered with capacity, not promise: Allah *can* show it; the timing is His.
* **`[23:96]`** "Repel evil by what is better. We are aware of what they describe."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-conduct law mid-ruku (cf. `[41:34]` "Repel evil with good, and the person who was your enemy becomes like an intimate friend", `[13:22]`); "what they describe" repeats the ruku-5 refrain (23:91).
* **`[23:97]`** "And say, “My Lord, I seek refuge with You from the urgings of the devils." **`[23:98]`** "And I seek refuge with You, my Lord, lest they become present.”"
  * **Gloss:** Double refuge — from the promptings and from the presence; the refuge idiom pairs with `[7:200]` ("take refuge with Allah") and the whole of surah 114 (114:4–5 "the sneaky whisperer… whispers into the hearts").
* **`[23:99]`** "Until, when death comes to one of them, he says, “My Lord, send me back." **`[23:100]`** "That I may do right in what I have neglected.” By no means! It is just a word that he utters. And behind them is a barrier, until the Day they are resurrected."
  * **Gloss:** The deathbed rewind denied — the same request at `[63:10]` ("if only You would delay me… so that I may be charitable"), answered there as well by unreturned time; *barzakh* — "a barrier" — the corpus's same word for sea-partings (`[25:53]`, `[55:20]`) and Day-sundries (`[18:52]`, `[34:54]`), here given its interworld force (Source: the word's classical eschatological usage — verify).
* **`[23:101]`** "When the Horn is blown, no relations between them will exist on that Day, and they will not ask after one another."
  * **Gloss:** "the Horn" — this edition's single "Horn" (rg-verified); everywhere else the same event is "the Trumpet" (`[50:20]`, `[39:68]`, `[27:87]`, `[20:102]`, `[74:8]`); kinship nullified, cf. `[2:254]` "neither trading, nor friendship, nor intercession", `[70:10]` "No friend will care about his friend."
* **`[23:102]`** "Those whose scales are heavy—those are the successful." **`[23:103]`** "But those whose scales are light—those are they who have lost their souls; in Hell they will dwell forever."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict of 23:1 re-issued by weight; near-twin at `[7:8]` ("Those whose weights are heavy—it is they who are the successful"), with 23:103's loss-of-soul clause doubled verbatim-style at `[7:9]` ("it is they who have lost their souls"); cf. `[101:6]`–`[101:8]`, `[6:12]`.
* **`[23:104]`** "The Fire lashes their faces, and therein they grimace." **`[23:105]`** "“Were not My revelations recited to you, and you kept on rejecting them?”"
  * **Gloss:** Face-first disgrace, then the charge-file read out: 23:105 restates 23:66 with "revelations" (*āyāt*) for "Verses" — the recitation was the whole case.
* **`[23:106]`** "They will say, “Our Lord, our wretchedness prevailed over us, and we were a people astray." **`[23:107]`** "Our Lord! Bring us out of this. If we ever returned, we would truly be evil.”"
  * **Gloss:** The excuse: *shiqāwa* — "wretchedness" — the same noun-family Ta-Ha opened with ("We did not reveal the Quran to you to make you suffer," `[20:2]`; root identity per lexicons — *Source: agent inference — verify*); the second request (bring us out) repeats the denied rewind of 23:99 — the Fire is where requests recycle.
* **`[23:108]`** "He will say, “Be despised therein, and do not speak to Me." **`[23:109]`** "There was a group of My servants who would say, `Our Lord, we have believed, so forgive us, and have mercy on us; You are the Best of the merciful.'"
  * **Gloss:** Silence of court — "do not speak to Me" — and the memory of the other group; 109's prayer (with the book's stray backtick-quote typography [sic]) is the formulaic "Best of the merciful," while 7:151/12:92/21:83 render the kin formula "Most Merciful of the merciful."
* **`[23:110]`** "But you made them a target of ridicule, until they made you forget My remembrance; and you used to laugh at them." **`[23:111]`** "Today, I have rewarded them for their endurance. They are the ones who are the triumphant.”"
  * **Gloss:** The Meccan mockers named at last (23:67's laughter audited); endurance (*ṣabr*-family word, "for their endurance") paid with triumph — the k-sabr note's wage grammar (cf. `[39:10]` "paid their wages in full, without reckoning").
* **`[23:112]`** "He will say, “How many years did you remain on earth?”" **`[23:113]`** "They will say, “We remained a day, or part of a day; but ask those who keep count.”" **`[23:114]`** "He will say, “You remained only for a little while, if you only knew."
  * **Gloss:** Earth-time compresses on questioning — the sleepers of `[18:19]` and the century-man of `[2:259]` ("a day, or part of a day") and Ta-Ha's murmurers (`[20:103]`–`[20:104]`) all speak this scene's idiom; "ask those who keep count" is sarcasm inside the question.
* **`[23:115]`** "Did you think that We created you in vain, and that to Us you will not be returned?”
  * **Gloss:** The play-creation question — *labitha* "in vain" (cf. `[38:27]` "We did not create the heaven and the earth… in vain", `[3:191]` "You did not create this in vain") — resurrection's whole case compressed into one rhetorical verb-pair: futility or return.
* **`[23:116]`** "So Exalted is Allah, the Ruler, the Real. There is no god except He, the Lord of the Noble Throne."
  * **Gloss:** The close's doxology: *al-Malik al-Ḥaqq* — "the Ruler, the Real" — a divine-name pair unique in the corpus (rg-verified); "Noble Throne" (*al-‘arsh al-karīm*) likewise occurs only here — vs 23:86's "Splendid," 27:26's "Sublime," 85:15's "Glorious" — the correction of the task's "Glory be to Allah, Lord of the Throne" (that wording belongs to 23:91/21:22).
* **`[23:117]`** "Whoever invokes another god besides Allah—he has no proof thereof—his reckoning rests with his Lord. The disbelievers will not succeed."
  * **Gloss:** The law against rival-invocation with its burden-of-proof clause (cf. `[17:39]` "Do not set up with Allah another god, or else you will be thrown in Hell, rebuked and banished"); "will not succeed" — 23:1 reversed, the ring sealed.
* **`[23:118]`** "And say, “My Lord, forgive and have mercy, for You are the Best of the merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The last command is the mocked servants' own prayer (109) — the surah ends by making the community recite the victors' words; the closing *khatima* doubles as du'a.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`barzakh`** ("a barrier," 23:100):
  * **Rendering:** "And behind them is a barrier, until the Day they are resurrected" — one line of interworld doctrine.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:53]`, `[55:20]` (seas), `[18:52]`, `[34:54]`, `[41:5]`, `[17:45]` (Day-screens) — same English word, this verse the eschatological-narrow use (classically the *barzakh* interval — *Source: agent inference — verify*).
* **`mīzān` / "scales" (23:102–103):**
  * **Rendering:** heavy/light, success/loss; "the Horn" (23:101) is the only "Horn" in the book, vs "Trumpet" ×7 elsewhere (observable drift, rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:8]`, `[101:6]`–`[101:8]`, `[21:47]` (mustard-seed, in [p02104](../s021_al-anbiya/202610141675-p02104_who-will-guard-you-and-the-mustard-seed-scales.md) range).
* **`shiqāwa`** ("our wretchedness," 23:106):
  * **Rendering:** "our wretchedness prevailed over us."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:2]` "not… to make you suffer" — if the roots match as classically held, the Quran's denial that revelation is misery (Ta-Ha) is answered here by the damned locating their misery in themselves (*Source: agent inference — verify root identity*).
* **`al-Malik al-Ḥaqq`** ("the Ruler, the Real," 23:116): unique name-pair (rg); the doxology's Throne clause re-lists the "seven heavens" Owner of 23:86 — first asked, now confessed without the question.
