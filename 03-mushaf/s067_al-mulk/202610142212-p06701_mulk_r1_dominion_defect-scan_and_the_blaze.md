# Blessed is He in whose hand is the dominion: the defect-scan and the Blaze's cross-examination (67:1–14)
**UID:** 202610142212
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 67:1–14 (Ruku 1 of 2 in al-Mulk)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[67:1]` – `[67:14]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku builds tawhid by inventory rather than assertion: dominion sits in a hand that created death-and-life as a test (67:1–2), layered heavens that survive a repeatable defect-scan (67:3–4), and a lower heaven whose ornaments are also ammunition (67:5); the same sovereignty then runs the Blaze's courtroom replay — roaring, batch-wise interrogation, and the inmates' own verdict on their hearing and reasoning (67:6–11) — before turning to the secret-fearers and the One "Aware of the inner thoughts" (67:12–14).
* **Placement:** Opening ruku of the Mulk diptych; the sky-facing half. Ruku 2 (67:15–30) inverts the vantage to earth and body.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[67:1]`** "Blessed is He in whose hand is the sovereignty, and Who has power over everything."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's title-claim: mulk (sovereignty) held *in a hand* — an idiom of personal control, not abstract force; "power over everything" makes the test of 67:2 possible.
* **`[67:2]`** "He who created death and life—to test you—as to which of you is better in conduct. He is the Almighty, the Forgiving."
  * **Gloss:** Existence is staged as examination; note the pairing of attributes that closes the ruku-family — Almighty (force) with Forgiving (exit). "Better in conduct" is comparative, not maximum-deed.
* **`[67:3]`** "He who created seven heavens in layers. You see no discrepancy in the creation of the Compassionate. Look again. Can you see any cracks?"
  * **Gloss:** The defect-scan opens: tafawut (discrepancy) is denied as a finding, and the reader is handed the instrument — "Look again."
* **`[67:4]`** "Then look again, and again, and your sight will return to you dazzled and exhausted."
  * **Gloss:** Iteration is the methodology; the failure is the observer's, not the creation's. Task-flag resolved: the corpus renders *dazzled*, not "humiliated".
* **`[67:5]`** "We have adorned the lower heaven with lanterns, and made them missiles against the devils; and We have prepared for them the punishment of the Blaze."
  * **Gloss:** One object, two functions: beauty (adornment) and defense (missiles) — the sky is both lamp and turret; the eschatological sentence for the devils is appended in the same breath.
* **`[67:6]`** "For those who reject their Lord, there is the torment of Hell. What an evil destination!"
  * **Gloss:** The courtroom's charge-sheet; "destination" (su'a) frames the Fire as arrival, not accident.
* **`[67:7]`** "When they are thrown into it, they will hear it roaring, as it seethes."
  * **Gloss:** The Fire is given its own audibility and agitation — a defendant that breathes.
* **`[67:8]`** "It almost bursts with fury. Every time a batch is thrown into it, its keepers will ask them, “Has no warner come to you?”"
  * **Gloss:** Two images in one ayah: the bursting vessel, and the interrogation protocol. The keepers' question is rhetorical only in form — the batch structure implies an inventory of arrivals.
* **`[67:9]`** "They will say, “Yes, a warner did come to us, but we disbelieved, and said, ‘Allah did not send down anything; you are very much mistaken.’”"
  * **Gloss:** The confession is precise: witnesses acknowledged, and the crime quoted verbatim — "Allah did not send down anything" is the denial of the kalam channel itself.
* **`[67:10]`** "And they will say, “Had we listened or reasoned, we would not have been among the inmates of the Blaze.”"
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual indicts both faculties they refused (hearing + intellect); the ruku's method (look again, listen again) is shown in its negative.
* **`[67:11]`** "So they will acknowledge their sins. So away with the inmates of the Blaze."
  * **Gloss:** Acknowledgment arrives when it can no longer function; the closing curse ("so away") is the verdict sentence. Task-flag: this, not 67:7, is the confession line.
* **`[67:12]`** "As for those who fear their Lord in secret— for them is forgiveness and a great reward."
  * **Gloss:** The pivot verse: the same Lord feared *secretly*, the faculty the inmates refused.
* **`[67:13]`** "Whether you keep your words secret, or declare them—He is Aware of the inner thoughts."
  * **Gloss:** Speech-hiding is no defense: secrecy and declaration collapse under one knowledge. This grounds the sovereignty as omniscience.
* **`[67:14]`** "Would He not know, He Who created? He is the Refined, the Expert."
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's final proof-by-rhetorical-question: the Creator cannot be opaque to His own making; "the Refined (latif), the Expert (khabir)" close the sky-facing argument.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`al-Mulk`** (sovereignty, dominion) — 67:1.
  * **Rendering:** "sovereignty"; title of the surah (corpus header "SOVEREIGNTY").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:26]` "O Allah, Owner of Sovereignty... In Your hand is all goodness" — the same hand-holding idiom (rg-verified).
* **`khusuf / yaqsof`** (the earth collapsing) — 67:16, in ruku 2, is the operative threat; this ruku's term is **`tafawut`** (discrepancy) in 67:3.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[71:15]` "Allah created seven heavens in layers" — the layers claim restated in Nuh (rg-verified; "in layers" appears in these two ayahs).
* **`khasi'`** (dazzled, abased vision) — 67:4.
  * **Rendering:** "dazzled" (not "humiliated" as the task quoted).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[75:7]` "When vision is dazzled" (rg-verified) — the same root used at Qiyamah's onset.
* **`Saqar`** — 67:10, 67:11 render as "the Blaze"; the name itself appears as "Saqar" only in `[74:26]`, `[74:27]`, `[74:42]`, `[54:48]` (rg-verified).
* **`latif / khabir`** (the Refined, the Expert) — 67:14, the ruku's closing pair.
