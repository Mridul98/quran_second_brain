# Are you confident that the One in heaven...? The tractable earth, the two walkers, and the trust (67:15–30)
**UID:** 202610142213
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 67:15–30 (Ruku 2 of 2 in al-Mulk)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[67:15]` – `[67:30]` (16 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Sovereignty is now argued from below: the earth was made tractable for travel and eating (67:15) but can collapse or be stormed (67:16–17); birds hang by nothing but the Compassionate (67:19); provision can be withheld (67:21); and the whole ruku sorts humanity by one image — the walker bent on his own design versus the walker upright on a straight path (67:22) — closing in the surah's tawakkul sentence (67:29) and one final water-dare (67:30).
* **Placement:** Second ruku; the diptych's inversion of perspective (sky→earth, cosmos→conscience). Frames: 67:1 "in whose hand is the sovereignty" ↔ 67:30 "who will bring you... water?" — dominion proved by what can be withdrawn.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[67:15]`** "It is He who made the earth manageable for you, so travel its regions, and eat of His provisions. To Him is the Resurgence."
  * **Gloss:** "Manageable" (dhalūl — domesticated, like livestock you ride) turns geology into hospitality; travel and eat are the permitted responses; the return clause re-anchors to 67:1.
* **`[67:16]`** "Are you confident that the One in heaven will not cause the earth to collapse beneath you as it spins?"
  * **Gloss:** Task-verified: the phrase is "the One in heaven" and "collapse... as it spins" (khusuf with yamūr), not "swallow". The security of ground is a held concession. Precedent: `[28:81]` Qarun — "We caused the earth to cave in on him" (rg-verified).
* **`[67:17]`** "Or are you confident that the One in Heaven will not unleash against you a violent storm? Then you will know what My warning is like."
  * **Gloss:** Second dare, second withdrawal (hasib); note capitalization drift "Heaven" vs 67:16 "heaven" — translator's inconsistency. The divine voice enters mid-verse ("My warning").
* **`[67:18]`** "Those before them also denied the truth; and how was My disapproval?"
  * **Gloss:** The destruction-archive clause (cf. 67:5's devils, ruku 1's Blaze); "how was" is the idiom of the verdict after the fact.
* **`[67:19]`** "Have they not seen the birds above them, spreading their wings, and folding them? None holds them except the Compassionate. He is Perceiver of everything."
  * **Gloss:** Flight as continuous creation: wings spread and folded, held by nothing but Him — the sky's physics cited as mercy-physics. Twin: `[16:79]` "None sustains them except Allah" (rg-verified).
* **`[67:20]`** "Or who is this who is a force for you to protect you against the Compassionate? The disbelievers are in nothing but delusion."
  * **Gloss:** The partners-question in military dress — a "force" (jund) to shield against the Rahim: the absurdity is the argument.
* **`[67:21]`** "Or who is this that will provide for you, if He withholds His provision? Yet they persist in defiance and aversion."
  * **Gloss:** The economic dare; provision (rizq) as the test-case of sovereignty; the reaction verb — persistence, not persuasion.
* **`[67:22]`** "Is he who walks bent on his own design better guided, or he who walks upright on a straight path?"
  * **Gloss:** THE guidance-parable: task-verified — "better guided" is the hinge and the answer is built into the question. Itani renders the stumbling-turned-walker (ma'ib/mukibb — *Source: classical reading — verify*) as "bent on his own design"; the surah answers 67:16–21's "who is this?" series with an image of gait. Link: k-guidance (forward).
* **`[67:23]`** "Say, “It is He who produced you; and made for you the hearing, and the vision, and the organs. But rarely do you give thanks.”"
  * **Gloss:** The sense-inventory; "the organs" (af'idah) is Itani's render where `[32:9]`/`[16:78]` say "the brains" and `[23:78]` "the feelings" (rg-verified drift) — and "rarely do you give thanks" belongs to the gratitude family k-shukr cites at `[23:78]` ("little gratitude you show").
* **`[67:24]`** "Say, “It is He who scattered you on earth, and to Him you will be rounded up.”"
  * **Gloss:** Scatter-and-gather (hashr) pairs 67:15's "Resurgence"; twin: `[42:29]` "…the creatures He has spread throughout them; and He is Able to gather them at will" (rg-verified).
* **`[67:25]`** "And they say, “When will this promise be fulfilled, if you are truthful?”"
  * **Gloss:** The mockery's question — the same "when" the surah will answer twice more (67:26, 70:1; cf. 67:8's warner-question flipped: here the mockers ask the Prophet).
* **`[67:26]`** "Say, “Knowledge is with Allah, and I am only a clear warner.”"
  * **Gloss:** The prophet's role is narrowed to warning; timing knowledge stays divine. Twin: `[41:47]` "To Him is referred the knowledge of the Hour" (rg-verified).
* **`[67:27]`** "But when they see it approaching, the faces of those who disbelieved will turn gloomy, and it will be said, “This is what you used to call for.”"
  * **Gloss:** The answered "when": faces darkened, and the irony-line "this is what you used to call for" — cf. 70:44 "This is the Day which they were promised" (both rg-verified).
* **`[67:28]`** "Say, “Have you considered? Should Allah make me perish, and those with me; or else He bestows His mercy on us; who will protect the disbelievers from an agonizing torment?”"
  * **Gloss:** The mercy gambit: the prophet's survival is not the point — either way, no protector exists. "Have you considered?" (a'raytum) — the surah's recurring dare-phrase (67:16, 17, 30).
* **`[67:29]`** "Say, “He is the Compassionate. We have faith in Him, and in Him we trust. Soon you will know who is in evident error.”"
  * **Gloss:** Task-verified: "the Compassionate" (not "Most Merciful"), and the tawakkul clause "in Him we trust" (alayhi tawakkalna) is present — creed + reliance in one line, then an epistemic "soon you will know" (cf. 67:16 "then you will know").
* **`[67:30]`** "Say, “Have you considered? If your water drains away, who will bring you pure running water?”"
  * **Gloss:** The closing dare: sovereignty named by what it can withdraw (ghawr — the water sunk); "pure running water" (ma'in) — cf. `[76:6]` "A spring... making it gush abundantly" and `[76:18]` "Salsabeel" (rg-verified). The book ends where it began: His hand, your thirst.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`dhalūl`** (tractable/manageable earth) — 67:15.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[71:19–20]` "Allah made the earth a spread for you. That you may travel its diverse roads" (rg-verified) — the same hospitality idiom in Nuh's sermon.
* **`sirat mustaqim`** (straight path) — 67:22.
  * **Rendering:** "upright on a straight path"; the parable's answer-term.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[1:6]` "Guide us to the straight path" (the petition this image answers — see k-guidance's evidence list).
* **`tawakkul`** ("in Him we trust") — 67:29 — the verse's own second clause; the surah's exit posture after four dare-questions.
* **`ma'in`** (pure running water) — 67:30 — cf. `[76:6]`, `[76:18]` (Salsabeel), rg-verified.
