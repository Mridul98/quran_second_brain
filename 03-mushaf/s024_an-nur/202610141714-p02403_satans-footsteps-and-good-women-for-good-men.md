# Satan's footsteps and good women for good men (24:21–26)
**UID:** 202610141714
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 24:21–26 (Ruku 3 of 9 in an-Nur)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[24:21]` – `[24:26]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** After the slander episode: a general law of the soul (no footsteps of Satan — purity is God's work, not yours), a law of the tongue for the wronged rich (pardon and overlook, "Do you not love for Allah to pardon you?"), the curse on slanderers of chaste women with limbs testifying, and the acquittal chiasmus — "good women are for good men."
* **Placement:** Ruku 3; the moral seal on ruku 2's incident, closing the chastity argument before the household laws of ruku 4 begin.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[24:21]`** "O you who believe! Do not follow Satan’s footsteps. Whoever follows Satan’s footsteps—he advocates obscenity and immorality. Were it not for Allah’s grace towards you, and His mercy, not one of you would have been pure, ever. But Allah purifies whomever He wills. Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Satan's program is fahsha'; human purity (tazkiya) is not self-produced — "Allah purifies whomever He wills"; the lawla-refrain's fourth (and final) appeal, now with a "not one of you would have been pure, ever" clause.
* **`[24:22]`** "Those of you who have affluence and means should not refuse to give to the relatives, and the needy, and the emigrants for the sake of Allah. And let them pardon, and let them overlook. Do you not love for Allah to pardon you? Allah is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The affluent are commanded to keep giving — and pardon and overlook (in English, "pardon" recurs in the rhetorical question; the pardon/overlook pair as afwa/safh — *Source: classical Arabic — verify*); the rhetorical question makes mutual pardon the mirror of divine pardon.
* **`[24:23]`** "Those who slander honorable, innocent, believing women are cursed in this life and in the Hereafter. They will have a terrible punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The qadhf-slanderers get a double curse; "honorable, innocent, believing women" — three adjectives for muhsanat, where 24:4 used "chaste."
* **`[24:24]`** "On the Day when their tongues, and their hands, and their feet will testify against them regarding what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The body's courtroom: speech, grasp, and walk all become witnesses against the speaker.
* **`[24:25]`** "On that Day, Allah will pay them their account in full, and they will know that Allah is the Evident Reality."
  * **Gloss:** Full settlement; the divine name rendered "the Evident Reality" — the truth that finally appears.
* **`[24:26]`** "Bad women are for bad men, and bad men are for bad women, and good women are for good men, and good men are for good women. Those are acquitted of what they say. There is forgiveness for them, and a generous provision."
  * **Gloss:** The chiasmus: elemental pairing of good with good; the acquitted ("Those") are the slandered good ones — forgiveness plus "a generous provision," the standard reward-formula.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`footsteps`** (khwat): `[24:21]` (twice).
  * **Rendering:** "Satan's footsteps."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:168]` "do not follow the footsteps of Satan. He is to you an open enemy" and `[2:208]` "do not follow the footsteps of Satan; he is to you an outright enemy" — the same formula addressed to "O you who believe" at 2:208; an-Nur repeats the corpus's anti-Satan grammar verbatim in style.
* **`pure / purifies`** (z-k-y): `[24:21]`.
  * **Rendering:** "not one of you would have been pure, ever. But Allah purifies whomever He wills."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:129]`/`[2:151]` the messenger who will "purify them"; `[2:222]` "He loves those who keep clean" — the tazkiya dossier: purity as divine action and its beloved practice.
* **`testify (tongues, hands, feet)`**: `[24:24]`.
  * **Rendering:** limb-testimony against the person.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[41:21]` "And they will say to their skins, 'Why did you testify against us?'… 'Allah, Who made all things speak, made us speak'" — the same scene dramatized further in Fussilat; the two verses belong to one dossier.
* **`Evident Reality`**: `[24:25]`.
  * **Rendering:** unique in the corpus (rg-verified single hit) — Itani's coat for the divine name/attribute in this verse; the phrase never recurs corpus-wide (53:58, checked and rejected as a parallel — it reads "None besides Allah can unveil it") — *Source: observable translator's term choice*.
* **`good women / bad women`** (tayyib): `[24:26]`.
  * **Rendering:** symmetrical plural pairs; classical readers apply the verse to the slandered person of the episode and to the chaste generally (*Source: classical tafsir — verify*).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:31]` "a generous provision" and `[8:4]`/`[22:50]`/`[34:4]` "forgiveness, and a generous provision" — 24:26's closer is the corpus's standing reward-formula, not a unique phrase.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:208]` — identical "O you who believe… do not follow the footsteps of Satan" command; an-Nur's 21 is the slander-ruku's version of Baqarah's post-Hajj moral seal.
* **Parallel:** `[41:21]` — tongues/hands/feet/skins testimony; pair-read with 24:24–25 for the Day's evidence procedure.
* **Within surah:** `[24:26]` "Those are acquitted of what they say" resolves `[24:12]`'s command to think well — the innocent named; `[24:23]` re-prices 24:4's court-penalty as otherworldly curse.
* **Parallel:** `[2:216]`'s reversal ("bad is good for you") is answered socially here: pardon (22) and acquittal (26).
