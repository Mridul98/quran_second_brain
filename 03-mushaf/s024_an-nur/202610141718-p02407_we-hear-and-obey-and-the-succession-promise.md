# "We hear and we obey" and the succession promise (24:51–57)
**UID:** 202610141718
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 24:51–57 (Ruku 7 of 9 in an-Nur)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[24:51]` – `[24:57]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Against 24:47–50's turnaway claimants, the believers' single-line response — "We hear and we obey" — is named success itself; obeisance, fear, and taqwa are the winners' recipe (51–52); oath-swearing drill-sergeants are told obedience will be recognized without theatrics (53); the Messenger's authority is a delivered message, not a coerced heart (54); and the ruku vaults to the surah's great promise: succession on earth, the religion established, security traded for fear — on the one condition of unassociated worship (55), backed by prayer-zakat-obedience mercy (56) and the escape-denial to the deniers (57).
* **Placement:** Ruku 7; the obedience-and-promise hinge between the cosmos-ruku and the household close.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[24:51]`** "The response of the believers, when they are called to Allah and His Messenger in order to judge between them, is to say, “We hear and we obey.” These are the successful."
  * **Gloss:** The mirror of 24:48: same summons, opposite reflex; the verdict-word — "the successful" (muflihun).
* **`[24:52]`** "Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger, and fears Allah, and is conscious of Him— these are the winners."
  * **Gloss:** Obedience + fear + taqwa = the falah-list; "winners" (fa'izun) rhymes 51's "successful."
* **`[24:53]`** "And they swear by Allah with their solemn oaths, that if you commanded them, they would mobilize. Say, “Do not swear. Obedience will be recognized. Allah is experienced with what you do.”"
  * **Gloss:** The performative-loyalty group is cut off: no oath needed — deeds are recognized, and Allah is "experienced" (khabir) with the theater.
* **`[24:54]`** "Say, “Obey Allah and obey the Messenger.” But if they turn away, then he is responsible for his obligations, and you are responsible for your obligations. And if you obey him, you will be guided. It is only incumbent on the Messenger to deliver the Clarifying Message."
  * **Gloss:** The double-obedience formula, then the jurisdiction split — each soul its own ledger; obedience to the Messenger = guidance; his burden is delivery, not conversion.
* **`[24:55]`** "Allah has promised those of you who believe and do righteous deeds, that He will make them successors on earth, as He made those before them successors, and He will establish for them their religion—which He has approved for them—and He will substitute security in place of their fear. They worship Me, never associating anything with Me. But whoever disbelieves after that—these are the sinners."
  * **Gloss:** The four-gift wa'd: khilafa (succession, twice anchored to prior peoples), tamkin of an approved religion, security replacing fear, and the single rider — unassociated worship; "after that" makes the promise a covenant-edge for believers.
* **`[24:56]`** "Pray regularly, and give regular charity, and obey the Messenger, so that you may receive mercy."
  * **Gloss:** The platform verse: salat, zakat, obedience — the standard Medinan triad (as at 22:41's community brief) for mercy.
* **`[24:57]`** "Never think that those who disbelieve can escape on earth. Their place is the Fire; a miserable destination."
  * **Gloss:** The escape-clause: no evasion inside God's earth; destination stated flatly.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`We hear and we obey`**: `[24:51]`.
  * **Rendering:** the believers' verbatim creed-response to judgment-summons.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:285]` "We hear and we obey. Your forgiveness, our Lord" — the faithful's formula at Baqarah's close; `[4:46]` the twisted twin "We hear and we disobey… Had they said, 'We hear and we obey,'… it would have been better for them"; `[2:93]` Israel's "We hear and disobey" at the calf — the phrase-pair is a whole corpus motif: hear+obey vs. hear+disobey.
* **`successors`** (khala'if): `[24:55]` twice.
  * **Rendering:** "He will make them successors on earth, as He made those before them successors."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:30]` "I am placing a successor on earth"; `[6:165]` "It is He who made you successors on the earth"; `[7:129]` "Perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy, and make you successors in the land"; `[10:14]`, `[27:62]`, `[43:60]` — the khilafa word-family; 24:55 is its communal-promise form, 7:129 its Moses-form.
* **`establish… their religion`** (tamkin) + **`substitute security in place of their fear`**: `[24:55]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[28:5–6]` "to make them leaders, and to make them inheritors… to establish them in the land" — the Pharaoh-exodus promise-grammar as the nearest twin; `[106:4]` "has secured them against fear" — the aman-word's smaller commercial echo; note: no mithaq word-form in 24:55 — this is promise (wa'd), not covenant, grammar (*Source: agent inference from corpus word-forms — verified by rg*).
* **`mobilize`** (infiar/nafir): `[24:53]`.
  * **Rendering:** "if you commanded them, they would mobilize" — the muster-word; the same root's "go forth" usage recurs in the expedition surahs (cf. the 9:41-family); the 24 hit is the oath-theater, not the campaign.
* **`escape`** (a'jiz): `[24:57]`.
  * **Rendering:** "can escape on earth" — God-not-being-outdone.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[29:22]` "You cannot escape, on earth or in the heaven" — near-verbatim twin of the same clause.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:285]`/`[4:46]` — the hear-and-obey pair; 24:51 is the formula's purest single-verse statement in the corpus.
* **Parallel:** `[7:129]` + `[28:5–6]` — succession-as-promise precedent: the "those before them" clause of 24:55 points straight at this Israel-narrative grammar.
* **Parallel:** `[29:22]` — the escape-denial twin of 24:57.
* **Within surah:** `[24:54]`'s "if you obey him, you will be guided" answers `[24:47]`'s failed claimants; `[24:51]` reopens 24:48's summons with the right reflex — the two ruku are a matched pair of courtrooms.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical lists — verify).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 24:55 is read classically as the promise of the community's steadying/caliphate; confessional readings diverge over its referents and its "as He made those before them" analogy — *Source: classical (and post-classical confessional) tafsir — verify; the text itself lists faith, deeds, and unassociated worship as its conditions, nothing more.*
