# The slander that was good for you (24:11–20)
**UID:** 202610141713
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 24:11–20 (Ruku 2 of 9 in an-Nur)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[24:11]` – `[24:20]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's legal qadhf statute (24:4) turns on a live event: a "band" inside the community circulated an accusation, and the revelation reframes it — "Do not consider it bad for you, but it is good for you" — then legislates the etiquette of rumor: think well, say "an obvious lie," produce four witnesses or be named liars, and never love to see immorality spread.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2; the narrative application of ruku 1's laws and the emotional center of the surah's first half.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[24:11]`** "Those who perpetrated the slander are a band of you. Do not consider it bad for you, but it is good for you. Each person among them bears his share in the sin. As for him who played the major role—for him is a terrible punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The perpetrators are internal — "a band of you"; the reversal verse: the scandal's public exposure of hypocrisy and its legislation-in-response make it good; the ringleader ("the major role") gets "a terrible punishment" — note: no angels in the wording (see §6).
* **`[24:12]`** "Why, when you heard about it, the believing men and women did not think well of one another, and say, “This is an obvious lie”?"
  * **Gloss:** The default for believers toward believers: positive assumption plus one sentence of verdict — "an obvious lie."
* **`[24:13]`** "Why did they not bring four witnesses to testify to it? If they fail to bring the witnesses, then in Allah’s sight, they are liars."
  * **Gloss:** 24:4's quorum re-deployed: evidence or the label "liars," before Allah.
* **`[24:14]`** "Were it not for Allah’s favor upon you, and His mercy, in this world and the Hereafter, you would have suffered a great punishment for what you have ventured into."
  * **Gloss:** The lawla-refrain with a double horizon ("in this world and the Hereafter"); "ventured into" treats rumor as trespass.
* **`[24:15]`** "When you rumored it with your tongues, and spoke with your mouths what you had no knowledge of, and you considered it trivial; but according to Allah, it is serious."
  * **Gloss:** The gravity-calibration verse: tongue-weight vs. Allah-weight — the trivial/heavy reversal.
* **`[24:16]`** "When you heard it, you should have said, “It is not for us to repeat this. By Your glory, this is a serious slander.”"
  * **Gloss:** A taught script for the moment of hearing: refuse the repeat, glorify God, name the speech "a serious slander."
* **`[24:17]`** "Allah cautions you never to return to the like of it, if you are believers."
  * **Gloss:** Recidivism barred; the caution is itself a proof of faith.
* **`[24:18]`** "Allah explains the Verses to you. Allah is Knowing and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The kalam-clause: exposition as mercy — the event produced legislation, not just consolation.
* **`[24:19]`** "Those who love to see immorality spread among the believers—for them is a painful punishment, in this life and in the Hereafter. Allah knows, and you do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The motive-crime: *loving* the spread of fahsha among believers; "Allah knows, and you do not know" — the same closer as `[2:216]`.
* **`[24:20]`** "Were it not for Allah’s grace upon you, and His mercy, and that Allah is Clement and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The refrain closes unfinished again (as at 24:10) — clemency is why the community survived its own tongues.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`slander`** (ifk): `[24:11]`, `[24:16]`, `[24:23]`.
  * **Rendering:** "the slander" as a known event ("Those who perpetrated the slander").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:156]` "their saying against Mary a monstrous slander" — the same word for the accusation against Mary; classical readers exploit the Mary/Aisha parallel (*Source: classical tafsir — verify*); `[49:11]` "nor shall you slander one another" — the general etiquette; `[4:112]` "has taken a slander and a clear sin"; `[68:11]` "Backbiter, spreader of slander."
* **`a band of you`** (ʿusbah): `[24:11]`.
  * **Rendering:** "are a band of you" — the evil is intra-community, not outsider attack.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[72:1]` "a band of jinn listened in" — the same English word for a party (72:1's Arabic is nafar, not ʿusbah — *Source: classical Arabic — verify*); an-Nur keeps the band human.
* **`immorality / obscenity and immorality`** (fahsha'): `[24:19]` (here "immorality"), `[24:21]` ("obscenity and immorality").
  * **Rendering:** drift within one surah: "immorality spread" (19) vs. the pair (21); `[2:169]` renders the same root "evil and vice."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:90]` "He forbids immorality, and injustice, and oppression" — the fahsha'-word family across the corpus.
* **`Were it not for (lawla) grace and mercy`**: `[24:10]`, `[24:14]`, `[24:20]`, `[24:21]`.
  * **Rendering:** a four-appeal cluster across ruku 1–3 (rg-verified: only 24:10, 14, 20, 21 in the surah); each sentence hangs unfinished — grammar as theology (punishment withheld mid-sentence).
  * **Elsewhere:** the same counterfactual grace-formula appears across Medinan surahs (cf. `[2:64]` "Were it not for Allah’s grace and mercy towards you, you would have been among the losers"); in s024 it is a structural signature — *Source: agent inference from the corpus count — verify*.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical lists — verify); this is the surah's incident-ruku — law becomes commentary on a live scandal.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** classical consensus attaches 24:11–20 to the "incident of the slander" (al-ifk) against the Prophet's household during a return from campaign; the text names no protagonist — *Source: consensus of classical tafsir (the Bukhari/tafsir accounts) — verify in print*. **Correction logged:** the task's phrase "ten punishing angels" is **not** Itani anywhere in s024 — the word "angel" does not occur in the surah at all; the ringleader's punishment is only "a terrible punishment" (24:11). Any angel-descent detail belongs to transmitted reports outside this text, not to the translation.
