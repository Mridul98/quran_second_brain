# The mandated chapter and the lash statutes (24:1–10)
**UID:** 202610141712
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 24:1–10 (Ruku 1 of 9 in an-Nur)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[24:1]` – `[24:10]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** An-Nur opens with a self-declaration — a chapter "revealed, and made obligatory" — then delivers its first law package: hundred lashes for the adulterer and adulteress, eighty plus testimony-ban for unproven accusation of chaste women, and a five-fold oath procedure for spousal accusation, all bracketed by "Were it not for Allah's grace upon you, and His mercy."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1; the statute-keystone the rest of the surah elaborates (the slander episode in ruku 2 applies the accusation-law, the home/gaze laws of ruku 4 extend its chastity grammar).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[24:1]`** "A chapter that We have revealed, and made obligatory, and revealed in it clear Verses, that you may take heed."
  * **Gloss:** The book announces itself as mandatory revelation with clarity inside — the "take heed" purpose-clause matches the kalam/revelation self-descriptions of the Medinan statute-surahs.
* **`[24:2]`** "The adulteress and the adulterer—whip each one of them a hundred lashes, and let no pity towards them overcome you regarding Allah’s Law, if you believe in Allah and the Last Day. And let a group of believers witness their punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The zina flogging: fixed hundred, no mercy-interference with "Allah's Law," public execution before "a group of believers" — punishment as communal boundary-work.
* **`[24:3]`** "The adulterer shall marry none but an adulteress or an idolatress; and the adulteress shall marry none but an adulterer or an idolater. That has been prohibited for the believers."
  * **Gloss:** A marriage-bar: the practice pairs like with like outside the believing community; "That has been prohibited for the believers" closes the ring.
* **`[24:4]`** "Those who accuse chaste women, then cannot bring four witnesses, whip them eighty lashes, and do not ever accept their testimony. For these are the immoral."
  * **Gloss:** The qadhf statute: accusation of the "chaste women" (muhsanat) requires four witnesses; failure = eighty lashes plus permanent testimony-rejection, and the label "the immoral."
* **`[24:5]`** "Except for those who repent afterwards, and reform; for Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The repentance escape-clause — testimony and standing restored per classical reading (*Source: classical fiqh — verify; the text itself names the exception, its consequences are jurisprudence*).
* **`[24:6]`** "As for those who accuse their own spouses, but have no witnesses except themselves, the testimony of one of them is equivalent to four testimonies, if he swears by Allah that he is truthful."
  * **Gloss:** The spousal exception: no external witnesses exist, so the husband's oath-count stands in for four testimonies — the li'an procedure begins.
* **`[24:7]`** "And the fifth time, that Allah’s curse be upon him, if he is a liar."
  * **Gloss:** The oath escalates to self-imposed curse at the fifth step — a religious sanction in place of evidence.
* **`[24:8]`** "But punishment shall be averted from her, if she swears four times by Allah, that he is a liar."
  * **Gloss:** Her counter-oaths avert the punishment — the procedure is adversarial and symmetric in oath.
* **`[24:9]`** "And the fifth time, that Allah’s wrath be upon her, if he is truthful."
  * **Gloss:** Her fifth step invokes wrath, not curse — the pairing (curse on him/wrath on her) is the text's own asymmetry.
* **`[24:10]`** "Were it not for Allah’s grace upon you, and His mercy, and that Allah is Conciliatory and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The refrain breaks the sentence off unfinished — grace is why the procedure exists at all; "Conciliatory" (tawwab) also answers 24:5's repenters.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`made obligatory`** (farada): `[24:1]`.
  * **Rendering:** "A chapter that We have revealed, and made obligatory" — "made obligatory" occurs only here in the corpus (rg-verified); an-Nur is the one surah that declares itself *mandatory*.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:114]`-type "places of worship" law-grammar aside, the clarity/purpose clause parallels `[24:34]` "We have sent down to you clarifying revelations… and advice for the righteous."
* **`adulteress / adulterer`** (zani/zaniya): `[24:2]`.
  * **Rendering:** plain "adulteress/adulterer" for the unmarried-and-married alike in Itani's English.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:5]` "not in adultery, nor as mistresses" and `[4:25]` "if they commit adultery, their punishment shall be half that of free women" — the same root across the corpus; the marital status behind the Arabic term is a classical-fiqh debate the translation does not settle (*Source: classical fiqh — verify*).
* **`chaste women`** (muhsanat): `[24:4]`.
  * **Rendering:** "chaste women" here; `[24:23]` renders the slander targets "honorable, innocent, believing women" — the chastity family gets two English coats in one surah.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:5]` "So are chaste believing women, and chaste women from the people who were given the Scripture"; `[24:33]` "if they desire to remain chaste" — same root, same word "chaste."
* **`four witnesses`**: `[24:4]`, `[24:13]`.
  * **Rendering:** the evidentiary quorum for accusation of unchastity.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:15]` "you must have four witnesses against them, from among you" — the older corpus parallel; classical readers dispute which statute abrogates which (*Source: classical fiqh — verify*).
* **`li'an oaths (four testimonies… fifth…)`**: `[24:6–9]`.
  * **Rendering:** oath-counts with curse/wrath escalation.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:61]` "let us invoke Allah's curse on the liars" — the mubahala's five-fold invocation pattern; the nearest structural twin in the corpus.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:222]` "Allah loves the repentant, and He loves those who keep clean" — 24:5's repent-and-reform clause inside the same mercy-frame.
* **Parallel:** `[24:13]` re-runs 24:4's "four witnesses" standard against the slander band — the statute and its narrative application sit in consecutive ruku.
* **Within surah:** `[24:10]`'s "Were it not for Allah's grace upon you, and His mercy" is the ruku-refrain that returns at 24:14, 20, 21 — grace brackets law.
* **Parallel:** `[34:20]` "except for a group of believers" — same phrase "a group of believers" that 24:2 makes a courtroom audience.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical lists — verify); the ruku is the surah's legal opening, read by classical jurists as one of the densest hadd-statute blocks in the Quran (*Source: classical fiqh — verify*).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the punishment content above is stated as text-claims; the classical readings add stipulations the text does not spell out here (e.g., stone vs. lashing for the married person, conditions of testimony) — *Source: classical fiqh — verify; not adjudicated in this note*.
