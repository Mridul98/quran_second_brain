# Permission, gazes, and marriage (24:27–34)
**UID:** 202610141715
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 24:27–34 (Ruku 4 of 9 in an-Nur)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[24:27]` – `[24:34]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Chastity moves from courtroom to household: don't enter other people's homes without permission and greeting, lower the gazes and guard the private parts — women drawing coverings over their breasts within a named intimacy-circle — marry off the singles with the promise that poverty is no veto ("If they are poor, Allah will enrich them"), abstain if you cannot, free aspiring slaves, and never compel girls to prostitution.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4; the privacy-and-provision block that ruku 5's Light verse sits atop — walls, gazes, and houses of God form the surah's spatial theology.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[24:27]`** "O you who believe! Do not enter homes other than your own, until you have asked permission and greeted their occupants. That is better for you, that you may be aware."
  * **Gloss:** The threshold law: two rites before crossing a door — isti'dhan (permission) and salam to its people; "that you may be aware" makes etiquette an awareness-training.
* **`[24:28]`** "And if you find no one in them, do not enter them until you are given permission. And if it is said to you, “Turn back,” then turn back. That is more proper for you. Allah is aware of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** Even an empty home is not an invitation; "Turn back" is a complete answer and obedience to it is "more proper."
* **`[24:29]`** "There is no blame on you for entering uninhabited houses, in which are belongings of yours. Allah knows what you reveal and what you conceal."
  * **Gloss:** The exemption: storehouses, campsites — property-homes with no private life to intrude on; the "no blame" formula appears for the first time in the surah (returns at 24:60–61).
* **`[24:30]`** "Tell the believing men to restrain their looks, and to guard their privates. That is purer for them. Allah is cognizant of what they do."
  * **Gloss:** The male address first: gaze, then private parts; "purer" (azka) — the same purity register as 24:21.
* **`[24:31]`** "And tell the believing women to restrain their looks, and to guard their privates, and not display their beauty except what is apparent thereof, and to draw their coverings over their breasts, and not expose their beauty except to their husbands, their fathers, their husbands' fathers, their sons, their husbands' sons, their brothers, their brothers' sons, their sisters' sons, their women, what their right hands possess, their male attendants who have no sexual desires, or children who are not yet aware of the nakedness of women. And they should not strike their feet to draw attention to their hidden beauty. And repent to Allah, all of you believers, so that you may succeed."
  * **Gloss:** The mirror command plus the covering: khimar ("coverings") drawn over the bosom; a named exemption-list for beauty's audience (kin, women, dependents, desire-less attendants, unaware children); the foot-strike clause extends the gaze-law to sound; the unit's closer is communal repentance "so that you may succeed."
* **`[24:32]`** "And wed the singles among you, and those who are fit among your servants and maids. If they are poor, Allah will enrich them from His bounty. Allah is All-Encompassing, All-Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Marriage as communal duty; the poverty-clause reverses economics — provision follows obedience, not the other way around.
* **`[24:33]`** "And let those who do not find the means to marry abstain, until Allah enriches them from His bounty. If any of your servants wish to be freed, grant them their wish, if you recognize some good in them. And give them of Allah’s wealth which he has given you. And do not compel your girls to prostitution, seeking the materials of this life, if they desire to remain chaste. Should anyone compel them—after their compulsion, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The other direction: celibacy is legislated for the unable ("abstain"); slaves get a right to petition manumission and masters a duty to fund it; the prostitution-compulsion ban names its motive ("the materials of this life") and promises forgiveness to the coerced.
* **`[24:34]`** "We have sent down to you clarifying revelations, and examples of those who passed on before you, and advice for the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The block's seal: revelation as clarification, precedent, and admonition — the kalam-formula of 24:1 restated mid-surah.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ask permission / turn back`** (isti'dhan): `[24:27–28]`, seeds ruku 8–9.
  * **Rendering:** "asked permission and greeted their occupants."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:53]` "Do not enter the homes of the Prophet, unless you are given permission to come for a meal… and when you ask his wives for something, ask them from behind a screen; that is purer for your hearts and their hearts" — the same privacy grammar with a screen, and the same word "purer" as 24:30.
* **`coverings`** (khimar): `[24:31]` "draw their coverings over their breasts."
  * **Rendering:** the bosom-covering command; "what is apparent thereof" left undefined in translation — its scope is a classical dispute (*Source: classical fiqh — verify*).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[24:60]` "outer clothing" (a different garment-word in the Arabic, one leniency-class); `[33:59]` "to lengthen their garments" — the corpus's veil-lexicon needs 24:31 + 24:60 + 33:59 read together; watch for the English collision at `[18:57]` "coverings over their hearts" (a different Arabic word, same English).
* **`beauty / apparent / hidden`** (zinah): `[24:31]` three times.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:33]` "do not display yourselves, as in the former days of ignorance"; `[24:60]` "provided they do not flaunt their finery" — the zinah display-cluster.
* **`restrain their looks`** (ghadd al-basar): `[24:30]`, `[24:31]`.
  * **Rendering:** identical verb for men and women — the gaze is legislated symmetrically before the covering clause is added for women.
  * **Elsewhere:** unique construction in the corpus (the verb cluster "restrain" appears with other objects elsewhere — `[3:134]` "restrain anger", `[4:77]` "restrain your hands").
* **`enrich`** (aghna): `[24:32]`, `[24:33]`.
  * **Rendering:** "Allah will enrich them from His bounty" / "until Allah enriches them from His bounty" — the double promise wraps the marriage law.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:25]` "If any of you lack the means to marry free believing women…" — the same means-logic of marriage across the corpus.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)

* **Parallel:** `[24:31]`'s "so that you may succeed" ↔ `[23:1]` "Successful are the believers" — the falah-word links the two surahs' program-verses.
