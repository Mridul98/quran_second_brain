# The Light verse and its darkness-mirror (24:35–40)
**UID:** 202610141716
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 24:35–40 (Ruku 5 of 9 in an-Nur)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[24:35]` – `[24:40]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's namesake: Allah as the Light of the heavens and the earth, parabolized as lamp-in-glass fed by an neither-east-nor-west olive whose oil glows before fire touches it — "Light upon Light" — installed in houses where God's name is raised and glorified morning and evening by traders-un-distracted; and the mirror: the disbeliever's deeds as a mirage and as stacked darkness in a sea, sealed by "He to whom Allah has not granted a light has no light."
* **Placement:** Ruku 5; the pivot between the household-light (27–34: walls, gazes, marriages) and the cosmos-light (41–46: a glorifying creation) — the surah's theological summit.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[24:35]`** "Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. The allegory of His light is that of a pillar on which is a lamp. The lamp is within a glass. The glass is like a brilliant planet, fueled by a blessed tree, an olive tree, neither eastern nor western. Its oil would almost illuminate, even if no fire has touched it. Light upon Light. Allah guides to His light whomever He wills. Allah thus cites the parables for the people. Allah is cognizant of everything."
  * **Gloss:** Seven nested images — light, pillar/setting, lamp, glass, star-like brilliancy, blessed olive beyond east/west, self-glowing oil — climaxing in the doubled noun "Light upon Light," then the operational clause: *guidance* to His light, whom He wills; parable-citing as pedagogy; omniscience as closer. (Itani's "pillar" for mishkat is the edition's rendering — the familiar "niche" appears nowhere in this corpus.)
* **`[24:36]`** "In houses which Allah has permitted to be raised, and His name is celebrated therein. He is glorified therein, morning and evening."
  * **Gloss:** The light has a location: lifted houses (masajid per classical reading — *Source: classical tafsir — verify*) where the Name is celebrated; the daily bracket "morning and evening."
* **`[24:37]`** "By men who neither trading nor commerce distracts them from Allah’s remembrance, and from performing the prayers, and from giving alms. They fear a Day when hearts and sights are overturned."
  * **Gloss:** The house-keepers defined by non-distractibility — market without amnesia; prayer and zakat; fear of the Day of cardiac and ocular upheaval.
* **`[24:38]`** "Allah will reward them according to the best of what they did, and He will increase them from His bounty. Allah provides for whomever He wills without reckoning."
  * **Gloss:** Reward graded to their *best*, then overflow "from His bounty," then the un-counted provision formula (see §3).
* **`[24:39]`** "As for those who disbelieve, their works are like a mirage in a desert. The thirsty assumes it is to be water. Until, when he has reached it, he finds it to be nothing, but there he finds Allah, Who settles his account in full. Allah is swift in reckoning."
  * **Gloss:** The first mirror-image: expectant thirst, vanishing water — but the void is not empty: "there he finds Allah"; swift reckoning answers "without reckoning" (38).
* **`[24:40]`** "Or like utter darkness in a vast ocean, covered by waves, above which are waves, above which is fog. Darkness upon darkness. If he brings out his hand, he will hardly see it. He to whom Allah has not granted a light has no light."
  * **Gloss:** The second mirror: layered ocean-darkness, hand-invisible; "Darkness upon darkness" is the inverted rhyme of "Light upon Light"; the closing clause universalizes — light is granted, not possessed.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`light`** (nur): `[24:35]` ×5 (incl. "Light upon Light"), `[24:40]` ×2, `[24:46]` "enlightening revelations."
  * **Rendering:** noun, genitive, and verb-family all from the n-w-r root; the surah title itself.
  * **Elsewhere (the verified light-dossier):** `[5:15]` "A light from Allah has come to you, and a clear Book"; `[14:1]` "bring humanity from darkness to light"; `[2:257]` "He brings them out of darkness and into light"; `[6:1]` "made the darkness and the light"; `[39:22]` "follows a light from His Lord"; `[57:12]` "their light radiating ahead of them"; `[66:8]` "complete our light for us"; `[9:32]`/`[61:8]` "They want to extinguish Allah's light… Allah refuses except to complete His light." The task's proposed link 24:35 ↔ 57:12 ↔ 66:8 holds: 57:12 "radiating ahead" and 66:8 "streaming before" describe the eschatological share of the very light 24:35 grants "whomever He wills."
* **`pillar (on which is a lamp)`** (mishkat): `[24:35]`.
  * **Rendering:** the edition's choice; "niche" is zero-hit corpus-wide (rg-verified); "pillar" elsewhere carries other words (`[13:2]` "heavens without pillars", `[89:7]` "Erum of the pillars").
  * **Elsewhere:** the lamp/glass/olive chain is unique; compare the Prophet as "an illuminating beacon" `[33:46]` and the sun as "a lamp" `[71:16]` — the corpus re-uses lamp-lexicon for guided persons and cosmic lights.
* **`morning and evening`**: `[24:36]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:42]` "And glorify Him morning and evening"; `[48:9]` "praise Him morning and evening"; `[76:25]` "mention the Name of your Lord, morning and evening" — the doxology-bracket cluster; the phrase "morning and evening" itself occurs in 10 corpus lines (rg-verified), the non-doxological uses elsewhere being `[6:52]`, `[18:28]`, `[19:11]`, `[19:62]`, `[25:5]`, `[40:46]`.
* **`without reckoning`** (bighair hisab): `[24:38]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:37]` "Allah provides to whom He wills without reckoning" (word-identical); `[2:212]` "without measure"; `[39:10]` the steadfast "paid their wages in full, without reckoning" — the provision formula of the pious-traders verse.
* **`mirage`** (sarab): `[24:39]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[78:20]` "the mountains are set in motion, and become a mirage" — two hits only; a vanishing-substance word in both.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:17]` "when it illuminated all around him, Allah took away their light, and left them in darkness" — the corpus's first light/darkness parable; 24:39–40 is its expanded diptych (mirage + deep).
* **Parallel:** `[2:114]` "Who is more unjust than him who forbids the remembrance of Allah's name in places of worship, and contributes to their ruin?" — the negative of 24:36: houses *raised* vs. houses ruined.
* **Parallel:** `[62:11]` "they scramble towards it, and leave you standing" — the inverse portrait of 24:37's undistracted traders.
* **Within surah:** `[24:35]`'s "Light upon Light" ↔ `[24:40]`'s "Darkness upon darkness" — the unit's rhyme-pivot; `[24:38]` "without reckoning" ↔ `[24:39]` "swift in reckoning" — arithmetic inverted inside consecutive verses.
* **Parallel:** `[44:2]` "By the Enlightening Scripture" and `[27:13]` "Our enlightening signs" — 24:46's adjective is the book's own claim; the kalam link is real (see index §4).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical lists — verify) — remarkable for containing the Quran's most quoted mystical verse; the reading of 24:35 splits classically: light-of-guidance/metaphorical (muta'awwila) vs. "real" divine-name readings of the salaf, and the lamp-terms map variously onto heart, prophet, scripture, or believer in transmitted interpretations — *Source: classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir-type registers and Sufi commentaries) — verify; the text states the parable and leaves the mapping open ("Allah thus cites the parables")*.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** none specific transmitted for 24:35 itself.
