# Surah 13: THUNDER (ar-Ra’d)
**UID:** 202610141561
**Type:** Index / Surah Map
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Classically DISPUTED. Printed mushaf heads say Medinan; the commentators are split. Medinan camp: Ibn Abbas (one route), al-Kalbi, Muqatil, Qatadah, al-BaydawI, Abu al-Su’ud. Meccan camp: al-Hasan, ‘Ikrimah, ‘Ata’, Jabir, Fakhreddin Razi, Ibn ‘Ashur (and Mujahid’s narration from Ibn Abbas). al-Suyuti’s reconciliation: Meccan except a few ayahs (12–13 and 43 are the named candidates). *Source: transmitted camp-reports as canvassed in the classical surah-introductions (al-Itqan-type lists; Elmalili/Alusi summaries) — agent labeling, verify in print.*
* **The Bukhari point (verify):** the claim that *Sahih al-Bukhari* contains a hadith placing this surah’s revelation in Madinah could NOT be confirmed. Bukhari’s relevant material sits in *Al-Adab Al-Mufrad* (722, 724): Ibn az-Zubayr’s practice at hearing thunder — “Glory be to the One whom the thunder glorifies with praise of Him, and the angels from fear of Him” (a Companion’s practice; Bukhari grades nothing sound in that chapter). The actual Medinan-driving reports are the asbab at 13:8–14: the visit of ‘Amir ibn al-Tufayl and Arbad ibn Qays to Madinah and Arbad’s death by thunderbolt — transmitted by at-Tabarani from Ibn ‘Abbas and quoted at length by Ibn Kathir, whose chains Ibn Kathir himself flags as containing weak narrators; Ibn ‘Ashur calls these reports feeble and keeps the surah Meccan. *Source: Ibn Kathir and Ibn ‘Ashur on 13:8–14 as reported (web-retrieved 2026-10-14) — verify.*
* **Size:** 43 ayahs, 6 ruku (per `data/ruku_map.json`: 7+11+8+5+6+6 = 43). One sajdah-ayah by the transmitted lists, at 13:15. Revelation order 96 in the classical Ibn-Abbas-derived enumeration (a Medinan-camp list counts it 97th); Mushaf position 13, between Yusuf (12) and Ibrahim (14). *Source: transmitted nuzul-order tables (Tanzil docs / al-Zanjani, retrieved 2026-10-14) — verify.*
* **Central theme:** The surah answers a demand for a spectacular miracle by pointing at the sky the questioners are standing under: pillarless heavens, regulated sun and moon, lightning of fear and hope, and a thunder that praises Him (13:13 — the surah’s name). Against the spectacle-demand it sets a covenant ethics — keep the promise, join what Allah has commanded joined, repel evil with good (13:20–25) — and a consolation: hearts find comfort in the remembrance of Allah (13:28). It closes by stripping the Messenger of every function except clear delivery: “your duty is to inform, and Ours is the reckoning” (13:40), with Allah as the sufficient witness (13:43).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Cosmos as the answer to spectacle (1–18):** the Book-claim under letters (1) → the sky-without-pillars inventory (2–4) → resurrection-scoffers and their yokes (5–6) → the miracle-demand and the warner’s limitation (7), then ruku 2 shows the living thunder answering the same demand (13:13).
* **Covenant as the real sign (19–26):** sight of the truth (19) sorts people by promise-keeping (20–24) vs. promise-breaking and severing (25); provision dispensed and restricted (26) relativizes the whole worldly stake.
* **Refusal of the spectacular Quran (27–31):** the demand recurs; the answer is guidance-to-the-repentant (27), the comfort verse (28), then the rhetorical “even if” — mountains moved, earth shattered, dead speaking would not change the hard hearts (31).
* **The prophetic pattern (32–37):** ridicule-respite-seizure as the historical norm (32), partners unmasked as words (33), the Garden sequel (35), and the Arabic code of law with a warning to the Messenger himself (36–37).
* **The closing contraction of roles (38–43):** every era its scripture (38–39), the Messenger’s duty reduced to informing (40), Allah’s judgment unstoppable (41–42), and the final witness (43).
