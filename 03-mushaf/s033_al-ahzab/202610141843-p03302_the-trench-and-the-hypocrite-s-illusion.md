# The Trench remembered: a wind, unseen forces, and the hypocrite's illusion (33:9–20)
**UID:** 202610141843
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 33:9–20 (Ruku 2 of 9 in al-Ahzab)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[33:9]` – `[33:20]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The Battle-of-the-Trench ruku: the believers are told to remember Allah's blessings when the confederate armies came and God answered with a wind and forces they could not see; the siege's terror is named (eyes dazed, hearts at throats, doubts about Allah); and the hypocrites' speech — "Allah and His Messenger promised us nothing but illusion" — is dissected as a disease of the heart, exposed in stinginess, rolling eyes, and sharp tongues.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 9; the outer-front drama the surah opens on. It turns ruku 1's label "hypocrites" (33:1) into a full portrait and sets up the believers' counter-word at 33:22 (ruku 3).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[33:9]`** "O you who believe! Remember Allah’s blessings upon you, when forces came against you, and We sent against them a wind, and forces you did not see. Allah is Observant of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The *shukr* imperative ("Remember Allah's blessings") framed as military memory; the two divine counters — a wind ("the wind" of the Trench storm) and "forces you did not see" (unseen hosts; the identical unseen-hosts phrase at `[9:40]`). Task-verified wording: "blessings" (not "favor"), "forces" (not "armies").
* **`[33:10]`** "When they came upon you, from above you, and from beneath you; and the eyes became dazed, and the hearts reached the throats, and you harbored doubts about Allah."
  * **Gloss:** Pincer-geometry of the siege ("from above… from beneath") and panic rendered somatically: dazed eyes, "hearts reached the throats," and "doubts about Allah" (*zann*) — the human low-point, before the test is named.
* **`[33:11]`** "There and then the believers were tested, and were shaken most severely."
  * **Gloss:** The sabr-frame: *ibtala* ("tested") and *zulzila* ("shaken most severely") at the place and hour of crisis — the storm-word of `[2:214]` and the "test" idiom the sabr note tracks.
* **`[33:12]`** "When the hypocrites and those in whose hearts is sickness said, “Allah and His Messenger promised us nothing but illusion.”"
  * **Gloss:** The *nifaq* line proper: the label "hypocrites" plus "those in whose hearts is sickness" (a verbatim twin of `[8:49]`); their verdict on the promise is *ghurur* — "nothing but illusion." Task's "diseased" ≠ Itani's "sickness"; and the *tremble* is 33:10–11, not here.
* **`[33:13]`** "And when a group of them said, “O people of Yathrib, you cannot make a stand, so retreat.” And a faction of them asked the Prophet to excuse them, saying, “Our homes are exposed,” although they were not exposed. They only wanted to flee."
  * **Gloss:** The demoralizing speech-act and the false exemption ("Our homes are exposed… although they were not exposed"). "Yathrib" appears exactly once in the corpus (rg-verified here) — the pre-covenant name of the city spoken by those who wanted out.
* **`[33:14]`** "Had it been invaded from its sides, and they were asked to dissent, they would have done so with little hesitation."
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual exposes the loyalty as circumstantial: breach the city's edges and they would have apostatized (*shirk*) at once (Source: the verse's own conditional; the referent — the city — is agent inference — verify).
* **`[33:15]`** "Although they had made a pledged to Allah, in the past, that they will not turn their backs. A pledge to Allah is a responsibility."
  * **Gloss:** Covenant-grammar returns: a prior pledge not to "turn their backs" (*tawallun* — flight from battle), and the ruling "a pledge to Allah is a responsibility" — the *mithaq*-note motif.
* **`[33:16]`** "Say, “Flight will not benefit you, if you flee from death or killing, even then you will be given only brief enjoyment.”"
  * **Gloss:** Fleeing neither saves nor extends: at most "brief enjoyment" (*mata' qalil*) — the Quran's phrase for the world's short lease (cf. `[9:38]`).
* **`[33:17]`** "Say, “Who is it who will shield you from Allah, if He intends adversity for you, or intends mercy for you?” Besides Allah, they will find for themselves neither friend nor helper."
  * **Gloss:** The *tawhid* of protection: no shield against God's intent, adverse or merciful; "neither friend nor helper" — the exclusivity the tawhid note defines.
* **`[33:18]`** "Allah already knows the hinderers among you, and those who say to their brethren, “Come and join us.” Rarely do they mobilize for battle."
  * **Gloss:** Foreknowledge of the sabotage network: *suddan* ("the hinderers") and the pull-away invitation "Come and join us," with the observation that they "rarely mobilize" (cf. `[4:78–79]`).
* **`[33:19]`** "Being stingy towards you. And when fear approaches, you see them staring at you— their eyes rolling—like someone fainting at death. Then, when panic is over, they whip you with sharp tongues. They resent you any good. These have never believed, so Allah has nullified their works; a matter easy for Allah."
  * **Gloss:** The full portrait in motion: stinginess (*bukhl* — the infaq-note inverse), the death-faint rolling eyes (a verbatim twin of `[47:20]`), and, once safe, "whip you with sharp tongues"; the verdict "These have never believed" nullifies their works — the nifaq note's "the trade does not profit."
* **`[33:20]`** "They assumed that the confederates had not withdrawn. But were the confederates to advance, they would wish they were in the desert with the Bedouins, inquiring about your news. And if they were among you, they would have done little fighting."
  * **Gloss:** The wish to be absent ("with the Bedouins, inquiring about your news") and the arithmetic of their presence: "they would have done little fighting" — the same desert-Arab lagging as `[48:11–15]` (nifaq note).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`"Remember Allah's blessings" (ni`ma)`** `[33:9]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Remember Allah's blessings upon you, when forces came against you" — favor-recall as the entry to a battle-memory.
  * **Elsewhere:** the identical *shukr*-opening `[5:11]` "O you who believe! Remember Allah's blessings upon you; when certain people intended to extend their hands against you" — the gratitude-audit formula (Shukr note) applied to deliverance.
* **`a wind / forces you did not see`** `[33:9]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We sent against them a wind, and forces you did not see."
  * **Elsewhere:** "forces you did not see" is the exact phrase of `[9:40]` (the cave); the angel-aid of Badr (`[3:124]` "three thousand angels," `[8:9]` "one thousand angels") is the corpus's other divine-reinforcement register — the Trench's aid here is wind + unseen hosts.
* **`"hearts is sickness" (marad)`** `[33:12]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the hypocrites and those in whose hearts is sickness."
  * **Elsewhere:** verbatim twin `[8:49]` (same battle context); the sickness-cluster spans `[2:10]`, `[5:52]`, `[9:125]`, `[47:20, 29]`, `[74:31]`, and here 33:32, 60 (Nifaq note's decay chain).
* **`"illusion" (ghurur)`** `[33:12]`:
  * **Rendering:** "promised us nothing but illusion."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[8:49]`'s hypocrites say "Their religion has deluded (*gharr*) these people" — same root, the delusion projected onto the believers' promise.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; classically and unanimously tied to the Battle of the Trench (al-Khandaq, 5 AH), with the Banu Qurayza aftermath in ruku 3 (Source: classical asbab reports — verify).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** "Yathrib" (33:13) is the city's older name; the identification of the "homes are exposed" excuse with specific households is transmitted, not in the corpus line (Source: classical reports — verify).
