# Surah 33: THE CONFEDERATES (al-Ahzab)
**UID:** 202610141841
**Type:** Index / Surah Map
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Medinan — unanimously so in the classical surah-lists; no transmitted notice attaches any ayah to Mecca. *Source: classical lists — verify in print (al-Itqan-type introductions).*
* **Order:** 90 per the Ibn-Abbas-derived enumeration table (same convention as the s024=102 / s022=103 indices). *Source: transmitted nuzul-order tables — verify.* Transmitted lists disagree: al-Ya'qubi places it after al-Hashr; the Ibn-al-Durays and Ja'far-al-Sadiq lists give 89; a Shahrastani list via Ali gives 84; al-Jabiri 95; Darwazah 97 — none of the classical orders carries internal dating of the passages, and the surah reads as a composite of Trench-, Banu Qurayza-, and household-revelation groups (Source: modern survey, Ünsal, *Ilahiyat Studies* 7.1 (2016), on the asbab data; verify).
* **Size:** 73 ayahs, 9 ruku (`data/ruku_map.json`: 8+12+7+7+6+12+6+10+5 = 73 ✓; manifest agrees: 73 ayahs). Mushaf position 33, between As-Sajdah (32) and Saba (34).
* **Name:** al-Ahzab — "The Confederates/The Parties," named for the coalition that besieged Madinah (the Battle of the Trench, 5/627 CE): "And when the believers saw the confederates…" (33:22); "They assumed that the confederates had not withdrawn" (33:20). The corpus title is THE CONFEDERATES.
* **Central theme:** The surah holds two fronts at once — the armies *outside* the city (ruku 2–3, the Trench and Banu Qurayza) and the household *inside* it (ruku 4–7, the Prophet's wives, homes, and persons) — and legislates the boundaries of the community that survived both: who counts as kin (33:4–6, 40), how the Messenger's family and dwellings are to be honored (33:28–34, 53), what speech is owed him (33:56–57, 69), and what the hypocrite's inner trade finally costs (33:12, 18–20, 60). Its structural hinge is the **pledge** (33:7–8, 15, 23) answered by the **Trust** (33:72): a people contracted to God, closing on the verdict for the hypocrites, the idolaters, and the believing men and women (33:73).

### Theme checks the task asked to verify honestly (against the corpus)
* `[33:9]` **Trench/armies ✓** — verbatim "O you who believe! Remember Allah's blessings upon you, when forces came against you, and We sent against them a wind, and forces you did not see." Itani renders *ni`ma* as **"blessings"** (not "favor") and *junud* as **"forces"** (not "armies"); the "wind and forces you did not see" are here, the unseen-hosts phrase twin of `[9:40]`.
* `[33:12]` **Hypocrites' speech ✓** — verbatim "When the hypocrites and those in whose hearts is sickness said, 'Allah and His Messenger promised us nothing but illusion.'" The *nifaq* link is text-supported: 33 uses the hypocrite label 6x (rg-verified: 33:1, 12, 24, 48, 60, 73), and the "hearts is sickness" formula is a verbatim twin of `[8:49]`. NOTE the task's "hearts are **diseased**" is not Itani's wording ("sickness"); and the "tremble" is 33:10–11 ("the hearts reached the throats… shaken most severely"), not 33:12.
* `[33:22]` **Believers' affirmation ✓** — verbatim "And when the believers saw the confederates, they said, 'This is what Allah and His messenger have promised us; and Allah and His messenger have told the truth.' And it only increased them in faith and submission." The "promised us of truth" claim is present; guidance/sabr fit (the "increased in faith" idiom twins `[3:173]`, `[9:124]`).
* `[33:25]` **NOT the domestic verse** — the task flagged "Prophet's domestic life (33:25?)," but `[33:25]` reads "Allah repelled the disbelievers in their rage… Allah thus spared the believers combat." The domestic/household material is ruku 4 (33:28–34) and the Zayd episode is `[33:37]`, *not* 33:25.
* `[33:37]` **Zayd episode ✓** — verbatim "you hid within yourself what Allah was to reveal" (task's "concealing" ✓ in sense) and "when **Zaid** ended his relationship with her, We gave her to you in marriage… no restriction for believers regarding the wives of their adopted sons." The text's *own* claim is legal (adopted-son marriage); **"Zaynab" is never named in the corpus (rg → 0 hits)** — her identification and the marriage biography are Source: classical narrative — verify.
* `[33:6]` **Family-law anchor ✓** — verbatim "The Prophet is more caring of the believers than they are of themselves, and his wives are mothers to them." Task's "closer… than themselves" is close, but Itani's word is "more caring of"; the wives-are-mothers clause is here, enforced at `[33:53]` "nor must you ever marry his wives after him."
* `[33:72]` **Trust verse ✓** — verbatim "We offered the Trust to the heavens, and the earth, and the mountains; but they refused to bear it… but the human being accepted it. He was unfair and ignorant." Task's "man undertook it — he was unjust, ignorant": Itani renders the subject as **"the human being,"** the verb as **"accepted it,"** and the two epithets as **"unfair and ignorant"** (not "man/unjust"). "The Trust" is the sole capital-T rendering in the corpus. NOTE: the khalifa/successor word does **not** appear in s033 (rg → 0); link to covenant is via *mithaq* (33:7–8, 15, 23), not via *khilafa*.
* `[33:56]` **Salawat verse ✓** — verbatim "Allah and His angels give blessings to the Prophet. O you who believe, call for blessings on him, and greet him with a prayer of peace."
* `[33:71]` **"a great victory" ✓ (task said 33:71?)** — verbatim "Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger has won a great victory." Itani's closing word is **"a great victory"** (not "a mighty conquest"); "mighty" belongs to `[33:25]` "Allah is Strong and Mighty." The "obey… great victory" idiom twins `[24:52]` and `[4:13]`.
* `[33:33]` **"stay in your homes" — PRESENT ✓** — verbatim "And settle in your homes; and do not display yourselves, as in the former days of ignorance." Itani renders *qarna* as "settle," and the same phrase does **not** recur elsewhere ("settle in your homes" = 1 corpus hit; the *jahiliyyah* word is "the former days of ignorance").

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Identity of the community (1–8):** the surah opens by fixing loyalties and lineages. Do not obey unbelievers or hypocrites (1); trust Allah as trustee (3); adopted sons are not sons — call them by their fathers (4–5); the Prophet has more care of the believers than they of themselves, his wives are their mothers, and kin take precedence "in Allah's Book" (6); the covenant of the prophets — Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad — is recalled (7–8).
* **The outer test: the Trench (9–27):** the wind and unseen forces are remembered (9), the siege's terror named (10–11), the hypocrites' "illusion" speech exposed (12–20) — the stingy, eye-rolling, tongue-whipping type (19); then the believers' counter-word, "He has told the truth" (22), the men true to their pledge (23), and God repelling the armies and granting the siege-breakers' land (25–27).
* **The inner test: the household (28–52):** the wives are given the choice — this world's finery or Allah, His Messenger, and the Home (28–34); the ten-pair virtue list universalizes the reward (35), and no believer may choose against Allah and His Messenger (36); the Zayd episode settles the adopted-son law (37–38) and declares Muhammad the seal of the prophets (40); remembrance, the blessed greeting, and the Prophet's marriage permissions follow (41–52).
* **Honor and speech (53–58):** the screen, the ban on marrying his wives after him, the salawat (56), and the curse on those who insult Allah and His Messenger (57–58) — the community's etiquette of the tongue.
* **Boundaries and the Hour (59–73):** the garment command (59); a final warning to the hypocrites, the sick-hearted, and the rumormongers of the City (60–62); the Hour deferred to Allah's knowledge (63) and the regret of the Fire — "if only we had obeyed Allah and obeyed the Messenger" (66–68); then the pattern of Moses abused and cleared (69), the command of straightforward speech (70), and the Trust that the heavens refused and the human being accepted (72), closing on the verdict for hypocrites, idolaters, and the believing men and women (73).
