# Fear Allah, the renormalized lineages, and the solemn pledge (33:1–8)
**UID:** 202610141842
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 33:1–8 (Ruku 1 of 9 in al-Ahzab)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[33:1]` – `[33:8]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The opening ruku fixes the community's loyalties before the siege narrative: the Prophet is told to fear Allah and not obey the unbelievers and hypocrites, to trust Allah alone; then three identity-corrections land in one breath — adopted sons are not real sons, wives named "mothers" are not mothers, and kin take precedence in Allah's Book — and the ruku closes on the covenant taken from the prophets, Noah through Jesus to Muhammad, a "solemn pledge" whose whole point is that God will question the sincere about their sincerity.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 9; the surah's address-and-identity preface to the Trench (ruku 2) and the household law (ruku 4–7). Its word "hypocrites" (33:1) sets the term that ruku 2 will dramatize.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[33:1]`** "O Prophet! Fear Allah, and do not obey the unbelievers and the hypocrites. Allah is Knowledgeable and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The opening address pairs *taqwa* ("Fear Allah") with a refusal — "do not obey the unbelievers and the hypocrites"; the two-name closing "Knowledgeable and Wise" grounds the command in God's knowing.
* **`[33:2]`** "And follow what is revealed to you from your Lord. Allah is fully aware of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The positive counter to 33:1's "do not obey": the only thing to follow is *ma yooqa ilayk* — "what is revealed to you from your Lord" (a kalam-lineage verse).
* **`[33:3]`** "And put your trust in Allah. Allah is enough as a trustee."
  * **Gloss:** *tawakkul*: reliance re-set on God alone — "Allah is enough as a trustee" (the same idiom as `[3:173]` "Allah is enough for us; He is the Excellent Protector").
* **`[33:4]`** "Allah did not place two hearts inside any man's body. Nor did He make your wives whom you equate with your mothers, your actual mothers. Nor did He make your adopted sons, your actual sons. These are your words coming out of your mouths. Allah speaks the truth, and guides to the path."
  * **Gloss:** A three-part correction: no one has two hearts (the hypocrite's double-heart image reversed onto physiology, not here onto faith — the literal claim is stated); *zihar* (naming a wife "as your mother") does not make her a mother; an adopted son is not a biological son — "These are your words coming out of your mouths." The clause "Allah speaks the truth, and guides to the path" pairs speech (kalam) with guidance (*sirat*).
* **`[33:5]`** "Call them after their fathers; that is more equitable with Allah. But if you do not know their fathers, then your brethren in faith and your friends. There is no blame on you if you err therein, barring what your hearts premeditates. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The legal consequence of 33:4: adoptees take their fathers' names; if the father is unknown, they are "your brethren in faith and your friends" — the faith-kinship netting; premeditated heart-intent remains the one blame-bearing residue.
* **`[33:6]`** "The Prophet is more caring of the believers than they are of themselves, and his wives are mothers to them. And blood-relatives are closer to one another in Allah’s Book than the believers or the emigrants, though you should do good to your friends. That is inscribed in the Book."
  * **Gloss:** The family-law anchor: the Prophet's *awla* (care/authority) over the believers exceeds their own; his wives are "mothers to them" (a status bond — cf. the marriage-ban at 33:53), yet inheritance-right runs to "blood-relatives… in Allah's Book." Task-verified: "closer… than themselves" is close, but Itani's word is "more caring of."
* **`[33:7]`** "Recall that We received a pledge from the prophets, and from you, and from Noah, and Abraham, and Moses, and Jesus son of Mary. We received from them a solemn pledge."
  * **Gloss:** The *mithaq*-verse: the covenant is taken from the prophets collectively and from "you" (Muhammad) specifically, the *ummiyyin*-chain named (Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus) and the doubled "solemn pledge" (mithaqan ghalizan — the identical phrase at `[4:154]`).
* **`[33:8]`** "That He may ask the sincere about their sincerity. He has prepared for the disbelievers a painful punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The covenant's purpose: the truthful questioned about their truthfulness (*sidq*) — the surah's root-motif (cf. 33:22 "told the truth," 33:23 "true to what they pledged") — with the disbelievers' "painful punishment" as the counter-verdict.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`zihar / "equate with your mothers"`** `[33:4]`:
  * **Rendering:** "your wives whom you equate with your mothers, your actual mothers" — a formulaic pre-Islamic divorce-oath voided here.
  * **Elsewhere:** the same law-form is the whole subject of `[58:2–3]` "Those of you who estrange their wives by equating them with their mothers—they are not their mothers"; 33:4 voids the status, 58 legislates the expiation (Source: structural observation on the English corpus).
* **`"his wives are mothers to them"`** `[33:6]`:
  * **Rendering:** a relational/status claim: "more caring of the believers than they are of themselves, and his wives are mothers to them."
  * **Elsewhere:** the marriage-ban that enforces this mother-status is `[33:53]` "nor must you ever marry his wives after him, for that would be an enormity with Allah."
* **`solemn pledge (mithaqan ghalizan)`** `[33:7]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We received from them a solemn pledge."
  * **Elsewhere:** the *solemn pledge* phrase is a corpus doublet — only `[4:154]` ("We received from them a solemn pledge," of Israel) and here — 33:7 universalizes to all prophets what 4:154 said of one people.
* **`the sincere / sincerity (sidq)`** `[33:8]`:
  * **Rendering:** "That He may ask the sincere about their sincerity."
  * **Elsewhere:** the same root drives 33:22 "Allah and His messenger have told the truth" and 33:23 "true to what they pledged to Allah" — the *sadaqa* family is the surah's positive word, *nifaq* its negative (see Knowledge note).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (Source: classical lists — verify); the lineage- and marriage- rulings read against the early Madinan community's adoption and oath-customs.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the "two hearts" clause is classically read as a rebuttal of a claim (e.g., that someone's heart holds two loves, or of a man whose tongue raced his heart); the Zayd-related *naskh* of adoption is narrated in 33:37 (Source: classical tafsir — verify; the corpus line itself makes only the general statement).
