# Prophecy in a few years and the outer aspect of this life (30:1–10)
**UID:** 202610141812
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 30:1–10 (Ruku 1 of 6 in ar-Rum)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[30:1]` – `[30:10]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens with a dated, falsifiable prediction — the defeated Romans will triumph "in a few years" — and anchors it in the speaker's character ("Allah never breaks His promise", 6); it then names the blindness that makes such promises unreadable: knowing only "an outer aspect of the worldly life" (7), cured by reflection within (8) and travel through ruined history (9–10).
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 6; prophecy → promise → epistemic diagnosis → empirical cure. Its promise-clause frames the whole surah: `[30:60]` "The promise of Allah is true" is the ring-close (Source: structural observation on the corpus).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[30:1]`** "Alif, Lam, Meem."
  * **Gloss:** Untranslated opening letters (see Fleeting note); the same cluster opens six surahs — al-Baqarah, Ali 'Imran, al-Ankabut, ar-Rum, Luqman, as-Sajdah (rg-verified).
* **`[30:2]`** "The Romans have been defeated."
  * **Gloss:** A news-headline as revelation: passive voice — the victor is unnamed, only the fact of defeat is stated, in the perfect tense, as if already sealed.
* **`[30:3]`** "In a nearby territory. But following their defeat, they will be victorious."
  * **Gloss:** Place, then reversal: defeat→victory in one breath — the task's "nearest land / will overcome" is other translations' wording; this edition: "a nearby territory... will be victorious" (correction verified).
* **`[30:4]`** "In a few years. The matter is up to Allah, in the past, and in the future. On that day, the believers will rejoice."
  * **Gloss:** A window, not a date: "a few years" (bid' sinin, classically glossed three-to-nine — Source: classical tafsir, Ibn Kathir/Tabari transmitting Ibn 'Abbas on 30:4 — verify); ownership of the command "in the past, and in the future"; the believers' emotion is written into the prediction.
* **`[30:5]`** "In Allah’s support. He supports whomever He wills. He is the Almighty, the Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** Rejoicing is redefined as divine patronage, distributed by will ("whomever He wills"); the refrain "the Almighty, the Merciful" is the near-exclusive signature of Ash-Shu'ara (26:9/68/104–191) with externals `[30:5]` and `[36:5]` (rg-verified).
* **`[30:6]`** "The promise of Allah—Allah never breaks His promise, but most people do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The epistemic clause: the guarantee is on the promise, not on recognition; the "most people do not know" motif begins (recurring at 30:8, 30:30; twin phrasing `[34:36]` "but most people do not know").
* **`[30:7]`** "They know an outer aspect of the worldly life, but they are heedless of the Hereafter."
  * **Gloss:** The diagnosis: a surface-competence ("an outer aspect" — corpus-unique, rg-verified) married to heedlessness; they can read history's fronts but not its authorship.
* **`[30:8]`** "Do they not reflect within themselves? Allah did not create the heavens and the earth, and what is between them, except with reason, and for a specific duration. But most people, regarding meeting their Lord, are disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** Cure one: self-reflection; the design-argument "except with reason, and for a specific duration" verbatim-twins `[46:3]` ("except with reason, and for a finite period") and truth-family `[15:85]`, `[10:5]`; the "meeting with their Lord" denial-twin `[13:2]` "certain of the meeting with your Lord."
* **`[30:9]`** "Have they not travelled the earth and seen how those before them ended up? They were more powerful than them, and they cultivated the land and developed it more than they developed it, and their messengers came to them with clear signs. Allah would never wrong them, but they used to wrong themselves."
  * **Gloss:** Cure two: ruin-tourism as empiricism — twin commands `[3:137]` "travel the earth and note the fate of the deniers" and `[29:20]` (the previous surah); power-plus-engineering did not save them; the theodicy clause "they used to wrong themselves" (family `[7:177]`); the "more powerful" motif `[9:69]`.
* **`[30:10]`** "Then, evil was the end of those who committed evil. That is because they rejected Allah’s revelations, and used to ridicule them."
  * **Gloss:** The boomerang law: ridicule returns on its riders (`[6:10]` "those who mocked them became besieged by what they ridiculed"; `[13:32]`, `[21:41]`); the crime is named: rejection plus mockery of revelation.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`bid' sinin`** ("a few years"):
  * **Rendering:** "In a few years" at 30:4; the same Arabic phrase renders "several years" at `[12:42]` (Joseph's extra prison-years) — observable term drift in one translation.
  * **Elsewhere:** window-language of this corpus: 30:4 is the only "few years"; the duration-theme continues 30:8 "a specific duration" and `[11:8]` "a stated time."
* **`al-wa'd` (promise):**
  * **Rendering:** "The promise of Allah—Allah never breaks His promise" (30:6); closing ring "The promise of Allah is true" (30:60).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:80]`, `[22:47]` carry the never-breaks clause verbatim; `[40:55]`, `[40:77]`, `[31:9]`, `[35:5]` the "is true" clause — the covenant note's promise-grammar, not pledge-grammar (no mithaq word in s030).
* **`zahir` ("an outer aspect"):**
  * **Rendering:** "They know an outer aspect of the worldly life" (30:7) — corpus-unique (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** its opposite is supplied in-surah: "the Hereafter" (30:7), "meeting their Lord" (30:8), the Hour-clauses of ruku 2 (12, 14, 16).
* **`Almighty, the Merciful`:**
  * **Rendering:** 30:5 closes the support-clause with the welded pair.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:9/68/104/122/140/159/175/191]` (refrain), `[36:5]` — power and mercy bound together wherever help is decreed.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the prediction was uttered, per the same lists, before its fulfillment — the surah's whole argument assumes an audience able to check it.
* **Asbab / narrative context (classical, labeled):** the Byzantine–Sasanian war — Persia defeated Byzantium in the early 610s CE in "the lowest/nearby land" (adna al-ard; Itani "a nearby territory"); classical reports have Quraysh rejoicing at their fellow-polytheists' victory and the believers expecting the People-of-the-Book Romans' reversal; the "few years" glossed 3–9; fulfillment read as Heraclius' counteroffensive (622–628), and some transmitted reports tie "on that day the believers will rejoice" to the day of Badr. *Source: classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir and al-Tabari's reports on 30:2–5) — verify dates in print.*
* **The book's empirical-prediction claim:** the text itself stakes its truth-claim on this dated reversal ("The promise of Allah... most people do not know", 6). *Source: agent inference from the dated wording — verify against the book's larger falsifiability argument.*
