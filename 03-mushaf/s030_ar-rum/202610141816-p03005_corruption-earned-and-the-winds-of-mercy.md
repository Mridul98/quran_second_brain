# Corruption earned, the unavertable Day, and the winds bearing good news (30:41–53)
**UID:** 202610141816
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 30:41–53 (Ruku 5 of 6 in ar-Rum)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[30:41]` – `[30:53]` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The public verdict: corruption on land and sea is what people's hands have earned, and the taste of consequence is pedagogical — "so that they might return" (41); roam the idolaters' ruins (42), re-issue the face-setting command before the unavertable Day (43), because every account is personal (44–45) — and the mercy the returns seek is already circulating: winds, ships, bounty, thanks (46), the messengers' precedent ending "It is incumbent on Us to help the believers" (47), the cloud-sequence proving the Reviver of the dead (48–50) — while the fickle who only disbelieve when the wind turns yellow (51) are the dead, deaf, and blind the preacher cannot reach (52–53).
* **Placement:** Ruku 5 of 6; the surah's moral low-point and mercy high-point fused; 30:43 re-issues 30:30; the thanks-clause of 30:46 finally lands what the ruku 3 inventory deferred.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[30:41]`** "Corruption has appeared on land and sea, because of what people’s hands have earned, in order to make them taste some of what they have done, so that they might return."
  * **Gloss:** The diagnosis in one line, with its four clauses: appearance, moral cause (the hands-formula of 30:36 now universalized — twin `[42:30]` "Whatever misfortune befalls you, it is because of what your hands have earned"), measured taste ("some of" — restraint), terminal hope ("so that they might return").
* **`[30:42]`** "Say, “Roam the earth, and observe the fate of those who came before. Most of them were idolaters.”"
  * **Gloss:** The field-method restated: near-verbatim twin `[29:20]` (the previous surah's command) and `[3:137]` "travel the earth and note the fate of the deniers"; the census result: idolatry, not weakness, was the common denominator.
* **`[30:43]`** "So devote yourself to the upright religion, before there comes from Allah a Day that cannot be averted. On that Day, they will be shocked."
  * **Gloss:** 30:30's command, now with an eschatological clock: "cannot be averted" — the avert-formula of `[11:8]` ("it will not be averted from them") and `[6:147]` ("His wrath cannot be averted"); the upright-religion idiom: `[98:5]`, `[6:161]`.
* **`[30:44]`** "Whoever disbelieves, upon him falls his disbelief. And whoever acts righteously— they are preparing for themselves."
  * **Gloss:** The self-account law — twins `[17:15]` "whoever is guided—is guided for his own good... whoever goes astray—goes astray to his detriment" and `[31:12]` "appreciative for the benefit of his own soul"; Luqman's arithmetic applied to the Day.
* **`[30:45]`** "So that He may reward those who have believed and done the righteous deeds out of His bounty. Indeed, He does not love the ungrateful."
  * **Gloss:** Reward is bounty, not wage; and the love-clause lands: "He does not love the ungrateful" — near-verbatim of `[2:276]` "Allah does not love any sinful ingrate" — welding this ruku's return-motif (41) to the gratitude audit (kufr = the cover-leg).
* **`[30:46]`** "And of His signs is that He sends the winds bearing good news, to give you a taste of His mercy, and so that the ships may sail by His command, and so that you may seek of His bounty, and so that you may give thanks."
  * **Gloss:** The sign-inventory returns with its missing terminus: winds as good-news-bearers (`[7:57]` "sends the wind ahead of His mercy"; `[27:63]` "heralds of His mercy" — rendering drift, rg-verified), ships/bounty/thanks nearly verbatim in `[16:14]` "you see the ships plowing through it, as you seek His bounties, so that you may give thanks"; the taste-word now mercy-side (33, 36, 41, 46 — one verb of pedagogy).
* **`[30:47]`** "Before you, We sent messengers to their people. They came to them with clear proofs. Then We took revenge on those who sinned. It is incumbent on Us to help the believers."
  * **Gloss:** Salvation-history compressed to a law: proofs, vengeance on the sinful, and a staggering divine self-obligation ("incumbent on Us") — twins `[40:51]` "Most surely We will support Our messengers and those who believe" and `[58:21]` "I will certainly prevail, I and My messengers."
* **`[30:48]`** "Allah is He who sends the winds. They stir up clouds. Then He spreads them in the sky as He wills. And He breaks them apart. Then you see rain drops issuing from their midst. Then, when He makes it fall upon whom He wills of His servants, behold, they rejoice."
  * **Gloss:** The meteorological close-up, beat-for-beat with `[24:43]` "you see rain drops emerging from its midst" (issuing/emerging drift); note the will-language twice — as-spread, as-distribution (cf. 30:37 provision-by-will).
* **`[30:49]`** "Although they were before this—before it was sent down upon them—in despair."
  * **Gloss:** The dateline of joy: despair as the before-picture — the despair-term of 30:12/30:36 re-visited as the soil of 30:48's joy; the ruku's rejoicing chain answers 30:4's "the believers will rejoice."
* **`[30:50]`** "So observe the effects of Allah’s mercy— how He revives the earth after it was dead. Indeed, He is the Reviver of the dead. He is Capable of everything."
  * **Gloss:** The third stop of the revival triad (19, 24, 50) — with the same analogical move as `[7:57]` ("Thus We bring out the dead—perhaps you will reflect"); "Reviver of the dead" as a divine epithet is corpus-unique here (rg-verified; cf. the verb at `[36:79]` "He who initiated them in the first instance will revive them").
* **`[30:51]`** "But if We send a wind, and they see it turning things yellow, they would continue thereafter to disbelieve."
  * **Gloss:** The flip-side: one yellowing wind and gratitude cancels — the yellowing-sequence twins `[39:21]` ("they wither and you see them yellowing") and `[57:20]`; the gratitude-audit proves that 30:48's rejoice was never worship, just weather-joy.
* **`[30:52]`** "You cannot make the dead hear, nor can you make the deaf hear the call when they turn away."
  * **Gloss:** The preacher's limit, near-verbatim with the previous surah: `[27:80]` "if they turn their backs and flee"; and the graves-clause `[35:22]` "you cannot make those in the graves hear" — the dead are not the uneducated, they are the departed.
* **`[30:53]`** "Nor can you guide the blind out of their error. You can make hear only those who believe in Our signs, and so have submitted."
  * **Gloss:** Blindness-third, verbatim-family with `[27:81]` ("You can make no one listen, except those who believe in Our verses; for they are Muslims"), `[43:40]`, `[6:36]` "Only those who listen will respond. As for the dead, Allah will resurrect them" — the ruku's own answer to its despair-epilogue.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fasad` (corruption):**
  * **Rendering:** "Corruption has appeared" — this verb-noun pair occurs only here (ẓahara + al-fasad; rg-verified); the edition elsewhere renders the same root as "trouble" (`[2:11]` "Do not make trouble on earth") or "spread corruption" (`[2:205]`, `[5:33]`, `[29:36]`, `[28:77]`, `[11:116]`, `[26:183]`) — observable drift.
  * **Elsewhere:** the counter-will is always "return/reform" (islah) — 2:11's irony ("We are only reformers") vs 30:41's stated purpose "so that they might return."
* **`kasb` — "what hands have earned/perpetrated":**
  * **Rendering:** 30:41 "because of what people's hands have earned"; 30:36 "because of what their hands have perpetrated" — one formula, two tenses of the same ruku-chain.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[42:30]` verbatim twin plus its mercy-half ("and yet He pardons much"); `[28:47]`, `[3:182]`/`[22:10]` "your hands have forwarded/advanced" — the hands idiom is the corpus's standard moral-causality grammar.
* **Set-the-face (hanif command):**
  * **Rendering:** 30:43 "devote yourself to the upright religion" — verb identical to 30:30, noun drifted ("religion of monotheism"→"upright religion").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:105]` "dedicate yourself to the true religion—a monotheist" (the command-twin); `[98:5]`/`[6:161]` the upright-religion noun; the clock: `[11:8]` "it will not be averted."
* **`shukr` terminus ("that you may give thanks"):**
  * **Rendering:** 30:46 ends its inventory exactly as the blessing-circuit demands.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:14]` near-verbatim triple (ships/bounty/thanks); `[28:73]` night-day inventory; `[16:78]` faculties inventory; `[7:10]` "but rarely do you give thanks"; `[31:31]` "every persevering, thankful person" — the shukr–sabr couplet.
