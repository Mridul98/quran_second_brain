# The Day of separation and the four times of glorification (30:11–19)
**UID:** 202610141813
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 30:11–19 (Ruku 2 of 6 in ar-Rum)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[30:11]` – `[30:19]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The same God who reverses history originates creation and repeats it, "then to Him you will be returned" (11); on the Day the guilty despair without intercessors, disowning their own partners (12–13), the crowd separates (14) into meadows and hauled torment (15–16) — and the proper human response is doxology at four turning points of the day (17–18), certified by the living/dead exchange performed daily in the land (19).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 6; the eschatological hinge between the political prophecy (ruku 1) and the signs inventory (ruku 3). Verse 19 hands the unit's proof ("Likewise you will be resurrected") to ruku 3's "and of His signs..." chain.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[30:11]`** "Allah originates creation, and then repeats it. Then to Him you will be returned."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's resurrection axiom, stated as origination-plus-repeat; verbatim-family `[10:4]` "He originates creation, and then He repeats it" and `[29:19]` (the immediately preceding surah: "This is easy for Allah").
* **`[30:12]`** "On the Day when the Hour takes place, the guilty will despair."
  * **Gloss:** First Hour-clause (of three: 12, 14, 16 — Source: observable structure); the root of hope-cut-off; contrast with the forbidden despair `[12:87]` "do not despair of Allah's comfort", `[15:56]`.
* **`[30:13]`** "They will have no intercessors from among their idols, and they will disown their partners."
  * **Gloss:** Two lifelines fail at once: the lobby (intercessors) and the line itself (partners disown them) — 6:94's scene ("your intercessors—those you claimed were your partners") performed here in one verse; disavowal-twin `[2:166]`, `[29:25]`.
* **`[30:14]`** "On the Day when the Hour takes place— on that Day they will separate."
  * **Gloss:** The separation-verb (farraqa): `[10:28]` "Then We will separate between them, and their partners will say, 'It was not us you were worshiping'" — the same courtroom divorce of worshipped and worshipers.
* **`[30:15]`** "As for those who believed and did good deeds—they will be delighted in meadows."
  * **Gloss:** The beneficiaries: "meadows" (riyad) — corpus-unique word for the delighted state (rg-verified); belief-plus-works, the surah's standing definition of the saved.
* **`[30:16]`** "But as for those who disbelieved, and rejected Our signs and the encounter of the Hereafter—those will be hauled into the torment."
  * **Gloss:** The mirror clause: "the encounter of the Hereafter" names again what 30:7's heedless denied; "hauled" — dragged, not walking: the verb is the shame.
* **`[30:17]`** "So glorify Allah when you retire at night, and when you rise in the morning."
  * **Gloss:** The "so" (fa) makes doxology the verdict's liturgical answer: evening and dawn; the time-pair twin is `[20:130]` "before the rising of the sun, and before its setting... during the hours of the night... at the borders of the day" and `[50:39]`.
* **`[30:18]`** "His is the praise in the heavens and on earth, and in the evening, and when you reach midday."
  * **Gloss:** The four turning-points close (night, morning, evening, midday — zuhr, corpus-unique for "midday"); praise is already His in the heavens and earth — the doxology-twin `[28:70]` "To Him belongs all praise in this life, and in the next", cf. `[45:36]`.
* **`[30:19]`** "He brings the living out of the dead, and He brings the dead out of the living, and He revives the land after it had died. Likewise you will be resurrected."
  * **Gloss:** The empirical certifier: the seed-cycle (`[6:95]` "splits the grain and the seed... brings the living from the dead"), the provision-question `[10:31]`, the prayer-teaching `[3:27]`, plus the rain-revival family (`[16:65]`, `[57:17]`, `[29:63]`, `[45:5]`, `[2:164]`) — then the analogical kicker: "Likewise you will be resurrected."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`shafa'a` (intercession):**
  * **Rendering:** "They will have no intercessors from among their idols" (30:13).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:94]` intercessors claimed as partners, lost; `[26:100]` "Now we have no intercessors"; `[36:23]` "their intercession will not avail me at all"; the doctrine's condition in `[20:109]` "except for him permitted by the Merciful" and `[2:48]`/`[2:254]` — intercession is never available on demand.
* **`ya's` (despair):**
  * **Rendering:** "the guilty will despair" (30:12); the same verb drives 30:36 ("they begin to despair") and 30:49 ("in despair" — before the rain).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:44]` seized-suddenly "and at once, they were in despair"; `[23:77]` the gate of agony; against it: `[12:87]`, `[15:55–56]` — despair of mercy is the non-believer's reflex (link 30:12↔30:49).
* **`riyad` (meadows):**
  * **Rendering:** "delighted in meadows" (30:15) — hapax in the corpus (rg-verified); pasture, not palace: green, open, grazing peace.
  * **Elsewhere:** contrasted in-surah with "hauled into the torment" (30:16) and with the "delighted" people's opposite audience at 30:36 (rejoicing that ends in despair).
* **`sabbih` (glorify) at the times:**
  * **Rendering:** 30:17–18: retire at night, rise in morning, evening, midday.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:130]`, `[50:39]` — sun-boundary glorifications; classical reports read 30:17–18 as naming the daily prayers (*Source: classical tafsir — Ibn 'Abbas-type reports transmitted by Ibn Kathir/Tabari on 30:17 — verify*).

* **Parallel:** `[6:94]` + `[10:28]` — 30:13–14's two disownings (intercessors gone; partners speak against their worshippers).
* **Parallel:** `[2:166]` + `[29:25]` — followed disown followers; "your destiny is Hell, and you will have no saviors."

* **Within surah:** 30:11 will be restated with its doxology at 30:27 ("something easy for Him... the highest attribute") — the ruku pair; 30:19 begins the revive-the-earth triad (19, 24, 50).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); creed-and-Hour register, no law.
* **Structure note:** three "On the Day when the Hour takes place" clauses (12, 14, 16) stage the ruku as courtroom, then the liturgy (17–18), then the field-proof (19) — verdict → praise → evidence (Source: structural observation on the corpus).
