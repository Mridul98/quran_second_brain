# The frown, the tablet, the food — and the Day of flight (80:1–42)
**UID:** 202610142270
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 80:1–42 (Ruku 1 of 1 in ‘Abasa)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[80:1]`–`[80:42]` (42 ayahs — the entire surah is one ruku in this edition's partition)
* **Thesis:** The surah's one subject is **where attention is spent**: a frown at an approaching blind man is reproved and inverted (80:1–10); the reproved moment is promoted to the status of "a Lesson… on honorable pages… by the hands of scribes" (80:11–16); the human is audited twice, at his origin and at his table (80:17–32); and the Day of the Deafening Noise dissolves every kin bond except each person's own record, partitioning all faces into two (80:33–42).
* **Placement:** Single-ruku surah between the two Day-surahs 79 and 81. Its middle panel is the corpus's clearest heavenly-archive text, and its food panel is the third and shortest of the juz-30 favor-catalogs after 78:6–16 and 79:27–33.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[80:1]`** "He frowned and turned away." **`[80:2]`** "When the blind man approached him."
  * **Gloss:** the text's own claims, stated neutrally: an unnamed third party frowns and turns away; the occasion is a blind man's approach. No name, title or place appears in either line.
* **`[80:3]`** "But how do you know? Perhaps he was seeking to purify himself." **`[80:4]`** "Or be reminded, and the message would benefit him."
  * **Gloss:** the reproof is framed as an epistemic check and offers two open possibilities for the man who approached — purification, or remembrance that benefits. The worth of the encounter is declared unknown to the one who turned away.
* **`[80:5]`** "But as for him who was indifferent." **`[80:6]`** "You gave him your attention." **`[80:7]`** "Though you are not liable if he does not purify himself."
  * **Gloss:** the counter-case, now in the second person: the offense is that man's indifference, the act complained of is giving him attention — and 80:7 bounds the complaint precisely, disclaiming liability for another's purification.
* **`[80:8]`** "But as for him who came to you seeking." **`[80:9]`** "In awe." **`[80:10]`** "To him you were inattentive."
  * **Gloss:** the triangle closes (seeker / in awe / "you were inattentive"): the charge is about allocation of attention, not about the blind man's condition.
* **`[80:11]`** "Do not. This is a Lesson." **`[80:12]`** "Whoever wills, shall remember it."
  * **Gloss:** the interrupted moment becomes a credential: the content is "a Lesson", and access to it runs through the will — 74:55 carries the identical English of 80:12 (verified).
* **`[80:13]`** "On honorable pages." **`[80:14]`** "Exalted and purified." **`[80:15]`** "By the hands of scribes." **`[80:16]`** "Noble and devoted."
  * **Gloss:** the Lesson's archive: pages that are honorable, exalted, purified, handled by scribes described as noble and devoted — "pages" and "scribes" are both single-occurrence renderings in the corpus (rg-verified).
* **`[80:17]`** "Perish man! How thankless he is!" **`[80:18]`** "From what did He create him?" **`[80:19]`** "From a sperm drop He created him, and enabled him."
  * **Gloss:** the first audit, on the human's origin: "Perish" (also 51:10, verified) frames the complaint as ingratitude — the kufr pole of the shukr circuit — and the answer to the origin-question is the lowliest possible material.
* **`[80:20]`** "Then He eased the way for him." **`[80:21]`** "Then He puts him to death, and buries him." **`[80:22]`** "Then, when He wills, He will resurrect him."
  * **Gloss:** four staged acts (ease, death, burial, resurrection), the last deferred explicitly to the divine will rather than to a natural sequence.
* **`[80:23]`** "But no, he did not fulfill what He has commanded him."
  * **Gloss:** verdict on the origin-audit: the command unpaid — the surah's second refusal-particle (after 80:11's "Do not.").
* **`[80:24]`** "Let man consider his food." **`[80:25]`** "We pour down water in abundance." **`[80:26]`** "Then crack the soil open." **`[80:27]`** "And grow in it grains."
  * **Gloss:** the second audit, from the table: one causal chain of three verbs — pouring, splitting, growing — the same spine as 78:14–15 and 50:9–11 (verified), and the same imperative form as 86:5 "Let man consider what he was created from" (verified).
* **`[80:28]`** "And grapes and herbs." **`[80:29]`** "And olives and dates." **`[80:30]`** "And luscious gardens." **`[80:31]`** "And fruits and vegetables."
  * **Gloss:** the produce list, ending in gardens: cf. 78:16 "luxuriant gardens" (verified near-identical), while 6:99's inventory adds pomegranates (verified).
* **`[80:32]`** "Enjoyment for you, and for your livestock."
  * **Gloss:** the purpose clause — verbatim twin of 79:33 "A source of enjoyment for you and for your animals" (verified): both catalogs terminate in enjoyment for humans and their animals.
* **`[80:33]`** "But when the Deafening Noise comes to pass."
  * **Gloss:** the Day arrives under its third unique sound-name in this block (79:6 "the Quake", 79:34 "the Great Cataclysm", 80:33 "the Deafening Noise" — all hapaxes, verified), and with the same "But when…" framing as 79:34.
* **`[80:34]`** "The Day when a person will flee from his brother." **`[80:35]`** "And his mother and his father." **`[80:36]`** "And his consort and his children." **`[80:37]`** "Every one of them, on that Day, will have enough to preoccupy him."
  * **Gloss:** five kin ties named in order — brother, mother, father, consort, children — all become objects of flight, and the reason is not indifference but overdraft: each self has its own sufficient concern (53:38–39 and 39:7 state the same principle legally, verified).
* **`[80:38]`** "Faces on that Day will be radiant." **`[80:39]`** "Laughing and rejoicing." **`[80:40]`** "And Faces on that Day will be covered with misery." **`[80:41]`** "Overwhelmed by remorse."
  * **Gloss:** the partition is written on faces; 75:22 has the identical first line (verified), and remorse is the counter-word of 34:33/10:54's hidden remorse (verified). The book capitalizes "Faces" mid-sentence in 80:40 (typography, quoted as printed).
* **`[80:42]`** "These are the faithless, the vicious."
  * **Gloss:** the surah ends by identifying rather than describing — "the vicious" is a corpus hapax (verified) — and the "Lesson" of 80:11 turns out to be precisely this partition.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"a Lesson"** — 80:11 "Do not. This is a Lesson."
  * **Rendering:** "Lesson" here; the cognate self-designation elsewhere is "reminder": `[73:19]`, `[76:29]` "This is a reminder…", `[81:27]` "It is only a Reminder to all mankind" (verified) — three English renderings of one family; whether the Arabic noun is identical cannot be settled from a translation-only corpus (*Source: agent inference on Itani's word-choice — verify*).
  * **Elsewhere:** "This is a Lesson" has a single corpus occurrence (rg-verified), and its will-pair `[74:55]` is verbatim identical to 80:12.
* **"honorable pages / scribes"** — 80:13–16
  * **Rendering:** four stacked attributes (honorable; exalted and purified; by the hands of scribes; noble and devoted); "pages" and "scribes" are each unique renderings in the corpus (verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[98:2]` "A messenger from Allah reciting purified scripts" and `[87:18–19]` "This is in the former scriptures. The Scriptures of Abraham and Moses." (verified) — the archive motif; cf. also `[81:10]` "When the records are made public".
* **"Enjoyment"** — 80:32 / 79:33
  * **Rendering:** "Enjoyment for you, and for your livestock" (80) vs "A source of enjoyment for you and for your animals" (79) — same purpose-clause, two animals-words (verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[50:11]` "As sustenance for the servants. And We revive thereby a dead town. Likewise is the resurrection." (verified) — provision as resurrection-evidence in the sibling telling.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan by the classical type-lists; some transmitted reports place the scene in the Medinan period, which would make the surah's Meccan sequencing a matter of topic-order. **Source: classical reports — verify.**
* **The crux, text-only:** 80:1–10 gives a frown and turning away by an unnamed "he", a blind man's approach, two possible outcomes for him, an indifferent man who received attention, an exoneration clause, and a rebuke of inattention. It names no party and states no motive for the frown.
* **Classical occasion-reports:** the tafsir tradition identifies the frowner as the Prophet Muhammad, the blind man as ‘Abdullah ibn Umm Maktud, and the indifferent addressee as a Quraysh notable whose conversion was being courted; the reports differ in detail and the surah itself carries none of these names. **Source: classical asbab reports (the standard telling, repeated in the Ibn Kathir / al-Tabari-type accounts) — verify against a printed source.** Kept separate from the textual claims above per the tafsir-honesty rule.
* **"Do not." (80:11):** read variously as a prohibition of the act, a correction of the assumption, or a transition. The corpus prints it as a two-word sentence before "This is a Lesson." — **Source: agent observation on the printed line — verify**; no reading privileged.
