# The Scrolls of Moses: the responsibility ledger, the creed run, the destroyed nations, the command to bow (53:33–62)
**UID:** 202610142134
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 53:33–62 (Ruku 3 of 3 in an-Najm)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[53:33]` – `[53:62]` (30 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A single cited scroll does the surah's closing work: against "him who turned away… gave a little, and held back" (33–34) the text produces "the Scrolls of Moses… and of Abraham, who fulfilled" (36–37), whose contents are personal responsibility end to end (38–42) and a creed run of divine attributes (43–49) that reads like a list of the very powers Meccan piety trusted (a star, the old tribes' fate) — so the destroyed nations (50–54) are precedent, the borrowed marvel-reframe is indictment (55), and the surah ends as worship-address: marvel, laugh, weep, bow (56–62).
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 3; it answers ruku 1's courtroom (the messenger speaks not from desire — here: no one gets what he *desires*, 24↔39) and ruku 2's ownership sentence (31) with the full ledger (38–42) and the attribute-run (43–49).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[53:33]`–`[53:34]`** "Have you considered him who turned away? / And gave a little, and held back?"
  * **Gloss:** The same "Have you considered?" interrogation as 19; the type re-specified: turning away, then stingy token-giving — "gave a little, and held back" is the infaq anti-type (kneading devotion into a transaction with a returned goods-policy).
* **`[53:35]`** "Does he possess knowledge of the unseen, and can therefore foresee?"
  * **Gloss:** The epistemic charge: his bargain feels safe only if the unseen is in his hands — a private-access claim of the exact type the whole surah dismantles (cf. `[53:26]`'s permission-only heaven; `[53:58]` "None besides Allah can unveil it").
* **`[53:36]`–`[53:37]`** "Or was he not informed of what is in the Scrolls of Moses? / And of Abraham, who fulfilled?"
  * **Gloss:** Scripture as the answer to private knowledge: *ṣuḥuf* — scrolls, the pre-Quran archive; Abraham "who fulfilled" (waffā) is the covenant-faithful one (cf. `[2:124]`). Everything that follows is cited *from* these scrolls.
* **`[53:38]`–`[53:39]`** "That no soul bears the burdens of another soul. / And that the human being attains only what he strives for."
  * **Gloss:** Ledger law 1+2: non-transferability of burden, and earnings-only attainment — the direct rebuttal of "whatever he desires" (24) and of vicarious salvation claims.
* **`[53:40]`–`[53:41]`** "And that his efforts will be witnessed. / Then he will be rewarded for it the fullest reward."
  * **Gloss:** Ledger law 3+4: effort enters the record ("witnessed") and is paid "the fullest reward" — asymmetry again: burden non-transferable, reward maximal (cf. 31's "with the best").
* **`[53:42]`–`[53:43]`** "And that to your Lord is the finality. / And that it is He who causes laughter and weeping."
  * **Gloss:** The ledger's horizon ("the finality" — every matter's terminus, cf. `[6:67]` "For every happening is a finality") and the first attribute: He authors *both* joy and grief — a single sovereignty over the emotional register the surah itself will demand (60: "laugh, and do not weep?").
* **`[53:44]`–`[53:46]`** "And that it is He who gives death and life. / And that it is He who created the two kinds—the male and the female. / From a sperm drop, when emitted."
  * **Gloss:** Attributes 2+3: death-and-life in that order; the male/female pair — the very "distribution" (22) that was mocked — is *created by Him*, out of "a sperm drop" (nutfa; twin `[80:19]` "From a sperm drop He created him"); creature-grammar settles the daughters-claim.
* **`[53:47]`–`[53:48]`** "And that upon Him is the next existence. / And that it is He who enriches and impoverishes."
  * **Gloss:** Attributes 4+5: "the next existence" — the second creation is *on Him*, a debt of God (exact twin phrase `[29:20]`); enrich/impoverish removes wealth-god from the board (cf. `[53:29]`'s present-life desirers).
* **`[53:49]`–`[53:50]`** "And that it is He who is the Lord of Sirius. / And that it is He who destroyed the first Aad."
  * **Gloss:** Attribute 6 lands on the brightest fixed star — Sirius (shi'ra), the star whose rising the ancients venerated for fortune: the surah's own star-oath (1) is reframeable, because its Lord is Allah; then the attribute-run meets its case-law: "the first Aad" (the earlier ones; cf. `[69:6]`). *Source: Sirius-veneration background — classical reports; agent inference — verify in print.*
* **`[53:51]`–`[53:52]`** "And Thamood, sparing no one. / And the people of Noah before that; for they were most unjust and most oppressive."
  * **Gloss:** "Sparing no one" (ma yaghādir — total erasure); Noah's people ranked "most unjust and most oppressive" — the catalog's superlative; al-Qamar stages each of these scenes at length (54:9–15, 18–21, 23–31).
* **`[53:53]`–`[53:54]`** "And He toppled the ruined cities. / And covered them with whatever covered them."
  * **Gloss:** The overturned townships (Lot's cities by transmitted identification; cf. `[11:82]` "We turned it upside down") — and again the deliberate blur "whatever covered them," the twin-fold vagueness of `[53:16]` "what covered it" inverted: one covering veiled the Lotus Tree in glory, this one buried a civilization.
* **`[53:55]`** "So which of your Lord's marvels can you deny?"
  * **Gloss:** ar-Rahman's drumbeat imported once (≈ `[55:13]`, ×31 there — rg-verified): the task's catalog-range correction belongs here — the refrain, not the catalog, sits at 55. Note the straight apostrophe in "Lord's" (artifact class).
* **`[53:56]`** "This is a warning, just like the first warnings."
  * **Gloss:** The Quran claims continuity, not novelty: this discourse is one more *nudhr* in the series that reached Aad, Thamud, Noah (50–52) — the precedent catalog (50–54) *is* "the first warnings."
* **`[53:57]`–`[53:58]`** "The inevitable is imminent. / None besides Allah can unveil it."
  * **Gloss:** What is near (the Hour — cf. `[54:1]` "The Hour has drawn near") is precisely what is undisclosed: proximity without schedule; disclosure is exclusively Allah's (the al-Qamar pair: "Is there anyone who would take heed?").
* **`[53:59]`–`[53:60]`** "Do you marvel at this discourse? / And laugh, and do not weep?"
  * **Gloss:** The final questions invert the audience's reflexes: they marvel where they should heed; laughter-without-tears becomes the diagnostic. The emotional-attribute verse answers back: *He* is "the one who causes laughter and weeping" (43) — the audience is playing with one side of a gift they deny.
* **`[53:61]`** "Lost in your frivolity?"
  * **Gloss:** One line for the state: heedless idle play. The task's "performing idle worship" is another translation's reading of this clause.
* **`[53:62]`** "So bow down to Allah, and worship!"
  * **Gloss:** The surah's two-word verdict: an act of prostration and an act of worship addressed to *Allah* alone — the Lotus-Tree vision (6–18) was nearness granted; this is nearness assigned to the reader.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ṣuḥuf` (Scrolls):** the Mosaic-Abrahamic archive cited as public record.
  * **Rendering:** "the Scrolls of Moses" (36); "Abraham, who fulfilled" (37).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[87:18]`–`[87:19]` "This is in the former scriptures. The Scriptures of Abraham and Moses"; `[20:133]` "the former scriptures" — the only other scroll-library loci in this edition (rg-verified).
* **`sa'y` (striving):** 39–40's effort-grammar.
  * **Rendering:** "attains only what he strives for" (39), "his efforts will be witnessed" (40).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:164]` "No soul gets except what it is due"; `[2:286]` "To its credit is what it earns" — the same ledger grammar.
* **`muntaha` (finality) and `the next existence`:** 42 and 47's horizon-words.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[79:44]` "To your Lord is its finality"; `[6:67]` "For every happening is a finality"; `[29:20]` "Then Allah will bring about the next existence" — exact phrase-twin of 47.
* **`shi'ra` (Sirius):** hapax (rg "Sirius": only `[53:49]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** none — the Lord-of-Sirius claim is tawhid applied to the cult of the star that "rises" for fortune.
