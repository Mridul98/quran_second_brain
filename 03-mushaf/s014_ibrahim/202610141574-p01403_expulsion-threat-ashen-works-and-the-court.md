# The expulsion ultimatum, ashen works, and the first emergence before Allah (14:13–21)
**UID:** 202610141574
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 14:13–21 (Ruku 3 of 7 in Abraham/Ibrahim)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[14:13]` – `[14:21]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Persecution reaches its legal form — "expel or apostatize" — and God answers with counter-legislation (inspiration: the wrongdoers destroyed, the believers settled, the promise conditioned on awe of His majesty, 14:13–14); the stubborn tyrant's disappointment (14:15) is then painted inside and outside — a putrid drink that will not go down (16–17), works like ashes on a storm-wind (18) — before the cosmos-argument (19–20) opens the courtroom: all emerge before Allah, and the follower-leader chain starts testifying against itself (21).
* **Placement:** The pivot ruku: the archive of ruku 2 gets its verdict scene; the ruku-boundary here cuts a single episode across itself — 14:21's emergence continues into ruku 4's Satan-speech (14:22), an observable artifact of the Hanafi 556-ruku partition.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[14:13]`** “Those who disbelieved said to their messengers, “We will expel you from our land, unless you return to our religion.” And their Lord inspired them: “We will destroy the wrongdoers.””
  * **Gloss:** The ultimatum (land or religion — both ours) answered by *wahy* to the messengers: a flat divine declaration of the wrongdoers' end.
* **`[14:14]`** ““And We will settle you in the land after them. That is for him who fears My Majesty, and fears My threats.””
  * **Gloss:** Expulsion inverted into inheritance — but the title deed is taqwa: fear of *His* majesty, not theirs.
* **`[14:15]`** “And they prayed for victory, and every stubborn tyrant came to disappointment.”
  * **Gloss:** One line collapses the whole combat cycle: the messengers' prayer for victory, the tyrant's disappointment — the exact phrase "every stubborn tyrant" is borrowed from Hud's Aad-dossier.
* **`[14:16]`** “Beyond him lies Hell, and he will be given to drink putrid water.”
  * **Gloss:** The after-picture of the disappointed tyrant: a drink staged as torture before it is swallowed — or not.
* **`[14:17]`** “He will guzzle it, but he will not swallow it. Death will come at him from every direction, but he will not die. And beyond this is relentless suffering.”
  * **Gloss:** The corpus's most surgical agony verse: attempted gulping, impossible swallowing, death besieging from every direction without release — "relentless suffering" closes the parenthesis.
* **`[14:18]`** “The likeness of those who disbelieve in their Lord: their works are like ashes, in a fierce wind, on a stormy day. They have no control over anything they have earned. That is the utmost misguidance.”
  * **Gloss:** The works-parable: a day's fire, the wind's claim — deeds with zero salvage; "no control over anything they have earned" is the bankruptcy verdict on the 14:7 economy.
* **`[14:19]`** “Do you not see that Allah created the heavens and the earth with truth? If He wills, He can do away with you, and bring a new creation.”
  * **Gloss:** The cosmological lever: the same truth-built creation that began can swap you out — resurrection as a footnote to creation, not a miracle-demand.
* **`[14:20]`** “And that is not difficult for Allah.”
  * **Gloss:** Two words removing the "impossible" objection entirely (near-verbatim twin of `[35:17]`).
* **`[14:21]`** “They will emerge before Allah, altogether. The weak will say to those who were proud, “We were your followers, can you protect us at all against Allah’s punishment?” They will say, “Had Allah guided us, we would have guided you. It is the same for us; whether we mourn, or are patient; there is no asylum for us.””
  * **Gloss:** The court convenes: followers sue their leaders; leaders counter that guidance was never in their hands either — and the scene's last clause admits both sides are equally stuck: mourning and patience now perform identically.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`stubborn tyrant`** (*jabbārin 'anīd*): 14:15's verdict-label.
  * **Rendering:** "every stubborn tyrant came to disappointment."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[11:59]` "they followed the lead of every stubborn tyrant" — verbatim phrase, in the Aad narrative 14:9 just invoked; the tyrant-club: `[40:27]`, `[44:31]`, `[10:83]` (Pharaoh as "a tyrant").
* **`inspired`** (*awḥā*): 14:13's channel of counter-legislation.
  * **Rendering:** "their Lord inspired them."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[41:6]` "it is inspired in me that your God is One God"; `[26:52]` / `[20:38]` "We inspired Moses" — the same verb used of messengers and even the earth (`[99:5]`); the kalam-note mode list begins here.
* **`new creation`** (*khalq jadīd*): 14:19's resurrection option.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:16]` — "If He wills, He can do away with you, and produce a new creation" (near-verbatim twin); the scoffers' phrase "a new creation" at `[13:5]`, `[32:10]`, `[50:15]` is the same term from the denial side.
* **`the weak / those who were proud`** (*muṭaṣdaf / mustakbir*): the court's two benches.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:166–167]` — the followed disown the followers and both wish for a redo; `[7:75]` — the proud argue status with the weak; the follower-chain motif is the corpus's standard afterlife-of-authority scene.
