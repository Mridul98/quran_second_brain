# The blessing exchanged, the prayer and spending commanded, the favors uncountable (14:28–34)
**UID:** 202610141576
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 14:28–34 (Ruku 5 of 7 in Abraham/Ibrahim)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[14:28]` – `[14:34]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The gratitude decree of 14:7 gets its case file: a people who "exchanged the blessing of Allah with blasphemy" and parked their followers in the house of perdition (14:28–29), popping rivals onto the road to bend it (14:30); the community's counter-liturgy is then commanded in one verse — prayer plus spending, secret and public, before the Day when trading and friendship are void (14:31) — and the favor-inventory behind that spending (creation, rain, fruit, ships, rivers, sun and moon, night and day, "something of all what you asked") closes on the verdict 14:7 predicted: incalculable blessings, and "the human being is unfair and ungrateful" (32–34).
* **Placement:** The Makkan application ruku: after the universal court and parable (rukus 3–4), the surah points at its actual auditors and their city's uncountable favors — and at the one verse whose asbab tradition makes it the surah's only Medinian candidate.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[14:28]`** “Have you not seen those who exchanged the blessing of Allah with blasphemy, and landed their people into the house of perdition?”
  * **Gloss:** The bad-exchange verse: *niʿma* traded for *kufr* (here rendered "blasphemy"), and the traders become landlords — "house of perdition" (*dār abār*), a corpus-unique eviction notice.
* **`[14:29]`** “Hell—they will roast in it. What a miserable settlement.”
  * **Gloss:** The apposition resolves the metaphor: the house is Hell; "roast" (*yaslawnahā*) answers their comfortable commerce.
* **`[14:30]`** “And they set up rivals to Allah, in order to lead away from His path. Say, “Enjoy yourselves; your destination is the Fire.””
  * **Gloss:** The exchange's mechanism: *andād* (rivals) placed as road-blocks on the path of 14:1; the command "Enjoy yourselves" is permission-as-sentence — short lease, known destination.
* **`[14:31]`** “Tell My servants who have believed to perform the prayers, and to give from what We have given them, secretly and publicly, before a Day comes in which there is neither trading nor friendship.”
  * **Gloss:** The Meccan twin of the Medinan spending-verse: prayer and infaq, both registers of disclosure (secret/public), with the Day-market clause as deadline — "My servants who have believed" is the surah's gentlest address so far.
* **`[14:32]`** “Allah is He Who created the heavens and the earth, and sends down water from the sky, and with it produces fruits for your sustenance. And He committed the ships to your service, sailing through the sea by His command, and He committed the rivers to your service.”
  * **Gloss:** The evidence inventory opens: cosmos, rain, produce, ships, rivers — *sakhkhara* ("committed... to your service") as the verb of grace-made-usable.
* **`[14:33]`** “And He committed the sun and the moon to your service, both continuously pursuing their courses, and He committed the night and the day to your service.”
  * **Gloss:** The inventory ascends to the sky-clocks — continuous, course-pursuing, all of it "at your service," which is precisely what makes rivals (14:30) an insult to the supply chain.
* **`[14:34]`** “And He has given you something of all what you asked. And if you were to count Allah’s blessings, you would not be able to enumerate them. The human being is unfair and ungrateful.”
  * **Gloss:** "Something of" — even the answered list is partial; the counting verse lands its verdict on *al-insān* in general: unjust about his own account, ingrate about God's — 14:7's second option in anthropological key.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`blessing(s)`** (*niʿmah*): the surah's most-recurring noun (14:6 blessings remembered, 14:28 blessing exchanged, 14:34 blessings uncountable).
  * **Rendering:** Itani drifts within the root: "blessings" (14:6), "the blessing of Allah" (14:28), "Allah's blessings" (14:34) — while `[16:18]` renders the identical counting-clause with "favors" ("if you tried to enumerate the favors of Allah").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:211]` — "Whoever alters the blessing of Allah after it has come to him—Allah is severe in retribution": the exchange idiom's Medinan twin.
* **`exchanged`** (*baddalū*): trade-verb of the bad deal.
  * **Elsewhere:** the nifaq-note's ledger family: `[2:174]` "exchange guidance for error", `[9:9]` "traded away Allah's revelations for a cheap price"; the honest counter-exchange is `[9:111]` (Paradise purchased) — 14:28/2:211 sit in between as the failed trade. *Source: cross-link per the nifaq note's catalog; referents differ — labeled backdrop, not identity.*
* **`rivals`** (*andād*): 14:30.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:22]` — "do not assign rivals to Allah while you know," the verse the tawhid note calls its keystone: same word, same position after a favor-inventory (2:21–22 ↔ 14:32–34 + 14:30) — the inventories end where the rival-making is forbidden.
* **`committed to your service`** (*sakhkhara*): 14:32–33, four times.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[45:12]` — "It is Allah who placed the sea at your service, so that ships may run through it by His command... and that you may give thanks"; `[16:12]` — night/day/sun/moon/stars "disposed by His command" — service-verbs that terminate, as Ibrahim's do, in gratitude clauses.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Near-verbatim twin:** `[2:254]` — "Spend from what We have given you, before a Day comes in which there is neither trading, nor friendship, nor intercession": 14:31's deadline clause, with intercession added in the Medinan version — one command, two revelations settings.
* **Twin inventory:** `[2:21–22]` — habitat/sky/water/fruits ending in the rivals-prohibition; 14:32–34 is the same argument with ships, rivers, and luminaries appended. The tawhid note's evidence list belongs on both.
* **Twin verse:** `[16:18]` — the incalculability clause verbatim family; and `[3:138]` / `[14:52]` show the proclamation-frame this ruku's inventory feeds.
* **Secret-and-public spending:** `[13:22]` "spend... secretly and openly" (ar-Ra'd ruku 3) and `[35:29]` "spend... secretly and publicly, expect a trade that will not fail" — the infaq note's circulation doctrine with 14:31's exact register-pair.
* **Within surah:** 14:34 "unfair and ungrateful" is 14:7's "if you are ungrateful" spoken as anthropology; 14:30's path-hijacking answers 14:3's "repel from the path... seek to make it crooked"; "Enjoy yourselves" (14:30) rhymes 14:3's "prefer the present life."
* **Badr resonance:** the transmitted reports on 28–29 (see §6) read "landed their people into the house of perdition" as the Quraysh leaders' own end at Badr — with `Ali's "the other clan is given enjoyment for a while" echoing 14:30's "Enjoy yourselves" and 14:42's deferral. *Source: at-Tabari's transmitted reports as retrieved 2026-10-14 — labeled tradition, verify; the text itself keeps the referent unnamed.*

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** the surah is Meccan by the classical lists, but this ruku carries its only Medinian exception-report: an-Nahhas (via Mujahid from Ibn `Abbas) and Qatadah: "Meccan except two ayahs" — 14:28–29, revealed at/after Badr concerning the slain pagan leaders; at-Tabari transmits the Badr referent from Ibn `Abbas, `Ali, Sa`id b. Jubayr, Qatadah, and Ibn Zayd (with the `Umar/`Ali "two most wicked clans" variant naming Makhzum/`Abd Shams or Umayya). *Source: web-retrieved 2026-10-14 (quranpedia/an-Nahhas 537; hodaalquran; quran.com Tabari on 14:28) — verify chain-grade in print.* No whole-surah Medinian claim was confirmed.
* **Asbab reach:** reports attach to 28–29 only; 30–34 remain unattributed context-extension. *Source: agent inference — verify.*
