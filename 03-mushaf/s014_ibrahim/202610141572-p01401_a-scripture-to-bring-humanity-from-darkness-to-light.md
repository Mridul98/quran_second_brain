# A Scripture sent to carry humanity from darkness to light (14:1–6)
**UID:** 202610141572
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 14:1–6 (Ruku 1 of 7 in Abraham/Ibrahim)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[14:1]` – `[14:6]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens by stating the Book's purpose in one breath — a Scripture revealed so that the Messenger may bring humanity out of darknesses into light, by their Lord's permission, onto the path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy — then grounds that purpose in a universal law of revelation (every messenger in his people's tongue, 14:4) and instantiates it in Moses, whose commission is the same itinerary plus a memory-assignment: remind them of the Days of Allah (14:5–6).
* **Placement:** Commissioning ruku. The 14:1 purpose-statement is the surah's spine; 14:5 re-states it verbatim in Moses' mouth, and the ruku closes on the gratitude engine (`[14:7]`) that takes over in ruku 2.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[14:1]`** “Alif, Lam, Ra. A Scripture that We revealed to you, that you may bring humanity from darkness to light—with the permission of their Lord—to the path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy.”
  * **Gloss:** Letters, then the Book-claim with a mission clause: the destination is a *path*, and even the transport happens "with the permission of their Lord" — the Messenger carries, God grants.
* **`[14:2]`** “Allah—to whom belongs what is in the heavens and the earth. And woe to the disbelievers from a severe torment.”
  * **Gloss:** Ownership grounds the mission: the One whose path it is owns both arenas; the first "woe" warning brackets the address.
* **`[14:3]`** “Those who prefer the present life to the Hereafter, and repel from the path of Allah, and seek to make it crooked—these are far astray.”
  * **Gloss:** The anatomy of refusal: preference, repulsion, and the project of straightening the road by bending it — the "crooked" seekers define the path's reality by trying to deform it.
* **`[14:4]`** “We never sent any messenger except in the language of his people, to make things clear for them. Allah leads astray whom He wills, and guides whom He wills. He is the Mighty, the Wise.”
  * **Gloss:** The epistemic rule: clarity, not spectacle — revelation arrives in the audience's own tongue; then the twin clauses of divine discretion show guidance and misguidance as His prerogative (cf. the permission clause of 14:1).
* **`[14:5]`** “We sent Moses with Our signs: “Bring your people out of darkness into light, and remind them of the Days of Allah.” In that are signs for every patient and thankful person.”” [sic — stray trailing quote-marker in corpus line]
  * **Gloss:** 14:1's program issued as a commission to Moses; "the Days of Allah" makes history a sermon, and the reader-audience is named: the patient-thankful pair (the surah's engine, staffed here).
* **`[14:6]`** “Moses said to his people, “Remember Allah’s blessings upon you, as He delivered you from the people of Pharaoh, who inflicted on you terrible suffering, slaughtering your sons while sparing your daughters. In that was a serious trial from your Lord.””
  * **Gloss:** The Exodus memory-assignment executed: blessing and trial (*balā'*) named in one breath — survival itself is the test that gratitude or ingratitude will answer.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Alif, Lam, Ra`** (muqatta'at): transliterated letter-names, numbered ayah 1, untranslated.
  * **Rendering:** Itani glosses nothing (edition-wide stance).
  * **Elsewhere:** the same three letters open `[10:1]`, `[11:1]`, `[12:1]`, `[15:1]` — Ibrahim sits mid-cluster (Yunus, Hud, Yusuf, Ibrahim, al-Hijr), and Yusuf is the Mushaf's immediately preceding letter-opening. See the Fleeting note.
* **`darkness → light`** (*ẓulumāt → nūr*): plural darknesses, singular light.
  * **Rendering:** "from darkness to light" (14:1), "out of darkness into light" (14:5) — Itani collapses the Arabic plural in both.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:257]` — the corpus's inversion verse: Allah brings believers "out of darkness and into light," disbelievers' lords "out of light and into darkness"; also `[5:16]`, `[57:9]`, `[65:11]` — the whole family pairs the motif with *bi-idhnillah* ("by His permission"), exactly as 14:1 does.
* **`the Days of Allah`** (*ayyām Allāh*: calamities/blessings as divine appointments in history).
  * **Rendering:** "the Days of Allah" in 14:5; the phrase occurs only twice in the corpus — here and `[45:14]` ("forgive those who do not hope for the Days of Allah").
* **`patient and thankful`** (*ṣabbār shakūr* — intensive active participles, one address).
  * **Rendering:** "every patient and thankful person" (14:5).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[31:31]` "every persevering, thankful person" — the near-verbatim twin; the pair is the corpus's named reader of deliverance-signs.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Twin verse:** `[2:257]` — darkness→light with the believer/ disbeliever reversal; 14:1 is the same journey stated as mission rather than verdict. *Source: observable corpus pairing; the "same journey" framing is agent inference.*
* **Twin verse:** `[65:11]` — a messenger "who recites to you Allah's Verses, clear and distinct, that he may bring... from darkness into light": messenger + clear recitation + light-transport, the exact 14:1/14:4 bundle.
* **Twin verse:** `[45:14]` — the only other "Days of Allah"; hope-for-the-Days is what 14:5 commands people to be reminded of.
* **Twin verse:** `[41:44]` — "Had We made it a Quran in a foreign language, they would have said, 'If only its verses were made clear'": 14:4's language principle argued from the other side; also `[13:37]` "an Arabic code of law."
* **Near-verbatim:** `[2:49]` and `[7:141]` — the Exodus formula (deliverance, terrible suffering, slaughtering sons/sparing women, "a tremendous trial from your Lord") recited as 14:6 is; Ibrahim's version is the shortest of the three.
* **Within surah:** `[14:5]` re-utters `[14:1]` — the ruku installs the theme as God's speech *about* Moses and then as Moses' speech; `[14:4]` guides/leads astray anticipates `[14:27]` "Allah gives firmness... and Allah leads the wicked astray."
* **Path motif:** `[1:6–7]` — the Fatihah's petition "Guide us to the straight path" answered in kind by "the path of the Almighty" (14:1); the guidance note's "road, not abstraction" fits Ibrahim's wording. *Source: structural observation on Itani's renderings; agent inference — verify.*
