# Satan's last speech and the parable of the two trees (14:22–27)
**UID:** 202610141575
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 14:22–27 (Ruku 4 of 7 in Abraham/Ibrahim)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[14:22]` – `[14:27]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The verdict session closes with the last defendant speaking: when "the issue is settled," Satan concedes the only honest item in his portfolio — Allah's promise was true, his was fraud, and he had no authority beyond being answered (14:22); the believers' counter-scene is gardens, permanence, and the greeting "Peace" (14:23); then the surah's central parable makes the entire contest botanical — the good word a deep-rooted, sky-reaching, season-fruiting tree; the bad word an uprooted thing with no stability — and Allah's own action names the winner: He firms the believers "with the firm word" in this life and the Hereafter (24–27).
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 opened the courtroom (14:21); this ruku finishes it and pivots from the end to the seed — the word that stands or falls is the same word 14:1 was revealed to carry people from darkness to light.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[14:22]`** “And Satan will say, when the issue is settled, “Allah has promised you the promise of truth, and I promised you, but I failed you. I had no authority over you, except that I called you, and you answered me. So do not blame me, but blame yourselves. I cannot come to your aid, nor can you come to my aid. I reject your associating with me in the past. The wrongdoers will have a torment most painful.””
  * **Gloss:** The most self-indicting speech in the book: promise-truth vs. promise-fraud, authority disclaimed ("I called; you answered"), mutual aid dissolved, and shirk repudiated retroactively — the followers' god was a caller with no hands.
* **`[14:23]`** “But those who believed and did good deeds will be admitted into gardens beneath which rivers flow, to remain therein forever, by leave of their Lord. Their greeting therein will be: “Peace.””
  * **Gloss:** The other verdict — admitted, eternal, and *bi-idhni Rabbihim*: even paradise runs on permission (echoing 14:1's "with the permission of their Lord"); the greeting is the word of safety itself.
* **`[14:24]`** “Do you not see how Allah presents a parable? A good word is like a good tree—its root is firm, and its branches are in the sky.”
  * **Gloss:** The parable's first half: a word with root-system and canopy — grounded down, upward-reaching, i.e., a statement that is also a living structure.
* **`[14:25]`** “It yields its fruits every season by the will of its Lord. Allah presents the parables to the people, so that they may reflect.”
  * **Gloss:** Yield without season-loss, again "by the will of its Lord" — productivity is creaturely, not self-generated; the reflection-clause trains the reader on the method itself.
* **`[14:26]`** “And the parable of a bad word is that of a bad tree—it is uprooted from the ground; it has no stability.”
  * **Gloss:** The counter-tree: no grip, no tenure, no fruit reported — a word ripped from the ground it never truly held.
* **`[14:27]`** “Allah gives firmness to those who believe, with the firm word, in this life, and in the Hereafter. And Allah leads the wicked astray. Allah does whatever He wills.”
  * **Gloss:** The parable decoded into divine action: *yuthabbit* — the firming verb of the rooted tree applied to people, across both abodes; misguidance of the wicked is stated, not explained, and closed by total volition.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`good word / bad word`** (*kalimah ṭayyibah / khabīthah*): 14:24, 14:26.
  * **Rendering:** "A good word is like a good tree"; "a bad word... a bad tree" — the word-pair is corpus-unique in its tree-parable form (`rg "good tree|bad tree" corpus/` → only 14:24/14:26).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:10]` "To Him ascends speech that is pure, and He elevates righteous conduct" — the same quality-root (ṭ-y-b) on the ascending-words axis; the good/bad opposition in `[7:157]` (commanding the good) is the ethical, not the verbal, register.
* **`firm word`** (*al-qawl al-thābit* — "the firm/steadfast saying"): 14:27's instrument of firmness.
  * **Rendering:** "with the firm word, in this life, and in the Hereafter" — the phrase is corpus-unique here.
  * **Classical reading:** the firm word as the word of faith / *lā ilāha illā Allāh*, with the verse read into the tomb-examination reports — *Source: classical tafsir on 14:27 (Ibn Kathir-type; the grave-Q&A hadiths attach to this verse in transmitted exegesis) — agent labeling, verify in print; the text itself says only "Allah gives firmness... with the firm word."*
* **`authority`** (*sulṭān*): 14:22's renunciation — "I had no authority over you."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:30]` "We had no authority over you" — verbatim in as-Saffat's leader-chain speech; the doctrine-cluster: `[15:42]` / `[17:65]` "you have no authority over My servants", `[16:99]`, `[34:21]`.
* **`Peace`** (*salām*): 14:23's greeting.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:10]` "their greeting therein is, 'Peace'"; `[33:44]` "Their greeting on the Day they meet Him is, 'Peace'"; `[56:26]` — the Garden's formulaic word.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (index §1); the tree-parable and court-session belong to the surah's late-Meccan argumentation register.
* **Interpretive history (labeled):** the equation "good tree = the believer" (and the date-palm as its illustrative species) is transmitted exegesis, not text. *Source: classical tafsir on 14:24–26 (Ibn Kathir-type reports) — agent labeling, verify.* The text proper keeps the referent as *kalimah* — a word — not a person.
