# Created in two days: the answering cosmos and the thunderbolt of Aad and Thamood (41:9–18)
**UID:** 202610142003
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 41:9–18 (Ruku 2 of 6 in Fussilat)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[41:9]` – `[41:18]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The argument migrates from the Book to the Builder: a creation liturgy counted in days — earth in two, provisions in four, the smoke-sky answered into seven universes with laws — performs the surah's proof that equals are impossible; and when the precedent peoples (Aad, Thamood) prove the same point in reverse, the ruku shows a cosmos that *obeyed* when summoned, against the humans who pleaded deafness in ruku 1.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 6; the tawhid engine of the surah. Its "Come, willingly or unwillingly" (41:11) is the first speech-scene of creation — answered by the skins' "Allah, Who made all things speak" (41:21); its "from before them and from behind them" (41:14) is quoted verbatim-in-pattern at 41:42 about the Book.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[41:9]`** "Say, “Do you reject the One who created the earth in two days? And you attribute equals to Him? That is the Lord of the Universe.”"
  * **Gloss:** The interrogative tawhid: creation-time against association (cf. `[2:22]` "do not assign rivals to Allah while you know"); "Lord of the Universe" — this edition's singular rendering of *rabb al-'ālamīn* (drift from 1:2 "Lord of the Worlds," rg-verified).
* **`[41:10]`** "He placed stabilizers over it; and blessed it; and planned its provisions in four days, equally to the seekers."
  * **Gloss:** Mountains as ballast ("stabilizers" — same rendering as `[31:10]`; drift to "mountains" at `[16:15]`), blessing, and a measured provisioning "equally to the seekers" — provision as a shared, searchable order (cf. `[51:22]` "in the heaven is your livelihood").
* **`[41:11]`** "Then He turned to the sky, and it was smoke, and said to it and to the earth, “Come, willingly or unwillingly.” They said, “We come willingly.”"
  * **Gloss:** The sky as *dukhan* (smoke — the namesake sign of surah 44, `[44:10]`); the double address answered: creation's obedient "We come willingly." Same willingly/unwillingly idiom as `[3:83]` (everything submitted) and `[13:15]` (everything prostrating). Ruku 1's screened humans did not answer; the inorganic world did (Source: agent observation on the ruku sequence).
* **`[41:12]`** "So He completed them as seven universes in two days, and He assigned to each universe its laws. And We decorated the lower universe with lamps, and for protection. That is the design of the Almighty, the All-Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Seven "universes" (this edition's rendering of *samāwāt*; cf. `[65:12]` "seven heavens," `[2:29]` "made them seven heavens"); each assigned its law — command descending through them (`[65:12]`); the lowest adorned with "lamps" (drift: `[67:5]` "lanterns… missiles against the devils," `[37:6]` "the beauty of the planets") — beauty and defense in one act; closed on "the Almighty, the All-Knowing," the Ha-Meem name-pair of `[40:2]` (and `[46:2]`'s "Honorable, the Wise").
* **`[41:13]`** "But if they turn away, say, “I have warned you of a thunderbolt, like the thunderbolt of Aad and Thamood.”"
  * **Gloss:** The warning-register from 41:4 becomes a named precedent: *ṣā'iqa* — the thunderbolt that struck Israel of old (`[2:55]` "the thunderbolt struck you") and that Allah "sends… striking with them whomever He wills" (`[13:13]`); the two peoples bracketed next.
* **`[41:14]`** "Their messengers came to them, from before them and from behind them, saying, “Do not worship anyone but Allah.” They said, “Had our Lord willed, He would have sent down angels; Therefore, we reject what you are sent with.”"
  * **Gloss:** Total address ("from before them and from behind them") and the stock reply — angels or nothing (answer: `[15:8]` "We do not send the angels down except with reason"); the human-messenger question of `[41:6]` re-enacted historically. The "therefore, we reject" clause keeps the edition's mid-sentence capital T (quote as-is).
* **`[41:15]`** "As for Aad, they turned arrogant on earth, and opposed justice, and said, “Who is more powerful than us?” Have they not considered that Allah, who created them, is more powerful than they? And they went on denying Our revelations."
  * **Gloss:** Power-boasting answered by the creation-argument of 41:9–12 — the made cannot out-might the Maker; "opposed justice" renders their tyranny in one phrase, and the sin-summary is denial of revelations (kin to 41:28's "disregarded Our revelations").
* **`[41:16]`** "So We unleashed upon them a screaming wind, for a few miserable days, to make them taste the punishment of shame in this life; but the punishment of the Hereafter is more shameful; and they will not be saved."
  * **Gloss:** Verbatim-twin punishment line: `[54:19]` "We unleashed upon them a screaming wind, on a day of unrelenting misery" (54:18 names Aad). The two-stage shame — this life and "more shameful" hereafter — the comparatives of humiliation against their pride.
* **`[41:17]`** "And as for Thamood, We guided them, but they preferred blindness over guidance. So the thunderbolt of the humiliating punishment seized them, because of what they used to earn."
  * **Gloss:** Guidance given and traded (the preference-verb's commerce image elsewhere: `[2:16]` "bartered error for guidance" — same sin-pattern; Source: agent observation); Thamood seized by the *ṣā'iqa*, "because of what they used to earn" — the kasb grammar of recompense.
* **`[41:18]`** "And We saved those who believed and were righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The precedent closes on rescue: the 41:8 formula re-styled ("believed and were righteous" = iman + amal) — salvation is the last word of history's case-file, echoing Noah-Lot rescue refrains across the corpus.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`rawāsiy` (stabilizers)**: firm mounts that keep the earth from shifting.
  * **Rendering:** "stabilizers" here and at `[31:10]`; "mountains" at `[16:15]` — this edition's functional vs. lexical split.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:15]` "And he cast mountains on the earth, lest it shifts with you… so that you may be guided" — the ballast read as guidance-sign; `[31:10]` verbatim-kin of 41:10's phrasing.
* **`dukhan` (smoke)**: the primordial state of the sky — and a Day-sign.
  * **Rendering:** "it was smoke" (41:11), creation-register.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[44:10]` "the Day when the sky produces a visible smoke" — surah 44 is named for it (SMOKE); same substance, two eschatological directions: origin and verdict (Source: agent observation).
* **`ṭawʿan/karhan` (willingly/unwillingly)**: the manner of creation's compliance.
  * **Rendering:** "Come, willingly or unwillingly… We come willingly" (41:11).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:83]` everything submitted "willingly or unwillingly"; `[13:15]` all prostrating "willingly or unwillingly" — the idiom's default home is cosmic obedience, though it also frames the jizya-payment of `[9:29]` and rejected spending of `[9:53]` (rg-verified); 41:21 will make the skins confess speech to the "One Who made all things speak," closing the loop with ruku 3 (Source: agent observation).
* **`ʿālam / samāwāt` (universe/heavens)**: 41:12's "seven universes," "each universe," "the lower universe."
  * **Rendering:** a distinctive Itani choice in this ruku — "universe(s)" where the corpus norm is "heavens" (`[65:12]`, `[2:29]`); "Lord of the Universe" (41:9) vs. 1:2 "Lord of the Worlds" both render *-al-'ālamīn*.
  * **Elsewhere:** "Lord of the Universe" also at `[32:2]`, `[6:71]` (and `[41:9]` here); `[51:47]` "We constructed the universe with power" — the cosmic-singular usage in its only other home (rg-verified cluster).
