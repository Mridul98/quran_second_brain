# "Do not listen… and talk over it": the counter-recitation, and the angels' "Do not fear, and do not grieve" (41:26–32)
**UID:** 202610142005
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 41:26–32 (Ruku 4 of 6 in Fussilat)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[41:26]` – `[41:32]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku names the liturgical crime — a coordinated strategy to drown the recitation with noise — and answers it with the two destinies side by side: the enemies of Allah recompensed with "the worst of what they used to do," while for those who said "Our Lord is Allah" and went straight, the angels descend with the corpus's calmest sentence — "Do not fear, and do not grieve" — and a wish-granting hospitality from the All-Forgiving.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 6; the pivot from the courtroom (ruku 3) to the ethics of the caller (ruku 5). It inverts ruku 1's plea: there they demanded "do what you want" (41:5); here the straight are promised "whatever you call for" (41:31). The "reward uninterrupted" of 41:8 gets its scene.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[41:26]`** "Those who disbelieve say, “Do not listen to this Quran, and talk over it, so that you may prevail.”"
  * **Gloss:** Revelation as a battlefield over attention: the imperative not-to-listen (fulfilling 41:4's "so they do not listen") plus *ilghāw* — "talk over it," this edition's unique rendering (rg-verified corpus-wide). The revealed opposite: `[7:204]` "When the Quran is recited, listen to it, and pay attention, so that you may experience mercy." And note the asymmetry — they strategize because the speech works (Source: agent observation).
* **`[41:27]`** "We will make those who disbelieve taste an intense agony, and We will recompense them according to the worst of what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** "An intense agony" — the same phrase-pair used at `[50:26]` ("toss him into the intense agony") and echoed in Ghafir's dossier; recompense pegged to the *worst* of their deeds — mercy-fronted formulas elsewhere give way here to full-value payback (Source: agent observation on Itani's "worst").
* **`[41:28]`** "Such is the recompense of Allah’s enemies—the Fire—where they will have their permanent home, in recompense for having disregarded Our revelations."
  * **Gloss:** The "enemies" of 41:19 receive their title; the crime is *taghāful* toward revelations ("disregarded") — the passive cousin of 41:26's active drowning-out; "permanent home" (ma'wā) frames both abodes in this ruku.
* **`[41:29]`** "Those who disbelieved will say, “Our Lord, show us those who led us astray—among jinn and humans—and we will trample them under our feet, so they become of the lowest.”"
  * **Gloss:** The companions of 41:25 surface in the Fire: a demand to *see* the seducers (jinn and humans — the 41:25 pair), to trample, to make them *aslāf* (lowest). Same repudiation-cluster as `[2:167]` ("we will disown them, as they disowned us") and `[14:21–22]` (weak vs. proud, and Satan's closing speech).
* **`[41:30]`** "Surely, those who say: “Our Lord is Allah,” and then go straight, the angels will descend upon them: “Do not fear, and do not grieve, but rejoice in the news of the Garden which you were promised."
  * **Gloss:** The axis turns: a shahadah plus *istiqāmah* ("go straight" — answering 41:6's "be upright towards Him"); the angels descend at their death or meeting (classical reading: at death and on the Day; Source: consensus of classical tafsir — verify in print); the consolation formula is the corpus's own: `[10:62]` "Allah’s friends have nothing to fear, nor shall they grieve," and the divine address at `[43:68]`. Book typography: the angels' quotation opens here and runs unbroken to the close of 41:32 (quote as-is; see artifact Fleeting note).
* **`[41:31]`** "We are your allies in this life and in the Hereafter, wherein you will have whatever your souls desire, and you will have therein whatever you call for."
  * **Gloss:** The angels' self-description: *awliyāʾ* — "allies in this life and in the Hereafter" (the same word rendered "friends" at `[10:62]`; drift rg-verified). Two clauses of answered desire: whatever souls crave, whatever tongues ask — the exact reversal of 41:5's "So do what you want, and so will we": there a shrug between strangers, here a grant from allies.
* **`[41:32]`** "As Hospitality from an All-Forgiving, Merciful One.”"
  * **Gloss:** *Qufrān* — "Hospitality" (corpus-cluster: `[3:198]` "hospitality from Allah"; `[18:107]` Gardens "for hospitality"; `[32:19]` "Gardens of Shelter— hospitality for what they used to do"; `[56:56]` "their hospitality on the Day of Retribution"; even the Fire is "hospitality" to the faithless at `[18:102]` and `[37:62]`). The ruku ends on the mercy-names of 41:2 bracketed by "All-Forgiving" — the surah's name-chain keeps its promise (internal close).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`laghw` (talk over it)**: idle/vain noise raised against the recitation.
  * **Rendering:** "talk over it" (41:26) — corpus-unique phrasing for this weapon (rg-verified); cf. the Saqar confession "we used to indulge with those who indulge" (`[74:45]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** the positive counterpart is command, not vocabulary: `[7:204]` listen-and-be-silent at recitation; 41:26 is its mirror-imaged strategy (Source: agent observation).
* **`awliyāʾ` (allies/friends)**: near-guardians of the soul's destiny.
  * **Rendering:** "We are your allies in this life and in the Hereafter" (41:31, angels speaking); "Allah's friends" at `[10:62]` — same root, two renderings across the corpus.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:32]` — the angels take the wholesome with "Peace be upon you; enter Paradise": 41:30–31 is that scene widened into the whole after-life alliance.
* **`khawf/ḥuzn` (fear/grief formula)**: the negated pair of consolation.
  * **Rendering:** "Do not fear, and do not grieve" (41:30).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:62]` (awliyaʾ have nothing to fear nor grieve) and `[43:68]` ("you have nothing to fear on that Day, nor will you grieve") — three-voice distribution: angelic here, divine there, narrator at 10:62 (Source: agent observation).
* **`qufrān` (Hospitality)**: the hosted reward, from the root that also means cover/bounty.
  * **Rendering:** capitalized "Hospitality" (41:32).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[32:19]`, `[3:198]`, `[18:107]`, `[56:56]` — for the Garden; `[18:102]`, `[37:62]` — for the Fire: the same hosting-word for both destinations, a corpus-wide irony.
