# Moses, the followers, and the reports of the towns: the grip, the two classes, the double exception (11:96–109)
**UID:** 202610141530
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 11:96–109 (Ruku 9 of 10 in Hud)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[11:96]` – `[11:109]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The six-town cycle compresses its seventh and greatest case — Moses sent with signs and a clear mandate, Pharaoh's people following a command "not wise," their leader pioneering them into the Fire — and then breaks the narrative open into pure law: these are reports of towns, some still standing, some mown; We did not wrong them; their substitute-gods availed them nothing and added only to their ruin; such is the grip (akhdh) of your Lord on sinning towns; and in that is a sign for whoever fears the Hereafter — the gathered, witnessed Day whose population splits into the miserable and the happy, each duration bracketed by "except as your Lord wills," until the closing word to the anxious believer: do not doubt — their worship is ancestral, and their due will be paid them in full.
* **Placement:** Ruku 9, the surah's jurisprudential distillation: ruku 10's commands will be addressed to the community that has just read the case-file.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[11:96]`** "And We sent Moses with Our signs and a clear mandate."
  * **Gloss:** The interlude opens on the commission formula (cf. 23:45's "clear authority" for the same dispatch) — Musa's mission enters with the full sign-inventory.
* **`[11:97]`** "To Pharaoh and his nobles, but they followed the command of Pharaoh, and the command of Pharaoh was not wise."
  * **Gloss:** The sin is precisely following — the elite's delegation of intellect to the tyrant; "was not wise" (mā kāna rashīdan), one clause of indictment.
* **`[11:98]`** "He will precede his people on the Day of Resurrection, and will lead them into the Fire. Miserable is the place he placed them in."
  * **Gloss:** The leader as vanguard of hell: priority in evil becomes priority at the Fire — the pastorate of ruin — Pharaoh's own self-presentation in Ghafir ('I do not show you except what I see, and I do not guide you except to the path of prudence', `[40:29]` verified) is the rhetoric this verse retorts to.
* **`[11:99]`** "They were followed by a curse in this, and on the Day of Resurrection. Miserable is the path they followed."
  * **Gloss:** The two-world curse formula (11:60's pattern), now with the doubled "miserable" (bi'sā) exclamation — curse and road.
* **`[11:100]`** "These are of the reports of the towns—We relate them to you. Some are still standing, and some have withered away."
  * **Gloss:** The second hinge (cf. 11:49): the town-reports as a genre; the evidence is landscape — standing ruins and erased names (27:52's homes in ruins; verified parallel).
* **`[11:101]`** "We did not wrong them, but they wronged themselves. Their gods, whom they invoked besides Allah, availed them nothing when the command of your Lord arrived. In fact, they added only to their ruin."
  * **Gloss:** The theodicy clause of the whole cycle — no divine injustice anywhere in the record; and the invoked ones not only fail but inflate the loss (the same "availed them nothing" of 15:84's Thamood verdict).
* **`[11:102]`** "Such is the grip of your Lord when He seizes the towns in the midst of their sins. His grip is most painful, most severe."
  * **Gloss:** The akhdh defined: a grip taken mid-sin (ghāran — inattention/while submerged), painful and severe — ruku 1's deferred punishment now shown with its tariff.
* **`[11:103]`** "In that is a sign for whoever fears the punishment of the Hereafter. That is a Day for which humanity will be gathered together— that is a Day to be witnessed."
  * **Gloss:** The audience filter: fear makes the reports a sign (āyah); the Day is styled a gathering and a witnessing — this pairing of Day-names occurs only here in the corpus (verified).
* **`[11:104]`** "We only postpone it until a predetermined time."
  * **Gloss:** The answer to ruku 1's sneer (11:8 "What holds it back?") — deferral is policy, not absence.
* **`[11:105]`** "On the Day when it arrives, no soul will speak without His permission. Some will be miserable, and some will be happy."
  * **Gloss:** Permission-bound speech; then the binary — the two classes (shaqiyy/saʿīd) of the surah's final portrait gallery.
* **`[11:106]`** "As for those who are miserable, they will be in the Fire. They will have therein sighing and wailing."
  * **Gloss:** Their audio-file: the only "sighing and wailing" (zafīr, shahīq) pair in the corpus (verified).
* **`[11:107]`** "Remaining therein for as long as the heavens and the earth endure, except as your Lord wills. Your Lord is Doer of whatever He wills."
  * **Gloss:** The first exception-clause on the Fire's duration, closed by total efficacy (faʿʿāl li-mā yurīd).
* **`[11:108]`** "And as for those who are happy, they will be in Paradise, remaining therein for as long as the heavens and the earth endure, except as your Lord wills—a reward without end."
  * **Gloss:** The mirrored exception on Paradise — the twin brackets became the classical locus for the debate on the eternity of the two abodes (see §6).
* **`[11:109]`** "So be not in doubt regarding what these people worship. They worship only as their ancestors worshiped before. We will pay them their due in full, without any reduction."
  * **Gloss:** The exit address: doubt dismissed, continuity named (the ancestral mechanic of 11:62, 87), and the ledger settled without diminution — their worship's wage will be paid in this world's coin only.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`clear mandate`** (sulṭān): 11:96.
  * **Rendering:** "mandate" — the sole corpus occurrence of that noun (verified: `rg -i "mandate"` → 11:96 only).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[23:45]`, `[40:23]`, `[51:38]` — "a clear authority" for the identical phrase (verified) — one Arabic term, two Itani renderings; cf. `[4:153]` "a clear authority."
* **`grip`** (akhdh): 11:102.
  * **Rendering:** "Such is the grip of your Lord when He seizes the towns... His grip is most painful, most severe" (11:102).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[69:10]` "an overpowering grip"; `[39:67]` "The entire earth will be in His grip on the Day of Resurrection" (verified) — the noun is rare; the verb-cluster (seized suddenly: 7:4, 7:95–96, 6:44) is the pattern-of-towns engine — see [The pattern of the towns](../s007_al-a-raf/202610141432-p00712_the-pattern-of-towns.md).
* **`except as your Lord wills`**: the double bracket, 11:107–108.
  * **Rendering:** the identical bracket twice — 11:107 (the Fire) and 11:108 (Paradise).
  * **Elsewhere:** exact phrase occurs only twice in the corpus — both here (verified); the near-form at `[2:255]` "except as He wills" — *Source: the double exception is the classical crux (whether either or both abodes are bounded) — read in Tabari/Ibn Kathir on 11:107–108 — agent labeling; verify.*
* **`We relate/narrate to you`** (q-ss narration-verbs): the genre-signature.
  * **Rendering:** 11:100 "We relate them to you"; 11:120 "Everything We narrate to you"; while the first hinge (11:49) uses "that we reveal to you" — three verbs for one claim in one surah (observable).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:99]` "Thus We narrate to you reports of times gone by"; `[28:3]` "We narrate to you from the history of Moses and Pharaoh—in truth"; `[12:3]` "We narrate to you the most accurate history" (all verified) — the q-ss cluster is the Quran's own label for unseen-narration; cf. `[27:76]`, `[26:69]`, `[5:27]`.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Doctrinal note:** the "except as your Lord wills" brackets have generated two classical positions (exception of mercy/entry-time; or a real boundedness of one or both abodes — the hadith of the Final Judgment's post-Fire exit is brought by some here) — *Source: classical debate registered in the tafsīr literature on 11:107–108 (Ibn Kathir/Tabari) — agent labeling, verify; the corpus line itself states only the bracket.*
* **Revelation context:** Meccan; the Pharaoh-unit stands where Yunus (10:75–90) had given the extended version — Hud's compression is homiletic, not narrative — *Source: agent structural observation — verify.*
