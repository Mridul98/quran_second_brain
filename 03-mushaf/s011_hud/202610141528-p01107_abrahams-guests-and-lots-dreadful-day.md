# Abraham's guests and Lot's dreadful day: the fatted calf, the laughter, and the marked stones (11:69–83)
**UID:** 202610141528
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 11:69–83 (Ruku 7 of 10 in Hud)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[11:69]` – `[11:83]` (15 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The cycle's middle interlude: the same messengers run two errands in one scene — to Abraham, hospitality answered by withheld hands, suspicion, then the laughter of the old woman and the double announcement (Isaac, then Jacob), the blessing on "the people of the house," and Abraham's pleading for Lot's town halted by "The command of your Lord has come"; then to Lot — the dreadful day, the guests-shielded crowd, "my daughters are purer," the angels' assurance, the night-exit with no looking back, and the city turned upside down under stones of baked clay "Marked from your Lord, and never far from the wrongdoers."
* **Placement:** Ruku 7, the fifth and sixth missions (Abraham's guests / Lot) delivered as one narrative block — mercy announced to one household, destruction executed on another, by the same envoys, in fourteen verses.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[11:69]`** "Our messengers came to Abraham with good news. They said, “Peace.” He said, “Peace.” Soon after, he came with a roasted calf."
  * **Gloss:** The mission opens as etiquette: the return of salam, the roasted calf brought "quickly" — Abrahamic hospitality in two verbs.
* **`[11:70]`** "But when he saw their hands not reaching towards it, he became suspicious of them, and conceived a fear of them. They said, “Do not fear, we were sent to the people of Lot.”"
  * **Gloss:** The etiquette of uneaten food (in that culture, a refusal of protection); the fear disclosed and answered with the second errand — one announcement with two destinations.
* **`[11:71]`** "His wife was standing by, so she laughed. And We gave her good news of Isaac; and after Isaac, Jacob."
  * **Gloss:** The woman at the scene laughs — and is given the two-stage news: a son, then a grandson after him (the laughter's meaning — wonder, joy, or grief — is exegetical; see §6).
* **`[11:72]`** "She said, “Alas for me. Shall I give birth, when I am an old woman, and this, my husband, is an old man? This is truly a strange thing.”"
  * **Gloss:** The impossibility arithmetic of two old persons — the strangeness she names is exactly the point of the announcement.
* **`[11:73]`** "They said, “Do you marvel at the decree of Allah? The mercy and blessings of Allah are upon you, O people of the house. He is Praiseworthy and Glorious.”"
  * **Gloss:** The rebuke-and-blessing: marvel turned back on the marveller; the ahl al-bayt benediction — and the only "Praiseworthy and Glorious" couplet in the corpus (verified).
* **`[11:74]`** "When Abraham's fear subsided, and the good news had reached him, he started pleading with Us concerning the people of Lot."
  * **Gloss:** The switch: relief flows into intercession — Abraham argues for a doomed town; the envoys' own question in the twin scene, "What is your business, O envoys?" `[51:31]` (verified), names the pleading's subject.
* **`[11:75]`** "Abraham was gentle, kind, penitent."
  * **Gloss:** A three-noun character certificate (aḥlām, awwāh, munīb) — inserted precisely here: gentleness and relenting are his nature, so the pleading is not obstinacy.
* **`[11:76]`** "“O Abraham, refrain from this. The command of your Lord has come; they have incurred an irreversible punishment.”"
  * **Gloss:** The halt: the amr-formula announced ahead of the execution; "irreversible" (māddī — passing-inevitable) forecloses pleading.
* **`[11:77]`** "And when Our envoys came to Lot, he was anxious for them, and concerned for them. He said, “This is a dreadful day.”"
  * **Gloss:** The same envoys under other eyes: distress double-tilted (tightened and grieved), the day named dread (ʿaṣīb).
* **`[11:78]`** "And his people came rushing towards him—they were in the habit of committing sins. He said, “O my people, these are my daughters; they are purer for you. So fear Allah, and do not embarrass me before my guests. Is there not one reasonable man among you?”"
  * **Gloss:** The rushing habit-soaked crowd; the offered daughters (the town's women under his prophetic guardianship, per classical reading), purity argued, guest-honor pleaded — and the corpus-unique appeal: "one reasonable man" (phrase occurs only here in the corpus, verified).
* **`[11:79]`** "They said, “You know well that we have no right to your daughters, and you know well what we want.”"
  * **Gloss:** The blunt admission: no claim on the women; the known want named as known — the sin's logic confessed in the refusal.
* **`[11:80]`** "He said, “If only I had the strength to stop you, or could rely on some strong support.”"
  * **Gloss:** The lonely-prophet cry: raw power or a strong branch (rukn) — weakness lamented, the 11:27 social-trap in reverse (there the weak followed; here the protector lacks the weak).
* **`[11:81]`** "They said, “O Lot, we are the envoys of your Lord; they will not reach you. So set out with your family during the cover of the night, and let none of you look back, except for your wife. She will be struck by what will strike them. Their appointed time is the morning. Is not the morning near?”"
  * **Gloss:** Identity revealed, safety guaranteed, exit liturgy issued: night-cover, no looking back, one named exception; the deadline at dawn — and the verse's own comment "Is not the morning near?"
* **`[11:82]`** "And when Our command came about, We turned it upside down, and We rained down on it stones of baked clay."
  * **Gloss:** The execution-formula; the overturn (kāsif) and the clay-stones rain.
* **`[11:83]`** "Marked from your Lord, and never far from the wrongdoers."
  * **Gloss:** The stones are registered/tagged — and the sentence universalized: the stones are "not far" from the wrongdoers, any town's, any day.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`roasted calf`**: the hospitality-test, 11:69.
  * **Rendering:** "he came with a roasted calf" (11:69).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[51:26]` — "a fatted calf" (verified; the parallel Dhariyat scene) — and the idol-calf of Israel uses the same animal-noun at `[7:148]`, `[20:88]` (verified): a roasted calf of discernment versus a fashioned calf of worship — *Source: agent observation on Itani's shared word — note as lexical coincidence, not typology, until checked.*
* **`people of the house`** (ahl al-bayt): 11:73's benediction-address.
  * **Rendering:** "The mercy and blessings of Allah are upon you, O people of the house" (11:73).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[51:36]` — Lot's town held "only one household of Muslims" (verified); `[12:26]` "a witness from her household"; `[28:8]` "Pharaoh's household" (verified) — in this edition the exact address-phrase "people of the house" occurs only at 11:73.
* **`stones of baked clay`**: 11:82's ordnance.
  * **Rendering:** "We rained down on it stones of baked clay... Marked from your Lord" (11:82–83).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[15:74]` — verbatim twin: "We turned it upside down, and rained down upon them stones of baked clay" (verified); `[51:33]` — "rocks of clay" (verified); 9:70 names the deed's class — "the Overturned Cities" (verified).
* **`the command has come`** (amr): 11:76 and 11:82, the dual-stroke engine.
  * **Rendering:** "The command of your Lord has come" (11:76); "when Our command came about" (11:82).
  * **Elsewhere:** 11:40, 66, 94 (all Hud — verified: the formula cluster belongs to this surah).
