# Nearer than the jugular: the recorder, the daze of death, the driver and the witness (50:16–29)
**UID:** 202610142116
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 50:16–29 (Ruku 2 of 3 in Qaf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[50:16]` – `[50:29]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The evidence turns inward: the same God who made the human knows the soul's whispers and is nearer than the jugular vein — with two seated receivers filing every word (16–18); so the daze of death that "you tried to evade" arrives in truth, the Trumpet sounds, and each soul shows up escorted by a driver and a witness whose testimony the companion will not own (19–29).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 is the surah's inner-court: ruku 1 proved capability from sky, earth and precedent; this ruku proves accountability from within the skin, then stages the arrival — screen lifted, vision keen, dossier presented.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[50:16]`** "We created the human being, and We know what his soul whispers to him. We are nearer to him than his jugular vein."
  * **Gloss:** Three claims stacked: creation, inner-knowledge (the soul's self-talk — classical idiom waswasa, *Source: classical lexicography — verify*), proximity past anatomy. "Jugular vein" is corpus-unique (rg). The line answers 50:4's earth-consumption from the other side: what the earth eats, We keep; what the heart hides, We know.
* **`[50:17]`** "As the two receivers receive, seated to the right and to the left."
  * **Gloss:** The recorder-duo, throned left and right of every person (the classical identification as the kiraman katibin is gloss, not text — *Source: classical tafsir on 50:17–18 — verify*); the corpus twin is `[82:10–12]` "Though over you are watchers. Honest recorders. They know everything you do" (rg-verified).
* **`[50:18]`** "Not a word does he utter, but there is a watcher by him, ready."
  * **Gloss:** The per-utterance clause: qawlin — every word — with a raqib "ready" ('atid); task's "the companion records what he does" is this verse in another edition's clothing (Itani files it under "word", not "deed" — observed drift).
* **`[50:19]`** "The daze of death has come in truth: “This is what you tried to evade.”"
  * **Gloss:** Death's intoxicating stupor ("the daze of death" — classical sakrat al-mawr — *Source: classical lexicography — verify*) arriving "in truth": the truth 50:5 denied has now shown its face; the second-person slip pulls the audience into the scene mid-narration.
* **`[50:20]`** "And the Trumpet is blown: "This is the Promised Day.""
  * **Gloss:** The Trumpet sounds to announce the Promised Day — Itani reads "And the Trumpet is blown: 'This is the Promised Day'"; the Trumpet sounds eight other times in the corpus (18:99, 20:102, 27:87, 36:51, 39:68, 69:13, 74:8, 78:18 — rg); the fullest twin is `[39:68]` — sounded, all stunned, then sounded again and they rise up looking on.
* **`[50:21]`** "And every soul will come forward, accompanied by a driver and a witness."
  * **Gloss:** Every soul escorted: "driver and a witness" ✓ verbatim — one escorting, one testifying, per soul; the dual-escort establishes the courtroom procedure for the reckoning that follows.
* **`[50:22]`** "“You were in neglect of this, so We lifted your screen from you, and your vision today is keen.”"
  * **Gloss:** Task's cover/sharp-sight line ✓ ("We lifted your screen... your vision today is keen"); the screen (sitr) belongs to a motif-family: hearts screened `[41:5]`, eyes screened from "My message" `[18:101]`, and the Day when they "will be screened from their Lord" `[83:15]` (all rg-verified) — here the screen is cut, and the cost of the cut is that everything recorded is now seen.
* **`[50:23]`** "And His escort will say, “This is what I have ready with me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The qarīn ("escort" — the same word as 50:27's "His escort"; corpus-noun-uses complete at these two + the verb at `[9:6]` "escort him to his place of safety", rg) presents the file: "what I have ready" — the 'atid-work of 50:18 finally delivered.
* **`[50:24]`** ""Throw into Hell every stubborn disbeliever."
  * **Gloss:** The dump-command opens: stubbornness (kaffar — classical lexicography, verify) as the first trait; the imperative "Throw" addresses the escorts/angels of execution.
* **`[50:25]`** "Preventer of good, aggressor, doubter.""
  * **Gloss:** Three more traits stacked: obstruction of good (mu'tadin), aggression, doubt (murtab) — the 50:5 "confused state" and 50:15 "doubt of a new creation" finally labeled; the roster style matches the surah's own precedent-register (12–14): the type recurs.
* **`[50:26]`** "“Who fabricated another god with Allah; toss him into the intense agony.”"
  * **Gloss:** The fifth and summit trait: setting up another god alongside Allah — the shirk that all the k-tawhid verses police; the verb ("fabricated") puts partnership as a made thing, not a found one; "intense agony" names the Fire's burn-level (sa'ir — classical lexicography, verify).
* **`[50:27]`** "His escort will say, “Our Lord, I did not make him rebel, but he was far astray.”"
  * **Gloss:** The companion's disclaimer — the second qarīn (50:23's is 27's; the pair brackets the dump-list): the inner accomplice pleads non-causation: "he was far astray" (ba'id — classical lexicography, verify) — influence without authorship, the surah's answer to blame-shifting.
* **`[50:28]`** "He will say, “Do not feud in My presence— I had warned you in advance."
  * **Gloss:** The Judge silences the courtroom quarrel (feuding — litigation-words, the classical scene of blame-shifting): evidence already served, "I had warned you in advance" — the warner-office of 50:2 is now the receipt on file.
* **`[50:29]`** "The decree from Me will not be changed, and I am not unjust to the servants.”"
  * **Gloss:** Two closing locks: decree-immobility and justice-guarantee; the formula "I am not unjust to the servants" is corpus-verified verbatim-family at `[8:51]`, `[22:10]`, `[41:46]` and near at `[3:182]` — the surah's verdict lands on a standing divine self-description, not a new argument.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **wariid (jugular vein):** 50:16 — corpus-unique (rg); proximity-in-anatomy as the witness stand.
  * **Elsewhere:** none; sharpened against 50:4 (earth-consumption known) and 50:18 (word-by-word filing): inside, outside and past the jugular, the person is fully documented.
* **raqib/'atid + recorders:** 50:17–18 "two receivers... a watcher by him, ready".
  * **Rendering:** three recorder-images in three verses: receivers (hafiz), watcher (raqib), ready ('atid); then the qarīn "escort" files it (23).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[82:10–12]` "watchers... Honest recorders... They know everything you do" (rg) — the surah-cluster to read together with 50:17–18; k-kalam's book-grammar again.
* **qarīn (escort/companion):** 50:23, 50:27 — the same figure testifies twice and disclaims once.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:6]` verb "escort him to his place of safety" (rg) — the root's only other corpus use in this form; the qarīn's dual role (filer 23 / disclaimer 27) is the surah's answer to the companion-logic that runs through the Meccan rosters (cf. the "confederates" assembly at `[38:13]`, rg — same dramatis personae, not same word).
* **sitr (screen):** 50:22 "We lifted your screen from you."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[41:5]` "Our hearts are screened from what you call us to"; `[83:15]` "they will be screened from their Lord"; `[18:101]` "whose eyes were screened to My message" (all rg) — the screen is self-imposed this side of death (41:5), court-imposed on the Day (83:15), and here, for one terrible moment, cut away.
