# Hell asked "Are there any more?": six days without fatigue, and the praise-hours (50:30–45)
**UID:** 202610142117
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 50:30–45 (Ruku 3 of 3 in Qaf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[50:30]` – `[50:45]` (16 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The scene cuts to the Fire, interviewed about its appetite ("Are you full? / Are there any more?"), while Paradise is drawn near for those who feared the All-Merciful unseen — then the surah re-opens its central exhibit one last time (the destroyed strong, the heart that can listen, six days with no fatigue) and ends on the Prophet's consolation-package: endure, praise at the praise-hours, and remember that you hold no dictatorship over anyone — only a Quran that reminds those already capable of fear.
* **Placement:** The resolution ruku: 50:38 answers the 50:15 bracket-question; 50:39–40 quote the surah's own sabr-formula (verbatim family with `[20:130]`, `[38:17]`); 50:45 closes the warner-frame opened at 50:2.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[50:30]`** "On the Day when We will say to Hell, “Are you full?” And it will say, “Are there any more?”"
  * **Gloss:** The Fire asked if it is sated — and answers by asking for more; the scene completes the "fill Hell" decrees of `[11:119]`, `[32:13]`, `[38:85]` ("I will fill Hell with jinn and humans, altogether" — rg-verified family): appetite and fulfillment are both on the divine side.
* **`[50:31]`** "And Paradise will be brought closer to the pious, not far away."
  * **Gloss:** Distance inverted: the Fire was 30's distance; Paradise is "brought closer... not far away" — proximity as reward-grammar for the pious.
* **`[50:32]`** ""This is what you were promised—for every careful penitent."
  * **Gloss:** The promise-motif of `[51:5]`/`[51:22]` ("What you are promised is true... in the heaven is your livelihood, and what you are promised") in Meccan rhyme; "careful penitent" resumes the awwab-line from 50:8 (50:8, 32, 33 — three receiver-terms in one surah, rg-internal).
* **`[50:33]`** "Who inwardly feared the Most Gracious, and came with a repentant heart."
  * **Gloss:** The receiver defined: fear of the Rahman when no one is watching (khushur — *Source: classical lexicography — verify*) — taqwa's interior pole (see k-sabr_and_taqwa's "watchfulness"); "a repentant heart" turns the awwab inward: the third penitence-word in three verses.
* **`[50:34]`** "Enter it in peace. This is the Day of Eternity.”"
  * **Gloss:** The entry-greeting (salam into the Garden) and the Day's third name in this ruku: "the Day of Eternity" (corpus-unique, rg) — beside "the Promised Day" (50:20) and "the Day of Emergence" (50:42): the surah names the Day three times, each naming a different property — promise-kept, emergence-from-graves, never-ending.
* **`[50:35]`** "Therein they will have whatever they desire—and We have even more."
  * **Gloss:** Desire-fulfillment plus a surplus-clause — the promise is not a ceiling; "and We have even more" (ziyada) — classically cited in the vision-of-God reports; the corpus line itself claims only surplus (*Source: agent inference — verify before reading the beatific vision into it*).
* **`[50:36]`** "How many generations before them, who were more powerful than they, did We destroy? They explored the lands—was there any escape?"
  * **Gloss:** The precedent-register (50:12–14) re-summoned with an archaeology twist: the strong searched the map for an exit and never found one — "explored" is corpus-unique here (rg).
* **`[50:37]`** "In that is a reminder for whoever possesses a heart, or cares to listen and witness."
  * **Gloss:** The tadhkira-receiver again (cf. 50:8): a heart (qalb) that owns anything, and a listening posture "and witness" — the two conditions for the evidence to land; "possesses a heart" is corpus-unique (rg).
* **`[50:38]`** "We created the heavens and the earth and what is between them in six days, and no fatigue touched Us."
  * **Gloss:** THE keystone (task claim, corrected): six days, not three ("three days" gets 0 corpus hits; the six-days set is rg-complete: `[7:54]`, `[10:3]`, `[11:7]`, `[25:59]`, `[32:4]`, `[50:38]`, `[57:4]`); "and no fatigue touched Us" answers 50:15 ("Were We fatigued by the first creation?") and rhymes verbatim-motif with `[46:33]` ("was never tired by creating them... Able to revive the dead?" — rg). The theological gloss — that this clause negates any divine "rest" (as in the scriptural-ancestor tradition) — is doctrinal: *Source: agent inference/classical comparative reading — verify in print.* The text's own use is capability-for-resurrection.
* **`[50:39]`** "So endure what they say, and proclaim the praises of your Lord before the rising of the sun, and before sunset."
  * **Gloss:** The Prophet's release-clause opens: sabr against words (verbatim sibling `[20:130]` "So bear patiently what they say, and celebrate the praises of your Lord before the rising of the sun, and before its setting"; and `[38:17]` "Be patient in the face of what they say" — rg); the praise-hours (tasbih) begin with dawn and sunset.
* **`[50:40]`** "And glorify Him during the night, and at the end of devotions."
  * **Gloss:** The praise-hours continue: night-glorification plus "at the end of devotions" (Itani) vs the twin surah's "at the receding of the stars" (`[52:49]`) — two renderings of the night's final hours (observable drift); classically read as the daily prayers' appointments (*Source: classical tafsir on 50:39–40 — verify*).
* **`[50:41]`** "And listen for the Day when the caller calls from a nearby place."
  * **Gloss:** The ear turns to the Day: a caller, proximate ("from a nearby place" — the voice that 50:16's inner-knowledge promised would be audible); the imperative "listen" shifts the Prophet's audience from words-of-denial to the coming Shout.
* **`[50:42]`** "The Day when they will hear the Shout in all truth. That is the Day of Emergence."
  * **Gloss:** The single Shout heard "in all truth" — the truth denied at 50:5; "the Day of Emergence" (classically yawm al-nushur — *Source: classical lexicography — verify* — the same term behind 50:11's "the resurrection") names the exodus from graves.
* **`[50:43]`** "It is We who control life and death, and to Us is the destination."
  * **Gloss:** Task's "give life and death" ✓ in substance: Itani "control life and death" — sovereignty over both transitions, and the destination single: to Us; the royal plural of 50:1 now functions as verdict.
* **`[50:44]`** "The Day when the earth will crack for them at once. That is an easy gathering for Us."
  * **Gloss:** The earth's crack answers the sky's cracklessness (50:6 "it has no cracks" — internal rhyme, rg on "crack"): what has no seams above will split instantly below; "easy gathering" closes 50:11's "Likewise is the resurrection" — the analogy is now the event.
* **`[50:45]`** "We are fully aware of what they say, and you are not a dictator over them. So remind by the Quran whoever fears My warning."
  * **Gloss:** Final commission (task claim ✓ verbatim): divine awareness (echo of 50:16) + the Prophet's disarmament ("not a dictator" — corpus-unique rendering, rg) + targeting by the Book, not the sword: the Quran itself selects its audience ("whoever fears My warning") — the surah that swore by the Glorious Quran (50:1) ends with it doing the reminding; k-kalam's channel-claim, structurally framed.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **six days (creation span):** 50:38 — "We created the heavens and the earth and what is between them in six days, and no fatigue touched Us."
  * **Rendering:** Itani keeps "six days" flat, no gloss; the Throne-clause that usually accompanies it (7:54, 11:7, 25:59, 32:4, 57:4) is *absent* here — 50:38 pairs the span instead with anti-fatigue (observable structural choice).
  * **Elsewhere:** the seven-verse set (7:54, 10:3, 11:7, 25:59, 32:4, 50:38, 57:4 — rg-complete); the no-fatigue pair: 50:15 and `[46:33]`.
* **the Day's three names:** "the Promised Day" (50:20), "the Day of Eternity" (50:34), "the Day of Emergence" (50:42) — each corpus-unique in its exact form (rg); the surah's eschatology is a naming-system: promise → permanence → emergence.
  * **Elsewhere:** the promise-noun recurs in `[51:5]`/`[51:22]` and `[50:32]` ("what you were promised"); "the Day of Judgment" elsewhere in 51:12/69 etc. (see the s051 notes).
* **tadhkira (reminder):** 50:37 "a reminder"; the thread 50:8 → 50:37 → 50:45.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[51:55]` "And remind, for the reminder benefits the believers" — near-verbatim commission-twin of 50:45 (both surahs Meccan neighbors); see [k-guidance](../../02-knowledge/202610132000-k-guidance_hudan_lil_muttaqin.md).
* **sabr under words:** 50:39 "So endure what they say."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:130]` "bear patiently what they say" + praise-hours; `[38:17]` "Be patient in the face of what they say"; `[16:127]` "So be patient. Your patience is solely from Allah" (all rg) — the Prophet-consolation formula par excellence: endurance paired with liturgy, never with retaliation.
