# Adam's two sons: the first murder and the law of the one life (5:27–34)
**UID:** 202610141206
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:27–34 (Ruku 5 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:27]` – `[5:34]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The "true story of Adam's two sons" — an offering accepted from the righteous, murder consented to by a prompted soul, and a raven's lesson in remorse — becomes the causal preamble ("Because of that") to the one-life law: whoever kills one is as if he killed all mankind, and whoever saves one, as if he saved all; the unit then legislates the terror-crime (hirabah) with its mercy-valve of prior repentance.
* **Placement:** Second scene of the refusals cycle: ruku 4 refused the land; this ruku murders the brother. The killing of the innocent brother rhymes with the killed prophets of `[5:70]` and grounds the surah's penal architecture (5:33–34, then 5:38–40).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:27]`** — "And relate to them the true story of Adam's two sons: when they offered an offering, and it was accepted from one of them, but it was not accepted from the other. He Said, “I will kill you.” He Said, “Allah accepts only from the righteous.”"
  * **Gloss:** Sacrifice sorted by righteousness, not blood; the accepted one's quiet "Allah accepts only from the righteous" provokes the murder vow. (Corpus capitalizes "He Said" mid-line; quoted verbatim.)
* **`[5:28]`** — "“If you extend your hand to kill me, I will not extend my hand to kill you; for I fear Allah, Lord of the Worlds.”"
  * **Gloss:** The victim's refusal-to-retaliate: fear of Allah defines his non-aggression.
* **`[5:29]`** — "“I would rather you bear my sin and your sin, and you become among the inmates of the Fire. Such is the reward for the evildoers.”"
  * **Gloss:** He hands the reckoning to Allah: the murderer will carry two sins — the corpus's stark twin of "the one life" logic to come.
* **`[5:30]`** — "Then His soul prompted him to kill his brother, so he killed him, and became one of the losers."
  * **Gloss:** The psychology of the crime compressed: prompt → act → loss.
* **`[5:31]`** — "Then Allah sent a raven digging the ground, to show him how to cover his brother's corpse. He said, “Woe to me! I was unable to be like this raven, and bury my brother's corpse.” So he became full of regrets."
  * **Gloss:** A bird teaches burial; the murderer becomes the first mourner — remorse arrives too late, and precisely on time for the law.
* **`[5:32]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "Because of that We ordained for the Children of Israel: that whoever kills a person— unless it is for murder or corruption on earth—it is as if he killed the whole of mankind; and whoever saves it, it is as if he saved the whole of mankind."
  * "Our messengers came to them with clarifications, but even after that, many of them continue to commit excesses in the land."
  * **Gloss:** The one-life law: each human life carries the weight of all humanity; the clause grounds it in the fratricide ("Because of that") and notes the excesses that continued despite clarification.
* **`[5:33]`** — "The punishment for those who fight Allah and His Messenger, and strive to spread corruption on earth, is that they be killed, or crucified, or have their hands and feet cut off on opposite sides, or be banished from the land. That is to disgrace them in this life; and in the Hereafter they will have a terrible punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The hirabah (warring-against-God-and-corruption) penalty ladder, stated as text: four graduated options and a two-world sanction; the verse's own stated purpose is disgrace/deterrence.
* **`[5:34]`** — "Except for those who repent before you apprehend them. So know that Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The exception clause carved into the penal text itself — repentance before capture removes the penalty.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"corruption on earth" (fasād):**
  * **Rendering:** "corruption on earth" in `[5:32]`, `[5:33]`, and (within surah) `[5:64]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:205]` "he strives to spread corruption on earth, destroying properties and lives"; `[13:25]` "those who violate the promise to Allah, after pledging to keep it... and spread corruption on earth" — covenant-breaking *is* corruption; `[11:116]`.
* **"crucified" (ṣalb):**
  * **Rendering:** as a penalty option in `[5:33]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:124]` and `[20:71]`/`[26:49]` — Pharaoh's threat to the magicians ("then I will crucify you all"); `[12:41]` — Joseph's dream interpretation: "the other will be crucified"; `[4:157]` — the claimed crucifixion of the Messiah. The corpus's crucifixion allusion is thus royal tyranny + a false report — note the resonance when the surah later denies the killing/crucifixion claim (observable corpus fact).
* **"inmates of the Fire" (aṣḥāb al-nār):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:29]`, `[5:86]`; "inmates of Hell" at `[5:10]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:39]`, `[2:81]`, `[64:10]`, `[7:36]`, `[7:44]` — the settled eschatological class label.
* **"soul prompted" (nafs ṭammat / sawwala):**
  * **Rendering:** "Then His soul prompted him" `[5:30]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:53]` "The soul commands evil, except those on whom my Lord has mercy" — the psychology-of-crime twin verse; cf. within-surah `[5:80]` "what their souls prompts them to do."

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Life-for-all-life:** `[6:151]`, `[17:33]`, `[25:68]` — "do not kill the soul which Allah has made sacred / forbidden, except in the course of justice": the killing-prohibition clause echoed across the corpus (verified via `rg`); 5:32 universalizes the same prohibition into arithmetic.
* **Within surah causal chain:** `[5:33]` ↔ `[5:32]` — "corruption on earth" is the hinge word linking the one-life law to the hirabah penalty.
* **Repentance parallel:** `[5:34]` ↔ `[5:39]` (next ruku: "whoever repents after his crime, and reforms, Allah will accept his repentance") — the penal block's twin mercy clauses (observable structure).
* **Cain/Abel transmitted frame:** the brothers are unnamed in the corpus text; classical reports name them Qabil and Habil, with the sparrow/raven detail and the murder method elaborated (*Source: classical transmitted reports on 5:27–31 — verify*).
* **Fiqh note (neutral):** the four hirabah options and the "before you apprehend them" exception are classically sorted by scenario (stage of the crime, place, timing) in the fiqh literature; none of that sorting is in the text — *Source: classical — verify.* The text itself presents the ladder and the exception plainly (observable).
