# Do not forbid the good; the unintended oath and its atonement (5:87–93)
**UID:** 202610141213
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:87–93 (Ruku 12 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:87]` – `[5:93]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The covenant's daily-speech and daily-appetite law: do not forbid what Allah permitted (even asceticism can breach the commitment of 5:1), oaths are graded by intention — the unintended unpunished, the binding atoned in a feed-ten-or-clothe-or-free-or-fast ladder — and intoxicants, gambling, idolatry, and divination are cut out as Satan's handiwork whose true damage is social: strife, hatred, and the missed prayer.
* **Placement:** The middle of the surah's law triptych (12–14: appetite/oath, hunt/sanctuary, question/testimony). It closes the dietary argument begun at 5:3–5 and supplies the personal-contract counterpart of the communal "fulfill your commitments."

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:87]`** — "O you who believe! Do not prohibit the good things Allah has permitted for you, and do not commit aggression. Allah does not love the aggressors."
  * **Gloss:** The double limit: self-denial may not legislate over permission, and any prohibition beyond Allah's is itself aggression.
* **`[5:88]`** — "And eat of the lawful and good things Allah has provided for you; and be conscious of Allah, in whom you are believers."
  * **Gloss:** The positive rule — the 2:172/16:114 formula — with taqwa attached to the table.
* **`[5:89]`** — four clause-chunks:
  * "Allah does not hold you accountable for your unintended oaths, but He holds you accountable for your binding oaths."
  * "The atonement for it is by feeding ten needy people from the average of what you feed your families, or by clothing them, or by freeing a slave."
  * "Anyone who lacks the means shall fast for three days. That is the atonement for breaking your oaths when you have sworn them."
  * "So keep your oaths. Thus Allah makes clear His Revelations to you, that you may be grateful."
  * **Gloss:** The oath-law in full: intention grades accountability; expiation is a graded ladder ending in three days' fast for the unable; the whole legislation aims at gratitude, and its practical command is "keep your oaths" (the private 5:1).
* **`[5:90]`** — "O you who believe! Intoxicants, gambling, idolatry, and divination are abominations of Satan’s doing. Avoid them, so that you may prosper."
  * **Gloss:** The four-name prohibition as *rijs* (abomination) attributed to Satan's work; avoidance framed as the condition of prospering.
* **`[5:91]`** — "Satan wants to provoke strife and hatred among you through intoxicants and gambling, and to prevent you from the remembrance of Allah, and from prayer. Will you not desist?"
  * **Gloss:** The damage analysis: social (strife/hatred) and cultic (dhikr/prayer) — the rationale given inside the ruling.
* **`[5:92]`** — "Obey Allah and obey the Messenger, and be cautious. If you turn away—know that the duty of Our Messenger is clear communication."
  * **Gloss:** Obedience bracketed by caution; the delivery-doctrine (5:67, 5:99) returns as the limit of the Messenger's liability.
* **`[5:93]`** — "Those who believe and do righteous deeds will not be blamed for what they may have eaten, provided they obey, and believe, and do good deeds, then maintain piety and faith, then remain righteous and charitable. Allah loves the charitable."
  * **Gloss:** The exoneration for the past: a six-step ladder (obedience, faith, good work, piety, faith renewed, righteousness + charity) converting retro-guilt into no blame — and the surah's love-formula closes on the charitable (muḥsinīn).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"oaths" (aymān) — "unintended / binding":**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:89]` "unintended oaths" / "binding oaths".
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:225]` "Allah does not hold you responsible for your unintentional oaths, but He holds you responsible..." — the near-twin verse with spelling drift (unintentional/unintended — observable via `rg`); `[2:224]` "do not allow your oaths in Allah's name to hinder you from virtue"; `[66:2]` "Allah has decreed for you the dissolution of your oaths"; `[16:91]`, `[16:94]` — the oath-corpus; and within-surah `[5:53]` "strongest oaths" (the perjured) vs. `[5:89]` (the atoned).
* **"atonement" (kaffārah):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:89]` "the atonement for it... for breaking your oaths"; and `[5:45]` "whoever forgoes it in charity, it will serve as atonement" — the word occurs *only at these two places* in the corpus (verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** the ladder's kin: `[58:4]` "whoever cannot find the means must fast for two consecutive months... and if he is unable, then the feeding of sixty needy persons" (verified — same unable→fast/feeding grammar); `[2:196]` "three days" in the ḥajj-expiation (verified phrase); `[2:184]` feeding a needy person as redemption.
* **"intoxicants... gambling" (khamr, maysir):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:90]` "Intoxicants, gambling, idolatry, and divination."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:219]` "They ask you about intoxicants and gambling. Say, 'There is gross sin in them, and some benefits for people'" (verified) — the earlier stage of the same two-word pair; the classical reading arranges 2:219 → 4:43 (prayer Sobriety, verified line) → 5:90 as a gradual abrogation sequence (*Source: classical consensus on the khamr verses — verify*).
* **"abominations" (rijs):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:90]` "abominations of Satan's doing."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[74:5]` "abandon abominations"; `[6:121]` "for it is abomination" (unblessed meat) (verified) — the *rijs* family marks what Satan engineers, food and mind alike.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; 5:87 classically addressed to would-be ascetics who forbade themselves lawful things (a report names one Companion's vow of night-prayer, fasting, and celibacy) — *Source: classical occasion reports on 5:87 — verify.*
* **Fiqh honesty:** whether the three expiation options in 5:89 are alternatives or a graded order, and the exact status of the "unintended oath" (laghw), are classical disputes resolved outside the verse's own punctuation — *Source: classical fiqh on 5:89 — verify.* The text as quoted presents one ladder: feed/clothe/free → unable → fast three.
