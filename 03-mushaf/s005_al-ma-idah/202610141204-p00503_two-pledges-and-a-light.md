# Two pledges and a light: the broken mīthāq answered by a clarifying Book (5:12–19)
**UID:** 202610141204
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:12–19 (Ruku 3 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:12]` – `[5:19]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Allah's pledges to Israel and to the Christians are recited, breached, and cursed — and into the breach steps a Messenger who clarifies what was hidden, a light, and a clear Book, so that no one can say "no preacher has come"; the unit's first Christological correction (`[5:17]`) stakes the same claim the surah will interrogate at its end.
* **Placement:** The pledge theology of 5:7 turns outward to the older communities; the "breach → curse → pardon-command" pattern (`[5:13]`) prefaces the refusals narrative of ruku 4, and `[5:17]` opens the surah's Christological arc (with `[5:72]` as its twin).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:12]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "Allah received a pledge from the Children of Israel, and We raised among them twelve chiefs."
  * "Allah said, “I am with you; if you perform the prayer, and pay the alms, and believe in My messengers and support them, and lend Allah a loan of righteousness; I will remit your sins, and admit you into Gardens beneath which rivers flow."
  * "But whoever among you disbelieves afterwards has strayed from the right way.”"
  * **Gloss:** The pledge with its five conditions (prayer, alms, belief in and support of the messengers, the righteous *qard*) and its two outcomes; "twelve chiefs" echoes the twelve tribes of the wilderness.
* **`[5:13]`** — "Because of their breaking their pledge, We cursed them, and made their hearts hard. They twist the words out of their context, and they disregarded some of what they were reminded of. You will always witness deceit from them, except for a few of them. But pardon them, and overlook. Allah loves the doers of good."
  * **Gloss:** Breach → curse → hard heart → textual distortion: the four-stage diagnosis; then the surprising hinge: "pardon them, and overlook" — the curse clause is itself pardoned within the verse (observable structure).
* **`[5:14]`** — "And from those who say, “We are Christians,” We received their pledge, but they neglected some of what they were reminded of. So We provoked enmity and hatred among them until the Day of Resurrection; Allah will then inform them of what they used to craft."
  * **Gloss:** The second pledge, neglected; sectarian enmity inside Christianity is read as covenant consequence, deferred to the Day.
* **`[5:15]`** — "O People of the Book! Our Messenger has come to you, clarifying for you much of what you kept hidden of the Book, and overlooking much. A light from Allah has come to you, and a clear Book."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-move: clarification *and* overlooking — the Messenger's pedagogy mirrors the divine pardon of 5:13.
* **`[5:16]`** — "Allah guides with it whoever follows His approval to the ways of peace, and He brings them out of darkness into light, by His permission, and He guides them in a straight path."
  * **Gloss:** Guidance vocabulary: *subul al-salam*, darkness→light, the straight path — the Fatihah petition answered in the surah's own terms.
* **`[5:17]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "They disbelieve those who say, “Allah is the Christ, the son of Mary.” Say, “Who can prevent Allah, if He willed, from annihilating the Christ son of Mary, and his mother, and everyone on earth?”"
  * "To Allah belongs the sovereignty of the heavens and the earth and what is between them. He creates whatever He wills, and Allah has power over everything."
  * **Gloss:** The first of three Christological verdicts (17, 72, 116); the thought-experiment of total annihilation reduces the claim to creaturely contingency.
* **`[5:18]`** — "The Jews and the Christians say, “We are the children of Allah, and His beloved.” Say, “Why then does He punish you for your sins?” In fact, you are humans from among those He created. He forgives whom He wills, and He punishes whom He wills. To Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and what lies between them, and to Him is the return."
  * **Gloss:** Election-inlanguage ("children... beloved") is answered by the punishment test and by "you are humans" — the shared creaturely status grounding 5:19's address.
* **`[5:19]`** — "O People of the Book! Our Messenger has come to you, making things clear to you—after a cessation of messengers—so that you cannot say, “No preacher has come to us, and no warner.” In fact, a preacher has come to you, and a warner; and Allah is Capable of everything."
  * **Gloss:** The envoy sent after the interval (*fatrah*) closes the argument's courtroom: the excuse of no messenger is foreclosed.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`mīthāq` ("pledge"):** the ratifying bond, here twice received and twice broken.
  * **Rendering:** "received a pledge" `[5:12]`, "breaking their pledge" `[5:13]`, "their pledge" `[5:14]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:63]` (pledge + the Mount); `[3:81]` "Allah received the covenant of the prophets"; `[5:7]` within-surah (the believers' own covenant) — three covenants in three verses: believers, Israel, Christians (observable corpus fact).
* **"twist the words out of their context" (taḥrīf):**
  * **Rendering:** 5:13 "twist the words out of their context"; 5:41 "distort words from their places" (drift within the surah).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:46]` "Among the Jews are some who take words out of context, and say, 'We hear and we disobey'" — the verbal twin and the inverted covenant formula.
* **"loan of righteousness" (qard ḥasan):** lending to Allah.
  * **Rendering:** `[5:12]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[57:18]` "who have loaned Allah a loan of righteousness" — *exact* phrase kin; `[2:245]` "offer Allah a generous loan"; `[64:17]` "lend Allah a good loan" — the giving-economy of the Quran (ties directly to the infraq-vs-riba note).
* **"ways of peace" (subul al-salām):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:16]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:25]` "Allah invites to the Home of Peace, and guides whomever He wills to a straight path"; `[6:127]` "For them is the Home of Peace with their Lord" — the noun and verb family of *salām* as the destination of guidance.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the pledge-curse-pardon sequence presumes contact and conflict with organized Jewish and Christian communities in the surah's legal phase.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** "after a cessation of messengers" (`[5:19]`) is classically read as the *fatrah* between Jesus and Muhammad (*Source: classical consensus on 5:19 — verify*); the phrase "twelve chiefs" is classically read as the tribal leaders under Moses (*Source: classical — verify*).
