# Whom to ally with: the Party of Allah and the racing sick hearts (5:51–56)
**UID:** 202610141209
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:51–56 (Ruku 8 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:51]` – `[5:56]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Covenant loyalty gets its politics: the believers are forbidden the *wali*-alliance with Jews and Christians "who are allies of one another", the sick-hearted who race to them break their loudest oaths and lose, and whoever defects from the religion will find Allah replacing them with a people He loves — an alliance-triangle of Allah, His Messenger, and the praying, zakat-giving, bowing believers, whose party is the victorious one.
* **Placement:** The boundary ruku following the scripture argument: 5:48–50 judged *between* the communities; 5:51–56 now fences *within*. The apostasy replacement clause (5:54) answers the refusals cycle; the bowing-ally verse (5:55) closes with worship as the covenant's identifying mark.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:51]`** — "O you who believe! Do not take the Jews and the Christians as allies; some of them are allies of one another. Whoever of you allies himself with them is one of them. Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people."
  * **Gloss:** The *wilāyah* prohibition; its logic is reciprocal ("some of them are allies of one another") and its consequence identity ("is one of them"). Read against `[5:5]` and `[5:82]` it governs *allegiance*, not table or marriage (see cross-refs; interpretive framing labeled).
* **`[5:52]`** — "You will see those in whose hearts is sickness racing towards them. They say, “We fear the wheel of fate may turn against us.” But perhaps Allah will bring about victory, or some event of His making; thereupon they will regret what they concealed within themselves."
  * **Gloss:** The inner-community sprinters: their motive is fear of fortune's turn; their hiddenness will surface as regret.
* **`[5:53]`** — "Those who believe will say, “Are these the ones who swore by Allah with their strongest oaths that they are with you?” Their works have failed, so they became losers."
  * **Gloss:** Perjury exposed: oaths that pledged solidarity evaporate when the test comes — the surah's oath-lexicon appears here, at the edge of its expiation-law (5:89).
* **`[5:54]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "O you who believe! Whoever of you goes back on his religion—Allah will bring a people whom He loves and who love Him, kind towards the believers, stern with the disbelievers."
  * "They strive in the way of Allah, and do not fear the blame of the critic."
  * "That is the grace of Allah; He bestows it upon whomever He wills. Allah is Embracing and Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** The replacement clause: apostasy is answered not by coercion but by substitution — a people defined by mutual love with Allah, gentleness inside, firmness outside, and immunity to critic-blame; "Embracing" (wāsiʿ) names grace's width.
* **`[5:55]`** — "Your allies are Allah, and His Messenger, and those who believe—those who pray regularly, and give charity, and bow down."
  * **Gloss:** The positive triangle; the three marks (prayer, charity, bowing) make loyalty visible in worship.
* **`[5:56]`** — "Whoever allies himself with Allah, and His Messenger, and those who believe—surely the Party of Allah is the victorious."
  * **Gloss:** The party formula: alignment → hizb → victory; the surah's answer to "the wheel of fate" of 5:52.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`walī` / `awlīyāʾ` ("allies"):** protector, intimate patron, party-member.
  * **Rendering:** "allies" / "allies himself" / "befriend" (at `[5:57]`, next ruku) — the root's political register in this ruku.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:28]` "Believers are not to take disbelievers for friends instead of believers"; `[4:139]` "Those who ally themselves with the disbelievers instead of the believers"; `[4:144]` "Do not befriend disbelievers rather than believers" — the same prohibition-verb family; the counter-clause `[3:28]` itself carries "unless it is to protect your own selves" (verified text).
* **"those in whose hearts is sickness" (marad):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:52]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:10]` "In their hearts is sickness, and Allah has increased their sickness"; `[8:49]`; `[33:12]`, `[33:60]`; `[9:125]` — the settled hypocrite-diagnosis idiom (observable cluster).
* **"the Party of Allah" (ḥizb Allāh):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:56]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[58:19]` "These are the partisans of Satan. Indeed, it is Satan's partisans who are the losers" — the mirror image of the party formula; `[58:22]` completes the loyalty test ("even if they were their parents...").
* **"the blame of the critic":**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:54]` — phrase unique to this verse in the corpus (verified via `rg`).
  * **Elsewhere:** the idiom's kin are the fear-of-people formulas: `[5:44]` "do not fear people, but fear Me"; `[2:150]-family`; contrast `[5:52]`'s fear of the "wheel of fate" (in-surah opposition, observable).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within-surah gradient (tension worth a fleeting note):** `[5:51]` sits beside `[5:5]` (intermarriage and shared food) and `[5:82]` (the Christians "nearest in affection") and `[60:8]` ("Allah does not prohibit you from dealing with them kindly and equitably"): the corpus fences *alliance* while opening *commerce, marriage, and kindness* — the boundary is political-military, not social (agent reading of the corpus juxtaposition — verify; classical readings differ in scope, *Source: classical — verify*).
* **Character portrait:** `[5:54]` ↔ `[48:29]` "Those with him are stern against the disbelievers, yet compassionate amongst themselves" — the same doubled temperament at surah-end.
* **Oath web:** `[5:53]` "swore by Allah with their strongest oaths" ↔ `[16:38]` "they swear by Allah with their most solemn oaths" (verified cluster: 6:109, 16:38) and the surah's own oath-law at `[5:89]`.
* **Bow as identity:** `[5:55]` "bow down" ↔ `[2:34]`/`[15:98]` the Adamic command, `[3:43]` "bow down, and kneel with those who kneel" — the rukuʿ as the community's physical signature.
* **Replacement logic:** `[5:54]` answers the refusals of ruku 4: where Israel's cowardice sat down, a new people "whom He loves" stands up (agent inference from narrative placement — verify).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan alliance-law; classical occasion reports attach 5:51–56 to individuals who publicly renounced tribal/treaty protection (e.g., a chief's declaration shifting his *wilāyah*) — *Source: classical reports on 5:51–57 — verify*; the text itself is categorical address to the community.
* **Terminology caution:** "kind towards the believers, stern with the disbelievers" and "do not take the Jews and the Christians as allies" are rendered here exactly as in the corpus; any application (friendship, citizenship, war-time alliance) is extra-textual — *Source: classical and modern fiqh readings of wilāyah — verify.*
