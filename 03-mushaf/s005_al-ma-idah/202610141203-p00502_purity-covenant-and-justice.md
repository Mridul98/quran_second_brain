# Purification, the remembered covenant, and justice even toward the hated (5:6–11)
**UID:** 202610141203
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:6–11 (Ruku 2 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:6]` – `[5:11]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Body, oath, and court are purified together: the ablution law teaches that Allah "does not intend to burden you" but to purify; the covenant remembered ("We hear and we obey") then demands justice even against hatred — and Allah, who knows what hearts contain, rewards the upright and warns the rejectors.
* **Placement:** Directly after the covenant frame of 5:1: purity law (`[5:6]`) as covenant marker, the remembered pledge (`[5:7]`) as its memory, impartial justice (`[5:8]`) as its public face, and the reward/punishment pair (`[5:9–10]`) plus a historical mercy (`[5:11]`) as its sanctions.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:6]`** — four clause-chunks:
  * "O you who believe! When you rise to pray, wash your faces and your hands and arms to the elbows, and wipe your heads, and your feet to the ankles."
  * "If you had intercourse, then purify yourselves."
  * "If you are ill, or travelling, or one of you returns from the toilet, or you had contact with women, and could not find water, then use some clean sand and wipe your faces and hands with it."
  * "Allah does not intend to burden you, but He intends to purify you, and to complete His blessing upon you, that you may be thankful."
  * **Gloss:** The full wudu anatomy in one verse, then junūb purification, then the tayammum concession for illness, travel, relieving oneself, and sexual contact without water — sealed by the principle: the law's intent is purification, not burden.
* **`[5:7]`** — "And Remember Allah’s blessings upon you, and His covenant which He covenanted with you; when you said, “We hear and we obey.” And remain conscious of Allah, for Allah knows what the hearts contain."
  * **Gloss:** The believers' own founding sentence — "We hear and we obey" — is made the object of remembrance; covenant is memory before it is law. (Corpus capitalizes "And Remember"; quoted verbatim.)
* **`[5:8]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "O you who believe! Be upright to Allah, witnessing with justice; and let not the hatred of a certain people prevent you from acting justly."
  * "Adhere to justice, for that is nearer to piety; and fear Allah. Allah is informed of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's ethic in one imperative: witness for Allah with *qist*; hatred may not tilt judgment; justice is "nearer to piety" — the taqwa vocabulary of 5:2's "virtuous conduct and conscience" made juridical.
* **`[5:9]`** — "Allah has promised those who believe and work righteousness: they will have forgiveness and a great reward."
  * **Gloss:** The covenant's blessing clause, in miniature.
* **`[5:10]`** — "As for those who disbelieve and reject Our revelations—these are the inmates of Hell."
  * **Gloss:** The curse clause; the pair 5:9/5:10 restates the Fatihah-Baqarah sorting formula.
* **`[5:11]`** — "O you who believe! Remember Allah's blessings upon you; when certain people intended to extend their hands against you, and He restrained their hands from you. So reverence Allah, and in Allah let the believers put their trust."
  * **Gloss:** A remembered deliverance — hands raised against the community and then withheld — converts blessing into trust (tawakkul); the second "Remember Allah's blessings" refrain of the surah.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`mīthāq` ("covenant which He covenanted"):** the ratified pledge.
  * **Rendering:** "covenant... covenanted" in `[5:7]` (verb+noun cognate accented in English); "pledge" in `[5:12]`/`[5:13]` (drift observable in corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:63]` "And recall when We received a pledge from you, and raised the Mount above you: 'Take what We have given you earnestly'"; `[4:46]` the inverted formula "We hear and we disobey" — 5:7's "We hear and we obey" is its mirror image.
* **"clean sand" (ṣaʿīd, tayammum):** earth-as-water-substitute.
  * **Rendering:** "some clean sand" in `[5:6]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:43]` — the twin verse: drunkenness, travel, illness, "coming from the place of relieving," contact with women, no water → "seek clean [ground] and wipe your faces and hands" (quoted partially; the excuse-list is verbal kin, observable via `rg`).
* **`qisṭ` ("witnessing with justice"):** equity, even-handed testimony.
  * **Rendering:** "witnessing with justice" / "Adhere to justice" in `[5:8]`; "equitably" in `[5:42]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:135]` "Stand firmly for justice, as witnesses to Allah, even if against yourselves, or your parents, or your relatives" — the near-verbatim ethics twin; `[60:8]` "Allah loves the equitable."
* **"knows what the hearts contain" (ḍamīr):** interiority as evidence.
  * **Rendering:** `[5:7]`; cf. "Allah is informed of what you do" `[5:8]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:38]` "He knows what the hearts contain"; `[57:6]` — the interior-knowledge formula grounding the justice command.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within surah:** `[5:2]` — "let not the hatred of people who barred you... incite you to aggression" escalates here to a positive duty: act *justly* toward the hated (observable repetition of the same clause stem).
* **Legal twin:** `[2:39]` — "But as for those who disbelieve and reject Our signs—these are the inmates of the Fire" — 5:10 is the same formula (*Source: observable corpus parallel; note "signs"/"revelations" term drift*).
* **Historical echo:** `[48:24]` — "It is He who withheld their hands from you, and your hands from them, in the valley of Mecca" — the event class remembered in 5:11 (classical reading: Ḥudaybiyyah-era plots; *Source: classical — verify*).
* **Refrain web:** "Remember Allah's blessings upon you" recurs at `[5:7]`, `[5:11]`, `[5:20]`, `[5:110]` and in `[33:9]`, `[14:6]`, `[35:3]`, `[3:103]` — the Medinan memory-formula (observable corpus fact).
* **Ethics note tie:** 5:2's hunt/ihram limits and this verse's justice-for-the-hated together ground the covenant-marker reading of purity and fairness.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; 5:6 is the Quran's fullest single ablution verse and the scriptural base of the fiqh of wudu and tayammum (details — order, wiping vs. washing the feet, waterless intention — absent from the text; *Source: classical fiqh readings of 5:6 — verify*).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the "restrained hands" of 5:11 is classically linked to a specific assassination or betrayal plot against the Prophet (*Source: classical reports on 5:11 — verify*); the corpus text itself is unspecific (observable fact).
