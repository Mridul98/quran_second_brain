**UID:** 202610141201

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Medinan — classical consensus for the whole surah. Among the *very last* surahs to be revealed: revelation order 112 of 114 in the transmitted classical enumeration (several reports call it the final complete surah, the "last surah revealed"). *Source: transmitted classical ordering and surah-length classification (mi’un tier) — agent labeling per classical lists — verify.*
* **Size:** 120 ayahs, 16 ruku (Hanafi/Idad-e-Quran partition per `data/ruku_map.json`).
* **Central theme:** The surah's first words — "Fulfill your commitments" (`[5:1]`) — are its frame: it is the covenant-fulfillment surah. Israel's covenant history is retold as a *scandal of refusals* (the Holy Land `[5:20–26]`, the fratricide `[5:27]`, the broken pledge `[5:12–14]`); dietary and purity law (`[5:3–6]`, `[5:87–96]`) functions as covenant markers binding a community to its Lord; the Christological correction (`[5:72–78]`, `[5:116–118]`) closes the oldest breach by returning Jesus' own words to his worshippers; and even the private contract of an oath is legislated with mercy for the unintentional (`[5:89]`). The surah ends where a covenant ends: the Day the truthful profit from their truthfulness (`[5:119]`) and total sovereignty (`[5:120]`).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Covenant lawgiving and its frame (ruku 1–3):** "Fulfill your commitments" opens; food, sanctity, purity, and justice are stipulated; the pledges of Israel and the Christians are recited and their breach diagnosed (5:1–19).
* **The scandal of refusals (ruku 4–6):** the Holy Land refused (`5:20–26`), the brother murdered (`5:27–31`), and the resulting one-life law (`5:32–33`) — covenant history as anatomy of the hard heart; penalties with repentance exceptions follow (`5:34–43`).
* **The scripture argument (ruku 7–9):** Torah and Gospel judged on their own terms (`5:44–47`), the Quran sent confirming and superseding (`5:48`), then communal boundary-setting (`5:51–58`) ending in conditional blessing for those who *hold* the books (`5:65–66`).
* **The Messenger and the Messiah (ruku 10–11):** the command to deliver without fear (`5:67`) answers the mockery; the Christological correction runs through the surah (`5:72–77`) and finds its tenderest counter-image in the weeping Christians (`5:82–86`).
* **Covenant markers of daily life (ruku 12–14):** food again (`5:87–88`), oaths and atonement (`5:89–93`), hunt, sanctuary, and the Messenger's duty (`5:94–100`), then dangerous questions and the law of last-will testimony (`5:101–108`).
* **The heavenly courtroom (ruku 15–16):** the messengers are interrogated (`5:109`), Jesus' graces recounted ("by My leave" five times, `5:110`), the table is asked and sent (`5:112–115`), and the surah's own namesake becomes the final question to the deified pair (`5:116–120`) — closing on the ring verse of sovereignty (`[5:120]` ↔ `[5:17]`, `[5:1]`).
