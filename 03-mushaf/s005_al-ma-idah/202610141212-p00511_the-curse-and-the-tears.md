# Cursed by the tongues of David and Jesus; the weeping Christians (5:78–86)
**UID:** 202610141212
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:78–86 (Ruku 11 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:78]` – `[5:86]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The covenant's inside track: Israel's rebels were cursed "by the tongue of David and Jesus son of Mary" because they neither restrained each other's wrong nor feared Allah's wrath befriending the rejectors — and then the ruku turns to the counter-community: those who say "We are Christians," nearest in affection, weeping at the revelation and asking to be counted among the witnesses — the surah's warmest verdict issued inside its sharpest correction.
* **Placement:** Ruku 10 corrected Christian creed; ruku 11 grades Christian *response* — and the pairing (5:82–85 within a hostile-boundary block) is the surah's most quoted interfaith gradient, directly relevant to the alliance ruku's 5:51.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:78]`** — "Cursed were those who disbelieved from among the Children of Israel by the tongue of David and Jesus son of Mary. That is because they rebelled and used to transgress."
  * **Gloss:** Imprecation as prophetic speech-act: two tongues, David's and Jesus', record the curse; the crime is rebellion-plus-transgression (the covenant's own escalation pattern).
* **`[5:79]`** — "They used not to prevent one another from the wrongs they used to commit. Evil is what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The curse's mechanism named: communal silence toward sin — the mirror duty of 5:8's active justice.
* **`[5:80]`** — "You will see many of them befriending those who disbelieve. Terrible is what their souls prompts them to do. The wrath of Allah fell upon them, and in the torment they will remain."
  * **Gloss:** The alliance-breach inside Israel itself (cf. ruku 8's warning); inner prompting + wrath + permanence.
* **`[5:81]`** — "Had they believed in Allah and the Prophet, and in what was revealed to him, they would not have befriended them. But many of them are immoral."
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual: faith in Allah, the Prophet, *and what was revealed to him* would have rewired the friendship — belief as the cause of loyalty.
* **`[5:82]`** — "You will find that the people most hostile towards the believers are the Jews and the polytheists. And you will find that the nearest in affection towards the believers are those who say, “We are Christians.” That is because among them are priests and monks, and they are not arrogant."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's two-superlative verdict — most hostile / nearest in affection — with its stated cause: clergy (rabbān wa-qissīs) and monastic humility, "not arrogant."
* **`[5:83]`** — "And when they hear what was revealed to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflowing with tears, as they recognize the truth in it. They say, “Our Lord, we have believed, so count us among the witnesses.”"
  * **Gloss:** The response-scene: hearing → tears → recognition → petition to be enrolled among the witnesses (the testimony-vocabulary of ruku 14 arrives in prayer-form).
* **`[5:84]`** — "“And why should we not believe in Allah, and in the truth that has come to us, and hope that our Lord will include us among the righteous people?”"
  * **Gloss:** The rhetorical self-justification: belief is owed to truth-that-has-come; hope of being joined to the righteous.
* **`[5:85]`** — "Allah will reward them for what they say— Gardens beneath which rivers flow, where they will stay forever. Such is the reward of the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The reward formula in full; "for what they say" ties speech (5:83–84) to recompense.
* **`[5:86]`** — "But as for those who disbelieve and deny Our signs—these are the inmates of the Fire."
  * **Gloss:** The curse clause closes the ruku in the surah's standard sorting formula.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"cursed" (laʿna):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:78]` "Cursed were those who disbelieved... by the tongue of David and Jesus."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:88]` "Rather, Allah has cursed them for their ingratitude"; `[4:52]` "Those are they whom Allah has cursed. Whomever Allah curses, you will find no savior for him"; within surah `[5:13]` ("We cursed them"), `[5:60]`, `[5:64]` — the laʿn web the ruku sits inside (observable).
* **David (Dāwūd):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:78]` as a *tongue* — the psalmist's imprecation.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[17:55]` "and to David We gave the Psalms" (verified via `rg` "Psalms"); `[38:26]` "O David, We made you a ruler in the land, so judge between the people with justice" — David the just judge, the flip of 5:78's cursing tongue; `[21:78–80]` judgment and armor-taught; `[2:251]` Goliath; `[34:10]` mountains echoing with him. Classically, the curse is tied to the Psalter's imprecations against the rebellious (*Source: classical reports on 5:78 — verify*).
* **"priests and monks" (qissīsīn wa-ruhbān):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:82]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[57:27]` "We gave him the Gospel, and instilled in the hearts of those who followed him compassion and monasticism — they invented it" (verified) — the ruhbānity credited here as affection's cause and famously scrutinized there.
* **"tears" (dumūʿ):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:83]` "eyes overflowing with tears."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:92]` "they went away, with their eyes overflowing with tears" — the same phrase at the tearful excluded (verified) — tears as the sign of hearts that cannot carry out what they long for (agent observation — verify).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; 5:82–85 are classically linked to the Negus ('Ashama) and the Abyssinian delegation weeping at the recited Quran (*Source: classical reports on 5:83 — verify*); the corpus text names no occasion (observable).
* **Scope caution:** "most hostile... nearest in affection" is the corpus's own superlative pair, unqualified in the Arabic here as translated; classical and modern readers dispute whether it is a period-political or permanent verdict (*Source: agent inference — verify; treat both as open*).
