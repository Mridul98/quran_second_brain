# Do not ask what will trouble you; the two witnesses of the bequest (5:101–108)
**UID:** 202610141215
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:101–108 (Ruku 14 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:101]` – `[5:108]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The covenant's mouth and its last hour: questions that would harden into law are forbidden because a prior people asked and then disbelieved; the pre-Islamic camel-superstitions are denied any divine institution (liars fabricate about Allah); the self is its own charge; and when death approaches in a foreign land, two reliable witnesses must swear Allah's testimony against any price — a perjury clause built into the law of the final contract.
* **Placement:** Last legislative ruku of the surah: 5:101 answers 5:87's self-legislating prohibition from the question side; the bequest-witness law (106–108) uses the oath-and-atonement grammar of ruku 12 and the two-just-men instrument of ruku 13, before the surah turns to the courtroom of the Day.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:101]`** — "O you who believe! Do not ask about things that would trouble you if disclosed to you. But if you were to ask about them while the Quran is being revealed, they will become obvious to you. Allah forgives that. Allah is Forgiving and Clement."
  * **Gloss:** The etiquette of revelation: questions during descent crystallize answers; what remains unasked is forgiven.
* **`[5:102]`** — "A people before you asked about them, but then came to reject them."
  * **Gloss:** The precedent clause: disclosure can become a test failed.
* **`[5:103]`** — "Allah did not institute the superstitions of Bahirah, Saibah, Wasilah, or of Hami; but those who disbelieve fabricate lies about Allah—most of them do not understand."
  * **Gloss:** Four named pagan camel-customs denied any institutional status (*Source: classical zoological lore for what each animal-sign meant — verify*; the names occur nowhere else in the corpus — verified via `rg`); the real "institutors" are liars about Allah.
* **`[5:104]`** — "And when it is said to them, “Come to what Allah has revealed, and to the Messenger,” they say, “Sufficient for us is what we found our forefathers upon.” Even if their forefathers knew nothing, and were not guided?"
  * **Gloss:** The refusal formula: tradition cited as a revealed-book substitute; the verse's own "Even if...?" leaves the scandal rhetorical.
* **`[5:105]`** — "O you who believe! You are responsible for your own souls. He who has strayed cannot harm you if you are guided. To Allah is you return, all of you, and He will inform you of what you used to do."
  * **Gloss:** Responsibility privatized without isolation: the guided cannot be harmed by the stray — but return is collective and informative. (Corpus reads "To Allah is you return"; quoted verbatim.)
* **`[5:106]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "O you who believe! When death approaches one of you, let two reliable persons from among you act as witnesses to the making of a bequest, or two persons from another people if you are travelling in the land and the event of death approaches you."
  * "Engage them after the prayer. If you have doubts, let them swear by Allah: “We will not sell our testimony for any price, even if he was a near relative, and we will not conceal Allah’s testimony, for then we would be sinners.”"
  * **Gloss:** The travel-death provision: two of your own — or, on the road, two others — detained after prayer to swear the testimony-priceless oath; "Allah's testimony" is the thing held in trust.
* **`[5:107]`** — "If it is discovered that they are guilty of perjury: let two others take their place, two from among those responsible for the claim, and have them swear by Allah, “Our testimony is more truthful than their testimony, and we will not be biased, for then we would be wrongdoers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The counter-oath: the injured party's two stand in the perjurers' place — perjury is answered by escalated testimony, not by penalty alone.
* **`[5:108]`** — "That makes it more likely that they will give true testimony, fearing that their oaths might be contradicted by subsequent oaths. So fear Allah, and listen. Allah does not guide the disobedient people."
  * **Gloss:** The law's psychology: the chance of later contradiction is the deterrent; the ruku ends on fear-and-listening.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan commercial-travel reality behind 5:106's "two persons from another people" — the verse's plain sense permits non-Muslim witnesses for a Muslim's deathbed bequest abroad; the majority of classical fiqh restricted testimony to believers, resolving the tension via abrogation and occasion-reports — *Source: classical dispute on 5:106–107 (occasions cited include a Companion's death at Medayn) — verify.* Presented here neutrally: the text as quoted sets the two-track rule.
* **Asbab for 5:103:** the Bahirah/Saibah/Wasilah/Hami definitions are transmitted zoological lore (a she-camel freed for a deity; a camel whose last offspring was a male, etc.) — *Source: classical reports on 5:103 — verify*; the Quranic verse itself lists names only (observable fact).
