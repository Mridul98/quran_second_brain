# Means of approach, the thief's hand, and the listeners for falsehood (5:35–43)
**UID:** 202610141207
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:35–43 (Ruku 6 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:35]` – `[5:43]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Between the one-life law and the scripture argument, the surah stakes success itself: seek the means of approach to Allah; no ransom will save; the thief's hand, the repentant criminal, and the swift-to-disbelief listeners for falsehood who shop for verdicts — all stand under the same sovereignty that "punishes whom He wills, and forgives whom He wills."
* **Placement:** The penal block (5:33–40) is completed with the theft verse and its repentance clause; the bad-faith litigants of 5:41–43 hand the case over to ruku 7's judgment theology (Torah/Gospel/Quran continuum).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:35]`** — "O you who believe! Be conscious of Allah, and seek the means of approach to Him, and strive in His cause, so that you may succeed."
  * **Gloss:** The success formula: taqwa + wasilah + striving; the verse the classical *wasilah* discussions attach to prayerful approach (label below).
* **`[5:36]`** — "As for those who disbelieve, even if they owned everything on earth, and the like of it with it, and they offered it to ransom themselves from the torment of the Day of Resurrection, it will not be accepted from them. For them is a painful punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The ransom verse: no escrow redeems the rejector — the eschatological anti-market.
* **`[5:37]`** — "They will want to leave the Fire, but they will not leave it. For them is a lasting punishment."
  * **Gloss:** Exit refused; the penalty's permanence stated flatly.
* **`[5:38]`** — "As for the thief, whether male or female, cut their hands as a penalty for what they have reaped—a deterrent from Allah. Allah is Mighty and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The theft penalty, addressed in one sentence to both sexes, with its stated ends: recompense "for what they have reaped" and deterrence; the text is presented here neutrally — the fiqh conditions (threshold, custody, judge) are not in the verse (*Source: classical fiqh elaboration on 5:38 — verify*).
* **`[5:39]`** — "But whoever repents after his crime, and reforms, Allah will accept his repentance. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The mercy valve again (cf. 5:34): repentance-and-reform reopens forgiveness.
* **`[5:40]`** — "Do you not know that to Allah belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth? He punishes whom He wills, and He forgives whom He wills. And Allah is Capable of everything."
  * **Gloss:** The sovereignty sentence resolves the penal block: punishment and forgiveness are both His prerogative.
* **`[5:41]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "O Messenger! Do not let those who are quick to disbelief grieve you—from among those who say with their mouths, “We believe,” but their hearts do not believe; and from among the Jews—listeners to lies, listeners to other people who did not come to you."
  * "They distort words from their places, and they say, “If you are given this, accept it; but if you are not given it, beware.”"
  * "Whomever Allah has willed to divert, you have nothing for him from Allah. Those are they whose hearts Allah does not intend to purify. For them is disgrace in this world, and for them is a great punishment in the Hereafter."
  * **Gloss:** Verbal belief without heart-belief, second-hand listeners, and verdict-shopping: the courtroom of bad faith; divine diversion is named as the underside of hearts Allah does not intend to purify.
* **`[5:42]`** — "Listeners to falsehoods, eaters of illicit earnings. If they come to you, judge between them, or turn away from them. If you turn away from them, they will not harm you in the least. But if you judge, judge between them equitably. Allah loves the equitable."
  * **Gloss:** The optional jurisdiction: the Prophet may judge these litigants or decline — but if he judges, equity is binding; "Allah loves the equitable" re-lands the 5:8 ethic in court.
* **`[5:43]`** — "But why do they come to you for judgment, when they have the Torah, in which is Allah’s Law? Yet they turn away after that. These are not believers."
  * **Gloss:** The scandal named: they abandon their own book of divine law and forum-shop; the pivot into ruku 7's Torah-Gospel-Quran argument.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`wasīlah` ("means of approach"):** that by which nearness is sought.
  * **Rendering:** "the means of approach to Him" `[5:35]` — the word occurs *only here* as a noun of devotion (observable fact; cf. "hand"/"spear" vocabulary of 5:94).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[17:57]` "Those they call upon are themselves seeking means of access to their Lord, vying to be nearer" — the verbal noun and the nearness idiom that define the term's range.
* **"ransom" (fidya/ʿadl):**
  * **Rendering:** "to ransom themselves" `[5:36]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:48]` and `[2:123]` "no ransom will be accepted from it"; `[3:91]` "even the earth full of gold would not be accepted"; `[10:54]`; `[57:15]` — the refusal-of-ransom formula is a stable eschatological topos (observable cluster).
* **"the thief" (sāriq/sāriqah):**
  * **Rendering:** "the thief, whether male or female" `[5:38]` — the word occurs *only here* in the corpus (verified via `rg`); no other surah legislates theft.
  * **Elsewhere:** the crime-lexicon neighbors are "illicit earnings" `[5:42]`, "forbidden wealth" `[5:63]`, "devour wealth wrongfully" `[2:188]`-family — the surah's money-ethics surrounds the hand-law (in-surah web).
* **"listeners to lies" (sammāʿūn):**
  * **Rendering:** "listeners to lies, listeners to other people" `[5:41]`, "Listeners to falsehoods" `[5:42]` — doubled epithet within the surah (observable).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:13]` "twist the words out of their context" and `[4:46]` "take words out of context" — the distortion family these listeners practice.
