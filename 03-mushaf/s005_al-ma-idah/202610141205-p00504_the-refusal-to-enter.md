# The refusal to enter the Holy Land: "Go forth, you and your Lord, and fight" (5:20–26)
**UID:** 202610141205
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:20–26 (Ruku 4 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:20]` – `[5:26]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Moses recalls blessings and commands entry into the assigned Holy Land; the people's refusal — "we will not enter it, ever, as long as they are in it" — is answered by two trusting men, by Moses' complaint, and by forty years of wandering: the surah's paradigm scene of the hard heart refusing the covenant's promise.
* **Placement:** The scandal of refusals opens here (ruku 4–6): the pledge breached in ruku 3 now shows its behavioral signature — the same phraseology ("they turned back," "losers") the surah will apply to its own audience's tests (`[5:54]`).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:20]`** — "When Moses said to his people, “O my people, remember Allah’s blessings upon you, when He placed prophets among you, and made you kings, and gave you what He never gave any other people.”"
  * **Gloss:** The blessing-refrain (5:7, 5:11) now put in Moses' mouth: prophets, kingship, unmatched giving — the memory he will invoke against their refusal.
* **`[5:21]`** — "“O my people, enter the Holy Land which Allah has assigned for you, and do not turn back, lest you return as losers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The command: an assigned land; the warning is a reversal image — *turn back* → *return as losers*.
* **`[5:22]`** — "They said, “O Moses, there are tyrannical people in it; we will not enter it until they leave it. If they leave it, we will be entering.”"
  * **Gloss:** The refusal's conditional politeness: they will enter *only if* — a deferral dressed as a promise.
* **`[5:23]`** — "Two men of those who feared, but whom Allah had blessed, said, “Go at them by the gate; and when you have entered it, you will prevail. And put your trust in Allah, if you are believers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The minority report: fear of Allah + divine blessing yields the tactics of faith — enter by the gate, trust, prevail; belief is defined operationally.
* **`[5:24]`** — "They said, “O Moses, we will not enter it, ever, as long as they are in it. So go ahead, you and your Lord, and fight. We are staying right here.”"
  * **Gloss:** The refusal's naked form — outsourcing the fight to Moses and his Lord while sitting: the surah's hardest sentence of cowardice-theology.
* **`[5:25]`** — "He said, “My Lord! I have control only over myself and my brother, so separate between us and between the wicked people.”"
  * **Gloss:** Moses' complaint: the community has shrunk to two; he asks for a verdict separating believers from the defiant.
* **`[5:26]`** — "He said, “It is forbidden for them for forty years. They will wander aimlessly in the land. So do not grieve over the defiant people.”"
  * **Gloss:** The verdict: the land *forbidden* them (ironic reversal of 5:21's "assigned"), forty years of wandering; a consolation line to Moses — "do not grieve."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"Holy Land" (al-arḍ al-muqaddasa):**
  * **Rendering:** "the Holy Land which Allah has assigned for you" `[5:21]` — the phrase occurs *only here* in this corpus (observable fact via `rg`).
  * **Elsewhere:** the concept travels unnamed in the Exodus material of `[2:57]` (settling) and the promise cycles of `[7:137]`; the assignment language (*qallallaahu laha*) is corpus-unique to this scene.
* **"forty":** the generation's measure.
  * **Rendering:** "forbidden for them for forty years" `[5:26]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:51]` "We appointed for Moses forty nights. Then you took to worshiping the calf"; `[7:142]` "thirty nights, and completed them with ten"; `[46:15]` "His bearing and weaning take forty years" — forty as the biblically-weighted term of a generation (classical pairing of 5:26 with 2:51; *Source: classical consensus on the wilderness generation — verify*).
* **"put your trust in Allah" (tawakkul):**
  * **Rendering:** `[5:23]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:159]` "...and put your trust in Allah"; `[33:3]` "put your trust in Allah. Allah is enough as a trustee"; `[14:12]` "why should we not trust in Allah, when He has guided us in our ways?"; `[11:56]` "I have placed my trust in Allah, my Lord and your Lord" — the formula of every messenger scene.
* **"kings" (mulūk):**
  * **Rendering:** "made you kings" `[5:20]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:246]` "Appoint a king for us" — the later Israelite demand for kingship, with Saul anointed in the same breath the corpus gives; `[2:251]` David given "sovereignty and wisdom."
