# The covenant opens the law: commitments, meals, and the hunting taboo (5:1–5)
**UID:** 202610141202
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 5:1–5 (Ruku 1 of 16 in al-Ma’idah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[5:1]` – `[5:5]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's first word — "Fulfill your commitments" — frames everything that follows as covenant fine print: permitted and forbidden foods, sanctity, and even table- and marriage-fellowship with the People of the Book are stipulations of the pledge.
* **Placement:** Opening ruku. It sets the "fulfillment" frame that `[5:7]` (the covenant by which He urged you) and `[5:12–14]` (the two broken pledges) will exploit; its ihram hunting taboo is a bracket word returned with penalties in ruku 13 (`[5:95–96]`).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[5:1]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "O you who believe! Fulfill your commitments."
  * "Livestock animals are permitted for you, except those specified to you; but not wild game while you are in pilgrim sanctity."
  * "Allah decrees whatever He wills."
  * **Gloss:** One global imperative, two concrete rulings, and a sovereignty formula: whatever Allah decrees is itself a commitment to be fulfilled.
* **`[5:2]`** — four clause-chunks:
  * "O you who believe! Do not violate Allah’s sacraments, nor the Sacred Month, nor the offerings, nor the garlanded, nor those heading for the Sacred House seeking blessings from their Lord and approval."
  * "When you have left the pilgrim sanctity, you may hunt."
  * "And let not the hatred of people who barred you from the Sacred Mosque incite you to aggression."
  * "And cooperate with one another in virtuous conduct and conscience, and do not cooperate with one another in sin and hostility. And fear Allah. Allah is severe in punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The sanctuary order is declared inviolable; hunting resumes only when sanctity lifts; the old grievance (the barred Sacred Mosque) must not become aggression; and the surah's ethical axiom — cooperate in righteousness and taqwa — is planted here and restated at `[5:8]`.
* **`[5:3]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "Prohibited for you are carrion, blood, the flesh of swine, and animals dedicated to other than Allah; also the flesh of animals strangled, killed violently, killed by a fall, gored to death, mangled by wild animals—except what you rescue, and animals sacrificed on altars; and the practice of drawing lots. For it is immoral."
  * "Today, those who disbelieve have despaired of your religion, so do not fear them, but fear Me. Today I have perfected your religion for you, and have completed My favor upon you, and have approved Islam as a religion for you."
  * "But whoever is compelled by hunger, with no intent of wrongdoing—Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The negative diet-list closes with two cultic abuses (altar sacrifice, lot-divination); between the list's ends, two "Today" sentences interrupt with a declaration of religion-completed; the necessity clause restores mercy at the list's edge.
* **`[5:4]`** — "They ask you what is permitted for them. Say, “Permitted for you are all good things, including what trained dogs and falcons catch for you.” You train them according to what Allah has taught you. So eat from what they catch for you, and pronounce Allah’s name over it. And fear Allah. Allah is Swift in reckoning."
  * **Gloss:** The positive counterpart: all *good* things, and the trained hunter's catch — conditioned on Allah's name being pronounced over it; even the animals' training is "what Allah has taught you."
* **`[5:5]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "Today all good things are made lawful for you. And the food of those given the Scripture is lawful for you, and your food is lawful for them. So are chaste believing women, and chaste women from the people who were given the Scripture before you, provided you give them their dowries, and take them in marriage, not in adultery, nor as mistresses."
  * "But whoever rejects faith, his work will be in vain, and in the Hereafter he will be among the losers."
  * **Gloss:** Covenant fellowship expands to shared tables and intermarriage with chaste People of the Book — the most socially expansive ruling of the opening — then a hard apostasy warning closes the frame.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ʿuhud` ("commitments"):** pl. of *ʿahd* — pledges, contracts, stipulations.
  * **Rendering:** "your commitments" in `[5:1]` (its only corpus occurrence as this imperative).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:76]` "whoever fulfills his commitments and maintains piety—Allah loves the pious"; `[16:91]` "Fulfill Allah’s covenant when you make a covenant, and do not break your oaths after ratifying them" — the same imperative family binding creed to contract.
* **"pilgrim sanctity" (iḥrām):** the consecrated state forbidding hunting.
  * **Rendering:** "pilgrim sanctity" — the phrase occurs *only* at `[5:1]`, `[5:2]`, `[5:95]`, `[5:96]` (observable corpus fact via `rg`): it literally brackets the surah's law.
  * **Elsewhere:** no other surah uses this English phrase; cf. the ḥajj conduct law of `[2:196–197]`.
* **"Sacred Month":** the sanctified truce-months.
  * **Rendering:** "Sacred Month" in `[5:2]` and `[5:97]`; but "Holy Month" in `[2:217]` and lowercase "sacred month" in `[2:194]` — translator term-choice drift (observable in corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:194]` "The sacred month for the sacred month"; `[2:217]`; `[9:5]` "When the Sacred Months have passed" — retaliation regulated, not abolished, by the months.
* **`ṭayyibāt` ("good things"):** what is wholesome, pleasing, lawful.
  * **Rendering:** "all good things" in `[5:4–5]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:172]` "Eat of the good things We have provided for you, and give thanks"; `[16:114]` "Eat of the lawful and good things Allah has provided for you" (5:88 echoes it verbatim within the surah); `[3:93]` "All food was permissible to the Children of Israel, except what Israel forbade for itself" — the diet-law's history behind 5:3.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan throughout (classical consensus); the community now legislated at rest — contrast with the emergency law of al-Baqarah's later ruku.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the "Today I have perfected your religion" sentence (`[5:3]`) is classically reported as descending at the Farewell Pilgrimage (*Source: classical transmitted reports on 5:3 — verify*); the corpus itself gives no occasion (observable fact). 5:2's Ḥudaybiyyah backdrop: see `[48:25]` link above (*Source: classical consensus — verify*).
