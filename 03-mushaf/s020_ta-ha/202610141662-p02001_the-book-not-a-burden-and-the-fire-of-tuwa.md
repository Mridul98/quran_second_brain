# Not revealed to make you suffer — the fire, the staff, the call at Tuwa (20:1–24)
**UID:** 202610141662
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 20:1–24 (Ruku 1 of 8 in Ta-Ha)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** ``[20:1]`` – ``[20:24]`` (24 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah disarms fear before it tells a story: this Book was not sent to make anyone miserable; it is a reminder for whoever already fears — and the proof of that reassurance is a shepherd chosen at a fire, armed with his own walking-stick, and sent to the most dangerous king alive.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 8; letters and doxology (1–8) frame the first act of the Moses narrative (9–24), which continues in ruku 2. Note for the record: the corpus wording is “to make you suffer” ([20:2]), not “miserable”; [20:12] says “Take off your shoes,” not “sandals” — both verified against the corpus.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[20:1]`** Ta, Ha.
  * **Gloss:** Transliterated letter-names, no semantic content — the signature of the 29 letter-openings; [20:1] is listed in the fleeting survey note.
* **`[20:2]`** We did not reveal the Quran to you to make you suffer.
  * **Gloss:** The Book's negative self-definition: revelation is not *shiqwa*, misery — reassurance addressed to the prophet weighed down by the commission.
* **`[20:3]`** But only as a reminder for him who fears.
  * **Gloss:** The positive counterpart: a “reminder” whose audience is qualified by fear — guidance-with-conditions, the k-guidance note's grammar (its wording checked: the note speaks of stance-conditions, not “miserable,” so link at 20:3 only).
* **`[20:4]`** A revelation from He who created the earth and the high heavens.  **`[20:5]`** The Most Merciful; on the Throne He settled.
  * **Gloss:** The sender's credentials: Creator of earth and the “high heavens” (phrase unique in the corpus), and the istiwa line “on the Throne He settled” (cf. [13:2], [57:4], [7:54]).
* **`[20:6]`** To Him belongs everything in the heavens and the earth, and everything between them, and everything beneath the soil.  **`[20:7]`** If you speak aloud—He knows the secret, and the most hidden.
  * **Gloss:** Ownership all the way down “beneath the soil” (unique phrase), and the address “If you speak aloud—He knows the secret, and the most hidden” — total knowledge as comfort, not threat.
* **`[20:8]`** Allah, there is no god but He, His are the Most Beautiful Names.
  * **Gloss:** Creed in one breath: the name, the negation, “His are the Most Beautiful Names” (elsewhere [7:180], [59:24]; [17:110] renders it “the Best Names” — drift observable in corpus).
* **`[20:9]`** Has the story of Moses reached you?
  * **Gloss:** The narrative hinge: “Has the story of Moses reached you?” — the surah argues its reassurance with a story, not a syllogism.
* **`[20:10]`** When he saw a fire, he said to his family, “Stay; I have noticed a fire; Perhaps I can bring you a torch therefrom, or find some guidance by the fire.”
  * **Gloss:** A family halts at a fire; Moses hopes for a torch “or find some guidance by the fire” — the double meaning of guidance pre-loads the surah's theme.
* **`[20:11]`** Then, when he reached it, he was called, “O Moses.  **`[20:12]`** I—I am your Lord. Take off your shoes. You are in the sacred valley of Tuwa.  **`[20:13]`** I have chosen you, so listen to what is revealed.
  * **Gloss:** Called from the fire; sandals logic: the ground is holy, so “Take off your shoes… the sacred valley of Tuwa” — the corpus's only other “sacred valley” is [79:16], also Tuwa. Chosen, and told to listen.
* **`[20:14]`** I—I am Allah. There is no God but I. So serve Me, and practice the prayer for My remembrance.
  * **Gloss:** Creed inside the story: “There is no God but I. So serve Me, and practice the prayer for My remembrance” — “practice the prayer” is Itani's unique verb-choice here (elsewhere “observe the prayer”).
* **`[20:15]`** The Hour is coming—but I keep it almost hidden—so that each soul will be paid for what it endeavors.  **`[20:16]`** And do not let him who denies it and follows his desire turn you away from it, lest you fall.
  * **Gloss:** The Hour kept hidden so every soul is paid for what it endeavors; warning against “him who denies it and follows his desire,” “lest you fall” — the fall-word's first appearance of three (81, 121).
* **`[20:17]`** And what is that in your right-hand, O Moses?”  **`[20:18]`** He said, “This is my staff. I lean on it, and herd my sheep with it, and I have other uses for it.”
  * **Gloss:** “And what is that in your right-hand” — dangling closing quote and the hyphenated “right-hand” are the book's typography [sic] (20:69 prints “right hand” plain). Answer: the staff's plain inventory — leaning, sheep, other uses.
* **`[20:19]`** He said, “Throw it, O Moses.”  **`[20:20]`** So he threw it—thereupon it became a moving serpent.  **`[20:21]`** He said, “Take hold of it, and do not fear. We will restore it to its original condition.
  * **Gloss:** Throw → “a moving serpent”; then “Take hold of it, and do not fear” — the ordinary object tested, reversed, and restored; mastery over fear rehearsed before Pharaoh.
* **`[20:22]`** And press your hand to your side; it will come out white, without a blemish—another sign.  **`[20:23]`** That We may show you some of Our greatest signs.  **`[20:24]`** Go to Pharaoh; He has transgressed.”
  * **Gloss:** Hand white “without a blemish — another sign”; purpose clause “some of Our greatest signs”; commission: “Go to Pharaoh; He has transgressed.” — the capital H reads of Pharaoh [sic, book typography].

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Ta, Ha`** [20:1]
  * **Rendering:** letter-names transliterated, untranslated — the edition refuses to gloss them.
  * **Elsewhere:** [19:1] “Kaf, Ha, Ya, Ayn, Saad.” — the adjacent letter-surah; see the fleeting survey (linked below).
* **`dhikr` (reminder)** [20:3]
  * **Rendering:** “But only as a reminder for him who fears.”
  * **Elsewhere:** [5:94] “that Allah may know who fears Him at heart”; [14:14] “for him who fears My Majesty, and fears My threats” — fear as the condition the reminder seeks.
* **`istawa` (the Throne)** [20:5]
  * **Rendering:** “The Most Merciful; on the Throne He settled.”
  * **Elsewhere:** [13:2] “settled on the Throne,” [57:4] “settled over the Throne,” [7:54] “established Himself on the Throne” — one image, four wordings (observable in corpus); the Throne itself at [2:255].
* **`al-asma' al-husna`** [20:8]
  * **Elsewhere:** [7:180] and [59:24] carry the identical phrase; [17:110] prints “the Best Names” — term-choice drift within this book, per the artifacts note's class-2 caveat.
