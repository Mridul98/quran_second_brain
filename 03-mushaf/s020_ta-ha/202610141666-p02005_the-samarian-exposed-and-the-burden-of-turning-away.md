# Aaron's defense, the Samarian exposed, and the burden of turning away (20:90–104)
**UID:** 202610141666
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 20:90–104 (Ruku 5 of 8 in Ta-Ha)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** ``[20:90]`` – ``[20:104]`` (15 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The aftermath stages three interrogations — the people's stubborn vow, Aaron's prevented intervention (“Son of my mother”), and the Samarian's confessed method — before the calf is burned and scattered and the god re-set: “your god is Allah, the One besides whom there is no other god.” Then the ruku pivots from narrative to eschatology: the reports of times gone by are cargo, and whoever turns from this Message carries a burden into the Trumpet Day when the best criminal will remember world-life as “only a day.”
* **Placement:** Ruku 5 of 8; completes the calf affair (90–98) and executes the turn to the Hour (99–104) that ruku 6 continues.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[20:90]`** Aaron had said to them before, “O my people, you are being tested by this. And your Lord is the Merciful, so follow me, and obey my command.”
  * **Gloss:** Aaron's pre-crime sermon, quoted after the fact: “you are being tested by this. And your Lord is the Merciful” — mercy named at the moment of trial; “follow me, and obey my command” — the colleague who preached while the prophet was gone.
* **`[20:91]`** They said, “We will not give up our devotion to it, until Moses returns to us.”
  * **Gloss:** The people's vow of delay: “We will not give up our devotion to it, until Moses returns” — idolatry reclassified as a scheduling conflict; they were already waiting for Moses, but facing the wrong way.
* **`[20:92]`** He said, “O Aaron, what prevented you, when you saw them going astray.
  * **Gloss:** The interrogation of Aaron begins — the sentence split mid-breath across two verses, the quote left open [sic].
* **`[20:93]`** From following me? Did you disobey my command?”
  * **Gloss:** …“From following me? Did you disobey my command?” — the charge stated at its hardest.
* **`[20:94]`** He said, “Son of my mother, do not seize me by my beard or my head. I feared you would say, `You have caused division among the Children of Israel, and did not regard my word.'“
  * **Gloss:** “Son of my mother” — the same maternal plea Aaron uses in [7:150] (verified twin, same speaker); his defense: he feared the accusation of having split Israel — unity as the reason for restraint.
* **`[20:95]`** He said, “What do you have to say, O Samarian?”
  * **Gloss:** The turn to the culprit: “What do you have to say, O Samarian?” — a trial conducted by questions, not accusations.
* **`[20:96]`** He said, “I saw what they did not see, so I grasped a handful from the Messenger’s traces, and I flung it away. Thus my soul prompted me.”
  * **Gloss:** The confession — verify the task's wording against the corpus: it prints “I grasped a handful from the Messenger's traces, and I flung it away. Thus my soul prompted me.” (no “concealed in hand” line exists in this corpus); the Messenger-whose-trace is Gabriel's mount in the classical glosses. *Source: consensus of classical tafsir reports — agent inference — verify.*
* **`[20:97]`** He said, “Begone! Your lot in this life is to say, ‘No contact.’ And you have an appointment that you will not miss. Now look at your god that you remained devoted to—we will burn it up, and then blow it away into the sea, as powder.”
  * **Gloss:** The sentence: exile-by-word — “Your lot in this life is to say, ‘No contact.’” (unique rendering, verified) — plus “an appointment that you will not miss,” flipping Pharaoh's boast of 20:58 onto its author; and his god is burned, blown into sea-powder.
* **`[20:98]`** Surely your god is Allah, the One besides whom there is no other god. He comprehends everything in knowledge.
  * **Gloss:** The creed re-set after the spectacle of debris: “Surely your god is Allah, the One besides whom there is no other god. He comprehends everything in knowledge” — the knowing God against the wordless calf (89).
* **`[20:99]`** Thus We narrate to you reports of times gone by; and We have given you a message from Our Presence.
  * **Gloss:** The surah's own theory of narrative: “Thus We narrate to you reports of times gone by; and We have given you a message from Our Presence” — “Our Presence” is unique in the corpus (verified); history is propaedeutic to this Book.
* **`[20:100]`** Whoever turns away from it will carry on the Day of Resurrection a burden.
  * **Gloss:** Turn from it and “carry on the Day of Resurrection a burden” — the ornaments carried in (87) are now carried out, sin-payload.
* **`[20:101]`** Abiding therein forever. And wretched is their burden on the Day of Resurrection.
  * **Gloss:** The burden abides; “wretched is their burden” — cargo with no port.
* **`[20:102]`** On the Day when the Trumpet is blown— We will gather the sinners on that Day, blue.
  * **Gloss:** “On the Day when the Trumpet is blown” — the identical phrase of [27:87] (verified; cf. [39:68] “sounded,” [18:99] “blown”) — and the sinners gathered “blue,” the corpus's only color-word for that muster (verified).
* **`[20:103]`** Murmuring among themselves: “You have lingered only for ten.”
  * **Gloss:** Murmured arithmetic among them: “You have lingered only for ten.” — the world's tally, discounted on the spot.
* **`[20:104]`** We are fully aware of what they say, when the most exemplary of them in conduct will say, “You have lingered only a day.”
  * **Gloss:** The best of them corrects it down: “You have lingered only a day” — cf. [23:114] “You remained only for a little while” (verified); the ruku that opened with a forty-night absence ends on a one-day world.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **“handful… traces”** [20:96]
  * **Rendering:** “I grasped a handful from the Messenger's traces” — *qabḍa* + traces; the task's “I had what my hand concealed” is not this corpus (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** “handful” occurs nowhere else in the corpus (verified); the scene has no twin outside s020.
* **“No contact”** [20:97]
  * **Rendering:** the Samarian's social death spoken as a quotable line — a unique rendering (verified); the underlying idiom (hajr, avoidance) is classically glossed as untouchability. *Source: classical gloss reported — agent inference — verify.*
* **`sur` (the Trumpet)** [20:102]
  * **Elsewhere:** [27:87] identical “On the Day when the Trumpet is blown”; [18:99], [39:68] the sound-event of the muster — three settings, one instrument.
* **“burden”** [20:100]
  * **Rendering:** turning away from the Message = carrying weight at the reckoning.
  * **Elsewhere:** the ornament-loads of [20:87] (“loads of the people's ornaments”) are the literal cargo this metaphor loads again — within-surah, verified wording.
