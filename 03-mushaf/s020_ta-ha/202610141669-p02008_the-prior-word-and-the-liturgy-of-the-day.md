# The prior word, the liturgy of the day, and the universal wait (20:129–135)
**UID:** 202610141669
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 20:129–135 (Ruku 8 of 8 in Ta-Ha)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** ``[20:129]`` – ``[20:135]`` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The finale turns the history-sermon into the prophet's own liturgy: a prior word buys everyone time; the day is ringed with glorification “that you may be satisfied”; the eyes are ordered off the splendor that is only a test; the people are commanded to prayer — and the surah that opened by denying that this Book would make anyone miserable ends on shared suspense: “Everybody is waiting, so wait.”
* **Placement:** Ruku 8 of 8; the personal-address closing mirrors the opening reassurance (20:2) at the scale of a single prophetic day: patience, tasbih, uncovetous eyes, and deferred verdict.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[20:129]`** Were it not for a word that issued from your Lord, the inevitable would have happened, but there is an appointed term.
  * **Gloss:** The respite's legal basis: “Were it not for a word that issued from your Lord, the inevitable would have happened, but there is an appointed term” — the prior-word formula twin of [11:110] “Were it not for a prior word from your Lord, it would have been settled between them” (verified).
* **`[20:130]`** So bear patiently what they say, and celebrate the praises of your Lord before the rising of the sun, and before its setting. And during the hours of the night glorify Him, and at the borders of the day, that you may be satisfied.
  * **Gloss:** The shape of the respite: patience with what they say, then the tasbih clock — “before the rising of the sun, and before its setting… the hours of the night… the borders of the day” — [50:39] and [11:114] supply near-verbatim halves (verified); terminus: “that you may be satisfied” — cf. [93:5] “And your Lord will give you, and you will be satisfied” (verified).
* **`[20:131]`** And do not extend your glance towards what We have given some classes of them to enjoy—the splendor of the life of this world— that We may test them thereby. Your Lord’s provision is better, and more lasting.
  * **Gloss:** The eye-command — verified as 20:131: “do not extend your glance towards what We have given some classes of them to enjoy—the splendor of the life of this world— that We may test them thereby”: the spectacle is a trial, not an endorsement (cf. Pharaoh's “splendor” [10:88], Korah's parade [28:79], both verified); and the comparative the magicians sang at 20:73 returns as covenant: “Your Lord's provision is better, and more lasting” — twin of [87:17] (verified).
* **`[20:132]`** And exhort your people to pray, and patiently adhere to it. We ask of you no sustenance, but it is We who sustain you. The good ending is that for righteousness.
  * **Gloss:** The domestic liturgy: “exhort your people to pray, and patiently adhere to it” — patience + prayer as one compound, the 2:45 grammar of the k-sabr note; the ministry's economics: “We ask of you no sustenance, but it is We who sustain you”; and the corpus's only “The good ending is that for righteousness” (verified).
* **`[20:133]`** And they say, “Why does he not bring us a miracle from his Lord?” Were they not given enough miracles in the former scriptures?
  * **Gloss:** The last demand of the Meccan interlocutors: “Why does he not bring us a miracle from his Lord?” — [29:50] prints the same request (verified) — answered with Scripture-sufficiency: “Were they not given enough miracles in the former scriptures?”
* **`[20:134]`** Had We destroyed them with a punishment before him, they would have said, “Our Lord, if only You had sent us a messenger, we would have followed Your revelations before we were humiliated and disgraced.”
  * **Gloss:** The pre-empted excuse: a people destroyed before a messenger would plead, “if only You had sent us a messenger” — the respite of 129 is mercy with evidence attached.
* **`[20:135]`** Say, “Everybody is waiting, so wait. You will know who the people of the straight path are, and who is rightly-guided.
  * **Gloss:** The close: “Everybody is waiting, so wait. You will know who the people of the straight path are, and who is rightly-guided” — the surah's last word is the Fatihah's petition-word, *sirat* ([1:6] “Guide us to the straight path”; the programmatic answer [6:153], per the k-guidance note); verdict deferred to the end of the appointed term. NOTE: the task's “closing” quote is [20:75], not here; the genuine close is this verse.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **“a word that issued”** [20:129]
  * **Rendering:** the decree-withheld formula.
  * **Elsewhere:** [11:110] “a prior word from your Lord” (verified) — the same respite grammar; cf. “appointed term” recurring across the corpus as the stay of execution.
* **“better, and more lasting”** [20:131]
  * **Elsewhere:** [87:17] “Though the Hereafter is better, and more lasting” (verified); within-surah [20:73] “Allah is Better, and more Lasting,” [20:127] “more severe, and more lasting” — the surah's comparative spine, three registers.
* **`sirat` (straight path)** [20:135]
  * **Rendering:** “the people of the straight path” — the road defined socially at the surah's end, exactly as [1:7] defines it socially at the book's beginning.
  * **Elsewhere:** [1:6–7]; [6:153] (both cited in the k-guidance note — dedup: link, don't restate).
* **`rizq` (provision)** [20:131–132]
  * **Rendering:** “Your Lord's provision is better” / “it is We who sustain you” — the comparative that turns envy into liturgy.
  * **Elsewhere:** the shukr note's provision-circuit (16:18 inventory-limit; 14:7 increase-decree) is this ruku's operating theology.
