# The night flight, the drowned pursuer, and the lowing calf (20:77–89)
**UID:** 202610141665
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 20:77–89 (Ruku 4 of 8 in Ta-Ha)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** ``[20:77]`` – ``[20:89]`` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Escape and betrayal in one movement: Israel is ordered out by night and delivered through a dry path while Pharaoh's state swallows itself — and the rescued people repay the rendezvous in the forty-night absence by melting the looted ornaments into a calf that lows. The ruku's verdict is a guidance sentence: “Pharaoh misled his people, and did not guide them.”
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 8; the exodus-Sinai-calf arc compressed, handing the interrogation scene (Aaron, the Samarian, the burning) to ruku 5.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[20:77]`** And We inspired Moses: “Travel by night with My servants, and strike for them a dry path across the sea, not fearing being overtaken, nor worrying.”
  * **Gloss:** “Travel by night with My servants” — [26:52] prints the same order with the tail inverted (“Travel with My servants by night. You will be followed,” verified); the promised “dry path” arrives unstruck here, and Lot's night-flight twin [11:81] “during the cover of the night” (verified) shades the genre; immunity is doubled: “not fearing being overtaken, nor worrying.”
* **`[20:78]`** Pharaoh pursued them with his troops, but the sea overwhelmed them, and completely engulfed them.  **`[20:79]`** Pharaoh misled his people, and did not guide them.
  * **Gloss:** Pursuit turns to drowning — “the sea overwhelmed them, and completely engulfed them” — and the epitaph of leadership: “Pharaoh misled his people, and did not guide them” — the surah's own thesis stated as misguidance.
* **`[20:80]`** O Children of Israel! We have delivered you from your enemy, and promised you by the right side of the Mount, and sent down to you manna and quails.
  * **Gloss:** Salvation inventory: delivered from the enemy, promised “by the right side of the Mount” (the same right-side idiom as [28:30] “from the right side of the valley,” verified), and fed “manna and quails” — verbatim kin of [2:57] (verified).
* **`[20:81]`** Eat of the good things We have provided for you, but do not be excessive therein, lest My wrath descends upon you. He upon whom My wrath descends has fallen.
  * **Gloss:** The dietary clause becomes covenantal: eat the good provision, do not overstep, “lest My wrath descends upon you. He upon whom My wrath descends has fallen” — the fall-word returns (16, 81, 121).
* **`[20:82]`** And I am Forgiving towards him who repents, believes, acts righteously, and then remains guided.
  * **Gloss:** God's self-description parked between rescue and relapse: “Forgiving towards him who repents, believes, acts righteously, and then remains guided” — a four-step ladder; grace pre-provided for the calf they have not made yet.
* **`[20:83]`** “And what made you rush ahead of your people, O Moses?”  **`[20:84]`** He said, “They are following in my footsteps; and I hurried on to You, my Lord, that you may be pleased.”
  * **Gloss:** Heaven's interrogative: “what made you rush ahead of your people, O Moses?” — and the eager answer, “that you may be pleased” (lowercase you [sic]); the forty nights sit behind this exchange: [7:142] gives the arithmetic (thirty + ten), [2:51] the phrase (both verified).
* **`[20:85]`** He said, “We have tested your people in your absence, and the Samarian misled them.”
  * **Gloss:** God answers before the people can: the test ran in the absence, and “the Samarian misled them” — the name Samarian appears in the corpus ONLY in this surah (verified; [7:148]'s calf story never names its maker).
* **`[20:86]`** So Moses returned to his people, angry and disappointed. He said, “O my people, did your Lord not promise you a good promise? Was the time too long for you? Or did you want wrath from your Lord to descend upon you, so you broke your promise to me?”
  * **Gloss:** “So Moses returned to his people, angry and disappointed” — verbatim twin of [7:150]'s phrase (verified); his complaint piles three questions onto one charge: “so you broke your promise to me?”
* **`[20:87]`** They said, “We did not break our promise to you by our choice, but we were made to carry loads of the people’s ornaments, and we cast them in. That was what the Samarian suggested.”
  * **Gloss:** The people's defense: they did not choose to break; the ornaments were carried in, cast in — “That was what the Samarian suggested” — agency diffused by suggestion.
* **`[20:88]`** So he produced for them a calf—a mere body which lowed. And they said, “This is your god, and the god of Moses, but he has forgotten.”
  * **Gloss:** The calf: “a mere body which lowed” — the identical phrase of [7:148] (verified) — and the slogan “This is your god, and the god of Moses, but he has forgotten”: a claim of divine forgetfulness the surah has already answered at 20:52 (“nor does He forget”).
* **`[20:89]`** Did they not see that it cannot return a word to them, and has no power to harm them or benefit them?
  * **Gloss:** The absurdity check: the idol “cannot return a word… no power to harm… or benefit” — the calf's CV, recited by the surah that will make the sun itself unable to speak next ruku's dawn-liturgy.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **“the Samarian”** [20:85]
  * **Rendering:** al-Sāmirī as “the Samarian,” named only in this surah across the corpus (verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** [7:148] narrates the same calf without naming anyone — the tafsir literature debates who he is (Moses-tradition Samaritan vs. an Israelite idolater); the corpus itself declines the question. *Source: classical dispute reported — agent inference — verify.*
* **“a mere body which lowed”** [20:88]
  * **Elsewhere:** [7:148] “a calf made from their ornaments— a body which lowed” — the one shared phrase across the two tellings (verified).
* **“manna and quails”** [20:80]
  * **Elsewhere:** [2:57] “We sent down to you manna and quails” — verbatim; [7:160] reuses the whole clause inside the spring-block; the food-memory is Ta-Ha's fastest link back to Baqarah.
* **“right side of the Mount”** [20:80]
  * **Elsewhere:** [28:30] “called from the right side of the valley” (verified) — the same spatial formula marks both theocracy's appointments.
