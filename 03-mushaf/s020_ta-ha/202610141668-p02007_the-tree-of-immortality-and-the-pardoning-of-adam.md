# The Tree of Immortality, the fall, and the three-verb pardon (20:116–128)
**UID:** 202610141668
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 20:116–128 (Ruku 7 of 8 in Ta-Ha)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** ``[20:116]`` – ``[20:128]`` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The covenant with Adam is unpacked as drama: prostration commanded and refused; a Garden engineered against need (no hunger, thirst, nakedness, or swelter); one whispered offer — “the Tree of Immortality, and a kingdom that never decays” — and the fall; then the corpus's most compact repentance arc: “his Lord recalled him, and pardoned him, and guided him.” The terms are then universalized: whoever follows guidance neither strays nor suffers (the surah's opening promise cashed); whoever turns from the Reminder lives confined and is raised blind — forgotten as he forgot.
* **Placement:** Ruku 7 of 8; the Adam pericope seeded at 20:115; 20:123 closes the suffer-frame opened at 20:2, and 20:128 re-opens the destroyed-generations motif for the finale.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[20:116]`** And when We said to the angels, “Bow down to Adam.” They bowed down, except for Satan; he refused.
  * **Gloss:** The prostration command and the one refusal — Satan's “he refused” planted before the Garden scene, so the next whisper has a résumé.
* **`[20:117]`** We said, “O Adam, this is an enemy to you and to your wife. So do not let him make you leave the Garden, for then you will suffer.
  * **Gloss:** The enemy warning plus the surah's hinge-word: “for then you will suffer” — node two of the suffer-chain (2 → 117 → 123; observable in this translation).
* **`[20:118]`** In it you will never go hungry, nor be naked.
  * **Gloss:** Garden spec, negative definition: never hungry, never naked — want deleted from the map.
* **`[20:119]`** Nor will you be thirsty in it, nor will you swelter.”
  * **Gloss:** …never thirsty, “nor will you swelter” — the corpus's only “swelter” (verified); comfort described as the absence of exposure.
* **`[20:120]`** But Satan whispered to him. He said, “O Adam, shall I show you the Tree of Immortality, and a kingdom that never decays?”
  * **Gloss:** The pitch: “the Tree of Immortality, and a kingdom that never decays” — immortality offered as the forbidden's fruit; the A'raf twin tempts with “lest you become angels, or become immortals” ([7:20], verified) — same bait, different wording.
* **`[20:121]`** And so they ate from it; whereupon their bodies became visible to them, and they started covering themselves with the leaves of the Garden. Thus Adam disobeyed his Lord, and fell.
  * **Gloss:** They eat; bodies become visible; leaf-covering; “Thus Adam disobeyed his Lord, and fell” — the fall-word's third and heaviest use (16, 81, 121).
* **`[20:122]`** But then his Lord recalled him, and pardoned him, and guided him.
  * **Gloss:** The turn in three verbs — “his Lord recalled him, and pardoned him, and guided him”: the task's “repentance pair” is this couplet 121–122 (fall → recovery); its Baqarah twin is [2:37] “Adam received words from his Lord, so He relented towards him” (verified).
* **`[20:123]`** He said, “Go down from it, altogether; some of you enemies of some others. But whenever guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance, will not go astray, nor suffer.
  * **Gloss:** The demotion terms and the cash of 20:2: guidance-followers “will not go astray, nor suffer” — the surah's promise to the fearful prophet now made universal law; “Go down from it, altogether” re-voices [2:36/2:38] (“Go down from it, all of you… guidance comes to you from Me,” verified).
* **`[20:124]`** But whoever turns away from My Reminder, for him is a confined life. And We will raise him on the Day of Resurrection blind.”
  * **Gloss:** The counterpart: turning from “My Reminder” yields “a confined life” (the corpus's only “confined,” verified) and resurrection blind — cf. [17:97] “gather them… blind, dumb, and deaf” (verified).
* **`[20:125]`** He will say, “My Lord, why did You raise me blind, though I was seeing?”
  * **Gloss:** The blind man's lawsuit: “why did You raise me blind, though I was seeing?” — sight as the memory of what was given.
* **`[20:126]`** He will say, “Just as Our revelations came to you, and you forgot them, today you will be forgotten.”
  * **Gloss:** The verdict's mirror: “Just as Our revelations came to you, and you forgot them, today you will be forgotten” — reciprocity resolving the surah's forgetting-contrapuntal (52, 88, 115).
* **`[20:127]`** Thus We recompense him who transgresses and does not believe in the revelations of his Lord. The punishment of the Hereafter is more severe, and more lasting.
  * **Gloss:** The transgressor's recompense stated in the comparative register: “more severe, and more lasting” — the magicians' “Better, and more Lasting” (73) in terror-mode.
* **`[20:128]`** Is it not instructive to them, how many generations before them We destroyed, in whose settlements they walk? Surely in that are signs for people of understanding.
  * **Gloss:** The archaeological sermon: dead generations “in whose settlements they walk” — the travel-through-ruins motif; and the inventory formula returns, “signs for people of understanding” (echo of 20:54, verified wording).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **“Tree of Immortality”** [20:120]
  * **Rendering:** the tree named by its bait — a unique naming (verified against 7:20's angels/immortals phrasing).
  * **Elsewhere:** [7:20] (verified) — same scene, temptation worded differently; no corpus verse calls it “the wheat” or any species; the naming stays allegorical.
* **suffer-chain** [20:117, 20:123]
  * **Rendering:** the three “suffer” lines — [20:2], [20:117], [20:123] — bracket the surah's claim about revelation's purpose (verified by rg; underlying roots debated — *Source: agent inference — verify*).
* **“confined life” / “blind”** [20:124]
  * **Rendering:** “a confined life” unique in the corpus; the blind raising mirrors 17:97's face-blind-dumb-deaf muster (both verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** [17:97]; within-surah [20:102]'s blue muster is the sibling image — sensory privation as recompense.
