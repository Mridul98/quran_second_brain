# One grain, one bare rock, one rain-fed garden — and one burning loss (2:261–266)
**UID:** 202610140946
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:261–266 (Ruku 36 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:261]` – `[2:266]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's richest parable-cluster: giving is agriculture — 700-fold on living soil, zero on the smooth rock of show-off, and a tornado question to the hoarder — while the same rain that doubles a hillside garden strips a bare stone, so the only difference between the two is what is underneath.
* **Placement:** Opens the charity-vs-usury sequence (ruku 36–38) directly after Abraham's resurrection lectures: the God who multiplies grain and revives bones is the same One whose economy rewards spending; this is the ruku the canonical note on the two economies anchors in.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:261]`** "The parable of those who spend their wealth in Allah’s way is that of a grain that produces seven spikes; in each spike is a hundred grains. Allah multiplies for whom He wills. Allah is Bounteous and Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** 700:1, and the arithmetic deliberately fails — Allah multiplies beyond the parable; "Knowing" says the return tracks the intention, not the amount.
* **`[2:262]`** "Those who spend their wealth in the way of Allah, and then do not follow up what they spent with reminders of their generosity or with insults, will have their reward with their Lord—they have nothing to fear, nor shall they grieve."
  * **Gloss:** The spoiling clause: reminders (mann) and hurt (adha) after giving annul the reward's hereafter-signature; the salvation-refrain ("nothing to fear nor grieve") is attached to gift-etiquette.
* **`[2:263]`** "Kind words and forgiveness are better than charity followed by insults. Allah is Rich and Clement."
  * **Gloss:** A word beats a wound-gift; "Allah is Rich" — the economy does not need your money, so manners are the real transaction.
* **`[2:264]`** "O you who believe! Do not nullify your charitable deeds with reminders and hurtful words, like him who spends his wealth to be seen by the people, and does not believe in Allah and the Last Day. His likeness is that of a smooth rock covered with soil: a downpour strikes it, and leaves it bare—they gain nothing from their efforts. Allah does not guide the disbelieving people."
  * **Gloss:** Riya' anatomy: soil-deep piety, a rock for a heart; rain — the very mercy-water — tests it and strips it; show is liquidated at the first real need, and the verse closes on the guidance-doctrine: God does not steer the self-deceived.
* **`[2:265]`** "And the parable of those who spend their wealth seeking Allah’s approval, and to strengthen their souls, is that of a garden on a hillside. If heavy rain falls on it, its produce is doubled; and if no heavy rain falls, then dew is enough. Allah is seeing of everything you do."
  * **Gloss:** The twin of 2:264: same rain, different substrate; a self that is "strengthened" needs no market — dew suffices; "seeking Allah’s approval" defines the only solvent motive.
* **`[2:266]`** "Would anyone of you like to have a garden of palms and vines, under which rivers flow—with all kinds of fruit in it for him, and old age has stricken him, and he has weak children—then a tornado with fire batters it, and it burns down? Thus Allah makes clear the signs for you, so that you may reflect."
  * **Gloss:** The closing what-if: wealth at its most defended (fruit, old age, helpless heirs) lost to wind-and-fire — the rhetorical frame for "do you want to be the owner of a garden that burns?" (Source: the reading of 2:266 as the show-off/hoarder's verdict is classical and text-adjacent; the garden-owner's identity is disputed in tafsir — verify.)

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`spend in Allah’s way`** (infāq): the ruku's governing verb — four occurrences in six verses.
  * **Rendering:** "those who spend their wealth in Allah’s way" (2:261, 262, 264, 265).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:254]` (ruku 34) "Spend from what We have given you"; `[2:195]` (ruku 24) "spend in the cause of Allah"; `[2:219]` "The surplus" — the whole infaq-arc from war-fund to parable (verified).
* **`reminders and insults`** (mann wa adhā): the after-poison of giving.
  * **Rendering:** "do not follow up what they spent with reminders of their generosity or with insults" (2:262); "charity followed by insults" (2:263); "with reminders and hurtful words" (2:264).
  * **Elsewhere:** the pair occurs only in this ruku in the surah (verified via rg on "reminders|insults|hurtful") — the law of the tongue after the hand.
* **`the parable / likeness`** (mathal): the surah's testing genre.
  * **Rendering:** "The parable of those who spend..." (2:261); "His likeness is that of a smooth rock" (2:264); "the parable of those who spend... is that of a garden" (2:265).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:17]` fire-kindled, `[2:26]` the gnat, `[2:171]` the caller and the beasts — the opening parables sorted hypocrites; these sort givers (verified).
* **`to be seen by the people`** (riyāʾ): worship as audience-capture.
  * **Rendering:** "who spends his wealth to be seen by the people, and does not believe in Allah and the Last Day" (2:264).
  * **Elsewhere:** cf. `[2:204]` — "he calls Allah to witness what is in his heart, while he is the most hostile of adversaries" — inverted witness-seeking, both in this surah's Medinan run (verified).
