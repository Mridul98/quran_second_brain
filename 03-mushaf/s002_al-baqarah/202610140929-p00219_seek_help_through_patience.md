# Help through patience: the steadfast, the rites, and one God (2:153–163)
**UID:** 202610140929
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:153–163 (Ruku 19 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:153]` – `[2:163]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The community that now must fight is given its stamina kit — patience and prayer (the 2:45 formula re-issued almost verbatim), the slain are alive though you do not perceive, and life will be tested by subtraction (fear, hunger, loss of wealth, lives, fruits) — with the calamity-phrase "To Allah we belong" as the response-code; then the Safa-and-Marwa rites are legalized, the warning flipped hard at those who suppress proofs (curse of God, angels, and humanity — except repentance and *proclamation*), and the ruku lands on the surah's creed-anchor: Your God is one God, the Benevolent, the Compassionate.
* **Placement:** The endurance section before the sign-catalogue (2:164–166) and the food-law block (2:168+); the tawhid-clause of 2:163 is the theological hinge between the Abraham-section's community-creed and the cosmology ruku that opens ruku 20.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:153]`** "O you who believe! Seek help through patience and prayers. Allah is with the steadfast."
  * **Gloss:** The istīʿānah-formula of 2:45 re-issued to *the believers* (not Israel): same two instruments, and a new promise — divine company with the patient (*ṣābirīn*).
* **`[2:154]`** "And do not say of those who are killed in the cause of Allah, 'Dead.' Rather, they are alive, but you do not perceive."
  * **Gloss:** Vocabulary control over grief: the slain are not dead — a correction of ordinary speech itself, "but you do not perceive."
* **`[2:155]`** "We will certainly test you with some fear and hunger, and some loss of possessions and lives and crops. But give good news to the steadfast."
  * **Gloss:** The subtraction-test enumerated (five subtractions, all "some" — *min*); the warner becomes the good-news-bringer mid-clause.
* **`[2:156]`** "Those who, when a calamity afflicts them, say, 'To Allah we belong, and to Him we will return.'"
  * **Gloss:** The response-code — ownership and return in one line, the theology of 2:28 compressed into liturgy; the istirjāʿ phrase.
* **`[2:157]`** "Upon these are blessings and mercy from their Lord. These are the guided ones."
  * **Gloss:** Double return (blessings *and* mercy) and the verdict: *hādūn* — the guided, back to 2:5's "upon guidance."
* **`[2:158]`** "Safa and Marwa are among the rites of Allah. Whoever makes the Pilgrimage to the House, or performs the Umrah, commits no error by circulating between them. Whoever volunteers good—Allah is Appreciative and Cognizant."
  * **Gloss:** Two named hills, classified as *shaʿāʾir* (rites/symbols of Allah); permissive phrasing ("commits no error") — the classical reports explain the discomfort that needed the permission (see Context); the close elevates voluntary good: God is *Shakūr* (Appreciative) — His gratitude-grammar answers human *shukr* (2:152).
* **`[2:159]`** "Those who suppress the proofs and the guidance We have revealed, after We have clarified them to humanity in the Scripture— those—Allah curses them, and the cursers curse them."
  * **Gloss:** The concealment-chain (2:42, 2:76, 2:140) reaches its verdict: curse from God *and* "the cursers" — creation as co-lausitor; the suppressed content is specifically "the proofs and *the guidance*."
* **`[2:160]`** "Except those who repent, and reform, and proclaim. Those—I will accept their repentance. I am the Acceptor of Repentance, the Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The three Rs of repair — repent, reform, *proclaim* (the crime was suppression; the cure is publication) — and the pronoun shifts to first person: "I will accept" (Source: observable pronoun-shift; classical authors note it, Ibn Kathir/Tanwīr al-Adhbān-style remarks — label as consensus observation).
* **`[2:161]`** "But as for those who reject faith, and die rejecting—those—upon them is the curse of Allah, and of the angels, and of all humanity."
  * **Gloss:** The mirror clause: the suppressors' unrepentant end — the curse now specifies its court: God, angels, all people.
* **`[2:162]`** "They will remain under it forever, and the torment will not be lightened for them, and they will not be reprieved."
  * **Gloss:** Three closures: permanence, no mitigation, no respite — the same triple denial as 2:86.
* **`[2:163]`** "Your God is one God. There is no god but He, the Benevolent, the Compassionate."
  * **Gloss:** The anchor clause: from "your God" (relation) to *ilāhun wāḥid* (doctrine) to the negation-and-affirmation ("no god but He") to the two mercy-names — the whole tawhid of the surah in one line, placed right where the sign-list begins.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ṣabr`** (patience): 2:153; "the steadfast" (*ṣābirīn*) 2:153, 2:155, 2:157.
  * **Rendering:** "patience" (the act) / "the steadfast" (the people) — one root, two English registers.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:45]` (near-verbatim command doublet); `[2:249]` "Allah is with the steadfast" — the same promise-clause in Saul's story; `[2:250]` "Our Lord, pour down patience on us" (Source: observable; refs verified via full-file read; the Knowledge note's anchors [2:45]/[2:153]/[2:155–157] are this ruku's lines).
* **`shaʿāʾir`** (rites/symbols): 2:158 "among the rites of Allah."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:128]` "show us our rites"; `[2:200]` "When you have completed your rites" — the Abrahamic request → the codified symbols (Source: observable chain; the Arabic identity across sites agent inference — verify).
* **`ḥajj / ʿumrah`**: 2:158 "makes the Pilgrimage... or performs the Umrah."
  * **Rendering:** drift within the surah: *ḥajj* is translated as "Pilgrimage" here (2:158) but transliterated as "Hajj" at `[2:196]` ("carry out the Hajj and the Umrah"); *ʿumrah* stays transliterated both times — the permissive ayah is where the vocabulary is not yet domesticated (Source: observable; both refs verified).
* **`ilāhun wāḥid`** (one God): 2:163.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:133]` "One God; and to Him we submit" (the deathbed creed, earlier in this surah); `[4:171]` "Allah is only one God" — the same phrase used against trinitarian speech (Source: refs verified; 4:171 via corpus).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the passage presumes armed conflict (the slain of 2:154, the fear-and-loss list of 2:155) — classical placement around Badr/Uḥud is transmitted, not in-text (Source: named classical references, Ibn Kathir on 2:154 — keep background).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** reports on 2:158 explain the companions' reluctance to circle Safa and Marwa because the hills bore pre-Islamic idols; the verse's "commits no error" is read as permission clearing that scruple (Source: named classical references, e.g., reports cited by Ibn Kathir/al-Tabari on 2:158 — the detail is background; the verse itself gives no cause).
