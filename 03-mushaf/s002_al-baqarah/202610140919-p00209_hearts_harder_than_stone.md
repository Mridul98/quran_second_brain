# A murdered man revived, and hearts harder than rock (2:72–82)
**UID:** 202610140919
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:72–82 (Ruku 9 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:72]` – `[2:82]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The heifer's purpose is revealed — a disputed homicide solved by striking the dead man with a piece of the cow — and the miracle that should soften produces the surah's starkest image: hearts like rock, or harder, since rocks gush, split, and tumble in awe while they do not; the ruku then indicts their Word-handling — deliberate distortion, hearsay-spirituality, forged scripture sold for a price, and a borrowed promise of a short stay in the Fire — before the two-eternity verdict: besieged by sins, abiding in Fire, versus believers abiding in Paradise.
* **Placement:** Second half of the heifer episode (ruku 8 asked the questions; ruku 9 shows the disease the questions served); the hardening motif here sets up the repeated "Allah is not unaware of what you do" refrain that punctuates the Israel-section.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:72]`** "And recall when you killed a person, and disputed in the matter; but Allah was to expose what you were hiding."
  * **Gloss:** The unsolved murder and the dispute; God's intent is *exposure* — the miracle is forensic.
* **`[2:73]`** "We said, 'Strike him with part of it.' Thus Allah brings the dead to life; and He shows you His signs, that you may understand."
  * **Gloss:** The strike with a piece of the heifer; the aside generalizes: resurrection-signs exist "that you may understand" — the same purpose-clause as the manna-scene's gratitude goals.
* **`[2:74]`** (clause-chunk a) "Then after that your hearts hardened. They were as rocks, or even harder."
  * **Gloss:** The post-miracle verdict: hardening *because of* the sign.
* **`[2:74]`** (clause-chunk b) "For there are some rocks from which rivers gush out, and others that splinter and water comes out from them, and others that sink in awe of Allah. Allah is not unaware of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** Three rock-behaviors — gush, split, tumble — against one heart-behavior: nothing; the comparison degrades the stones' competitors, and the refrain "Allah is not unaware of what you do" starts here as the section's signature warning.
* **`[2:75]`** "Do you hope that they will believe in you, when some of them used to hear the Word of Allah, and then deliberately distort it, even after understanding it?"
  * **Gloss:** The Prophet is asked to stop hoping; the crime-list of the scholars: hearing → understanding → deliberate distortion (*y-uḥarrifūn*).
* **`[2:76]`** "And when they come across those who believe, they say, 'We believe,' but when they come together privately, they say, 'Will you inform them of what Allah has disclosed to you, so that they might dispute with you concerning it before your Lord?' Do you not understand?"
  * **Gloss:** The audience-switching structure of 2:14 reappears among the People of the Book — leaking covenant-credentials to be used back at them.
* **`[2:77]`** "Do they not know that Allah knows what they conceal and what they reveal?"
  * **Gloss:** The reveal/conceal pair of 2:33 answers 2:76: the private meeting is not private.
* **`[2:78]`** "And among them are uneducated who know the Scripture only through hearsay, and they only speculate."
  * **Gloss:** Class two: the *ummiyyūn* whose Scripture is second-hand assumption — two diseases in two classes (distorting scholars, speculating laity).
* **`[2:79]`** "So woe to those who write the Scripture with their own hands, and then say, 'This is from Allah,' that they may exchange it for a little price. Woe to them for what their hands have written, and woe to them for what they earn."
  * **Gloss:** Three "woes" (*wayl*) in one verse — writing, attributing, earning; the forgery charge answers the "small price" of 2:41.
* **`[2:80]`** "And they say, 'The Fire will not touch us except for a number of days.' Say, 'Have you received a promise from Allah—Allah never breaks His promise—or are you saying about Allah what you do not know?'"
  * **Gloss:** The short-stay doctrine; the rebuttal is contractual: either a produced *ʿahd* from God, or invented speech about Him.
* **`[2:81]`** "Indeed, whoever commits misdeeds, and becomes besieged by his iniquities—these are the inmates of the Fire, wherein they will dwell forever."
  * **Gloss:** The image: sin as siege (*aḥāṭat*) — encircled, not merely guilty; "inmates of the Fire" back from 2:39.
* **`[2:82]`** "As for those who believe and do righteous deeds—these are the inhabitants of Paradise, wherein they will dwell forever."
  * **Gloss:** The paired eternity-verse: "inmates of the Fire" vs "inhabitants of Paradise" — one English family rendered two ways for two destinations (Source: translator's term choice, observable; both Arabic phrases are the same *aṣḥāb* construction, agent inference — verify).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`taḥrīf`** (distortion): 2:75 "deliberately distort it."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:41]` "They distort words from their places"; `[18:1]` "allowed in it no distortion" — the Quran's self-description is the negative image of this crime (Source: refs verified via corpus).
* **`wayl`** (woe): 2:79 threefold.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:22]` "Woe to those whose hearts are hardened against the mention of Allah" — woe and hardened hearts paired again outside the surah; `[43:65]`, `[75:34–35]` (Source: refs verified via corpus).
* **`qaswa`** (hardness): 2:74 "your hearts hardened... as rocks, or even harder."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:22]` "hearts... hardened" — same diagnosis; note 2:74's solution-flip: rocks are *expected* to soften before God (Source: refs verified).
* **`ʿahd`** (promise): 2:80 "Have you received a promise from Allah."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:40]` "fulfill your pledge to Me, and I will fulfill My pledge to you" — same Arabic family, Itani's "pledge" vs "promise"; the surah treats the covenant as exactly this kind of evidence-document, which is why 2:80's rebuttal is contractual: produce the ʿahd or admit invention (Source: observable rendering pair; Arabic identity agent inference — verify).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; 2:75's "hear the Word of Allah" is cited in the Kalam knowledge note's evidence — this is one of the few places the Quran speaks of *hearing* addressed speech and then *editing* it, making distortion a crime against a heard text (Source: observable; note link verified).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** classical reports on 2:79 name scriptural forgeries for economic advantage (rabbinic add-ons in rabbinic courts); on 2:80, the short-stay doctrine — "The Fire will not touch us except for a number of days" — reports vary in detail (Source: named classical references, Ibn Kathir/al-Tabari on 2:79–80; treat as transmitted background).
