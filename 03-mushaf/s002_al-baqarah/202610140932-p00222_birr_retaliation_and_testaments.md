# Righteousness is a person, not a compass — then the law of blood and of wills (2:177–182)
**UID:** 202610140932
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:177–182 (Ruku 22 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:177]` – `[2:182]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** After the food law, one sweeping verse redefines *birr* as the whole believing-and-giving person rather than prayer-direction — and the ruku then hands over three hard tests of that definition: lawful killing, honest wills, and reconciled estates.
* **Placement:** Pivot between the dietary block (ruku 21) and the worship-law cluster (fasting/Hajj, ruku 23–25); 2:177 is the surah's ethics hinge, and 2:178–182 begin concrete criminal and inheritance law. Source: structural reading of the ranges — agent inference.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:177]`** "Righteousness does not consist of turning your faces towards the East and the West. But righteous is he who believes in Allah, and the Last Day, and the angels, and the Scripture, and the prophets."
  * **Gloss:** Direction of prayer is demoted from the definition of virtue; the creed list (five objects) is the first half of the new definition.
* **`[2:177]`** "Who gives money, though dear, to near relatives, and orphans, and the needy, and the homeless, and the beggars, and for the freeing of slaves; those who perform the prayers, and pay the obligatory charity, and fulfill their promise when they promise, and patiently persevere in the face of persecution, hardship, and in the time of conflict."
  * **Gloss:** The second half — property, prayer, promise, patience — virtue is what costs something: money "though dear," steadfastness "in the time of conflict."
* **`[2:177]`** "These are the sincere; these are the pious."
  * **Gloss:** Verdict on the list: truthfulness (*sidq*) and piety (*taqwa*) land on doers, not orientees.
* **`[2:178]`** "O you who believe! Retaliation for the murdered is ordained upon you: the free for the free, the slave for the slave, the female for the female. But if he is forgiven by his kin, then grant any reasonable demand, and pay with good will. This is a concession from your Lord, and a mercy. But whoever commits aggression after that, a painful torment awaits him."
  * **Gloss:** Homicide law: equality in retaliation, but blood-money by the victim's kin's forgiveness is a mercy-valve; aggression after mercy forfeits it.
* **`[2:179]`** "There is life for you in retaliation, O people of understanding, so that you may refrain."
  * **Gloss:** The rationale: proportional, state-administered retribution ends vendetta — law as life-preserver.
* **`[2:180]`** "It is decreed for you: when death approaches one of you, and he leaves wealth, to make a testament in favor of the parents and the relatives, fairly and correctly—a duty upon the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** Deathbed bequests to parents and kin are made a duty — the word chosen for its bearers is again "the righteous" (muttaqin).
* **`[2:181]`** "But whoever changes it after he has heard it, the guilt is upon those who change it. Allah is All-Hearing, All-Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Tampering with a declared will transfers the sin to the changer; divine hearing/knowledge frames the threat.
* **`[2:182]`** "Should someone suspect bias or injustice on the part of a testator, and then reconciles between them, he commits no sin. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** A mediator who corrects suspected testator bias is blameless — equity over formalism; closes on forgiveness.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`birr`** (righteousness): comprehensive goodness; here rendered "Righteousness"/"righteous" in 2:177.
  * **Rendering:** "Righteousness does not consist..." — the same root family is "act righteously" (2:62), "do righteous deeds" (2:25).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:189]` — "It is not virtuous that you approach homes from their backs, but virtue is to be pious" — the *birr*-vs-ritual-form argument continues into the next ruku; `[2:197]` "the best provision is righteousness."
* **`the pious / muttaqin`**: rendered "the righteous" at 2:2 but "these are the pious" at 2:177 (and "a duty upon the righteous" at 2:180).
  * **Rendering:** drift noted — Itani alternates "righteous"/"pious"/"devout" for the taqwa family within one surah (Source: translator's term choice, observable in corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:2]`, `[2:194]`, `[2:212]` — the term threads from the surah's opening promise to its law codes.
* **`retaliation`** (qiṣāṣ): measured requital for killing.
  * **Rendering:** "Retaliation for the murdered is ordained upon you" (2:178); "life for you in retaliation" (2:179).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:194]` — "sacrilege calls for retaliation... retaliate against him in the same measure" — the same proportionality governs combat law in ruku 24; `[2:49]` Pharaoh's people "killing your sons" is the aggression this law replaces (Source: agent inference — verify).
* **`testament`** (waṣiyya): bequest at death's approach.
  * **Rendering:** "make a testament in favor of the parents and the relatives, fairly and correctly" (2:180).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:240]` — "a will shall provide their wives with support for a year" — the only other will-law in this surah, two ruku downstream (verified in corpus).
