# Divorce counted twice, boundaries named three times (2:229–231)
**UID:** 202610140939
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:229–231 (Ruku 29 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:229]` – `[2:231]` (3 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The divorce-count is capped at two revocable strikes, the wife's ransomed release is legitimated, and the whole economy of remarriage is fenced under one repeated title — "Allah’s limits" — so that ending a marriage becomes an act of law, not of caprice or mockery.
* **Placement:** The hinge of the family-law cascade (ruku 28–31): it converts 2:228's "degree over them" into a numbered procedure, and hands the mahr question forward to 2:236–237 (ruku 31). Short range; this note is correspondingly brief.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:229]`** "Divorce is allowed twice. Then, either honorable retention, or setting free kindly. It is not lawful for you to take back anything you have given them, unless they fear that they cannot maintain Allah's limits. If you fear that they cannot maintain Allah’s limits, then there is no blame on them if she sacrifices something for her release. These are Allah’s limits, so do not transgress them. Those who transgress Allah’s limits are the unjust."
  * **Gloss:** Two strikes, then binary outcome (keep honorably / release kindly); reclaiming gifts is banned except the wife-initiated ransom (khulʿ) when both fear the limits; the limits-phrases appear three times in one verse — the redundancy is the rhetoric.
* **`[2:230]`** "If he divorces her, she shall not be lawful for him again until she has married another husband. If the latter divorces her, then there is no blame on them for reuniting, provided they think they can maintain Allah's limits. These are Allah’s limits; He makes them clear to people who know."
  * **Gloss:** The third divorce is final in practice: the intervening-marriage rule exists to break the cycle of weaponized talaq; reunion now requires genuine, judged capacity to "maintain the limits."
* **`[2:231]`** "When you divorce women, and they have reached their term, either retain them amicably, or release them amicably. But do not retain them to hurt them and commit aggression. Whoever does that has wronged himself. And do not take Allah’s revelations for a joke. And remember Allah's favor to you, and that He revealed to you the Scripture and Wisdom to teach you. And fear Allah, and know that Allah is aware of everything."
  * **Gloss:** The term-deadline forbids hurtful return (waiting the wife out); the one-line command "do not take Allah's revelations for a joke" indicts play with the law itself; memory of the Book-as-teaching is the motive for taqwa.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`the limits of Allah`** (ḥudūd Allāh): the fence around permission.
  * **Rendering:** three times in 2:229, twice in 2:230; "do not transgress them."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:187]` — "These are the limits of Allah, so do not come near them" (fasting/marital window) — the same formula governs body-law here (verified).
* **`sacrifices something for her release`** (khulʿ): the wife's ransom of the mahr.
  * **Rendering:** "there is no blame on them if she sacrifices something for her release" (2:229).
  * **Elsewhere:** the mirror case at `[2:237]` — "give them half of what you specified—unless they forego the right" — both spouses' forfeiture-rights are legislated in the same family-law run (verified).
* **`for a joke`** (huzw): play/frivolity with revelation.
  * **Rendering:** "do not take Allah’s revelations for a joke" (2:231).
  * **Elsewhere:** the surah's earnestness-refrain — cf. `[2:204]`'s oath-abusers: speech-acts that treat the Name casually are the recurring crime (Source: agent inference from motif clustering — verify).
* **`Scripture and Wisdom`** (al-kitāb wa-al-ḥikma): the paired endowment.
  * **Rendering:** 2:231.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:129]` and `[2:151]` — Abraham's prayer "teach them the Book and wisdom" answered; `[2:269]` — "He gives wisdom to whomever He wills" (ruku 37) (verified).
