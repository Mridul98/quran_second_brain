# The Hajj's rites, the two petitions, and total surrender (2:197–210)
**UID:** 202610140935
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:197–210 (Ruku 25 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:197]` – `[2:210]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The pilgrimage is given its calendar, its speech-diet, and its memory-rhythms — then reduced to a test of which petition a person actually makes: this-world only, or good in both worlds — closing with the command to enter submission "wholeheartedly" before the clouds of the final Return.
* **Placement:** Second half of the Hajj block (with ruku 24) and the high point of the fasting→pilgrimage arc that began at 2:183; 2:208–210 then gathers the whole surah's legal momentum into one surrender-counsel before the historical narratives resume (2:243+). Source: structural reading — agent inference.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:197]`** "The Hajj is during specific months. Whoever decides to perform the Hajj—there shall be no sexual relations, nor misconduct, nor quarrelling during the Hajj. And whatever good you do, Allah knows it. And take provisions, but the best provision is righteousness. And be mindful of Me, O people of understanding."
  * **Gloss:** Ihram = a speech-and-conduct diet; the caravan proverb is re-cut: the best provision for the road is taqwa.
* **`[2:198]`** "You commit no error by seeking bounty from your Lord. When you disperse from Arafat, remember Allah at the Sacred Landmark. And remember Him as He has guided you. Although, before that, you were of those astray."
  * **Gloss:** Trade during Hajj is licensed; the stations (Arafat, the Sacred Landmark = Muzdalifa) are defined by remembrance, not by pre-Islamic standing-poetry.
* **`[2:199]`** "Then disperse from where the people disperse, and ask Allah for forgiveness. Allah is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** Conform to the community's flow; the prescribed utterance at the rite is istighfar.
* **`[2:200]`** "When you have completed your rites, remember Allah as you remember your parents, or even more. Among the people is he who says, “Our Lord, give us in this world,” yet he has no share in the Hereafter."
  * **Gloss:** Post-rite dhikr must out-rank ancestral pride; the first portrait: a prayer with no Hereafter clause.
* **`[2:201]`** "And among them is he who says, “Our Lord, give us goodness in this world, and goodness in the Hereafter, and protect us from the torment of the Fire.”"
  * **Gloss:** The second portrait: the balanced petition — the surah's model dua, both worlds plus firewall.
* **`[2:202]`** "These will have a share of what they have earned. Allah is swift in reckoning."
  * **Gloss:** Each petition gets its answer; the reckoning is fast, not delayed.
* **`[2:203]`** "And remember Allah during the designated days. But whoever hurries on in two days commits no wrong, and whoever stays on commits no wrong—provided he maintains righteousness. And obey Allah, and know that to Him you will be gathered."
  * **Gloss:** The days of tashriq: flexibility on timing, rigidity on taqwa; the "gathered" clause turns the rite toward the resurrection.
* **`[2:204]`** "Among the people is he whose speech about the worldly life impresses you, and he calls Allah to witness what is in his heart, while he is the most hostile of adversaries."
  * **Gloss:** Third portrait: the persuasive liar who recruits God as character-witness — oath as fraud.
* **`[2:205]`** "When he gains power, he strives to spread corruption on earth, destroying properties and lives. Allah does not like corruption."
  * **Gloss:** The speech of 2:204 cashes out as destruction; divine "not liking" is the moral verdict on corruption.
* **`[2:206]`** "And when he is told, “Beware of Allah,” his pride leads him to more sin. Hell is enough for him—a dreadful abode."
  * **Gloss:** The taqwa-reflex inverted: warning feeds his sin — the profile of the hypocrite of 2:11–14 in a business suit.
* **`[2:207]`** "And among the people is he who sells himself seeking Allah’s approval. Allah is kind towards the servants."
  * **Gloss:** Fourth portrait: the one who *sells himself* — the inversion of 2:90/2:102, where souls were sold at a loss.
* **`[2:208]`** "O you who believe! Enter into submission, wholeheartedly, and do not follow the footsteps of Satan; he is to you an outright enemy."
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's thesis-verse: no partial Islam; the same anti-Satan step-rule of 2:168, now aimed at the whole covenant.
* **`[2:209]`** "But if you slip after the proofs have come to you, know that Allah is Powerful and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** Slipping after clarity removes the excuse of ignorance — power and wisdom as the warning's two names.
* **`[2:210]`** "Are they waiting for Allah Himself to come to them in the shadows of the clouds, together with the angels, and thus the matter is settled? All things are returned to Allah."
  * **Gloss:** The final frame: what they defer to (Allah's coming in cloud-shadows) is exactly what the command warns against meeting unprepared; the verdict rests with God alone.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`provision`** (zād): road-provision for the journey.
  * **Rendering:** "take provisions, but the best provision is righteousness" (2:197).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:241]` — "divorced women shall be provided for" and `[2:212]` "Allah provides to whomever He wills without measure" — the same rizq/zad family runs from caravan to court to cosmology (verified in corpus).
* **`remember Allah`** (dhikr): ritual and heart remembrance.
  * **Rendering:** "remember Allah at the Sacred Landmark... remember Allah as you remember your parents... remember Allah during the designated days" (2:198–203 — three times in one ruku).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:152]` — "So remember Me, and I will remember you" — the reciprocity that makes dhikr effective; `[2:239]` — "remember Allah" in battlefield prayer (verified).
* **`enter into submission`** (adkhulū fī al-silm): whole-entry into Islam/surrender.
  * **Rendering:** "Enter into submission, wholeheartedly" (2:208).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:131]` — Abraham's "I have submitted to the Lord of the Worlds" and `[2:136]` "to Him we surrender" — the command re-issues Abraham's Islam, the faith of the shrine-builders two ruku before the qibla block (verified).
* **`sells himself`** (yabtaʿī nafsahu): self-sale for divine pleasure.
  * **Rendering:** "he who sells himself seeking Allah’s approval" (2:207).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:90]` and `[2:102]` — "Miserable is what they sold their souls for" — the trade-idiom appears three times in the surah, twice as loss, once as profit (verified).

## 6. Context Notes
