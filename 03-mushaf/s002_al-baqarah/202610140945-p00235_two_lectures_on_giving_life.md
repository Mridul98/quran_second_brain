# The confounded king, the century-sleeper, and the birds of Abraham's heart (2:258–260)
**UID:** 202610140945
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:258–260 (Ruku 35 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:258]` – `[2:260]` (3 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Straight after the Throne verse, three demonstrations turn its creed into argument: a king who claims the monopoly of life-and-death is checkmated by the sun, a skeptic who doubts a ruin's revival is lived through a century of it, and Abraham — who already believes — asks for his heart's relief and is given a hands-on resurrection lesson.
* **Placement:** The creedal application block (ruku 34–35) bridging legislation and the charity-economy (ruku 36–38): doubt is answered not with law but with evidence, then the surah turns to money.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:258]`** "Have you not considered him who argued with Abraham about his Lord, because Allah had given him sovereignty? Abraham said, “My Lord is He who gives life and causes death.” He said, “I give life and cause death.” Abraham said, “Allah brings the sun from the East, so bring it from the West,” so the blasphemer was confounded. Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people."
  * **Gloss:** The king's claim dies in one exchange: sparing-and-killing is a legal trick, but the sun is a load-bearing miracle — the argument migrates from politics to cosmology, and the verse's own verdict ("Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people") states the guidance-doctrine in miniature.
* **`[2:259]`** "Or like him who passed by a town collapsed on its foundations. He said, “How can Allah revive this after its demise?” Thereupon Allah caused him to die for a hundred years, and then resurrected him. He said, “For how long have you tarried?” He said, “I have tarried for a day, or part of a day.”"
  * **Gloss:** A single doubter is drafted into his own experiment; the day/century confusion measures what death feels like from the inside — nothing.
* **`[2:259]`** "He said, “No. You have tarried for a hundred years. Now look at your food and your drink—it has not spoiled—and look at your donkey. We will make you a wonder for mankind. And look at the bones, how We arrange them, and then clothe them with flesh.” So when it became clear to him, he said, “I know that Allah has power over all things.”"
  * **Gloss:** Three exhibits: unspoiled food (time stopped for provision), the donkey (decay left visible), re-knit bones (anatomy reassembled) — and the doubter's line becomes the reader's confession: "power over all things."
* **`[2:260]`** "And when Abraham said, “My Lord, show me how You give life to the dead.” He said, “Have you not believed?” He said, “Yes, but to put my heart at ease.” He said, “Take four birds, and incline them to yourself, then place a part on each hill, then call to them; and they will come rushing to you. And know that Allah is Powerful and Wise.”"
  * **Gloss:** The surah's most honest petition: faith seeking serenity, not proof; the hands-on liturgy of birds teaches the body what the mind already granted — and "Powerful and Wise" closes as the formula that makes miracle and method one act.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`gives life and causes death`** (yuhyī wa yumīt): God's exclusive pair of acts.
  * **Rendering:** 2:258 as Abraham's definition; 2:259 dramatized; 2:260 as Abraham's request ("show me how You give life to the dead").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:28]` — "you were dead and He gave you life, then He will put you to death, then He will bring you to life"; `[2:164]` — "revives the earth with it after it had died" (rain); `[2:243]` — "Die." Then He revived them — the same predicate spans creed, rain, and the fleeing multitude (verified).
* **`power over all things`** (qadīr ʿalā kull shayʾ): the omnipotence formula.
  * **Rendering:** "I know that Allah has power over all things" (2:259); "Allah is Powerful and Wise" (2:260).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:20]` "Allah is capable of everything"; `[2:109]` "Allah has power over all things"; `[2:148]` "capable of everything"; `[2:284]` "Allah is Able to do all things" — the formula is the surah's recurring ceiling-stone (verified).
* **`confounded`** (bukʿa): the argument-ender.
  * **Rendering:** "the blasphemer was confounded" (2:258).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:15]` — hypocrites "left them bewildered in their transgression" — the same root of cognitive collapse reserved for those whose claims exceed the cosmos (verified).
* **`heart at ease`** (taʾannā? — Itani: "to put my heart at ease"): faith's need for felt certainty.
  * **Rendering:** "Yes, but to put my heart at ease" (2:260) — unique in this surah (verified via rg).
  * **Elsewhere:** contrast `[2:55]` — "we will not believe in you unless we see Allah plainly": demand-as-condition vs. request-as-cure, the surah's two epistemologies of seeing (verified).

## 6. Context Notes
