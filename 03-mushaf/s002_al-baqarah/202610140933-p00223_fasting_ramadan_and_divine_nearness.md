# Fasting, the month of the Quran, and a God who is near (2:183–188)
**UID:** 202610140933
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:183–188 (Ruku 23 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:183]` – `[2:188]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Fasting is legislated as a stamina-school for righteousness, bracketed by two mercy-notes — "Allah desires ease for you" and "I Am near" — and the ruku closes by policing the other appetite: no consuming one another's wealth.
* **Placement:** First act of the worship-law cluster (fasting here; Hajj in ruku 24–25), immediately after the *birr* definition; the same surah that broadened virtue now narrows the body's windows.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:183]`** "O you who believe! Fasting is prescribed for you, as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may become righteous."
  * **Gloss:** Fasting is addressed to the believing community, legitimated by precedent ("as it was prescribed for those before you"), and given a taqwa-purpose, not an ascetic one.
* **`[2:184]`** "For a specified number of days. But whoever among you is sick, or on a journey, then a number of other days. For those who are able: a ransom of feeding a needy person. But whoever volunteers goodness, it is better for him. But to fast is best for you, if you only knew."
  * **Gloss:** Concessions first (sick, traveler), then the fidya option of feeding a needy person — and the ranked preference: voluntary extra goodness is better, fasting is best.
* **`[2:185]`** "Ramadan is the month in which the Quran was revealed. Guidance for humanity, and clear portents of guidance, and the Criterion. Whoever of you witnesses the month, shall fast it. But whoever is sick, or on a journey, then a number of other days. Allah desires ease for you, and does not desire hardship for you, that you may complete the number, and celebrate Allah for having guided you, so that you may be thankful."
  * **Gloss:** The month is named once, for what was revealed in it; the sick/traveler ruling repeats (corpus-observable emphasis); the theology sentence — "Allah desires ease for you" — converts legal relief into gratitude.
* **`[2:186]`** "And when My servants ask you about Me, I Am near; I answer the call of the caller when he calls on Me. So let them answer Me, and have faith in Me, that they may be rightly guided."
  * **Gloss:** A first-person nearness-verse dropped between fasting rulings: no intermediary is needed; the sequence itself teaches that the Lawgiver is reachable.
* **`[2:187]`** "Permitted for you is intercourse with your wives on the night of the fast. They are a garment for you, and you are a garment for them. Allah knows that you used to betray yourselves, but He turned to you and pardoned you. So approach them now, and seek what Allah has ordained for you, and eat and drink until the white streak of dawn can be distinguished from the black streak. Then complete the fast until nightfall. But do not approach them while you are in retreat at the mosques. These are the limits of Allah, so do not come near them."
  * **Gloss:** The fast's night is restored to marital life ("garment" — intimacy as covering, not transgression); the dawn/nightfall boundaries are named "the limits of Allah"; retreat (iʿtikaf) suspends the permission.
* **`[2:187]`** "Allah thus clarifies His revelations to the people, that they may attain piety."
  * **Gloss:** Closing refrain: clarification of detail is itself a mercy aimed at taqwa.
* **`[2:188]`** "And do not consume one another’s wealth by unjust means, nor offer it as bribes to the officials in order to consume part of other people’s wealth illicitly, while you know."
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's second appetite-law: no swallowing wealth through false claims or judge-bribes — the "consume" verb deliberately rhymes with carrion and usury.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fasting`** (ṣiyām): abstention as worship.
  * **Rendering:** "Fasting is prescribed for you" (2:183); "the night of the fast" (2:187); "redemption of fasting" (2:196).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:196]` — three days during Hajj, seven on return: the only other fasting in this surah, used as expiation — verified in corpus.
* **`Ramadan`**: the month-name appears exactly once in the surah.
  * **Rendering:** "Ramadan is the month in which the Quran was revealed" (2:185); elsewhere the month is "a specified number of days" (2:184).
  * **Elsewhere:** none in s002 (verified via rg — the name is a single-occurrence anchor in this corpus file).
* **`the Criterion`** (al-furqān): revelation as separator of true from false.
  * **Rendering:** "Guidance for humanity, and clear portents of guidance, and the Criterion" (2:185).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:53]` — "We gave Moses the Scripture and the Criterion, so that you may be guided" — Quran's self-description borrows the Mosaic pair, verified.
* **`the limits of Allah`** (ḥudūd): bounded permissions.
  * **Rendering:** "These are the limits of Allah, so do not come near them" (2:187).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:229]`, `[2:230]` — the phrase migrates into divorce law in ruku 29 ("These are Allah’s limits, so do not transgress them") — verified.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within surah:** `[2:2]`, `[2:5]` — "a guide for the righteous" answered: fasting's stated goal is "that you may become righteous" (2:183), and 2:185 makes the Book the means of that guidance.
* **Within surah:** `[2:174]`, `[2:275]` — the "consume/swallow" motif: bribe-takers "consume part of other people’s wealth" (2:188) beside concealers who "swallow nothing but fire" (2:174) and usury-eaters who "swallow usury" (2:275) — wealth-sins are eaten, not merely taken.
* **Within surah:** `[2:45]`, `[2:153]` — patience-and-prayer as stamina; fasting is that stamina scheduled.
* **Structural note:** 2:186 interrupts legal detail with a nearness-promise; classical readers take the placement as the answer to the supplicant's silent question during worship (Source: widely repeated observation in classical tafsir ad loc. — verify).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the "those before you" of 2:183 explicitly locates fasting in a prior revelatory lineage — an intra-text claim, observable in corpus.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** reports about pre-Islamic men abstaining through the night until touching one another was re-licensed, and about judges taking bribes, are standard in the tafsir literature (Source: consensus-type narrative reports, e.g., al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on 2:187–188 — specific wording not re-verified here).
