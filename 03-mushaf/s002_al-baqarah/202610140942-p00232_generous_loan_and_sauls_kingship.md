# Revived thousands, a generous loan, and a king nobody chose (2:243–248)
**UID:** 202610140942
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:243–248 (Ruku 32 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:243]` – `[2:248]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The law section yields to narrative: a nameless multitude that fled death and was commanded to die — then revived — frames the two real questions the community now faces (fight or not, fund or not), and Israel's own request for a king shows what asking-for-what-you-refuse-to-do costs.
* **Placement:** Opens the historical-test sequence (ruku 32–33, Saul→David) after the family-law close (2:242); 2:245's "loan to Allah" is the hinge into the charity-economy of ruku 36–38.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:243]`** "Have you not considered those who fled their homes, by the thousands, fearful of death? Allah said to them, “Die.” Then He revived them. Allah is Gracious towards the people, but most people are not appreciative."
  * **Gloss:** The anonymous epidemic-fleeing multitude: flight from death is answered by command to die and then revival — God's grace (fadl) meets human ingratitude (kufr); the pattern preaches itself: you cannot outrun what only God holds.
* **`[2:244]`** "Fight in the cause of Allah, and know that Allah is Hearing and Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** The parable lands as one imperative sentence — those who fled must now stand; divine hearing/knowledge means motives will be known at the line.
* **`[2:245]`** "Who is he who will offer Allah a generous loan, so He will multiply it for him manifold? Allah receives and amplifies, and to Him you will be returned."
  * **Gloss:** The war's economics: a "generous loan" to God, repaid manifold; the same God who straitens and enlarges provision holds the return — battlefield and marketplace are one trust-account.
* **`[2:246]`** "Have you not considered the notables of the Children of Israel after Moses? When they said to a prophet of theirs, “Appoint a king for us, and we will fight in the cause of Allah.” He said, “Is it possible that, if fighting was ordained for you, you would not fight?” They said, “Why would we not fight in the cause of Allah, when we were driven out of our homes, along with our children?” But when fighting was ordained for them, they turned away, except for a few of them. But Allah is aware of the wrongdoers."
  * **Gloss:** The request-with-strings: the notables volunteer to fight — then default; the "except for a few" clause is identical in form to their covenant failures at 2:64/2:83, and exile is named as their own history ("driven out of our homes").
* **`[2:247]`** "Their prophet said to them, “Allah has appointed Saul to be your king.” They said, “How can he have authority over us, when we are more worthy of authority than he, and he was not given plenty of wealth?” He said, “Allah has chosen him over you, and has increased him in knowledge and stature.” Allah bestows His sovereignty upon whomever He wills. Allah is Embracing and Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Criteria contest: the people score leadership by lineage and wealth; God's counter-list is knowledge and bodily presence; sovereignty is a divine bestowment, not an inheritance claim.
* **`[2:248]`** "And their prophet said to them, “The proof of his kingship is that the Ark will be restored to you, bringing tranquility from your Lord, and relics left by the family of Moses and the family of Aaron. It will be carried by the angels. In that is a sign for you, if you are believers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The sign: the lost Ark returns — carried by angels, holding relics of the Mosaic houses — kingship legitimated not by the crowd's criteria but by recovered sacred history.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`generous loan`** (qaḍr ḥasan): lending to God.
  * **Rendering:** "Who is he who will offer Allah a generous loan, so He will multiply it for him manifold?" (2:245).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:261]` — "The parable of those who spend their wealth in Allah’s way... Allah multiplies for whom He wills" (ruku 36) — the loan-metaphor's payoff arrives in the charity-block (verified); `[2:245]` itself: "Allah receives and amplifies" — the ledger of qabd/bast.
* **`not appreciative`** (akthar al-nas la yashkurūn): ingratitude as the majority condition.
  * **Rendering:** "most people are not appreciative" (2:243).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:56]` "so that you may be appreciative"; `[2:152]` "And thank Me, and do not be ungrateful"; `[2:276]` "Allah does not love any sinful ingrate" — the shukr-family threads from revived multitude to usury warning (verified).
* **`sovereignty / authority`** (mulk/sulṭān): who rules, and by whose gift.
  * **Rendering:** "How can he have authority over us" / "Allah bestows His sovereignty upon whomever He wills" (2:247).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:107]` — "to Allah belongs the sovereignty of the heavens and the earth" — the same word two ruku before the narrative, now distributed by divine will; `[2:251]` — David "sovereignty and wisdom" (verified).
* **`turned away`** (tawallaw): the covenant-default verb.
  * **Rendering:** "they turned away, except for a few of them" (2:246).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:83]` "Then you turned away, except for a few of you"; `[2:64]` "But after that you turned away" — the formula indicts three generations of the same people (verified).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within surah:** `[2:28]` — "you were dead and He gave you life, then He will put you to death, then He will bring you to life" — 2:243 is that creedal claim dramatized as story; the life-death-revival motif then recurs at `[2:259]` (the ruined town, ruku 35) — one argument told three times (verified).
* **Within surah:** `[2:246]` ↔ `[2:216]` — "Fighting is ordained for you, even though you dislike it": the surah states the rule, then shows Israel's dislike-with-capitulation as the cautionary case; 2:190–193's "if they cease" ethic is the foil.
* **Within surah:** `[2:249–251]` (next ruku) — Saul's test at the river and David's stone complete the kingship sign; read 2:246–253 as one narrative unit.
* **Ark relics:** `[2:48]/[2:123]` — the Day when no soul avails another stands against the claim that sacred objects (the Ark, the family relics) can be borrowed authority; here the Ark is God-given "tranquility," not magic — distinction is agent inference, verify.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the anonymous fleeing multitude and the unnamed "prophet" and Saul (Talut) are left unnamed in the text itself — the story is offered as pattern, not chronicle (Source: observable in corpus).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** identification of the fleeing people (plague-stricken town of Israel, per popular reports) and of the prophet (Samuel) comes from tafsir and transmitted histories, not the verse — standard identifications (Source: classical tafsir on 2:243, 2:246 — verify).
