# The Gabriel question, and the Babylon test (2:97–103)
**UID:** 202610140922
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:97–103 (Ruku 12 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:97]` – `[2:103]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The dispute moves to the delivery-channel: Gabriel is named as the one who drops this Book into the Prophet's heart by Allah's leave — so hostility to him is hostility to the whole unseen supply chain, and Allah answers enmity with enmity; then the surah flings open its strangest file: the devils' teaching under Solomon, the Babylonian test of Harut and Marut, witchcraft that harms the buyer — every rejection of the clear signs is a soul sold, and the counterfactual hangs at the end: belief and taqwa would have paid better.
* **Placement:** Turns the Israel-indictment from content-rejection to channel-rejection (they will accept a book, not its courier); 2:102 is the surah's only magic-narrative and its rare named-individual angel-pair — the oddity is deliberate: a people picky about revelation's mechanics ends up with a verse on occult mechanics.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:97]`** "Say, 'Whoever is hostile to Gabriel—it is he who revealed it to your heart by Allah’s leave, confirming what preceded it, and guidance and good news for the believers.'"
  * **Gloss:** The courier defended with his résumé: to the heart (channel), by Allah's leave (authorization), confirming (credential), guidance + good news (function).
* **`[2:98]`** "Whoever is hostile to Allah, and His angels, and His messengers, and Gabriel, and Michael—Allah is hostile to the faithless."
  * **Gloss:** The list expands to the whole unseen administration; the only named pair in the Quran is *these* two angels — and hostility to them is tried as hostility to God.
* **`[2:99]`** "We have revealed to you clear signs, and none rejects them except the sinners."
  * **Gloss:** Clarity re-asserted (*āyāt mubayyināt*); rejection re-classified as rebellion, not difficulty.
* **`[2:100]`** "Is it not that whenever they make a covenant, some of them toss it aside? In fact, most of them do not believe."
  * **Gloss:** The covenant-tossing habit (→ 2:27) — proof that the problem is contract-loyalty, not evidence.
* **`[2:101]`** "And when there came to them a messenger from Allah, confirming what they had, a faction of those who were given the Book threw the Book of Allah behind their backs, as if they do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The credential scene again (→ 2:41, 2:89) — and the absurd posture: holders of the Book acting as if they did not have it.
* **`[2:102]`** (clause-chunk a) "And they followed what the devils taught during the reign of Solomon. It was not Solomon who disbelieved, but it was the devils who disbelieved. They taught the people witchcraft and what was revealed in Babylon to the two angels Harut and Marut."
  * **Gloss:** Solomon's name cleared of disbelief — the counter-charge (Solomon as magician) is answered by relocating the teaching to devils and a Babylonian disclosure.
* **`[2:102]`** (clause-chunk b) "They did not teach anybody until they had said, 'We are a test, so do not lose faith.' But they learned from them the means to cause separation between man and his wife. But they cannot harm anyone except with Allah's permission."
  * **Gloss:** The disclosure carries its own warning label ("We are a test"); the abuse-case named (marital splitting); the limits clause — no harm without permission, the surah's recurring sovereignty-valve.
* **`[2:102]`** (clause-chunk c) "And they learned what would harm them and not benefit them. Yet they knew that whoever deals in it will have no share in the Hereafter. Miserable is what they sold their souls for, if they only knew."
  * **Gloss:** The buyer's ledger: harm, no benefit, no Hereafter-share — and the 2:90 formula "Miserable is what they sold their souls for" lands verbatim a second time.
* **`[2:103]`** "Had they believed and been righteous, the reward from Allah would have been better, if they only knew."
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual close: the better-deal formula ("would have been better" — khair — the same trade-grammar as the hypocrites' ruined bargain of 2:16).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Jibrīl / Mīkāʾīl`** (Gabriel / Michael): 2:97–98.
  * **Rendering:** transliterated in full ("Gabriel", "Michael").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[66:4]` "Allah is his Ally, as is Gabriel, and the righteous believers" — Gabriel's only other appearance by name in the corpus; Michael appears *only* at 2:98 (Source: refs verified via corpus; Michael-unique claim confirmed by full-corpus rg for "Michael").
* **`siḥr`** (witchcraft/sorcery): 2:102 "They taught the people witchcraft."
  * **Rendering:** "witchcraft" here; the same root renders "sorcery" in the Moses-contest scenes (`[10:77]` "Is this sorcery? Sorcerers do not succeed"; `[5:110]` "This is nothing but obvious sorcery"; `[27:13]` "This is obvious witchcraft"). Itani splits the root across "witchcraft/sorcery" depending on scene — observable drift worth watching (Source: refs verified via corpus).
* **`nuzira`** ("a test"): 2:102 "We are a test, so do not lose faith."
  * **Rendering:** "test" — same idea-word as the thunderbolt/trial of 2:49 (*balāʾ*); the angels' disclosure is itself a fitna-machine (Source: agent inference — verify Arabic identity of the two words).
* **`mithāq`** (covenant): 2:100 "whenever they make a covenant, some of them toss it aside."
  * **Elsewhere:** → full chain already traced: `[2:27]` violation after confirmation; `[2:83]` the ten-clause text; `[2:93]` "We hear and disobey" (Source: observable).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the asbab-reports for 2:97 state that a Jewish delegation objected to Gabriel specifically (as the angel of punishment), favoring Michael — transmitted in Ibn Kathir/al-Tabari on 2:97 (Source: named classical references; background, not text).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** Harut and Marut is the most report-heavy verse in the surah; classical exegetes split on the details (whether the "two angels" are literally angels, and how much of the story is Isra'iliyyat), with Ibn Kathir citing the variants cautiously — **the note deliberately quotes only the verse's own clauses** (Source: named disagreement; treat all expansion as unverified).
