# The longest verse: write it, witness it, fear the One who teaches you (2:282–283)
**UID:** 202610140949
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:282–283 (Ruku 39 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:282]` – `[2:283]` (2 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Two verses — one of them the surah's longest single ayah — turn the usury-block's "neither wronging nor being wronged" into procedural law: record deferred obligations, appoint scribes and witnesses with their dignity protected, and when no scribe is found on the road, let pledged security and mutual trust carry the weight.
* **Placement:** The paperwork appendix to the charity-vs-usury sequence: 2:281 set the Day of full payment, and 2:282–283 build the earthly audit trail that matches it; a short ruku by verse-count but the largest verse-unit in the surah (2:282 is classically counted as the longest verse of the Quran — Source: consensus report — verify).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:282]`** "O you who believe! When you incur debt among yourselves for a certain period of time, write it down. And have a scribe write in your presence, in all fairness. And let no scribe refuse to write, as Allah has taught him. So let him write, and let the debtor dictate. And let him fear Allah, his Lord, and diminish nothing from it."
  * **Gloss:** The command: writing for deferred obligations; the office of scribe is a taught trust (skill as divine education) and the debtor's dictation puts the admission in the obligor's own mouth.
* **`[2:282]`** "But if the debtor is mentally deficient, or weak, or unable to dictate, then let his guardian dictate with honesty. And call to witness two men from among you. If two men are not available, then one man and two women whose testimony is acceptable to all—if one of them fails to remember, the other would remind her. Witnesses must not refuse when called upon."
  * **Gloss:** Capacity clauses: proxy-dictation for the unable; witness thresholds with an explicit fallback (two-women reminder mechanism is stated functionally — "if one fails to remember, the other would remind her"); witnesses are duty-bound not to refuse.
* **`[2:282]`** "And do not think it too trivial to write down, whether small or large, including the time of repayment. That is more equitable with Allah, and stronger as evidence, and more likely to prevent doubt—except in the case of a spot transaction between you—then there is no blame on you if you do not write it down."
  * **Gloss:** No size-threshold for records — trivialities included; the triple rationale (equity, evidence, doubt-prevention) names the verse's philosophy; hand-to-hand spot trade is exempted.
* **`[2:282]`** "And let there be witnesses whenever you conclude a contract, and let no harm be done to either scribe or witness. If you do that, it is corruption on your part. And fear Allah. Allah teaches you. Allah is aware of everything."
  * **Gloss:** Contract-witnessing as norm and scribe/witness non-harm as protection of the profession; the verse closes on God's teaching and awareness — the classroom and the audit He runs.
* **`[2:283]`** "If you are on a journey, and cannot find a scribe, then a security deposit should be handed over. But if you trust one another, let the trustee fulfill his trust, and let him fear Allah, his Lord. And do not conceal testimony. Whoever conceals it is sinner at heart. Allah is aware of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** Travel law: pledge replaces paper; and where neither exists, trust is the final contract — with two sanctions: fulfillment duty on the trustee, and heart-sin on the concealer of testimony.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`debt`** (dayn): deferred obligation.
  * **Rendering:** "When you incur debt among yourselves for a certain period of time" (2:282).
  * **Elsewhere:** single-verse topic in this surah (verified: "debt" appears only at 2:282); the surah's other deferred-obligation laws are widows' provision `[2:240]` and the marriage tie `[2:235]` — the writing-motif links them (verified wording at 235).
* **`scribe / write`** (kātib, kitāba): the taught pen.
  * **Rendering:** "have a scribe write in your presence, in all fairness... as Allah has taught him" (2:282).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:79]` — those who "write the Scripture with their own hands" and claim divine authorship: the same verb, forgery vs. fairness — the surah holds up a truthful scribe against false writers (verified); cf. `[2:239]`/`[2:151]` teaching formula.
* **`witness / testimony`** (shahāda): compulsory visibility of obligation.
  * **Rendering:** "call to witness two men from among you... do not conceal testimony" (2:282–283).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:84]` — Israel "agreed, and were all witnesses" to the covenant; `[2:143]` — "that you may be witnesses to humanity"; `[2:140]` — "conceals a testimony he has from Allah" — the witness-theme's covenant register feeds straight into commercial law (verified).
* **`trust`** (amāna): the trustee's load.
  * **Rendering:** "let the trustee fulfill his trust" (2:283).
  * **Elsewhere:** the trustee-word pairs here with the concealment-audit of `[2:228]` (hidden wombs) and `[2:284]` (hidden inner states) — three concealment-law verses, one theology of what is trusted into hearts (Source: thematic grouping, agent inference — verify).
