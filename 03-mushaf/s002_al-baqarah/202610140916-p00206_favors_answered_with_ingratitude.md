# The favor recital: nine gifts, each met with grumbling (2:47–59)
**UID:** 202610140916
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:47–59 (Ruku 6 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:47]` – `[2:59]` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** After the invitation (2:40–46) comes the evidence-file: the surah runs Israel's memory-track — favor over nations, the no-ransom Day, Pharaoh, the parted sea, the forty nights, the calf, the pardon, the Scripture, the demand to see God, the thunderbolt, the revival, cloud-shade and manna, the town-and-gate test — and the pattern that emerges is grace answered by substitution.
* **Placement:** The indictment half of the Israel-section; the "And recall that..." (*wa-idh*) anaphora here becomes the surah's dominant narrative engine through 2:61 (and reappears at 2:51-57 with nine iterations).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:47]`** "O Children of Israel! Remember My favor which I bestowed upon you, and I that favored you over all nations."
  * **Gloss:** Re-address with *niʿmah/favor* — note the second address-verb changes from 2:40's "blessings" to "favor" (Source: observable rendering; classical Arabic distinction between *niʿmah* and *minnah* is agent inference — verify).
* **`[2:48]`** "And beware of a Day when no soul will avail another in the least, nor will any intercession be accepted on its behalf, nor will any ransom be taken from it, nor will they be helped."
  * **Gloss:** The Day-clause with all four exclusions — intercession, ransom, help, availing — the surah's recurring eschatological fine print (reused almost verbatim at 2:123).
* **`[2:49]`** "And recall that We delivered you from the people of Pharaoh. They inflicted on you terrible persecution, killing your sons and sparing your women. Therein was a tremendous trial from your Lord."
  * **Gloss:** Rescue #1 with its cost listed; "trial" (*balāʾ*) frames even deliverance as a test.
* **`[2:50]`** "And recall that We parted the sea for you, so We saved you, and We drowned the people of Pharaoh as you looked on."
  * **Gloss:** Rescue #2 — witnessed deliverance; "as you looked on" makes belief here un-arguable.
* **`[2:51]`** "And recall that We appointed for Moses forty nights. Then you took to worshiping the calf after him, and you turned wicked."
  * **Gloss:** The appointment (*wāʿadnā*) and the breach inside the absence-window — the calf is the first named idol of the surah.
* **`[2:52]`** "Then We pardoned you after that, so that you might be grateful."
  * **Gloss:** Pardon #1; the purpose-clause "so that you might be grateful" (*laʿallakum tashkurūn*) — the recital's recurring hoped-for response.
* **`[2:53]`** "And recall that We gave Moses the Scripture and the Criterion, so that you may be guided."
  * **Gloss:** Gift #1 (dual: Kitāb + Furqān) given for guidance — the Book as separator-sword.
* **`[2:54]`** "And recall that Moses said to his people, 'O my people, you have done wrong to yourselves by worshiping the calf. So repent to your Maker, and kill your egos. That would be better for you with your Maker.' So He turned to you in repentance. He is the Accepter of Repentance, the Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The penitential procedure spelled out: repent, "kill your egos" (*taḥbū*) — the classical reading takes this as the severe self-reckoning that then ended in divine relenting (Source: consensus of tafsir, Ibn Kathir on 2:54; the "egos" rendering is Itani's).
* **`[2:55]`** "And recall that you said, 'O Moses, we will not believe in you unless we see Allah plainly.' Thereupon the thunderbolt struck you, as you looked on."
  * **Gloss:** The demand for vision — answered by a striking; "as you looked on" repeats 2:50's phrase, irony intact.
* **`[2:56]`** "Then We revived you after your death, so that you may be appreciative."
  * **Gloss:** Gift #2: revival from the thunderbolt; gratitude again the stated purpose.
* **`[2:57]`** "And We shaded you with clouds, and We sent down to you manna and quails: 'Eat of the good things We have provided for you.' They did not wrong Us, but they used to wrong their own souls."
  * **Gloss:** Gift #3, threefold (shade, manna, quail) — with the recital's key defense: heaven is never actually injured by ingratitude; the wrong-doer eats his own damage.
* **`[2:58]`** "And recall that We said, 'Enter this town, and eat plentifully from it whatever you wish; but enter the gate humbly, and say, 'Pardon.' We will forgive your sins, and give increase to the virtuous.'"
  * **Gloss:** The test-script: abundance with two conditions — posture ("humbly," *suǧǧadan*) and the word "Pardon" (*ḥiṭṭah*).
* **`[2:59]`** "But the wrongdoers among them substituted words other than those given to them, so We sent down on the wrongdoers a plague from heaven, because of their wicked behavior."
  * **Gloss:** The failed take: they *substituted* (baddala) the given words — the first "substitution" verdict, and the word plays against 2:61's food-substitution (Source: observable chain; the "wicked behavior" = the swapped word per classical reports, Ibn Kathir on 2:59).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ʿijl`** (calf): 2:51 "you took to worshiping the calf"; 2:54.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:92]` "you adopted the calf in his absence"; `[2:93]` "their hearts became filled with the love of the calf" — one idol named four times as the signature breach (Source: observable, refs in s002 verified).
* **`furqān`** (the Criterion): 2:53 "the Scripture and the Criterion."
  * **Rendering:** "the Criterion" — consistent across the corpus.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:185]` "clear portents of guidance, and the Criterion"; `[25:1]` "Blessed is He who sent down the Criterion upon His servant" (source-surah of the name); `[21:48]` "We gave Moses and Aaron the Criterion" (Source: refs verified via corpus).
* **`balāʾ`** (trial): 2:49 "a tremendous trial from your Lord."
  * **Rendering:** "trial" — the disaster *and* the deliverance both classified as test (Source: observable; the classification reading is agent inference — verify).
* **`baddala`** (substitute): 2:59 "substituted words other than those given to them."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:61]` "Would you substitute worse for better?"; `[2:41]` "do not exchange My revelations for a small price" — the exchange-verb family (baddala/sharā) is this surah's word for infidelity-with-content (Source: observable; refs verified).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the recital genre of "remembering" is Quranic speech to a people with a written memory (Source: classical tafsir convention on 2:47; the parallel command to Moses at `[14:5]` reads verbatim "remind them of the Days of Allah" — ref verified via corpus).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** "the town" of 2:58 is identified in classical reports variously as Jericho/Ayla; "Pardon" translates *ḥiṭṭah*, whose meaning is transmitted, not glossed in-text (Source: named disagreement in Ibn Kathir/al-Tabari on 2:58 — the reports differ).
