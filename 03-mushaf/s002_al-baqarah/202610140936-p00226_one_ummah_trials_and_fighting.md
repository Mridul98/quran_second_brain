# One community, its dispute, its trial, its fighting (2:211–216)
**UID:** 202610140936
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:211–216 (Ruku 26 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:211]` – `[2:216]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** After the Hajj block, the surah widens the lens: history (a once-unified humanity split by envy into disputed scriptures) explains why the present community must expect the same trial — poverty, persecution, and the disliked obligation of fighting — and answers the first spending-question in the same breath.
* **Placement:** Bridge from law (ruku 21–25) to the narrative-test sequence (Saul/Goliath, ruku 32–33); the "They ask you..." formula starts here and continues through ruku 27–28, marking the question-driven legislation of the Medinan community.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:211]`** "Ask the Children of Israel how many clear signs We have given them. Whoever alters the blessing of Allah after it has come to him—Allah is severe in retribution."
  * **Gloss:** The precedent nation is summoned as exhibit: signs given, blessing altered — the warning is addressed to the new community by proxy.
* **`[2:212]`** "Beautified is the life of this world for those who disbelieve, and they ridicule those who believe. But the righteous will be above them on the Day of Resurrection. Allah provides to whomever He wills without measure."
  * **Gloss:** Apparent prosperity is cosmetic; mockery of believers is expected; the reversal at the Resurrection is stated with the note that provision follows divine will, not desert-as-seen.
* **`[2:213]`** "Humanity used to be one community; then Allah sent the prophets, bringing good news and giving warnings. And He sent down with them the Scripture, with the truth, to judge between people regarding their differences. But none differed over it except those who were given it—after the proofs had come to them—out of mutual envy between them. Then Allah guided those who believed to the truth they had disputed, in accordance with His will. Allah guides whom He wills to a straight path."
  * **Gloss:** Philosophy of religious history: unity → prophecy → scripture-as-judge → division by envy among the scripture-carriers → guidance back to the disputed truth.
* **`[2:214]`** "Or do you expect to enter Paradise before the example of those who came before you had reached you? Adversity and hardship had afflicted them, and they were so shaken up, that the Messenger and those who believed with him said, “When is Allah’s victory?” Indeed, Allah’s victory is near."
  * **Gloss:** Entry-fee honesty: the prior prophets' pattern is pain-then-near-victory; even the Messenger asks "when?" — doubt is not apostasy.
* **`[2:215]`** "They ask you what they should give. Say, “Whatever charity you give is for the parents, and the relatives, and the orphans, and the poor, and the wayfarer. Whatever good you do, Allah is aware of it."
  * **Gloss:** First "They ask you": the object of spending is listed by need-proximity — family, kin, orphans, poor, traveler. (Corpus shows the closing quotation mark of this ayah is missing in the source file — anomaly noted.)
* **`[2:216]`** "Fighting is ordained for you, even though you dislike it. But it may be that you dislike something while it is good for you, and it may be that you like something while it is bad for you. Allah knows, and you do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The disliked obligation, with the epistemic disclaimer: good/bad is God's knowledge, not human preference.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`one community`** (ummah wāḥida): humanity's original single body.
  * **Rendering:** "Humanity used to be one community" (2:213).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:128]` Abraham's prayer for "a community submissive to You"; `[2:148]` "To every community is a direction towards it turns"; `[2:134]`/`[2:141]` "That was a community that has passed" — ummah in this surah is always a people-with-a-direction (verified).
* **`ordained / prescribed`** (kutiba): imposed obligation.
  * **Rendering:** "Fighting is ordained for you" (2:216).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:183]` "Fasting is prescribed for you"; `[2:178]` "Retaliation for the murdered is ordained upon you"; `[2:246]` "if fighting was ordained for you, you would not fight?" — the same passive verb binds the three Medinan obligations and then audits them (verified).
* **`charity you give`** (infāq): spending as worship.
  * **Rendering:** "Whatever charity you give is for the parents..." (2:215).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:195]` "spend in the cause of Allah"; `[2:254]` "Spend from what We have given you, before a Day"; `[2:272]` "Any charity you give will be repaid to you in full" — the giving-verb that opens here reaches its eschatological anchor in ruku 34/37.
* **`victory`** (naṣr): relief-longed-for.
  * **Rendering:** "When is Allah’s victory? Indeed, Allah’s victory is near." (2:214).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:250]` — the believing company's battlefield dua "support us against the faithless people" — the same naṣr-root prayed at Qiyas's rock-face (verified).
