# Speech etiquette, verse abrogation, and the monopoly on Paradise dissolved (2:104–112)
**UID:** 202610140923
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:104–112 (Ruku 13 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:104]` – `[2:112]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** House rules for the new community's *speech* with its Prophet (retire the ambiguous word, say words of respect, and listen), then the doctrine that no verse is cancelled but by a better or like one — sovereignty is Allah's alone, so stop interrogating the Messenger the way Moses was interrogated; and finally the exclusivity-claim ("None will enter Heaven unless he is a Jew or a Christian") is met not with a counter-claim but with a definition: whoever *submits* to Allah and does good — no fear, no grief.
* **Placement:** The address shifts from "Children of Israel" back to "O you who believe!" for the first time since the opening — the community is now being given its manners, its doctrine of revelation-management, and its self-definition by deed rather than label.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:104]`** "O you who believe! Do not say ambiguous words, but say words of respect, and listen. The disbelievers will have a painful torment."
  * **Gloss:** A speech-correction on a loaded term: the word *rāʿinā* ("attend to us") was exploitable in Hebrew-adjacent mockery, so the community is taught an unambiguous synonym (*unẓur nā*) — etiquette as creed-defense; the bare "and listen" caps it (Source: classical asbab on 2:104, Ibn Kathir/al-Tabari — the wordplay itself is reported background, not text).
* **`[2:105]`** "It is never the wish of the disbelievers from among the People of the Book, nor of the polytheists, that any good should be sent down to you from your Lord. But Allah chooses for His mercy whomever He wills. Allah is Possessor of Sublime Grace."
  * **Gloss:** Envy diagnosed across both rival camps; election re-stated as Allah's prerogative ("chooses... whomever He wills" — the same phrase-structure rejected in 2:90).
* **`[2:106]`** "We never nullify a verse, nor cause it to be forgotten, unless We bring one better than it, or similar to it. Do you not know that Allah is capable of all things?"
  * **Gloss:** The abrogation doctrine (*naskh*): no verse scrapped — either replaced by its better or its like; the ground for the surah's later legal revisions (fasting, retaliation, wills all reuse this frame).
* **`[2:107]`** "Do you not know that to Allah belongs the sovereignty of the heavens and the earth, and that apart from Allah you have no guardian or helper?"
  * **Gloss:** The twin-doctrine knock-on: sovereignty (*mulk*) makes abrogation lawful; the "no guardian or helper" formula (also 2:86, 2:120, 2:257) removes every appeal outside Him.
* **`[2:108]`** "Or do you want to question your Messenger as Moses was questioned before? Whoever exchanges faith for disbelief has strayed from the right path."
  * **Gloss:** The interrogation-ban with precedent-citation — *this surah's own heifer narrative (2:67–71) is the exhibit*; "exchanges faith for disbelief" re-opens the trade-metaphor; "right path" (*sawāʾ al-sabīl* construction in Itani's "right path" — note it is *not* the Fatihah's *ṣirāṭ* word) (Source: observable; Arabic-word identification agent inference — verify).
* **`[2:109]`** "Many of the People of the Book wish to turn you back into unbelievers after you have believed, out of envy on their part, after the Truth has become clear to them. But pardon and overlook, until Allah brings His command. Allah has power over all things."
  * **Gloss:** The strategic instruction: forgive now — with a placeholder for a later command ("until Allah brings His command"), which the classical readership of this verse treats as a genuinely open-ended pause (Source: consensus-of-reports caution in Ibn Kathir on 2:109 — not claimed to point at a specific later verse here).
* **`[2:110]`** "And perform the prayer, and give alms. Whatever good you forward for yourselves, you will find it with Allah. Allah is Seeing of everything you do."
  * **Gloss:** The two-anchor ritual clause plus the forward-deposit idea — *qaddama* again, the same verb as 2:95's "what their hands have forwarded": deeds are remittances ahead of the finding (Source: observable rendering pair).
* **`[2:111]`** "And they say, 'None will enter Heaven unless he is a Jew or a Christian.' These are their wishes. Say, 'Produce your proof, if you are truthful.'"
  * **Gloss:** The exclusivity ticket-claim, named as *wishes* (the same word as 2:94's death-wish dare); the demand for evidence (*burhān*) is rhetorical — none exists.
* **`[2:112]`** "In fact, whoever submits himself to Allah, and is a doer of good, will have his reward with his Lord—they have nothing to fear, nor shall they grieve."
  * **Gloss:** The answer is not a competing label but a definition: submission + ihsān; and the no-fear formula (2:38, 2:62) is granted to the submitted one, whoever they are (Source: the formula-chain is observable; its deployment as *rebuttal* is agent inference — verify).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`rāʿinā`** (the retired word): 2:104 "Do not say ambiguous words."
  * **Rendering:** Itani *translates the correction* ("ambiguous words" / "words of respect") instead of transliterating *rāʿinā* — an edition-level choice that hides the pun it legislates (Source: observable translation choice; the pun's existence is reported background, Ibn Kathir on 2:104).
* **`āyah` + `naskh`** (verse / nullification): 2:106 "We never nullify a verse, nor cause it to be forgotten, unless We bring one better than it, or similar to it."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:101]` "When We substitute a verse in place of another verse—and Allah knows best what He reveals—they say, 'You are an impostor'" — the other abrogation-verse, with the accusation recorded (Source: ref verified).
* **`sabīl`** (way/path): 2:108 "strayed from the right path."
  * **Rendering:** "right path" here; "path" at `[2:217]` ("bar others from Allah's path"), "way" at `[2:261]` ("spend their wealth in Allah's way"), "way" at `[2:273]` ("restrained in the way of Allah") — one Arabic word, three English registers in one surah (Source: observable, refs verified via full-file read; contrast *ṣirāṭ* at `[1:6]`).
* **`mulk`** (sovereignty): 2:107 "to Allah belongs the sovereignty of the heavens and the earth."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:247]` "Allah bestows His sovereignty upon whomever He wills" (the Saul episode — sovereignty dispensed); `[3:26]` "Say, 'O Allah, Owner of Sovereignty. You grant sovereignty to whom You will...'" (Source: refs verified via corpus — 3:26 wording quoted exactly).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; 2:104–112 is early-community manners material — speech with the Prophet, verse-revision doctrine, and posture toward the People of the Book's claims. The classical *naskh* literature builds most of its legal theory on 2:106; this note keeps the doctrine at verse-level (Source: named classical references; the field-mapping is standard in ʿUlūm al-Qurʾān works, e.g., al-Suyūṭī's treatment of 2:106 — verify exact citation before formal use).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** reports on 2:104's wordplay and on the 2:111 claim's Jewish/Christian speakers are transmitted background, not text (Source: named classical references, Ibn Kathir on 2:104/2:111).
