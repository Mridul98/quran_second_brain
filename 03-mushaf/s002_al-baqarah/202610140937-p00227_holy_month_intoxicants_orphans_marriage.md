# Four questions, four re-weightings: month, minds, money, marriage (2:217–221)
**UID:** 202610140937
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:217–221 (Ruku 27 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:217]` – `[2:221]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A string of community questions — fighting in the Holy Month, intoxicants and gambling, what to give, orphans, intermarriage — is answered by re-weighing: the graver sins are named first, the surplus is the charity, the believer's worth outranks the idolater's appeal, and apostasy is the only true ruin.
* **Placement:** The second, largest block of "They ask you" legislation (started 2:215); it closes the fighting discussion of ruku 24–26 and opens the family-law cascade (ruku 28–31) with the body: what enters it (marriage), what clouds it (intoxicants), what binds it (kin).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:217]`** "They ask you about fighting during the Holy Month. Say, “Fighting during it is deplorable; but to bar others from Allah’s path, and to disbelieve in Him, and to prevent access to the Holy Mosque, and to expel its people from it, are more deplorable with Allah. And persecution is more serious than killing. They will not cease to fight you until they turn you back from your religion, if they can. Whoever among you turns back from his religion, and dies a disbeliever—those are they whose works will come to nothing, in this life, and in the Hereafter. Those are the inmates of the Fire, abiding in it forever."
  * **Gloss:** The month-violation is admitted as grave — then out-graded by four counter-crimes; the real red line is conscience-itself (apostasy unto death erases all works).
* **`[2:218]`** "Those who believed, and those who migrated and fought for the sake of Allah— those look forward to Allah’s mercy. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-portrait: faith + hijra + striving, still ending in hope for mercy, not claim on it.
* **`[2:219]`** "They ask you about intoxicants and gambling. Say, “There is gross sin in them, and some benefits for people, but their sinfulness outweighs their benefit.” And they ask you about what they should give: say, “The surplus.” Thus Allah explains the revelations to you, so that you may think."
  * **Gloss:** Two questions in one verse: a weighing verdict on khamr/maysir (sin > benefit — the step before the final ban elsewhere), and the giving-rule "the surplus" — what you don't need is what is yours to give.
* **`[2:220]`** "About this world and the next. And they ask you about orphans. Say, “Improvement for them is best. And if you intermix with them, then they are your brethren.” Allah knows the dishonest from the honest. Had Allah willed, He could have overburdened you. Allah is Mighty and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** Spending is to be thought about "in this world and the next"; guardianship of orphans is policed by knowledge of intent — and the law's own leniency ("could have overburdened you") is a mercy datum.
* **`[2:221]`** "Do not marry idolatresses, unless they have believed. A believing maid is better than an idolatress, even if you like her. And do not marry idolaters, unless they have believed. A believing servant is better than an idolater, even if you like him. These call to the Fire, but Allah calls to the Garden and to forgiveness, by His leave. He makes clear His communications to the people, that they may be mindful."
  * **Gloss:** Marriage law relocates value from class/attraction to faith — the two "calls" (Fire vs. Garden) make the household a front line of the same contest as 2:217's expulsion-from-path.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`persecution / oppression`** (fitna): the same sentence, two renderings.
  * **Rendering:** "persecution is more serious than killing" (2:217) vs. "Oppression is more serious than murder" (2:191) — verified identical statement, divergent wording across ruku 24 and 27 (Source: translator's term choice, observable).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:193]` "until there is no oppression"; `[2:10]` "In their hearts is sickness" — the root's psychological register.
* **`intoxicants and gambling`** (khamr, maysir): named together once in this surah.
  * **Rendering:** "There is gross sin in them, and some benefits for people, but their sinfulness outweighs their benefit." (2:219).
  * **Elsewhere:** the weighing idiom "their sinfulness outweighs" rhymes with 2:217's "more deplorable with Allah" — one moral-scale method used twice in one ruku (Source: observable in corpus).
* **`the surplus`** (ʿafw): the leftover, the unneeded.
  * **Rendering:** say, "The surplus" (2:219).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:3]` "give from what We have provided for them" — the first-surplus definition at the surah's opening, now legislated; `[2:274]` "spend their wealth by night and day" — the surplus in motion (verified).
* **`idolatress / idolater`** (mushrik/mushrikah): the paired exclusion.
  * **Rendering:** twice "even if you like her/him" (2:221).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:96]` "even more than the polytheists"; `[2:105]` "nor of the polytheists"; `[2:135]` "he was not an idolater" — the same root-word family returns at Abraham's own dis-identification (verified).
