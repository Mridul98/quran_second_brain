# O people: the architecture of worship, and the challenge to the doubters (2:21–29)
**UID:** 202610140913
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:21–29 (Ruku 3 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:21]` – `[2:29]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The address widens from the sorted groups to "O people!": worship the Lord who built the habitable world — earth-habitat, sky-roof, water, fruit — and on that known ledger "do not assign rivals to Allah while you know"; the ruku then issues the literary challenge to any doubter of the revealed Book, defends God's right to coin an example out of a gnat, and closes the creation argument with "He is aware of all things."
* **Placement:** The creedal foundation before the surah turns to Israel (2:40+) and to law; 2:21–22 restates Fatihah-style lordship (*rabb*) and 2:23 ties back to the Book-claim of 2:2 — the doubt addressed at the surah's start is prosecuted in court here.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:21]`** "O people! Worship your Lord who created you and those before you, that you may attain piety."
  * **Gloss:** First "O people!" (*ayyuhā al-nās*) of the surah; worship is grounded in creation and genealogy, aimed at *taqwā* — the same goal-word as the *muttaqīn* of 2:2.
* **`[2:22]`** "He who made the earth a habitat for you, and the sky a structure, and sends water down from the sky, and brings out fruits thereby, as a sustenance for you. Therefore, do not assign rivals to Allah while you know."
  * **Gloss:** Four favors, then one prohibition; the argument is from benefits already acknowledged — "while you know" makes shirk a knowing act, not an innocent error.
* **`[2:23]`** "And if you are in doubt about what We have revealed to Our servant, then produce a chapter like these, and call your witnesses apart from Allah, if you are truthful."
  * **Gloss:** The challenge (*taḥaddī*): a surah-like product, with any helpers; "Our servant" anchors the Book to a human recipient — revelation as speech delivered.
* **`[2:24]`** "But if you do not—and you will not—then beware the Fire whose fuel is people and stones, prepared for the disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** The challenge is answered before history: "and you will not" — a prophecy sealed at 610 CE and checkable across thirteen centuries (Source: agent inference on the rhetorical tense; the classical reading is consensus).
* **`[2:25]`** "And give good news to those who believe and do righteous deeds; that they will have gardens beneath which rivers flow. Whenever they are provided with fruit therefrom as sustenance, they will say, 'This is what we were provided with before,' and they will be given the like of it. And they will have pure spouses therein, and they will abide therein forever."
  * **Gloss:** Counterweight to the Fire: the *bi-shār* verb (same as 2:6's warning-root) — the recognition motif "this is what we were provided with before" echoes the earthly sustenance of 2:22.
* **`[2:26]`** "Allah does not shy away from making an example of a gnat, or something above it. As for those who believe, they know that it is the Truth from their Lord. But as for those who disbelieve, they say, 'What did Allah intend by this example?' He leads astray many thereby, and He guides many thereby; but He misleads thereby only the evildoers."
  * **Gloss:** A meta-parable defense: parables themselves become tests; guidance and misguidance are both *increased* by the same speech-act (Source: classical reading that the verse answers mockers of the gnat-mosquito example, Ibn Kathir on 2:26).
* **`[2:27]`** "Those who violate Allah’s covenant after its confirmation, and sever what Allah has commanded to be joined, and commit evil on earth. These are the losers."
  * **Gloss:** Who the evildoers are: covenant-breakers, kin-severers, earth-corrupters — a triad answering 2:11's slogan ("we are only reformers"); "the losers" (*khāsirūn*) completes the trade metaphor of 2:16.
* **`[2:28]`** "How can you deny Allah, when you were dead and He gave you life, then He will put you to death, then He will bring you to life, then to Him you will be returned?"
  * **Gloss:** The resurrection argument from biography: four transitions, and the question stands on the first one — denial is ungrateful amnesia.
* **`[2:29]`** "It is He who created for you everything on earth, then turned to the heaven, and made them seven heavens. And He is aware of all things."
  * **Gloss:** The inventory completes downward-up: earth made *for you*, heavens ordered; "aware of all things" closes the ruku's epistemology — the knower cannot be rivalled.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`taqwā`** (piety): 2:21 "that you may attain piety."
  * **Rendering:** "piety" here; the plural actors of 2:2 ("the righteous") are the same root *ʿ-t-q* (Source: standard Arabic morphology; rendering equivalence observable in corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:2]` "a guide for the righteous"; `[2:66]` "a lesson for the righteous" — the surah's first word and its running verdict keep the same audience in view.
* **`shurakāʾ`** (rivals/partners): 2:22 "do not assign rivals to Allah."
  * **Rendering:** "rivals" — plural of *sharīk*, the shirk word-family.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:165]` "those who take other than Allah as equals to Him" (*andād*, a different Arabic word for the same crime) — the surah names the rival-maker twice with two vocabularies (Source: observable corpus fact).
* **`sūrah`** (chapter): 2:23 "produce a chapter like these."
  * **Rendering:** "chapter."
  * **Elsewhere:** the challenge recurs expanded at `[10:38]` "Then produce a single chapter like it, and call upon whomever you can, apart from Allah, if you are truthful." and at `[52:34]` "So let them produce a discourse like it, if they are truthful." (Source: cross-surah refs verified via corpus, exact wording quoted).
* **`samāʾ`** (sky/heavens): 2:22 "the sky a structure" (singular, function); 2:29 "made them seven heavens" (plural, architecture).
  * **Rendering:** one word, two registers — "sky" for the weather-system, "heavens" for the ordered cosmos (Source: translator's term choice, observable in corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:164]` "In the creation of the heavens and the earth" (the sign-list of ruku 20); `[2:255]` "His Throne extends over the heavens and the earth."

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Answered petition:** `[1:2–5]` "Lord of the Worlds... It is You we worship, and upon You we call for help" ↔ `[2:21–22]` — lordship listed, then worship commanded; the Fatihah's praise becomes the surah's proof.
* **Within surah:** `[2:23]` "what We have revealed to Our servant" ↔ `[2:2]` "This is the Book" — 2:2's claim is the defendant here; the doubt prosecuted in 2:23 was answered in 2:2 by declaring the Book doubt-free (*lā rayba*).
* **Within surah:** `[2:26]` "He leads astray many thereby, and He guides many thereby" ↔ `[2:7]` the sealed disbelievers ↔ `[2:5]` the guided — misguidance is now stated as *responsive*: speech does the separating (links to Guidance note).
* **Within surah:** `[2:27]` covenant-violators ↔ `[2:40]` "fulfill your pledge to Me" — the general definition immediately precedes the specific nation addressed; also ↔ `[2:11–12]` the reformer-slogan.
* **Parallel:** `[31:10–11]` — "He created the heavens without pillars that you can see... ## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan surah, but 2:21–29 is Meccan in *texture* — tawhid-from-creation, resurrection, challenge-to-the-poets; the Medinan community is being re-taught its Meccan foundations (Source: agent inference — verify; the classification of content-style is not a transmitted datum).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** reports on the challenge verse (2:23) name individual poets who attempted imitation and failed — transmitted in the sīrah/tafsīr literature, not the text (Source: classical reports per Ibn Kathir on 2:23; treat as background, not quotation).
