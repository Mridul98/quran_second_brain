# The third type: those who claim belief and lose the trade (2:8–20)
**UID:** 202610140912
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:8–20 (Ruku 2 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:8]` – `[2:20]` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** After the guided (2:2–5) and the sealed disbelievers (2:6–7), a third group is introduced — people inside the community whose confession is verbal only — and thirteen verses dissect them: their self-deception, their "sick" hearts, their two catchphrases, their mockery, and finally two parables (a fire extinguished, a storm they cannot survive) ending on the trade that lost: "they have bartered error for guidance."
* **Placement:** Completes the opening sorting of humanity; the longest portrait-section of its kind in the Quran and the surah's first extended metaphor-pairing, before the address swings to all mankind at 2:21.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:8]`** "Among the people are those who say, 'We believe in Allah and in the Last Day,' but they are not believers."
  * **Gloss:** The confession recites the exact objects of 2:3–4's faith — unseen, Last Day — and the narrator simply denies it: "they are not believers."
* **`[2:9]`** "They seek to deceive Allah and those who believe, but they deceive none but themselves, though they are not aware."
  * **Gloss:** Deception aimed at the community rebounds on the deceiver; "though they are not aware" — the first of three *lā yaʿlamūn* strikes (2:9, 12, 18-ish awareness motif).
* **`[2:10]`** "In their hearts is sickness, and Allah has increased their sickness. They will have a painful punishment because of their denial."
  * **Gloss:** The diagnostic: *marad* — a heart-condition that compounds when indulged; the punishment is attributed to their lying/denial (*kadhabū*).
* **`[2:11]`** "And when it is said to them, 'Do not make trouble on earth,' they say, 'We are only reformers.'"
  * **Gloss:** Catchphrase one: corruption claimed as reform — the vocabulary of 2:27's covenant-breakers and 2:205's power-abuser.
* **`[2:12]`** "In fact, they are the troublemakers, but they are not aware."
  * **Gloss:** The narrator's verdict reverses their slogan verbatim (mufsidūn vs muṣliḥūn).
* **`[2:13]`** "And when it is said to them, 'Believe as the people have believed,' they say, 'Shall we believe as the fools have believed?' In fact, it is they who are the fools, but they do not know."
  * **Gloss:** Catchphrase two: belief-as-credential inverted into belief-as-foolishness (*suḥafāʾ*); again the verdict mirrors the slur.
* **`[2:14]`** "And when they come across those who believe, they say, 'We believe'; but when they are alone with their devils, they say, 'We are with you; we were only ridiculing.'"
  * **Gloss:** The mechanism exposed: speech switches by audience; "their devils" — the inner circle that instructs the performance.
* **`[2:15]`** "It is Allah who ridicules them, and leaves them bewildered in their transgression."
  * **Gloss:** Divine reply in their own register: the mockers are mocked back and left staggering (*ʿamāhum* — "bewildered").
* **`[2:16]`** "Those are they who have bartered error for guidance; but their trade does not profit them, and they are not guided."
  * **Gloss:** The economic formula of the whole surah's loss-account opens: *sharaw al-ḍalālah bi-l-hudā* — error purchased with guidance; three clauses: bad trade, no profit, no guidance.
* **`[2:17]`** "Their likeness is that of a person who kindled a fire; when it illuminated all around him, Allah took away their light, and left them in darkness, unable to see."
  * **Gloss:** Parable one: light gained then confiscated — the number slips from "a person" to "their light" mid-verse, already blurring individual and group (Source: observable grammar fact; the rhetorical effect is agent inference).
* **`[2:18]`** "Deaf, dumb, blind. They will not return."
  * **Gloss:** Three sensory negations answering 2:7's sealed heart/hearing/sight; "they will not return" — the trade is closed.
* **`[2:19]`** "Or like a cloudburst from the sky, in which is darkness, and thunder, and lightning. They press their fingers into their ears from the thunderbolts, in fear of death. But Allah surrounds the disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** Parable two: revelation as storm — light *and* lethal darkness; their posture is panic, not faith; the aside "Allah surrounds the disbelievers" frames the scene as already encircled.
* **`[2:20]`** "The lightning almost snatches their sight away. Whenever it illuminates for them, they walk in it; but when it grows dark over them, they stand still. Had Allah willed, He could have taken away their hearing and their sight. Allah is capable of everything."
  * **Gloss:** The storm's moral optics: they borrow light opportunistically and stall when challenged; closes the ruku on divine capability — the same triad (hearing/sight) as 2:7 and 2:18, here named as revocable at will.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`marad`** (sickness): 2:10 "In their hearts is sickness."
  * **Rendering:** "sickness" (moral); the same root renders physical illness elsewhere.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:184]` "whoever among you is sick" (legal exemption, body) — one Arabic word spanning heart-condition and bodily illness; the Quran's psychology of belief as health (Source: observable corpus fact; the "health" synthesis is agent inference — verify).
* **`sharā`** (to buy/sell, barter): 2:16 "bartered error for guidance... their trade does not profit."
  * **Rendering:** "bartered" (2:16).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:90]` "Miserable is what they sold their souls for"; `[2:175]` "It is they who exchange guidance for error, and forgiveness for punishment" — a commerce-of-loss chain across the surah (Source: observable corpus fact).
* **`yastahziʾūn`** (ridicule): 2:14 "we were only ridiculing"; 2:15 "It is Allah who ridicules them."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:212]` "and they ridicule those who believe" (Source: observable — same verb reused); also the fire-parable mockery of 2:17 has no verbal echo — note mockery by situation vs mockery by speech (agent inference — verify).
* **`ʿamā`** (bewilderment): 2:15 "leaves them bewildered in their transgression" — stumbling through sin while calling it strategy.
  * **Rendering:** "bewildered" — left reeling, led on in their transgression. Within al-Baqarah this root's only other English kin is the storm-parable's standing still (2:20); do not cite cross-surah parallels of this root without checking their corpus lines first (Source: observable from full-file read of s002; the lexeme-level claim is deliberately withheld pending verification).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within surah:** `[2:8]` "believe in Allah and in the Last Day" ↔ `[2:3–4]` — identical objects of faith, opposite verdicts; the ruku's whole point is that the formula without the traits is void (Source: observable textual fact).
* **Within surah:** `[2:7]` seal on heart/hearing/sight ↔ `[2:18]` "Deaf, dumb, blind" ↔ `[2:20]` "He could have taken away their hearing and their sight" — the sensory triad threads all three groups: the guided *use* their senses, the disbelievers are sealed, the hypocrites perform blindness.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical consensus for the whole surah). This is the Quran's longest anatomical study of *nifāq*; notably the noun "hypocrite" (*munāfiq*) does not occur in 2:8–20 — the type is described, never labeled (Source: observable corpus fact; confirmed by full-file reading).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** Classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir on 2:8; al-Tabari's reports) connects these portraits to ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy and the Medina hypocrite circle; the reports are transmitted, not in the verse text — keep as attribution, not quotation (Source: named classical references).
