# Unfinished marriages, the middle prayer, and a year of provision (2:236–242)
**UID:** 202610140941
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:236–242 (Ruku 31 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:236]` – `[2:242]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The family-law cascade closes its money cases — the dowry of a marriage never consummated, the widow's year of bequeathed housing, the divorced woman's equitable provision — interrupts itself once to command guarding the prayers, and ends with the refrain that God explains His revelations precisely so people understand.
* **Placement:** Final ruku of the divorce/nursing block (2:222–242); the inserted prayer-verses (2:238–239) weld the domestic statute back onto worship, then 2:243 pivots the surah from law to narrative (Saul and Goliath).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:236]`** "You commit no error by divorcing women before having touched them, or before having set the dowry for them. And compensate them—the wealthy according to his means, and the poor according to his means—with a fair compensation, a duty upon the doers of good."
  * **Gloss:** No blame for dissolving an unconsummated marriage — but a compensation graded by wealth, not by a fixed sum: the poor man's duty is as real as the rich man's.
* **`[2:237]`** "If you divorce them before you have touched them, but after you had set the dowry for them, give them half of what you specified—unless they forego the right, or the one in whose hand is the marriage contract foregoes it. But to forego is nearer to piety. And do not forget generosity between one another. Allah is seeing of everything you do."
  * **Gloss:** Specified mahr: half vests by contract; either side's waiver is praised, and the husband's "generosity" beyond the half is the taqwa-surplus; divine sight polices the settlement.
* **`[2:238]`** "Guard your prayers, and the middle prayer, and stand before Allah in devotion."
  * **Gloss:** The single abrupt interlude: protect the prayers, with one named out — "the middle prayer" — and stand to God as free persons (qunut).
* **`[2:239]`** "But if you are in fear, then on foot, or riding. And when you are safe, remember Allah, as He taught you what you did not know."
  * **Gloss:** Prayer is not suspended by danger — performed walking or mounted; safety's return is marked by remembrance grounded in having been taught.
* **`[2:240]`** "Those of you who die and leave wives behind—a will shall provide their wives with support for a year, provided they do not leave. If they leave, you are not to blame for what they do with themselves, provided it is reasonable. Allah is Mighty and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** Widows' housing-year as a bequest-duty; their own honorable choice respected; read against 2:234's four-months-ten-days term (see Cross-References — the two widow-laws sit one ruku apart).
* **`[2:241]`** "And divorced women shall be provided for, equitably—a duty upon the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** One line, the catch-all: provision (mut'a) for divorcees as equitable duty on the muttaqin — the word the surah opened with (2:2).
* **`[2:242]`** "Allah thus explains His revelations to you, so that you may understand."
  * **Gloss:** Block-close: clarification of detail is the pedagogical mercy; "so that you may understand" answers where 2:187 said "that they may attain piety" and 2:219 said "so that you may think."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`dowry`** (ṣadūqāt/mahr): the specified marriage payment.
  * **Rendering:** "before having set the dowry for them" (2:236); "half of what you specified" (2:237).
  * **Elsewhere:** the word appears only in these two verses in this surah (verified via rg); the reclaim-case is 2:229 (ruku 29) — one money-object governs both ruku.
* **`the middle prayer`** (al-ṣalāt al-wusṭā): singled out for guarding.
  * **Rendering:** "Guard your prayers, and the middle prayer" (2:238) — unique in the surah (verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** which prayer is "middle" is identified variously in classical reports (ʿasr, fajr, ʿishaʾ) — unnamed in the translation itself (Source: classical dispute reported in tafsir on 2:238; no specific chain re-verified).
* **`doers of good`** (muḥsinīn): ihsan as a duty-holder's title.
  * **Rendering:** "a duty upon the doers of good" (2:236); 2:241 uses "the righteous" for muttaqin beside it.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:112]` "whoever... is a doer of good, will have his reward"; `[2:195]` "Allah loves the charitable" (muhsin) — the root migrates from general virtue to divorce-settlement finance (verified).
* **`generosity`** (faḍl): surplus grace between people.
  * **Rendering:** "do not forget generosity between one another" (2:237).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:198]` — "You commit no error by seeking bounty from your Lord" — same faḍl root: pilgrims seek God's bounty in trade, spouses are told not to forget it with each other (verified).
