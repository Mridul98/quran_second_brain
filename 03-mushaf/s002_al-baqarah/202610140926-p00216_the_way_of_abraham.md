# Nobody forsakes the religion of Abraham but the self-fooling (2:130–141)
**UID:** 202610140926
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:130–141 (Ruku 16 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:130]` – `[2:141]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Abraham's religion is filed as the counter-claim to all label-wars: chosen in this world and righteous in the next, tested by the single word "Submit!" — which became his lifelong answer; his deathbed exhortation and Jacob's last question to his sons both end on the same note, "to Him we submit"; the command that follows — "Be Jews or Christians" answered by "Rather, the religion of Abraham, the Monotheist" — produces the surah's own creed-doi (2:136, six articles, "we make no distinction"), sealed by Allah's coloring (*sibghah*), and closed by the doublet (2:134=2:141): their ledger theirs, yours yours.
* **Placement:** The doctrinal peak of the Abraham-section: it converts the Abrahamic inheritance from genealogy into *islām*-as-submission, and plants the creed-list (2:136) that 2:285 will mirror at the surah's end.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:130]`** "Who would forsake the religion of Abraham, except he who fools himself? We chose him in this world, and in the Hereafter he will be among the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The rhetorical question relocates the fault: leaving Abraham's way is self-deception; election ("We chose him") is stated without transferability to his biological heirs.
* **`[2:131]`** "When his Lord said to him, 'Submit!' He said, 'I have submitted to the Lord of the Worlds.'"
  * **Gloss:** The whole career of Abraham in two verbs — command and immediate compliance; *aslama* here is the surah's own definition-work of Islam.
* **`[2:132]`** "And Abraham exhorted his sons, and Jacob, 'O my sons, Allah has chosen this religion for you, so do not die unless you have submitted.'"
  * **Gloss:** Deathbed politics: the father's will is not wealth but posture — "do not die unless you have submitted"; note "his sons, and Jacob" — Jacob listed inside/alongside Abraham's line (Source: observable textual oddity; grammatical readings differ — Ibn Kathir on 2:132 keeps both).
* **`[2:133]`** "Or were you witnesses when death approached Jacob, and he said to his sons, 'What will you worship after Me?' They said, 'We will worship your God, and the God of your fathers, Abraham, Ishmael, and Isaac; One God; and to Him we submit.'"
  * **Gloss:** The counter-interrogation: were you at *that* deathbed? — the answer-scripted confession names three fathers, "One God", and closes on the same submission-verb as 2:131; Islam is Jacob's dying wish, not a later sect.
* **`[2:134]`** "That was a community that has passed; for them is what they have earned, and for you is what you have earned; and you will not be questioned about what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The ledger-clause — first of its two occurrences; ancestors carry their own earnings.
* **`[2:135]`** "And they say, 'Be Jews or Christians, and you will be guided.' Say, 'Rather, the religion of Abraham, the Monotheist; he was not an idolater.'"
  * **Gloss:** The sectarian ultimatum, refused by preference ("Rather") — and the standard restated: *ḥanīf*, not a *mushrik*.
* **`[2:136]`** "Say, 'We believe in Allah; and in what was revealed to us; and in what was revealed to Abraham, and Ishmael, and Isaac, and Jacob, and the Patriarchs; and in what was given to Moses and Jesus; and in what was given to the prophets—from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them, and to Him we surrender.'"
  * **Gloss:** The creed-list in the first person plural: God, revealed-to-us (this Quran), the Abrahamic household, Moses and Jesus, all prophets — non-discrimination as the mark; it answers 2:4's "believe in what was revealed to you, and in what was revealed before you" and the charge of 2:85's part-belief.
* **`[2:137]`** "If they believe in the same as you have believed in, then they have been guided. But if they turn away, then they are in schism. Allah will protect you against them; for He is the Hearer, the Knower."
  * **Gloss:** Guidance by identical creed-content, schism (*shiqaq*) by turning; the Hearer/Knower pair from Abraham's prayer (2:127) re-secures the believer.
* **`[2:138]`** "Allah’s coloring. And who gives better coloring than Allah? 'And we are devoted to Him.'"
  * **Gloss:** *Sibghah* — the only Quranic use of the noun, a dye/ baptism-adjacent word used for God's marking: the believer's color is applied by Him, chosen by none; "we are devoted to Him" (*lahu naʿbud*) closes on worship alone (Source: hapax observable via corpus rg — "coloring" occurs only at 2:138; the baptismal resonance is reported background, not claimed — Ibn Kathir cites such reports cautiously).
* **`[2:139]`** "Say, 'Do you argue with us about Allah, when He is our Lord and your Lord, and We have our works, and you have your works, and we are sincere to Him?'"
  * **Gloss:** Debate over God Himself is broken on the common premise: same Lord, separate ledgers, sincerity directed to Him.
* **`[2:140]`** "Or do you say that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and the Patriarchs were Jews or Christians? Say, 'Do you know better, or Allah?' And who does greater wrong than he who conceals a testimony he has from Allah? Allah is not unaware of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The anachronism charge — reading the labels back onto the patriarchs; the witness-theology: concealing a testimony you hold from God is the greater wrong, continuing the concealment-chain of 2:42/2:76/2:79.
* **`[2:141]`** "That was a community that has passed. To them is what they have earned, and to you is what you have earned. And you will not be questioned about what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The ledger-clause repeated — the ruku's inclusion-band; 2:134 and 2:141 are one sentence twice, sealing the argument that inheritance is earned, never blooded.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`millah`** (religion/creed): 2:130 "the religion of Abraham"; 2:135 "Rather, the religion of Abraham."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:123]` "Follow the religion of Abraham, the Monotheist. He was not an idol-worshiper" — same phrase, same rendering, divine command to the Prophet; `[6:161]` "an upright religion, the creed of Abraham the Monotheist" — where *millat* renders "creed" (Source: refs verified via corpus; rendering drift creed/religion observable).
* **`ḥanīf`** (the Monotheist): 2:135.
  * **Rendering:** "the Monotheist" — consistent with `[16:123]` and `[6:161]`; the root means inclining-away-from-idols toward uprightness (Source: classical lexical consensus; rendering pair verified).
* **`aslama`** (submit): 2:131 "Submit!" / "I have submitted"; 2:132 "unless you have submitted"; 2:133 "to Him we submit"; 2:136 "to Him we surrender."
  * **Rendering:** one verb, two English words — "submit"/"surrender" within five verses (Source: observable); the noun-family returns at `[2:208]` "Enter into submission, wholeheartedly" and 22:78 "He named you Muslims" (Source: 22:78 verified via corpus).
* **`sibghah`** (coloring): 2:138 — the surah's single hapax; God's dye vs human labels.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the passage argues *against* the sectarian claims active in Medina (2:135's ultimatum is quoted speech); its method — re-describing the patriarchs as *muslimūn* — transfers the disputed inheritance without touching genealogy (Source: agent observation on 2:135/2:140, both in range).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** reports on 2:138 connect *sibghah* to a Jewish practice of dyeing children's baptism-like markings, and to Christian baptism — transmitted comparison, cited by classical authors with caution; treat as background and do not import into the gloss (Source: named classical references, Ibn Kathir on 2:138).
