# The open door and the heifer's evasions (2:62–71)
**UID:** 202610140918
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:62–71 (Ruku 8 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:62]` – `[2:71]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Immediately after the verdicts, the surah opens the door wide — believers, Jews, Christians, Sabians: whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day and acts righteously has nothing to fear — then snaps back to the covenant-file: the Mount raised, the Sabbath breach turned to apes, and the heifer command met with question after question until the people get exactly what they asked for and almost don't do it.
* **Placement:** The ruku's double movement — universal mercy clause (2:62) inside the Israel-indictment (2:63–71) — is the surah's signature editing: no exclusivism survives next to the recital. 2:62's formula is reused verbatim-style at 2:112 and 5:69 outside the surah.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:62]`** "Those who believe, and those who are Jewish, and the Christians, and the Sabeans—any who believe in Allah and the Last Day, and act righteously—will have their reward with their Lord; they have nothing to fear, nor will they grieve."
  * **Gloss:** Four labels, one criterion; the group-names hold, the verdict comes by faith-and-deed; the no-fear formula (2:38's) is applied to a wider frame than any prior ruku.
* **`[2:63]`** "And recall when We received a pledge from you, and raised the Mount above you: 'Take what We have given you earnestly, and remember what is in it, that you may attain righteousness.'"
  * **Gloss:** Covenant scene: the Mount as lifted warranty; "earnestly" (*bi-quwwatin*) and "that you may attain righteousness" — the *taqwā* goal-word again.
* **`[2:64]`** "But after that you turned away. Were it not for Allah’s grace and mercy towards you, you would have been among the losers."
  * **Gloss:** Turning-away (*tawallaw*) immediately after pledge-taking; the only stay is grace — "the losers" echoes 2:27/2:16 trade vocabulary.
* **`[2:65]`** "And you surely knew those of you who violated the Sabbath. We said to them, 'Be despicable apes!'"
  * **Gloss:** The known precedent: a transformed verdict — the word "apes" (*qirdah*) as public deterrent.
* **`[2:66]`** "Thus We made it a deterrent for their generation, and for subsequent generations, and a lesson for the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** Three audiences for one event; "a lesson for the righteous" — the *muttaqīn* word re-appears (see Note ruku 1 term entry).
* **`[2:67]`** "And recall when Moses said to his people, 'Allah commands you to sacrifice a heifer.' They said, 'Do you make a mockery of us?' He said, 'Allah forbid that I should be so ignorant.'"
  * **Gloss:** The command that names the surah; the first of four evasive responses — they answer an order with an accusation.
* **`[2:68]`** "They said, 'Call upon your Lord to show us which one.' He said, 'He says she is a heifer, neither too old, nor too young, but in between. So do what you are commanded.'"
  * **Gloss:** Question #1 → specification #1 (age); the answer's form "He says" (*yqūlu innahā*) keeps the chain of petition visible.
* **`[2:69]`** "They said, 'Call upon your Lord to show us what her color is.' He said, 'He says she is a yellow heifer, bright in color, pleasing to the beholders.'"
  * **Gloss:** Question #2 → specification #2 (color); each question narrows the free range of the first command — the classical lesson: looseness is lost by demanding looseness (Source: consensus of classical tafsir on 2:67–70, Ibn Kathir).
* **`[2:70]`** "They said, 'Call upon your Lord to show us which one; the heifers look alike to us; and Allah willing, we will be guided.'"
  * **Gloss:** Question #3 — with the pious hedge "and Allah willing"; their guidance is now conditional on God's will, an echo of 2:16's "they are not guided."
* **`[2:71]`** "He said, 'He says she is a heifer, neither yoked to plow the earth, nor to irrigate the field; sound without blemish.' They said, 'Now you have brought the truth.' So they slew her; though they almost did not."
  * **Gloss:** Specification #3 (unworked, unblemished) — the animal is now uniquely expensive; "Now you have brought the truth" is their first honest sentence; "though they almost did not" (*wa mā yadh-baḥūn*) — the closing verdict on reluctant obedience.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`baqarah`** (heifer): 2:67–71, five mentions; the surah's name-title.
  * **Rendering:** "heifer" throughout (not "cow").
  * **Elsewhere:** the noun occurs only in this episode of the Quran (Source: observable in this corpus — full-corpus rg for "heifer" returns s002 only); the corpus header itself names the surah "THE HEIFER (al-Baqarah)" — the story gives the surah its name (Source: observable).
* **`lā khawfa ʿalayhim`** (no-fear formula): 2:62 "they have nothing to fear, nor will they grieve."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:38]` (Adam's promise), `[2:112]` (whoever submits), `[2:262]` (spend without reminders), `[2:274]` (spend night and day), `[2:277]` — a five-station formula inside al-Baqarah alone (Source: observable chain; refs verified via full-file read).
* **`sābat`** (Sabbath): 2:65 "those of you who violated the Sabbath."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:163]` "when they violated the Sabbath" — same phrasing of the same breach; `[4:154]` "Do not violate the Sabbath" in the covenant-list; `[16:124]` "The Sabbath was decreed only for those who differed about it" (Source: refs verified via corpus).
* **`tawallā`** (turn away): 2:64 "you turned away."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:83]` "Then you turned away, except for a few of you" — the recital's habitual verb; `[2:137]` "if they turn away" (forward, Source: observable; Arabic identity agent inference — verify).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; 2:62 is classically cited in discussions of the salvific scope of prior communities — no consensus on whether it was abrogated (Ibn Kathir transmits both readings); the note keeps the verse's plain function: an open door *inside* an indictment, which is the surah's editorial voice (Source: named disagreement).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** reports connect the heifer episode to an unsolved murder among Israel, resolved by the cow's blood (2:72–73 follows immediately); the causal link heifer→homicide is transmitted in tafsīr, the text itself lets the "And recall when you killed a person" of 2:72 pick up the thread (Source: consensus-style reading, Ibn Kathir on 2:67–73; the textual seam is observable).
