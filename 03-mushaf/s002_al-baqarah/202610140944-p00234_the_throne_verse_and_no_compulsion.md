# Spend before the Day; the Throne, no compulsion, and the two custodians (2:254–257)
**UID:** 202610140944
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:254–257 (Ruku 34 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:254]` – `[2:257]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** One command to spend before a Day where no trading, friendship, or intercession remains opens the surah's creedal summit — the Throne verse's exhaustive monotheism — from which flow "no compulsion in religion" and the final sorting of humanity under two possible custodians: Allah into light, or the evil ones into darkness.
* **Placement:** The theological center of the legislation block: it gathers the surah's money-law (spend), intercession-law (2:48, 2:123), and guidance-law into a single God-description, immediately before Abraham's resurrection-arguments (ruku 35) and the charity parables (ruku 36–38).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:254]`** "O you who believe! Spend from what We have given you, before a Day comes in which there is neither trading, nor friendship, nor intercession. The disbelievers are the wrongdoers."
  * **Gloss:** All three worldly supports — exchange, alliance, mediation — will be absent; the only transfer that crosses into that Day is spending done as God's gift-acknowledgment.
* **`[2:255]`** "Allah! There is no god except He, the Living, the Everlasting. Neither slumber overtakes Him, nor sleep. To Him belongs everything in the heavens and everything on earth. Who is he that can intercede with Him except with His permission? He knows what is before them, and what is behind them; and they cannot grasp any of His knowledge, except as He wills. His Throne extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation does not burden Him. He is the Most High, the Great."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's longest single God-sentence: life without lapse, ownership without delegate, knowledge without gaps, sovereignty without fatigue — and intercession explicitly relocated to His permission, answering 2:254's deadline in real time.
* **`[2:256]`** "There shall be no compulsion in religion; the right way has become distinct from the wrong way. Whoever renounces evil and believes in Allah has grasped the most trustworthy handle; which does not break. Allah is Hearing and Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Because the Throne verse just established a God who needs no partners, faith cannot be coerced — the way has been made distinct; the "handle that does not break" is the opposite of every weight that fails on the Day of 2:254.
* **`[2:257]`** "Allah is the Lord of those who believe; He brings them out of darkness and into light. As for those who disbelieve, their lords are the evil ones; they bring them out of light and into darkness—these are the inmates of the Fire, in which they will abide forever."
  * **Gloss:** Two wardens, two directions: the same verbs (bringing-out) run in opposite vectors; lordship itself is plural for the disbelievers — every allegiance is a custodian dragging inward.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Throne`** (kursī): the seat/reach of sovereign care.
  * **Rendering:** "His Throne extends over the heavens and the earth" (2:255).
  * **Elsewhere:** single occurrence in this surah (verified via rg); the sentence rhymes lexically with `[2:29]` "He is aware of all things" and `[2:115]` "Allah is Omnipresent and Omniscient" — the corpus's other totality-predications (verified).
* **`compulsion`** (ikrāh): forced religion.
  * **Rendering:** "There shall be no compulsion in religion" (2:256) — unique in the surah (verified via rg).
  * **Elsewhere:** its logical precondition is stated at `[2:262]`-scale clarity: "the right way has become distinct from the wrong way" — the surah repeatedly grounds non-coercion in made-distinct guidance, cf. `[2:5]`, `[2:16]` trade-metaphors of the opening (verified).
* **`trustworthy handle`** (al-ʿurwa al-wuthqā): the unbreakable grip.
  * **Rendering:** "has grasped the most trustworthy handle; which does not break" (2:256).
  * **Elsewhere:** unique in this surah (verified via rg); the gripping-verb answers 2:254's failed hand-holds — trading, friendship, intercession — all of which do break.
* **`Lord / evil ones`** (waliyy / ṭāghūt): custody language.
  * **Rendering:** 2:257: "Allah is the Lord of those who believe... their lords are the evil ones"; 2:256 "Whoever renounces evil."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:107]` "apart from Allah you have no guardian or helper"; `[2:120]` "you will have in Allah neither guardian nor helper"; `[2:286]` "You are our Lord and Master" — the wali-family runs from the qibla-block to the surah's closing plea (verified); conceptually the same "rivals you cannot see" that `[2:22]` forbids — see tawhid note (Source: agent inference, verify).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan by surah-consensus; 2:255–256 are classically singled out — the Throne verse transmitted as the greatest verse of the Quran, and 2:256 read variously as a prohibition of forced conversion or as a revealed permission-with-conditions (the famous contextual reports) — both are transmitted readings, not the text's self-claim (Source: traditions cited in classical tafsir on 2:255/2:256 — verify before asserting).
