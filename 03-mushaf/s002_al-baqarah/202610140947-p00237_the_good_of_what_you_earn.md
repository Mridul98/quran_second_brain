# Give the good, not the refuse; give secretly; give to the dignified poor (2:267–273)
**UID:** 202610140947
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:267–273 (Ruku 37 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:267]` – `[2:273]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The parables become procurement law: give from the good of earnings and harvest — not the refuse you would take only grudgingly — because Satan's poverty-fear is the real counter-offer; secrecy beats publicity, guidance is not your job, and the surah paints its finest social portrait: the poor too proud to beg.
* **Placement:** Middle chamber of the charity-vs-usury sequence (ruku 36 parables → 37 rules → 38 verdicts); the eating-command of ruku 21 ("Eat of the good things") now completes its circuit as the giving-command ("Give of the good things").

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:267]`** "O you who believe! Give of the good things you have earned, and from what We have produced for you from the earth. And do not pick the inferior things to give away, when you yourselves would not accept it except with eyes closed. And know that Allah is Sufficient and Praiseworthy."
  * **Gloss:** Quality clause for charity: earned wealth and earth's produce, both "good"; the eye-closed phrase names the shame-test — you wouldn't take it back, so don't donate it out; "Sufficient" means God is not being paid off with leftovers.
* **`[2:268]`** "Satan promises you poverty, and urges you to immorality; but Allah promises you forgiveness from Himself, and grace. Allah is Embracing and Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** The economic-psychology verse: hoarding is Satanic eschatology (scarcity is his prophecy); the counter-promise pairs forgiveness with grace, and "Embracing" answers the scarcity lie with divine amplitude.
* **`[2:269]`** "He gives wisdom to whomever He wills. Whoever is given wisdom has been given much good. But none pays heed except those with insight."
  * **Gloss:** Interlude: the ability to see through 2:268's scare-trade is itself wisdom, given, not earned.
* **`[2:270]`** "Whatever charity you give, or a pledge you fulfill, Allah knows it. The wrongdoers have no helpers."
  * **Gloss:** Two ledger-lines — spontaneous giving and vowed obligation — both recorded; helperlessness is the wrongdoers' final balance.
* **`[2:271]`** "If you give charity openly, that is good. But if you keep it secret, and give it to the needy in private, that is better for you. It will atone for some of your misdeeds. Allah is cognizant of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** Public charity is allowed (good), private to the needy is better (higher), and secrecy carries an unexpected dividend — it atones.
* **`[2:272]`** "Their guidance is not your responsibility, but Allah guides whom He wills. Any charity you give is for your own good. Any charity you give shall be for the sake of Allah. Any charity you give will be repaid to you in full, and you will not be wronged."
  * **Gloss:** Triple "any charity you give" — to whom, why, and what-return — separates mission from outreach: you fund, God guides; the giver's profit is the giving itself, repaid "in full."
* **`[2:273]`** "It is for the poor; those who are restrained in the way of Allah, and unable to travel in the land. The unaware would think them rich, due to their dignity. You will recognize them by their features. They do not ask from people insistently. Whatever charity you give, Allah is aware of it."
  * **Gloss:** Beneficiary portrait: the occupied poor — restrained in God's cause, groundless by travel, self-respecting past recognition; "you will recognize them by their features" makes discernment the community's duty, and awareness God's.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`the good things`** (ṭayyibāt): the quality standard, from diet to charity.
  * **Rendering:** "Give of the good things you have earned" (2:267).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:168]`, `[2:172]` — "Eat of what is lawful and good" / "Eat of the good things We have provided for you" (ruku 21) — the eat→give circuit closes here (verified); `[2:57]` — the wilderness table.
* **`wisdom`** (ḥikma): insight as given gift.
  * **Rendering:** "He gives wisdom to whomever He wills" (2:269).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:129]`/`[2:151]` — the messenger "teaches you the Book and wisdom"; `[2:251]` — David given "sovereignty and wisdom"; `[2:231]` — "the Scripture and Wisdom to teach you" — the qunut-of-the-marketplace in the surah's gift-cluster (verified).
* **`the needy / the poor`** (masākīn, fuqarāʾ): recipients named precisely.
  * **Rendering:** "give it to the needy in private" (2:271); "It is for the poor..." (2:273).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:215]` — the five-channel list ("the poor, and the wayfarer"); `[2:177]` — "the needy, and the homeless, and the beggars" — 2:273's dignified non-beggars are the subset the birr-verse already honored (verified).
* **`a pledge you fulfill`** (nadr): vowed giving.
  * **Rendering:** "or a pledge you fulfill, Allah knows it" (2:270).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:177]` — "fulfill their promise when they promise" — covenant-keeping re-surfaced inside charity-law; the surah's promise-grammar spans both ruku (verified).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan zakat-era; "restrained in the way of Allah" (2:273) is classically read as volunteers bound to frontier service, hence immobile and untrade-rich (Source: standard reading in classical tafsir on 2:273 — verify).
