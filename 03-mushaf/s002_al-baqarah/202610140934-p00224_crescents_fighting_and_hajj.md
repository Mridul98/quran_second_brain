# Crescents, curtailed war, and the road to the House (2:189–196)
**UID:** 202610140934
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 2:189–196 (Ruku 24 of 40 in al-Baqarah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[2:189]` – `[2:196]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Two questions about form — the crescents, and how to fight — get answers that redirect both: time is a serviceable timetable, war is a restrained last resort, and the same months that regulate fasting now regulate the road to the Sacred House.
* **Placement:** Opens the Hajj block (ruku 24–25) on the heels of Ramadan (ruku 23); the fighting verses are the surah's first engagement rules, echoed and escalated in ruku 26–27. Source: sequence reading — agent inference.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[2:189]`** "They ask you about the crescents. Say, “They are timetables for people, and for the Hajj.” It is not virtuous that you approach homes from their backs, but virtue is to be pious. So approach homes from their doors, and observe Allah, that you may succeed."
  * **Gloss:** The moon's phases are demystified into calendars — for contracts and for Hajj; a proverb rebukes ritual shortcut: piety, not eccentric form, is *birr* (continuing 2:177's argument).
* **`[2:190]`** "And fight in the cause of Allah those who fight you, but do not commit aggression; Allah does not love the aggressors."
  * **Gloss:** Permission with two fences: only against combatants, and no transgression at all.
* **`[2:191]`** "And kill them wherever you overtake them, and expel them from where they had expelled you. Oppression is more serious than murder. But do not fight them at the Sacred Mosque, unless they fight you there. If they fight you, then kill them. Such is the retribution of the disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** The exiled may fight in the land of their exile; the phrase "Oppression is more serious than murder" supplies the moral arithmetic (fitna outweighs killing).
* **`[2:192]`** "But if they cease, then Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The first cease-fire valve — enemy cessation meets divine forgiveness, and the command stops.
* **`[2:193]`** "And fight them until there is no oppression, and worship becomes devoted to Allah alone. But if they cease, then let there be no hostility except against the oppressors."
  * **Gloss:** The aim is the end of fitna, not conversion; the valve repeats ("if they cease"), hostility reserved to aggressors as a class.
* **`[2:194]`** "The sacred month for the sacred month; and sacrilege calls for retaliation. Whoever commits aggression against you, retaliate against him in the same measure as he has committed against you. And be conscious of Allah, and know that Allah is with the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** Symmetry law: violations answer violations one-for-one — then the taqwa brake is stamped directly onto the battlefield.
* **`[2:195]`** "And spend in the cause of Allah, and do not throw yourselves with your own hands into ruin, and be charitable. Allah loves the charitable."
  * **Gloss:** War and pilgrimage need logistics: spending is commanded, and hoarding-by-neglect is named self-destruction; "Allah loves the charitable" is the surah's first divine love-verb for donors.
* **`[2:196]`** "And carry out the Hajj and the Umrah for Allah. But if you are prevented, then whatever is feasible of offerings. And do not shave your heads until the offering has reached its destination. Whoever of you is sick, or has an injury of the head, then redemption of fasting, or charity, or worship. When you are secure: whoever continues the Umrah until the Hajj, then whatever is feasible of offering. But if he lacks the means, then fasting for three days during the Hajj and seven when you have returned, making ten in all. This is for he whose household is not present at the Sacred Mosque. And remain conscious of Allah, and know that Allah is stern in retribution."
  * **Gloss:** The complete ruku of case-law: perform both pilgrimages "for Allah"; blocked pilgrims send offerings; the sick head may shave with redemption; combining Umrah-then-Hajj (tamattuʿ) requires an offering, or the 3+7 fast; distance from the Mosque defines scope.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fitna`** — rendered "oppression" here.
  * **Rendering:** "Oppression is more serious than murder" (2:191); "until there is no oppression" (2:193); the identical statement in 2:217 is rendered "persecution is more serious than killing."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:49]` "a tremendous trial from your Lord" and `[2:155]` "We will certainly test you" — same root family spans trial-persecution-oppression in this translation (Source: translator's term choice, observable).
* **`the sacred month`** (ḥurum): inviolable time.
  * **Rendering:** "The sacred month for the sacred month; and sacrilege calls for retaliation" (2:194); 2:217 renders the cognate as "the Holy Month."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:217]` — the question "about fighting during the Holy Month" resumes this exact ruling two ruku later.
* **`spend in the cause of Allah`** (infāq fī sabīl Allāh): outlays made as worship.
  * **Rendering:** "And spend in the cause of Allah" (2:195); cf. "who will offer Allah a generous loan" (2:245) and "those who spend their wealth in Allah’s way" (2:261).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:254]` — "Spend from what We have given you, before a Day comes in which there is neither trading, nor friendship" — the eschatological deadline of the same verb.
* **`Hajj / Umrah`**: the two pilgrimages named together.
  * **Rendering:** "carry out the Hajj and the Umrah for Allah" (2:196); "timetables... for the Hajj" (2:189).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:158]` — Safa and Marwa "among the rites of Allah... whoever makes the Pilgrimage... or performs the Umrah" (ruku 19) — the running between them is licensed here first (verified).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the fighting permissions are classically read against the Meccan patience commands, with 2:190 as the first general permission (Source: consensus of classical tafsir on the âyat al-qitâl — verify).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the "approach homes from their backs" custom is classically explained as a pre-Islamic ihram practice (Source: classical reports on 2:189, e.g., in Ibn Kathir — agent-verified against translation context only).
