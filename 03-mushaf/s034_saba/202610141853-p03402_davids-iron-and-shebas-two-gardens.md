# David's softened iron and Sheba's two gardens (34:10–21)
**UID:** 202610141853
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 34:10–21 (Ruku 2 of 6 in Saba')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[34:10]` – `[34:21]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Two golden ages, one sermon: David and Solomon receive mountains, birds, iron, wind, sprites and monuments — and are told the workshop itself is worship ("work with appreciation"); Sheba receives twin gardens and safe, easy roads — and loses them to ingratitude; what survives both cases is the "steadfast and appreciative" reader and God's guardianship over all.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 6; the surah's favor case-study, running the praise-doctrine of ruku 1 through history and handing the audit result to ruku 3's zero-ownership argument. The centerpiece for the shukr note (34:15–19).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[34:10]`** "We bestowed upon David favor from Us: “O mountains, and birds: echo with him.” And We softened iron for him."
  * **Gloss:** Subjugation as favor (ni'ma-word, the shukr note's first leg): creation echoes his worship, and metallurgy is made pliable — grace that runs through craft.
* **`[34:11]`** "“Make coats of armor, and measure the links well; and work righteousness. I am Observant of everything you do.”"
  * **Gloss:** Craftsmanship-as-worship verified, with wording correction: "coats of armor" (not "full coats of mail"), "work righteousness" (not "righteous deeds"). Engineering precision ("measure the links well") and ethics ("work righteousness") are commanded in one breath, under "I am Observant" — the workshop is already on the Day's record (cf. 34:3).
* **`[34:12]`** "And for Solomon the wind—its outward journey was one month, and its return journey was one month. And We made a spring of tar flow for him. And there were sprites that worked under him, by the leave of his Lord. But whoever of them swerved from Our command, We make him taste of the punishment of the Inferno."
  * **Gloss:** Solomon's triple grant — wind, tar (unique corpus word for *qitr*), sprite-labor — with a leash: disobedience inside the empire is still punished; "by the leave of his Lord" keeps the sovereignty of ruku 1 intact even at peak miracle.
* **`[34:13]`** "They made for him whatever he wished: sanctuaries, statues, bowls like pools, and heavy cauldrons. “O House of David, work with appreciation,” but a few of My servants are appreciative."
  * **Gloss:** The wish-list of a Christian-era-scale workshop ("sanctuaries, statues" — the same word for statues that Abraham smashes at `[21:52–57]`, here built, not worshipped); then the ruku's thesis line: work *with appreciation* — and the sober census: "but a few of My servants are appreciative."
* **`[34:14]`** "Then, when We decreed death for him, nothing indicated his death to them except an earthworm eating at his staff. Then, when he fell down, it became clear to the sprites that, had they known the unseen, they would not have remained in the demeaning torment."
  * **Gloss:** The unseen (ghayb, claimed by the jinn) fails its own claim: a gnawed staff outed a death. The ruku's punchline against unseen-knowledge without praise.
* **`[34:15]`** "In Sheba’s homeland there used to be a wonder: two gardens, on the right, and on the left. “Eat of your Lord’s provision, and give thanks to Him.” A good land, and a forgiving Lord."
  * **Gloss:** The two gardens, verified — wording corrections: "a wonder" (not "a sign"), "give thanks" (not "be grateful"), "A good land" (not "a pleasant land"). Prosperity framed as audit: eat, and name the Giver; land and Lord are both called forgiving-generous before the fall.
* **`[34:16]`** "But they turned away, so We unleashed against them the flood of the dam; and We substituted their two gardens with two gardens of bitter fruits, thorny shrubs, and meager harvest."
  * **Gloss:** Turning (the covenant verb) → dam-flood → substitution: same plot, inverted harvest; the "dam" (*sadd*, same word as the protective dam of `[18:95]`) becomes the instrument of judgment.
* **`[34:17]`** "We thus penalized them for their ingratitude. Would We penalize any but the ungrateful?"
  * **Gloss:** The shukr note's centerpiece line: *kufr* rendered here, not "disbelief" but "ingratitude"/"ungrateful" — covering a recognized favor, exactly 16:83's definition; the rhetorical question universalizes the audit.
* **`[34:18]`** "Between them and the towns We had blessed, We placed prominent towns, and We made the travel between them easy. “Travel between them by night and day, in safety.”"
  * **Gloss:** Flashback to the prosperity's second face: infrastructure ("prominent towns... travel... easy") and security "by night and day" — provision *and* safety, both audit items.
* **`[34:19]`** "But they said, “Our Lord, lengthen the distances of our journeys.” They wronged themselves; so We made them history, and We scattered them in every direction. In this are lessons for every steadfast and appreciative person."
  * **Gloss:** The prayer that requests scarcity ("lengthen the distances") is answered with dispersal; "We made them history" — verbatim twin at `[23:44]`. The audience filter "every steadfast and appreciative person" is the corpus's fourth "patient+thankful" couplet (with 14:5, 31:31, 42:33 — a rendering set the shukr note counts at three; this surah adds the fourth — observable).
* **`[34:20]`** "Satan was correct in his assessment of them. They followed him, except for a group of believers."
  * **Gloss:** The Sheba collapse is re-read as Satan's wager cashed — with a remnant exempted; belief is a faction, not a nationality.
* **`[34:21]`** "He had no authority over them; except that We willed to distinguish him who believes in the Hereafter, from him who is doubtful about it. Your Lord is Guardian over all things."
  * **Gloss:** The theodicy clause: Satan's "authority" was only a screening test; "Your Lord is Guardian over all things" — verbatim twin of `[11:57]` — closes the ruku with custody, not absence.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`shukr cluster (give thanks / appreciation / appreciative)`** `[34:13,15,19]`:
  * **Rendering:** "give thanks to Him" (15); "work with appreciation... a few... are appreciative" (13); "steadfast and appreciative" (19) — one root, three renderings inside twelve verses, all favor-facing.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:56]`, `[2:243]` ("so that you may be appreciative"; "most people are not appreciative"); `[31:12]` (Luqman); `[16:112]` (the unappreciative town — Sheba's parable-form).
* **`kufr as ingratitude`** `[34:17]`:
  * **Rendering:** "for their ingratitude... any but the ungrateful?" — this edition's deliberate doxological choice: the Sheba case is un-thanking, not un-believing.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:83]` ("recognize Allah's blessing, but then deny it, as most of them are ungrateful"), `[16:55]` ("To show ingratitude"), `[39:7]` ("He does not approve ingratitude"), `[28:82]` ("the ungrateful never prosper").
* **`made them history`** `[34:19]`:
  * **Rendering:** "so We made them history, and We scattered them in every direction."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[23:44]` verbatim twin ("and made them history. So away with a people who do not believe") — a fixed formula for civilizational deletion.
* **`Guardian over all things`** `[34:21]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Your Lord is Guardian over all things" (*hafiz*).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[11:57]` verbatim twin (Hud's confidence); contrast `[6:104]`/`[6:107]` where the prophet is *not* made "a guardian over" the people — the office belongs to God alone.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical lists) — Israelite royal lore (David/Solomon) and South-Arabian tribal memory (Sheba) serve the Meccan audience's own historiography (Source: classical lists — verify in print).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** "the flood of the dam" is classically linked to the Marib dam and the dispersal of the Himyarite tribes; the corpus itself names no dam and no date. *Source: classical tafsir and later historiography — agent inference — verify.*
