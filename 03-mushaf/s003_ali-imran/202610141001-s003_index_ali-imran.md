# Surah 3: THE FAMILY OF IMRAN (Ali ‘Imran)
**UID:** 202610141001
**Type:** Index / Surah Map
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Medinan — per classical consensus; revealed after al-Baqarah, at revelation order 89 (a minority of transmitted reports tie isolated verses to later moments, e.g. the mubahala verses 3:61 to the Najran delegation ~9–10 AH, and much of 3:121–179 to Uhud, Shawwal 3 AH — Source: classical maghazi/tafsir reports as collected by Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Kathir).
* **Size:** 200 ayahs, 20 ruku; the third-longest surah, directly after al-Baqarah in the Mushaf.
* **Central theme:** the coherence of the covenant people amid scriptural disputes and defeat. The surah argues that one religion — surrender (islam) to the one God — has run through every prophet since Adam (3:19, 3:33, 3:64–68, 3:81–84), that People of the Book have "differed only after knowledge came to them" (3:19, 3:65–80), and that the new community must not repeat their fragmentation (3:103–105). Its hermeneutic core, 3:7, sorts the Book's own verses into definitive and unspecific and diagnoses division-seeking as a disease of the heart. The longest sustained movement (3:121–179) is the Uhud narrative: a theodicy of defeat that re-reads the rout as test, distinction, and pardon.

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Creed & hermeneutic (1–20):** the letters, the Living One, the Book that confirms prior Books (3:3–4), and the decisive verse 3:7 that names deviation-seeking as the heart's disease; ends "Religion with Allah is Islam" (3:19).
* **The family of Imran in flesh (31–63):** the surah's namesake narrative — Mary, Zechariah, John, Jesus — recast so that the Messiah's family ends where the surah began: "worship none but Allah... that is a straight path" (3:51), "there is no god but Allah" (3:62).
* **The Najran argument (64–80):** the common word (3:64), Abraham claimed by no sect (3:65–68), and covenant integrity vs. Scripture-twisting (3:71–80) — the dialogue unit in the middle of the covenant debate.
* **One covenant, one House (81–101):** the prophets' universal pledge (3:81) and the charter of the believers' community, anchored at the first House at Bekka (3:96–97).
* **The community's constitution (102–120):** rope, ummah, midwifery of good and evil (3:103–110), then the two counter-economies of the heart — spending that is eaten by frost vs. hoarded wealth (3:116–120).
* **Uhud theodicy (121–179):** the surah's longest movement — deployment (3:121), Badr memory (3:13), faltering, flight, sorrow, security, pardon (3:152–155), the martyrs alive (3:169–171), response-after-wound (3:172–174) — defeat re-read as distinction and mercy.
* **Eschatological close (180–200):** hoards encircle (3:180), death tasted by every soul (3:185), the reflective prayer of the people of understanding (3:190–194), the answer to every worker male and female (3:195), and the last command: patience, mutual patience, firmness, taqwa (3:200).
