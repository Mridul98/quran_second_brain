# Taken up, not killed: the likeness of Jesus is as Adam (3:55–63)
**UID:** 202610141007
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 3:55–63 (Ruku 6 of 20 in Ali ‘Imran)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[3:55]` – `[3:63]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Allah speaks to Jesus directly (3:55): his life is terminated and he is raised — not crucified out of powerlessness — and his followers are ordered above the disbelievers until the Return, when disputed truth is judged; the ruku then compresses the whole Christological argument into one analogy (3:59: Jesus' likeness before Allah is Adam's — dust, then "Be") and, if dispute continues, issues the mubahala challenge: call our children and your children and invoke Allah's curse on the liars (3:61).
* **Placement:** Closing panel of the Imran narrative (33–63); hands the surah from narrative straight into the Najran dialogue of ruku 7.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[3:55]`** "Allah said, “O Jesus, I am terminating your life, and raising you to Me, and clearing you of those who disbelieve. And I will make those who follow you superior to those who disbelieve, until the Day of Resurrection."
  * **Gloss:** Divine first-person speech — three verbs (terminate, raise, clear) reverse the kill-plot of 3:54.
* **`[3:55]`** "Then to Me is your return; then I will judge between you regarding what you were disputing."
  * **Gloss:** The dispute itself is adjourned to God's bench — no human verdict here.
* **`[3:56]`** "As for those who disbelieve, I will punish them with a severe punishment, in this world and the next, and they will have no helpers."
  * **Gloss:** The two-world punishment clause (cf. 3:22's no-saviors line).
* **`[3:57]`** "And as for those who believe and do good works, He will give them their rewards in full. Allah does not love the unjust."
  * **Gloss:** Full reward — the same "paid in full" grammar as 3:25.
* **`[3:58]`** "This is what We recite to you of the Verses and the Wise Reminder."
  * **Gloss:** The recitation frame: the narrative just told is itself revealed speech, "the Wise Reminder."
* **`[3:59]`** "The likeness of Jesus in Allah’s sight is that of Adam: He created him from dust, then said to him, “Be,” and he was."
  * **Gloss:** The analogy that dissolves the exception: if fatherless proves divinity, Adam out-proves him.
* **`[3:60]`** "The truth is from your Lord, so do not be of those who doubt."
  * **Gloss:** One line of epistemic closure — truth's source names the response.
* **`[3:61]`** "And if anyone disputes with you about him, after the knowledge that has come to you, say, “Come, let us call our children and your children, and our women and your women, and ourselves and yourselves, and let us invoke Allah’s curse on the liars.”"
  * **Gloss:** The mubahala: mutual imprecation with the dearest lives lined up on each side — the only dispute-resolution rite left when argument is exhausted.
* **`[3:62]`** "This is the narrative of truth: there is no god but Allah. Allah is the Mighty, the Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's own verdict on its story — qisas al-haqq — closing on the shahada (echo of 3:18).
* **`[3:63]`** "But if they turn away—Allah knows the corrupt."
  * **Gloss:** Withdrawal left to divine knowledge; no compulsion clause, matching 3:20's duty-to-convey.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`tawaffa` / "terminating your life"**: 3:55 — Itani renders mutawaffika as life-termination, not "causing to sleep."
  * **Rendering:** whether this means natural death or sleep-before-return is a live classical disagreement; the translation picks the death-read while keeping "raising you to Me" (Source: dispute in al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on 3:55 — verify).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:42]` — "Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that have not died during their sleep" (verified) — the root's own vocabulary holds death and sleep together, which is why 3:55's rendering is a theological choice; the "raising to Me" verb for Jesus is stated only here in the corpus (observable).
* **`dhikr al-hakim` / "the Wise Reminder"**: 3:58.
  * **Rendering:** "Reminder" (dhikr) as a name of the Quran, here with "Wise."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:44]` "And We revealed to you the Reminder"; `[65:10]` "Allah has sent down to you a Reminder" — the honorific family without the "Wise" pairing, which is unique to this line (observable).
* **`mubahala` / “curse on the liars” — "invoke Allah's curse on the liars"**: 3:61.
  * **Elsewhere:** the imprecation form (witness-curse) is used in the spouses rite `[24:7]` ("that Allah's curse be upon him, if he is a liar" — verified) — the same legal grammar turned outward at a community dispute.
* **`qidam/haqq` — "the narrative of truth"**: 3:62 vs 3:58's "Verses and the Wise Reminder": two self-labels of the same recited unit.
