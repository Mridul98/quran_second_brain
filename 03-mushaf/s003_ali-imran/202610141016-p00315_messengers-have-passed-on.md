# Muhammad is no more than a messenger: the creed that survives a death (3:144–148)
**UID:** 202610141016
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 3:144–148 (Ruku 15 of 20 in Ali ‘Imran)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[3:144]` – `[3:148]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Triggered by the battlefield rumor that the Prophet was dead, this short ruku states the surah's most quoted line: Muhammad is no more than a messenger; messengers have passed on before him — will you turn on your heels if he dies or is killed? (3:144); death itself is by Allah's leave at a predetermined time, and every desire gets its partial wage, but the appreciative will be rewarded (3:145); the answer is the precedent-gallery: how many prophets fought with crowds of godly men who neither wavered, weakened, nor surrendered to grief at the wounds — whose entire strategy was one prayer, and who won both worlds (3:146–148).
* **Placement:** The theodicy's hinge: the 3:139–143 "do not waver" commands now receive the portrait of the waver-less — the companions of prophets past, with the same word for wavering (3:146) as the command (3:139).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[3:144]`** "Muhammad is no more than a messenger. Messengers have passed on before him."
  * **Gloss:** The two-sentence creed of succession — prophet as function, not foundation.
* **`[3:144]`** "If he dies or gets killed, will you turn on your heels? He who turns on his heels will not harm Allah in any way. And Allah will reward the appreciative."
  * **Gloss:** Heel-turning named as the real danger; the harmlessness clause frees the mission from the man; gratitude (shukr) enters as the reward-currency.
* **`[3:145]`** "No soul can die except by Allah’s leave, at a predetermined time. Whoever desires the reward of the world, We will give him some of it; and whoever desires the reward of the Hereafter, We will give him some of it; and We will reward the appreciative."
  * **Gloss:** Death is scheduled, so a rumor cannot kill; both rewards are granted "some of" — but only the appreciative are rewarded.
* **`[3:146]`** "How many a prophet fought alongside him numerous godly people? They did not waver for what afflicted them in the cause of Allah, nor did they weaken, nor did they give in."
  * **Gloss:** The exemplar army: three negations — no wavering, no weakness, no submission to pressure (sirr — "gave in"/surrendered inwardly).
* **`[3:147]`** "Their only words were, “Our Lord, forgive us our offences, and our excesses in our conduct, and strengthen our foothold, and help us against the disbelieving people.”"
  * **Gloss:** The battle-dua of the godly: forgiveness, footing, help — the 3:8 prayer pattern re-issued at the front line.
* **`[3:148]`** "So Allah gave them the reward of this world, and the excellent reward of the Hereafter. Allah loves the doers of good."
  * **Gloss:** The double wage, and the same love-clause as 3:134 — the muhsinun's ledger is balanced.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`inqilab/heels`**: "turn on your heels" — 3:144, then 3:149.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:143]` the qiblah-turn distinguishes "those who turn on their heels" (verified); `[6:71]` "turn back on our heels after Allah has guided us" (verified); `[8:48]` Satan himself "turned on his heels" (verified) — the idiom runs from creed-crises to routs.
* **`ajal musamma` / "predetermined time"**: 3:145.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:42]` "He releases the others until a predetermined time" (verified); `[11:104]`; `[7:34]` "For every nation is an appointed time" (verified) — death's schedule across individual and national registers.
* **`shakir` / "the appreciative"**: 3:144–145.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:158]` "Allah is Appreciative and Cognizant" (verified); `[8:26]` "so that you may be thankful" (verified) — shukr is this surah's Badr-memory virtue (3:123's "that you may be thankful").
* **`siddiyyin` / "godly people"**: 3:146 — rendered "godly"; the cognate title "woman of truth" for Mary at `[5:75]` (trace) — same root, two registers; "numerous godly people" as a battle-corps class is unique to this line.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[5:75]` — "The Messiah son of Mary was only a messenger, before whom other Messengers had passed away" (verified) — the identical formula applied to Jesus: the line that de-divinizes the Messiah is now aimed at the community's own grief.
* **Within surah:** `[3:139]` ↔ `[3:146]` — "do not waver" commanded, then the godly who did not waver exhibited; `[3:144]` ↔ `[3:149]` — heel-turning restated two ruku on; `[3:123/3:126]` ↔ `[3:145]` — Badr's lesson: victory came from Allah, not from the messenger's presence.
* **Parallel:** `[21:35]` — "Every soul will taste death. We burden you with adversity and prosperity—a test" (verified) — the same death-and-desire grammar that 3:185 will close the surah with.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the ruku is classically bound to the Uhud rumor that Muhammad was slain, and to the companion who, hearing it, lost resolve (the heel-turning idiom's live referent).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** reports in Ibn Ishaq / Ibn Kathir on 3:144 tie the verse to the day's misinformation (Source: transmitted maghazi reports — verify; the line itself was famously recited by Abu Bakr after the Prophet's actual death in 11 AH per sahih reports — Source: classical narrative, verify).
