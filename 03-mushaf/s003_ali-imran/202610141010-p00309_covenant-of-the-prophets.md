# The covenant of the prophets: believe in the messenger who verifies (3:81–91)
**UID:** 202610141010
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 3:81–91 (Ruku 9 of 20 in Ali ‘Imran)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[3:81]` – `[3:91]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Before anyone chose a religion, a covenant was taken: every prophet, given scripture and wisdom, pledged to believe and support the messenger who comes verifying what they hold (3:81) — an oath the prophets themselves affirmed and witnessed (3:81b), breaking which is mere deceit (3:82); the ruku then universalizes: all creation has surrendered to Allah, willingly or unwillingly (3:83), the believers' credo recites the whole prophetic inventory without distinction (3:84), and any other religion will not be accepted (3:85); the closing threat-paragraph warns against the hard case — belief, then witnessed rejection (3:86–88) — with the repentance opening (3:89) and the death-in-disbelief ransom clause (3:90–91) completing the covenant's fine print.
* **Placement:** Pivot between the Najran dialogue (64–80) and the community charter (92–120): the single-thread covenant that binds Torah, Gospel, and Quran.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[3:81]`** "Allah received the covenant of the prophets, “Inasmuch as I have given you of scripture and wisdom; should a messenger come to you verifying what you have, you shall believe in him, and support him.”"
  * **Gloss:** The metacovenant: prophets pledge to back the next verifier — succession written into the contract.
* **`[3:81]`** "He said, “Do you affirm My covenant and take it upon yourselves?” They said, “We affirm it.” He said, “Then bear witness, and I am with you among the witnesses.”"
  * **Gloss:** The oath scene with its mutual witnessing — God enlists the prophets as witnesses to their own yes.
* **`[3:82]`** "Whoever turns away after that—these are the deceitful."
  * **Gloss:** One word verdict: the defaulter is a fisq-class cheat, not a mere dissenter.
* **`[3:83]`** "Do they desire other than the religion of Allah, when to Him has submitted everything in the heavens and the earth, willingly or unwillingly, and to Him they will be returned?"
  * **Gloss:** The cosmic submission argument: the universe already performs the islam they debate — with a return clause.
* **`[3:84]`** "Say, “We believe in Allah, and in what was revealed to us; and in what was revealed to Abraham, and Ishmael, and Isaac, and Jacob, and the Patriarchs; and in what was given to Moses, and Jesus, and the prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them, and to Him we submit.”"
  * **Gloss:** The credo roll-call — nearly word-for-word the Baqarah liturgy (2:136) re-sung with a closing "we submit."
* **`[3:85]`** "Whoever seeks other than Islam as a religion, it will not be accepted from him, and in the Hereafter he will be among the losers."
  * **Gloss:** The acceptance rule; *khusr* (loss) framed as the account that cannot be paid.
* **`[3:86]`** "How will Allah guide a people who disbelieved after having believed, and had witnessed that the Messenger is true, and the clear proofs had come to them? Allah does not guide the unjust people."
  * **Gloss:** The guidance question after willful exit — evidence-burned belief cannot be re-guided on the same terms.
* **`[3:87]`** "Those—their penalty is that upon them falls the curse of Allah, and of the angels, and of all mankind."
  * **Gloss:** A three-court curse: God, angels, humanity — the witnesses of 3:18 now testifying against.
* **`[3:88]`** "Remaining in it eternally, without their punishment being eased from them, and without being reprieved."
  * **Gloss:** No mitigation, no respite — the two stay-clauses.
* **`[3:89]`** "Except those who repent afterwards, and reform; for Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The famous exception: even this exit door has a hinge — tawba plus islah.
* **`[3:90]`** "As for those who disbelieve after having believed, then plunge deeper into disbelief, their repentance will not be accepted; these are the lost."
  * **Gloss:** The escalation clause: persistence past the second exit forfeits the third.
* **`[3:91]`** "As for those who disbelieve and die disbelievers, even the earth full of gold would not be accepted from any of them, were he to offer it for ransom. These will have a painful torment, and will have no saviors."
  * **Gloss:** Earth-as-gold ransom rejected — the wealth-motif of 3:10 returns in its final form: nothing buys the Day.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`mithaq` / "covenant of the prophets"**: 3:81.
  * **Rendering:** "received the covenant" (akhadha) — the taking-hold verb.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:7]` "We received a pledge from the prophets... a solemn pledge" (verified) — the twin verse naming the same five-prophet circuit; `[2:83]` the Israel-covenant clause; `[7:172]` the primordial testimony — the covenant note's three layers, all present-tense here.
* **`aslama` / submission grammar**: 3:83–85.
  * **Rendering:** "submitted" (3:83), "to Him we submit" (3:84), "Islam as a religion" (3:85) — one root, three registers: cosmic, creedal, legal.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:136]` identical credo (verified); `[3:20]` "Have you surrendered?" — the surah's own earlier call.
* **`la'na` / "curse"**: 3:87.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:44]` "The curse of Allah is upon the wrongdoers" (verified); `[2:159]` "those who suppress the proofs and the guidance We have revealed... Allah curses them, and the cursers curse them" (verified) — concealment earns the curse; here it is post-witness rejection; the withdrawal-of-mercy register.
* **`taqallus` / "plunge deeper into disbelief"**: 3:90 — Itani's rendering of azdafar kufran: increase/discover-more of kufr; the verb is unique to this line in the corpus (observable).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the covenant-of-prophets verse functions as the surah's inter-scriptural constitution: verification, belief, support — three duties older than any sect.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 3:86–89 are classically read both generally (any apostate) and occasion-ally (reports about individuals who believed, left, and returned — e.g. in Ibn Kathir on 3:86, transmitting the case of al-Mughira's story and similar; Source: classical asbab reports — verify, and note the exception of 3:89 makes eventual return open in any reading).
