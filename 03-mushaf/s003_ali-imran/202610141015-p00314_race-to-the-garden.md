# Race to the Garden as wide as the heavens: riba refused, days alternated (3:130–143)
**UID:** 202610141015
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 3:130–143 (Ruku 14 of 20 in Ali ‘Imran)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[3:130]` – `[3:143]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Mid-battle, the surah inserts its economic and eschatological will: do not eat usury doubled and redoubled, fear Allah, obey — and then a sprint-verse: race to forgiveness and a Garden as wide as the heavens and the earth (3:130–133), whose residents are defined by five heart-habits — giving in ease and hardship, suppressing anger, forgiving people, and rebounding from sin without persistence (3:134–135); reward grammar then moves to history: travel the earth and see the fate of deniers (3:137–138); and finally, straight back to the battlefield's meaning: do not waver — you are the superiors if you believe — because wounds were dealt both ways and the days are alternated to test and distinguish (3:139–141), paradise first priced in striving and steadfastness (3:142), against the old wish for death now faced eye to eye (3:143).
* **Placement:** The theodicy's thesis ruku: between deployment (121–129) and mourning (144+), it explains why the alternation of days exists at all.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[3:130]`** "O you who believe! Do not feed on usury, compounded over and over, and fear Allah, so that you may prosper."
  * **Gloss:** The doubling prohibition — the surah's riba clause, placed mid-Uhud as community hygiene.
* **`[3:131]`** "And guard yourselves against the Fire that is prepared for the disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** The protective imperative pairing with prosper — same construction as 2:278's "fear Allah and forgo what remains of usury" (verified).
* **`[3:132]`** "And obey Allah and the Messenger, that you may obtain mercy."
  * **Gloss:** Obey → mercy: the chain of 3:31–32 at battlefield pitch.
* **`[3:133]`** "And race towards forgiveness from your Lord, and a Garden as wide as the heavens and the earth, prepared for the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The sprint-verse; the Garden's width is the cosmos's own dimensions.
* **`[3:134]`** "Those who give in prosperity and adversity, and those who restrain anger, and those who forgive people. Allah loves the doers of good."
  * **Gloss:** Three social habits of the muhsinun — spend when it hurts, choke back rage, pardon.
* **`[3:135]`** "And those who, when they commit an indecency or wrong themselves, remember Allah and ask forgiveness for their sins—and who forgives sins except Allah? And they do not persist in their wrongdoing while they know."
  * **Gloss:** The rebound clause: sin is assumed; the virtue is non-persistence with knowledge.
* **`[3:136]`** "Those—their reward is forgiveness from their Lord, and gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein forever. How excellent is the reward of the workers."
  * **Gloss:** Forgiveness opens the reward and gardens close it — work rewarded, wages praised.
* **`[3:137]`** "Many societies have passed away before you. So travel the earth and note the fate of the deniers."
  * **Gloss:** The siyaaha-command: tourism as theology of outcomes (6:11's twin — verified).
* **`[3:138]`** "This is a proclamation to humanity, and guidance, and advice for the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The surah labels its own history-section a public notice.
* **`[3:139]`** "And do not waver, nor feel remorse. You are the superior ones, if you are believers."
  * **Gloss:** The command to the morning-after morale: superiority conditional on belief, not outcome.
* **`[3:140]`** "If a wound afflicts you, a similar wound has afflicted the others. Such days We alternate between the people, that Allah may know those who believe, and take martyrs from among you. Allah does not love the evildoers."
  * **Gloss:** The reciprocity of wounds + the alternating-days theodicy + martyrdom as divine selection.
* **`[3:141]`** "So that Allah may prove those who believe, and eliminate the disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** The same test, two directions: purification inside, removal outside.
* **`[3:142]`** "Or do you expect to enter Paradise, before Allah has distinguished those among you who strive, and before He has distinguished the steadfast?"
  * **Gloss:** The entry-fee question — jihad and sabr named as the identifiers.
* **`[3:143]`** "You used to wish for death before you have faced it. Now you have seen it before your own eyes."
  * **Gloss:** The cruel irony: the warriors' old boast (cf. 2:94, 62:6's taunt — verified) answered literally.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`riba mudafa'` / "usury, compounded over and over"**: 3:130.
  * **Rendering:** the doubling adverb (dilan muda'afatan) — pre-Islamic riba's exact mechanism per classical tafsir (Source: consensus gloss, Ibn Kathir on 3:130 — verify).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:275–279]` (the full riba-war passage, verified), `[30:39]` "will not multiply with Allah" (verified) — 3:130 is the surah's one-line version of the two-economies note's thesis.
* **`sarah/`"race"**: 3:133 — "race towards forgiveness".
  * **Elsewhere:** `[57:21]` "Race towards forgiveness from your Lord; and a Garden as vast as the heavens and the earth" (verified — nearly verbatim, and 57:21 is classically read as this very verse's Hadid recitation); `[2:148]` "race towards goodness" (verified); `[23:61]` "It is they who race towards goodness. It is they who will reach it first" (verified).
* **`katm al-ghayz` / "restrain anger"**: 3:134 — unique rendering in the corpus (observable via `rg "anger"` — the only "restrain anger" line).
  * **Elsewhere:** the five-habit portrait pairs with `[2:177]`'s "patiently persevere in persecution" (verified) and answers `[1:7]` from the other side: the Fatihah's category "those against whom there is anger" is precisely the anger-unrestrained (observable corpus link).
* **`tabdīl al-ayyām` / "Such days We alternate"**: 3:140 — the alternation root (dawla) recurs of wealth: `[59:7]` "so that it may not circulate solely between the wealthy among you" (verified) — the same root's economy version of the battlefield clause: rotation prevents monopoly, human or divine.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the riba clause at 3:130 sits inside Uhud's long recitation, binding the community's economic sins to its military outcomes — a classical reading of the battle as consequence of the archers' world-love (3:145, 3:152).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 3:133's Garden width cited by reports as revealed in answer to a companion's wonder (Source: transmitted material in Ibn Kathir on 3:133 — verify); 57:21's parallel makes it at least a double-inscription.
