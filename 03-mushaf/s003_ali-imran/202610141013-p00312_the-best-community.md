# The best community and the frost that eats the spend (3:110–120)
**UID:** 202610141013
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 3:110–120 (Ruku 12 of 20 in Ali ‘Imran)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[3:110]` – `[3:120]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The identity sentence — "You are the best community that ever emerged for humanity" (3:110) — is immediately defined by function (advocate moral, forbid immoral, believe), not ethnicity, and softened by candor: among the People of Scripture are believers, most are sinners (3:110b); they will harass but not durably harm (3:111), bound by double ropes — from Allah and from people (3:112), unless they are the upright night-praying community, whom no good will be denied (3:113–115). Then the mirror flips to threat: disbelievers' wealth avails nothing (3:116), their spending is a frost-eaten harvest (3:117), and insiders must not take non-insiders as confidants who wish you harm (3:118–120) — endurance plus taqwa blunts even their schemes (3:120).
* **Placement:** Direct consequence of the rope ruku: unity (103) now gets its missiology and its border control; the wealth-parables of 3:116–117 re-open the economics of 3:92 before the Uhud narrative tests it in blood.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[3:110]`** "You are the best community that ever emerged for humanity: you advocate what is moral, and forbid what is immoral, and believe in Allah. Had the People of the Scripture believed, it would have been better for them. Among them are the believers, but most of them are sinners."
  * **Gloss:** Elect status = elected service; the very verse brackets its own rival community with fairness.
* **`[3:111]`** "They will do you no harm, beyond insulting you. And if they fight you, they will turn around and flee, then they will not be helped."
  * **Gloss:** A down-sizing: their real weapon is words; armed aggression self-defeats.
* **`[3:112]`** "They shall be humiliated wherever they are encountered, except through a rope from Allah, and a rope from the people; and they incurred wrath from Allah, and were stricken with misery."
  * **Gloss:** The humiliation-with-exception clause — and the rope idiom returns: divine covenant plus human treaty.
* **`[3:112]`** "That is because they rejected Allah’s revelations, and killed the prophets unjustly. That is because they rebelled and committed aggression."
  * **Gloss:** The charge-sheet repeats verbatim the 2:61 formula — sin named twice, with its two roots (rebellion, transgression).
* **`[3:113]`** "They are not alike. Among the People of the Scripture is a community that is upright; they recite Allah’s revelations throughout the night, and they prostrate themselves."
  * **Gloss:** The famous "they are not alike" — night-reciting prostrators split the category.
* **`[3:114]`** "They believe in Allah and the Last Day, and advocate righteousness and forbid evil, and are quick to do good deeds. These are among the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** Their three traits are this surah's own community charter (3:104, 3:110) — the night-community is not exotic, it is the template.
* **`[3:115]`** "Whatever good they do, they will not be denied it. Allah knows the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The non-denial of good across lines — the surah's most generous accounting rule.
* **`[3:116]`** "As for those who disbelieve, neither their possessions nor their children will avail them anything against Allah. These are the inhabitants of the Fire, abiding therein forever."
  * **Gloss:** 3:10 re-quoted nearly verbatim — the bookend that brackets the community section (102–120).
* **`[3:117]`** "The parable of what they spend in this worldly life is that of a frosty wind that strikes the harvest of a people who have wronged their souls, and destroys it. Allah did not wrong them, but they wronged their own selves."
  * **Gloss:** The anti-infaq parable: spend that multiplies nothing — a night-frost on self-injured farmers; loss is self-inflicted, never divine.
* **`[3:118]`** "O you who believe! Do not befriend outsiders who never cease to wish you harm. They love to see you suffer. Hatred has already appeared from their mouths, but what their hearts conceal is worse. We have made the messages clear for you, if you understand."
  * **Gloss:** Border control on intimacy (cf. 3:28) — concealed malice exceeds spoken malice.
* **`[3:119]`** "There you are, you love them, but they do not love you, and you believe in the entire scripture. And when they meet you, they say, “We believe;” but when they are alone, they bite their fingers in rage at you. Say, “Die in your rage; Allah knows what is within the hearts.”"
  * **Gloss:** The asymmetry named: you believe all scripture, they offer counterfeit belief and finger-biting rage — the whole Book vs the day-end defection (3:72).
* **`[3:120]`** "If something good happens to you, it upsets them; but if something bad befalls you, they rejoice at it. But if you persevere and maintain righteousness, their schemes will not harm you at all. Allah comprehends what they do."
  * **Gloss:** The formula sabr + taqwa as armor — this surah's Uhud equation (3:125, 3:179, 3:200) stated in advance.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`khayra ummah` / "best community"**: 3:110 — "ummah" rendered "community"; the phrase occurs nowhere else (observable; cf. `[2:143]` "moderate community" — the same word's other honorific, verified in 2:143).
* **`habl` (rope) again**: 3:112 — "a rope from Allah, and a rope from the people."
  * **Elsewhere:** 3:103's rope of Allah is the inner covenant; 3:112's double rope is the protected minority's cover; classically: dhimma via God's covenant and the treaty's terms (Source: Ibn Kathir on 3:112 — verify).
* **`sayb` / "frosty wind"**: 3:117 — the frost-parable unique here (observable); its twin in the spending-parables cluster is `[2:264–265]` (the smooth-rock garden vs the hillside garden — verified 2:265).
* **`mustabiqun` / "quick to do good deeds"**: 3:114 — the speed-verb (istibaq, racing ahead), part of the "virtuous rivalry" motif: `[2:148]` "Therefore, race towards goodness" and `[23:61]` "It is they who race towards goodness" (both verified) — and 3:133's race-to-the-Garden is this trait's eschatological frame.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the community-inventory of 3:110–115 answers both the believers' triumphalism and their despair — a census, not a cheer.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 3:110's "best community" is cited in classical debates on the ummah's witness-bearing at the Day (Ibn Kathir on 3:110 gathers the reports; Source: consensus readings + agent summary — verify).
