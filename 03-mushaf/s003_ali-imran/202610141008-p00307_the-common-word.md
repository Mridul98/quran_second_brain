# The common word: no lords besides Allah (3:64–71)
**UID:** 202610141008
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 3:64–71 (Ruku 7 of 20 in Ali ‘Imran)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[3:64]` – `[3:71]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's diplomat's ruku: a say-command invitation to People of the Book to a "term common between us and you" — worship none but Allah, associate nothing, take no lords besides Him — with witness-bearing as the fallback if they turn (3:64); then the Abraham argument in three moves: the sects cannot claim him (Torah and Gospel postdate him, 3:65), he was neither Jewish nor Christian but a submitting monotheist (3:67), and the people closest to him are those who followed him *and this prophet* (3:68) — ending on the complaint that some would love to lead the believers astray while confounding truth with falsehood (3:69–71).
* **Placement:** Opens the Najran dialogue movement (64–80), the practical test of 3:7's hermeneutic: here is one verse no sect can unspecific away.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[3:64]`** "Say, “O People of the Book, come to terms common between us and you: that we worship none but Allah, and that we associate nothing with Him, and that none of us takes others as lords besides Allah.”"
  * **Gloss:** The three-clause platform: exclusive worship, no association, no lordship outsourcing — tawhid in political-religious vocabulary.
* **`[3:64]`** "And if they turn away, say, “Bear witness that we have submitted.”"
  * **Gloss:** If the common word is refused, the only remaining act is testimony: record us as submitted.
* **`[3:65]`** "O People of the Book! Why do you argue about Abraham, when the Torah and the Gospel were not revealed until after him? Will you not reason?"
  * **Gloss:** Chronology as referee: the two later scriptures cannot own the earlier man.
* **`[3:66]`** "Here you are—you argue about things you know, but why do you argue about things you do not know? Allah knows, and you do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The epistemic scolding 3:7 promised — argument past the boundary of knowledge.
* **`[3:67]`** "Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian, but he was a Monotheist, a Muslim. And he was not of the Polytheists."
  * **Gloss:** The four-term identity set: not (Jew/Christian/polytheist) — but hanif and muslim.
* **`[3:68]`** "The people most deserving of Abraham are those who followed him, and this prophet, and those who believe. Allah is the Guardian of the believers."
  * **Gloss:** Kinship by following, not blood; the Guardian clause re-bases community identity.
* **`[3:69]`** "A party of the People of the Book would love to lead you astray, but they only lead themselves astray, and they do not realize it."
  * **Gloss:** The boomerang of misguidance — the astray-intention returns to its sender, unseen by him.
* **`[3:70]`** "O People of the Book! Why do you reject the revelations of Allah, even as you witness?"
  * **Gloss:** Their witness (to the prophet in their books) makes the rejection criminal, not ignorant.
* **`[3:71]`** "O People of the Book! Why do you confound the truth with falsehood, and knowingly conceal the truth?"
  * **Gloss:** Two offenses: mixing, and hiding with knowledge — the covenant-breach vocabulary proper.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sawa'` / "terms common"**: rendered "come to terms common between us and you" (3:64) — the "level place between us and you" (classical gloss).
  * **Rendering:** the phrase "terms common" occurs only here in the corpus; the cognate is rendered "equal" in `[41:10]` "four days, equal for those who ask" (verified).
* **`hanif` / "Monotheist"**: 3:67.
  * **Rendering:** capital-M "Monotheist" consistently for the Abraham- epithet.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:135]`, `[6:79]`, `[6:161]`, `[4:125]`, `[16:120]`, `[16:123]`, `[10:105]` — always Abrahamic orientation away from sect-ownership and idolatry.
* **`arbab` / "lords"**: "takes others as lords besides Allah" (3:64); cf. 3:80 "angels and the prophets as lords."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:31]` "They have taken their rabbis and their priests as lords instead of Allah" — the canonical twin, classically tied to the same Najran dialogue (Source: Tirmidhi-graded report cited by Ibn Kathir on 9:31 — verify); `[18:50]`, `[45:10]` for the wider lordship-outside-God motif.
* **`yalbisu` / "confound the truth with falsehood"**: 3:71.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:42]` "do not mix truth with falsehood, and do not conceal the truth while you know" — same double command, Baqarah's legal register.
