# Lowering voices and auditing news: manners at the Prophet's door (49:1–10)
**UID:** 202610142112
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 49:1–10 (Ruku 1 of 2 in al-Hujurat)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[49:1]` – `[49:10]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Two "O you who believe!" commands about the Prophet's presence (1–2) open a ruku that treats volume, news, and civil strife as one continuous object — the heart tested for piety (3) is the same heart that verifies a troublemaker's report (6), loves faith (7), and reconciles between brothers (9–10).
* **Placement:** The surah's first movement: micro-ethics of voice and information, ending on the fraternal statute that ruku 2 then protects with five prohibitions against the tongue.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[49:1]`** "O you who believe! Do not place your opinions above that of Allah and His Messenger, and fear Allah. Allah is Hearing and Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Command #1 — the preemption ban: no opinion "ahead of" Allah and His Messenger; taqwa brackets it ("fear Allah"), and the Hearing/Knowing pair is the surveillance the ethics assume.
* **`[49:2]`** "O you who believe! Do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet, and do not speak loudly to him, as you speak loudly to one another, lest your works be in vain without you realizing."
  * **Gloss:** Command #2 — volume as a salvation-risk: "lest your works be in vain without you realizing" (task claim ✓ — Itani's exact clause; the hubt-family: `[9:17]`, `[39:65]` — "your works will be in vain" for idolatry). The most alarming etiquette-verse in the corpus: a manner can abolish a life's deeds, unseen.
* **`[49:3]`** "Those who lower their voices before Allah’s Messenger—those are they whose hearts Allah has tested for piety. They will have forgiveness and a great reward."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-portrait: the voice-lowers as the heart-tested (imtihan — Allah "tested" qulubuhum "for piety"; cf. `[3:154]` "purifies what is in your hearts" — rg-verified); reward-triple forgiveness/great.
* **`[49:4]`** "Those who call you from behind the chambers—most of them do not understand."
  * **Gloss:** The title-verse (hujurāt — corpus-unique, rg): calling across architecture is calling across a boundary; "most of them do not understand" — the etiquette-failures diagnosed as comprehension-failures, not malice.
* **`[49:5]`** "Had they remained patient until you came out to them, it would have been better for them. But Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** Sabr as the correct social physics — wait for the door; then the surah's habit: divine forgiveness closes the rebuke (contrast 2:63+ hard closings).
* **`[49:6]`** "O you who believe! If a troublemaker brings you any news, investigate, lest you harm people out of ignorance, and you become regretful for what you have done."
  * **Gloss:** THE verification verse — epistemic ethics in one sentence: fasiq-report → tabayyan → cost-model (unwitting harm, then nadama "regretful"). The imperative is corpus-rare: the only other "investigate" is `[4:94]` (twice, on battlefield intelligence — rg); here it is civic. "Troublemaker" = the same word the hypocrites get in `[2:12]`.
* **`[49:7]`** "And know that among you is the Messenger of Allah. Had he obeyed you in many things, you would have suffered hardship. But Allah has given you the love of faith, and adorned it in your hearts, and made disbelief, mischief, and rebellion hateful to you. These are the rightly guided."
  * **Gloss:** "Know that" — the epistemology verse becomes psychology: faith *loved*, *adorned* (tazyin — the heart as interior decoration, cf. 49:14's "faith has not yet entered"); the three hated opposites: kufr, fusūq ("mischief"), 'isyān ("rebellion" — elsewhere `[17:41]`, `[5:68]`, `[6:110]` — rg); "These are the rightly guided" (verdict-form unique to 49:7; the phrase itself recurs at `[2:186]` "that they may be rightly guided", `[34:24]` "either you, or we, are rightly guided").
* **`[49:8]`** "A Grace and Favor from Allah. Allah is Knowing and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** An acclamation-clause (independently numbered): the whole disposition of 7 is fadl — unearned, yet "Knowing and Wise," i.e., not arbitrary.
* **`[49:9]`** "If two groups of believers fight each other, reconcile between them. But if one group aggresses against the other, fight the aggressing group until it complies with Allah’s command. Once it has complied, reconcile between them with justice, and be equitable. Allah loves the equitable."
  * **Gloss:** Civil-strife statute: three imperatives (islah → qital against the baghī → islah bi-'adl); "Allah loves the equitable" — verbatim twin `[5:42]`, `[60:8]` (rg-complete). Violence is bounded by compliance, and the exit is always reconciliation.
* **`[49:10]`** "The believers are brothers, so reconcile between your brothers, and remain conscious of Allah, so that you may receive mercy."
  * **Gloss:** The ontology before the law: brotherhood *is*, therefore reconciliation *must* (task claim ✓ verbatim). The taqwa clause (aww 49:10 "remain conscious") names mercy as the product — kinship, surgery, watchfulness, rahmah.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **ghalaba 'ala (raise above):** 49:2 "Do not raise your voices above the voice of the Prophet"; 49:3's antonym "lower their voices" (khafadū).
  * **Rendering:** Itani keeps the register literal — voices, loudness.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[31:19]` "And lower your voice. The most repulsive of voices is the donkey's voice" — the Luqman parallel (rg: the only other voice-volume verses); the brain's `[24:63]` address-manner link already recorded in p02409.
* **tabayyan (investigate):** 49:6 "investigate" — the news-audit imperative.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:94]` "When you journey in the way of Allah, investigate... so investigate" — the sole other occurrence, doubled within one verse; 4:94 polices killing on bad intelligence, 49:6 polices *social harm* on the same failure.
* **fasiq (troublemaker):** 49:6.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:12]` "In fact, they are the troublemakers, but they are not aware" (hypocrite-tag — k-nifaq territory); `[28:18]` Moses' counterpart, "You are clearly a troublemaker." The report's defect travels with the reporter's label.
* **islah / akh (reconcile / brother):** 49:9–10, both verbs and noun concentrated in two verses.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:128]` "reconciliation is best"; `[4:35]` the arbiter-pair (family scale of the same statute); `[3:103]` "by His grace you became brethren" — Itani renders the same kinship-word "brethren" there, "brothers" here (observable drift).
