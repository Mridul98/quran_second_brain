# The edge-worshipper, the despairing schemer, and all creation bowing (22:11–22)
**UID:** 202610141683
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 22:11–22 (Ruku 2 of 10 in al-Hajj)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[22:11]` – `[22:22]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A triptych of unstable or despairing worshippers — the man who "worships Allah on edge" (11–13), the one who thinks God will not help Muhammad and is told to try hanging himself to test his despair (15), and the two litigants who feud about their Lord (19–22) — set against the surah's calm center: Allah admits the believers (14), guides whom He wills (16), judges among the six religions (17), and receives the involuntary prostration of everything in the heavens and earth (18).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2; completes the opening sorting begun in 22:3–10 and ends on the fire-scene that flows into ruku 3's reward-pair.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[22:11]`** "And among the people is he who worships Allah on edge. When something good comes his way, he is content with it. But when an ordeal strikes him, he makes a turnaround. He loses this world and the next. That is the obvious loss."
  * **Gloss:** The famous portrait: worship held "on edge" (ṭarf — on the rim, uncentered; cf. the variant reading "on doubt" noted in the classical literature — *Source: transmission of qira'at notices — agent reconstruction, verify*); temperate-weather faith that pivots at the first trial; the verdict — a double bankruptcy, this world and the next.
* **`[22:12]`** "He invokes, instead of Allah, what can neither harm him nor benefit him. That is the far straying."
  * **Gloss:** The edge-worshipper's working definition of an idol: that which is causally inert — neither harmful nor beneficial; the straying is "far" (ḍalāl ba'īd).
* **`[22:13]`** "He invokes one whose harm is closer than his benefit. What a miserable master. What a miserable companion."
  * **Gloss:** The arithmetic sharpens: the invoked has negative expected value (harm nearer than benefit); the exclamations ("miserable master/companion") close the ledger on idolatry as bad patronage.
* **`[22:14]`** "Allah will admit those who believe and do righteous deeds into Gardens beneath which rivers flow. Allah does whatever He wills."
  * **Gloss:** The unit's axis verse: against the losers, the believers' admission; "Allah does whatever He wills" makes it an assertion, not a hope.
* **`[22:15]`** "Whoever thinks that Allah will not help him in this life and in the Hereafter—let him turn to heaven, then sever, and see if his cunning eliminates what enrages him."
  * **Gloss:** A deliberately grotesque dare: if you despair that God will help His Messenger, stretch a cord to the ceiling and strangle yourself and see if your gadgetry removes your chagrin — despair of help is treated as self-harm disguised as theology.
* **`[22:16]`** "Thus We revealed it as clarifying signs, and Allah guides whomever He wills."
  * **Gloss:** Revelation is what it is — bayyinat, clear verses; guidance remains God's initiative (the note's "road with conditions" grammar).
* **`[22:17]`** "Those who believe, and those who are Jewish, and the Sabeans, and the Christians, and the Zoroastrians, and the Polytheists—Allah will judge between them on the Day of Resurrection. Allah is witness to all things."
  * **Gloss:** The broadest religion-table in the corpus (six categories — the only Majus/“Zoroastrians” verse in the corpus): no sorting is outsourced to the community; the verdict is deferred to the Day; God witnesses all.
* **`[22:18]`** "Do you not realize that to Allah prostrates everyone in the heavens and everyone on earth, and the sun, and the moon, and the stars, and the mountains, and the trees, and the animals, and many of the people? But many are justly deserving of punishment. Whomever Allah shames, there is none to honor him. Allah does whatever He wills."
  * **Gloss:** The cosmos sujuds — celestial bodies, landmarks, flora, fauna, and most humans; the exception clause ("many are justly deserving") keeps the prostration of things honest while marking the refusal of persons; "whomever Allah shames, none honors" inverts the worldly status-ladder.
* **`[22:19]`** "Here are two adversaries feuding regarding their Lord. As for those who disbelieve, garments of fire will be tailored for them, and scalding water will be poured over their heads."
  * **Gloss:** "khitaaman" (adversaries) resumed — the two litigants (the believer and the denier, the hereafter's courtroom); the tailor image ("garments of fire") makes punishment a fitted robe.
* **`[22:20]`** "Melting their insides and their skins."
  * **Gloss:** The scalding does its work inward and outward — body-bound imagery sustained.
* **`[22:21]`** "And they will have maces of iron."
  * **Gloss:** A police measure for escape: iron clubs; the word for "maces" (miṭraq) occurs only in this verse in the corpus.
* **`[22:22]`** "Whenever they try to escape the gloom, they will be driven back to it: “Taste the suffering of burning.”"
  * **Gloss:** The closed loop — flight returns them, and the taste-command is the surah's recurring fire-refrain (cf. 22:9, 22:10).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`'ala ṭarf / "on edge"`** `[22:11]`:
  * **Rendering:** "worships Allah on edge" — the phrase "on edge" is unique in the corpus (zero other hits); Itani keeps the image of standing on a rim.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[29:10]` "Among the people is he who says, 'We have believed in Allah.' Yet when he is harmed on Allah's account, he equates the people's persecution with Allah's retribution" — the same opening formula and the ordeal-turn; `[3:144]` "He who turns on his heels will not harm Allah in any way" — the pivot under pressure named as retreat.
* **`neither harm nor benefit`** `[22:12]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:18]` "they worship, besides Allah, what neither harms them nor benefits them" and `[6:71]` "something that can neither benefit us nor harm us" — the same creedal test for idols, near-verbatim in both.
* **`khaṣmān` (the two adversaries)** `[22:19]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Here are two adversaries feuding regarding their Lord."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:204]` "he is the most hostile of adversaries" (khaṣim) — same root; the Day-of-lawsuit frame; the phrase "two adversaries" is found only here in the corpus (rg-verified).
* **`Zoroastrians / Majus`** `[22:17]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the Zoroastrians" — the only occurrence of the category in the corpus.
  * **Elsewhere:** the shorter tables `[2:62]` ("the Sabeans") and `[5:69]` ("the Sabians") — the same list-form; spelling drift between the two editions' renderings is observable.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** mixed-list readings keep this ruku in the surah's opening (Meccan-leaning) block; 22:17's plural-table is read by some as a Medinan inter-faith adjudication clause. *Source: classical lists — verify; the dispute is genuine, do not harmonize.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** classical commentaries transmit reports attaching `[22:15]` to companions who despaired when persecution mounted (one report ties it to a suicidal act); the genre's notices vary. *Source: reports collected in the tafsir literature — agent reconstruction, verify.*
