# The subjugated world and the gods with no warrant (22:65–72)
**UID:** 202610141690
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 22:65–72 (Ruku 9 of 10 in al-Hajj)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[22:65]`–`[22:72]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The inventory closes the argument: earth made subservient, ships running by command, sky held up by permission (65) — and yet "the human being is unappreciative" through the whole life-death-revival gift (66); each congregation has its appointed rite, so disputes are disarmed by "invite to your Lord" and deferred to God's judgment (67–69); total foreknowledge is filed in a book, easy for Allah (70); the invoked-without-Him have no warrant sent down and no knowledge behind them (71), and their faces, when the clear verses are recited, show disgust — answered by the surah's harshest counter-offer: the Fire (72).
* **Placement:** Ruku 9; the cosmic-polemic block that compresses the whole Meccan sign-repertoire and hands the surah to its final commission (ruku 10).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[22:65]`** "Do you not see that Allah made everything on earth subservient to you? How the ships sail at sea by His command? That He holds up the sky lest it falls on earth—except by His permission? Allah is Gracious towards the people, Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** Three subjections stacked — land-creations, seafaring, the suspended sky — each with its own grammar of permission; the closing names (ra'uf, rahim) say the whole setup is mercy, not machinery. Note the corpus's odd clause "How the ships sail at sea by His command?" — an interrogative-styled line mid-inventory in this edition.
* **`[22:66]`** "And it is He who gives you life, then makes you die, then revives you. The human being is unappreciative."
  * **Gloss:** The life cycle is the fourth gift, and the one-line verdict on the species: kuhur — ungratefulness as anthropology; the same cycle stated in Baqarah 2:28.
* **`[22:67]`** "For every congregation We have appointed acts of devotion, which they observe. So do not let them dispute with you in this matter. And invite to your Lord; you are upon a straight guidance."
  * **Gloss:** Rite-plurality is divine policy (the mansak clause of 22:34 restated for communities, not just nations); the response to dispute is non-argument — keep inviting; the prophet's standing is "straight guidance."
* **`[22:68]`** "But if they dispute with you, say, “Allah is fully aware of what you do.”"
  * **Gloss:** The escalation clause: awareness-of-works replaces winning; divine observation is the corpus's standard closing formula (e.g., `[2:110]` "Allah is Seeing of everything you do").
* **`[22:69]`** "Allah will judge between you on the Day of Resurrection regarding what you disagree about."
  * **Gloss:** The judgment-deferral formula of `[22:17]` aimed now at the disputants — disagreement is eschatologically parked.
* **`[22:70]`** "Do you not know that Allah knows everything in the heavens and the earth? This is in a book. That is easy for Allah."
  * **Gloss:** Omniscience with a filing system: everything in a book (umm al-kitab register), and the whole operation "easy for Allah" — the phrase's fixed corpus refrain.
* **`[22:71]`** "Yet they worship, besides Allah, things for which He sent down no warrant, and what they have no knowledge of. There is no savior for the transgressors."
  * **Gloss:** The legal test for idolatry: no sultan ("warrant") sent down and no knowledge — double absence; the transgressors' verdict: no savior (cf. 22:12's miserable masters).
* **`[22:72]`** "And when Our Clear Verses are recited to them, you will recognize disgust on the faces of those who disbelieve. They nearly assault those who recite to them Our Verses. Say, “Shall I inform you of something worse than that? The Fire! Allah has promised it to those who disbelieve. And what a wretched outcome!”"
  * **Gloss:** The reaction to recitation — scowling faces and near-violence — is met with a counter-news worse than their disgust: the promised Fire; "something worse than that" turns their own anger into evidence.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sakhkhara / subservient`** `[22:65]`:
  * **Rendering:** "made everything on earth subservient to you."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:54]` "the sun, and the moon, and the stars are subservient by His command"; `[45:12]` "It is Allah who placed the sea at your service, so that ships may run through it by His command" (rendered "service"); `[16:14]` "made the sea to serve you" — the same s-x-r root in the provision-inventory of other surahs; `[14:32]`, `[31:31]`, `[42:32]` keep the ships sailing by command.
* **`mansak / "acts of devotion"`** `[22:67]`:
  * **Rendering:** "For every congregation We have appointed acts of devotion" — same noun rendered "a rite" at `[22:34]` (intra-surah drift worth tracking).
  * **Elsewhere:** the plural manasik at `[2:128]` ("show us our rites") and `[2:200]` ("when you have completed your rites") — Abraham's request answered across two surahs.
* **`sultan / "warrant"`** `[22:71]`:
  * **Rendering:** "He sent down no warrant."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:153]` "We gave Moses a clear authority" — same noun rendered "authority"; the "send down no + sultan" construction (no revealed authorization) is the corpus's standard verdict-form for idolatry.
* **`kuhur / "unappreciative"`** `[22:66]`:
  * **Rendering:** "The human being is unappreciative."
  * **Elsewhere:** the English word-form recurs at `[16:112]` ("turned unappreciative") where the Arabic is kufr of blessings — the shukr-audit note's niʿma→kufr circuit; `[31:12]` "And whoever is unappreciative—Allah is Sufficient and Praiseworthy" — the exact theological pairing behind `[22:66]`'s one-line anthropology.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:28]` — "you were dead and He gave you life, then He will put you to death, then He will bring you to life" — the cycle compressed in `[22:66]`.
* **Parallel:** `[2:113]` "Allah will judge between them on the Day of Resurrection regarding their differences" and `[5:48]` "then He will inform you of what you had disputed" — the judgment-deferral of `[22:69]`, the standard end to inter-faith argument.
* **Parallel:** `[16:125]` "Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom" — the positive half of `[22:67]`'s "invite to your Lord."
* **Parallel:** `[57:22]` — "it is in a Book, even before We make it happen. That is easy for Allah" — the book+ease clause of `[22:70]`.
* **Within surah:** `[22:65]` restates `[22:5]`'s water→green earth and `[22:63]` repeats it verbatim-in-miniature; `[22:71]`'s warrant-less gods are the "what can neither harm him nor benefit him" of `[22:12–13]` re-prosecuted; `[22:66]`'s unappreciative human answers `[22:36]`'s "that you may be thankful."
