# The cattle rites: neither flesh nor blood reaches Allah — piety does (22:34–38)
**UID:** 202610141686
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 22:34–38 (Ruku 5 of 10 in al-Hajj)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[22:34]` – `[22:38]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The theology of sacrifice in five moves: every nation got a rite so God's name is pronounced over livestock (34); the humble portrait — trembling hearts, endurance, prayer, spending (35); the offerings are God's emblems — name Him over the lined-up animals, eat, feed (36); the surah's center of gravity — neither flesh nor blood reaches Allah, what reaches Him is *taqwa* (37); and the unit ends with God defending the believers and not loving the ungrateful traitor (38), the hinge that swings the surah into the permission to fight.
* **Placement:** Ruku 5; the payoff of the Abrahamic charter (ruku 4). The "sacrifice-vs-piety" argument is the most-quoted theological claim in the surah.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[22:34]`** "We have appointed a rite for every nation, that they may commemorate Allah's name over the livestock He has provided for them. Your God is One God, so to Him submit, and announce good news to the humble."
  * **Gloss:** Mansak (rite/slaughter-place) universalized: every nation got one, all pointing at the same act — naming God over provision; the creed clause ("One God, so submit") and the glad news to the lowly attach the ritual to humility.
* **`[22:35]`** "Those whose hearts tremble when Allah is mentioned, and those who endure what has befallen them, and those who perform the prayer and spend from what We have provided for them."
  * **Gloss:** The humble defined by four marks: heart-awe at God's mention, patience under what befalls, prayer, and spending from provision — inner tremor and outer endurance welded; verbatim cousin of `[8:2]`.
* **`[22:36]`** "We have made the animal offerings emblems of Allah for you. In them is goodness for you. So pronounce Allah's name upon them as they line up. Then, when they have fallen on their sides, eat of them and feed the contented and the beggar. Thus We have subjected them to you, that you may be thankful."
  * **Gloss:** The offerings (budn — large livestock) are sha'a'ir, emblems of God; the rite is the naming-over-the-standing-then-fallen animals, then the double feeding — the self-satisfied and the asking poor; the whole subjection exists "that you may be thankful": the shukr circuit (cf. 16:5–8; 36:71–73).
* **`[22:37]`** "Neither their flesh, nor their blood, ever reaches Allah. What reaches Him is the righteousness from you. Thus He subdued them to you, that you may glorify Allah for guiding you. And give good news to the charitable."
  * **Gloss:** The argument: material offerings travel no distance toward God — what ascends is taqwa, rendered here "righteousness"; subjection → glorification for guidance → good news to the muhsinin (the "charitable"): the rite's physics is entirely interior, and its audience is God.
* **`[22:38]`** "Allah defends those who believe. Allah does not love any ungrateful traitor."
  * **Gloss:** The pivot verse: God fights/defends for the believers (yuqatilu), while the ungrateful-traitor pair (kaffar, kha'an) names the covenant-breaker archetype — the sentence that hands the surah to 22:39's permission.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`mansak` (rite)** `[22:34]`:
  * **Rendering:** "a rite for every nation" — 22:67 renders the same word "acts of devotion" (drift within the surah).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:128]` "show us our rites" (manasik, plural of the same noun — Abraham's prayer that this surah answers); the word's root (n-s-k) ties rite to slaughter-place.
* **`taqwa` (rendered "righteousness" here)** `[22:37]`:
  * **Rendering:** "What reaches Him is the righteousness from you" — the word is taqwa; the same noun was rendered "piety" at `[22:32]` and "righteousness" at `[2:197]` ("the best provision is righteousness").
  * **Elsewhere:** 2:197 and 22:32 form a three-point map: provision = taqwa (Baqarah), veneration = taqwa of heart (Hajj 32), and the sacrifice's only valid cargo = taqwa (Hajj 37) — this is the canonical taqwa-argument in the ritual register.
* **`taghillu / "reaches"`** `[22:37]`:
  * **Rendering:** "never reaches Allah."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:136]` "the share of their idols does not reach Allah, yet the share of Allah reaches their idols" — the same verb in an anti-polytheist arithmetic; 22:37 completes the move: nothing material reaches Him in either direction.
* **`sa'il / beggar`** `[22:36]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the contented and the beggar" (al-mu'tarr and al-sa'il — the one who feels no need to press and the one who asks).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[51:19]` and `[70:25]` "for the beggar and the deprived" — same noun sa'il in the charity inventories; the Hajj meal is folded into the book's wider feeding-law.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[6:136]` — flesh-and-blood religion is a transaction in which the gods' share "reaches" their worshippers; 22:37 denies that any share reaches Allah at all. The two verses are the corpus's complete argument on sacrificial materialism.
* **Parallel:** `[36:71–72]` "We created for them... livestock that they own... We subdued them for them. Some they ride, and some they eat" and `[16:5–8]` — the subjection→gratitude circuit that 22:36's closing clause (`"that you may be thankful"`) explicitly runs.
* **Parallel:** `[8:2]` "The believers are those whose hearts tremble when Allah is mentioned" — the definition half of `[22:35]`; also `[39:23]` (skins and hearts soften/shiver).
* **Within surah:** `[22:28]` (name of Allah over livestock; eat and feed the unfortunate poor) is the same rite earlier in the charter; `[22:38]` → `[22:39]` — defense-of-believers becomes permission-to-fight in the next verse; `[22:32]` "piety of the hearts" pre-stages `[22:37]`.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** the cattle-rite block is counted among the Medinan-law verses in the mixed-list reports (sacrificial practice of the new community); other lists keep it Meccan. *Source: classical lists — verify.*
* **Asbab:** no single transmitted occasion for 22:37 itself; classical commentaries read it against pre-Islamic sacrificial practice (blood thrown at the Ka'bah etc.). *Source: transmitted notices in the tafsir genre — agent reconstruction, verify.*
* **Link judgment (task question):** the strongest knowledge-note tie of this ruku is the taqwa dossier ([Sabr and taqwa](../../02-knowledge/202610132003-k-sabr_and_taqwa.md)), because the argument's keyword literally *is* taqwa (22:37). The shukr note ([Shukr](../../02-knowledge/202610141600-k-shukr_gratitude_audit.md)) is genuinely present but as the rite's *telos* — "that you may be thankful" (22:36) — not as the reaching-through-Allah claim. Both links kept, with that division of labor recorded.
