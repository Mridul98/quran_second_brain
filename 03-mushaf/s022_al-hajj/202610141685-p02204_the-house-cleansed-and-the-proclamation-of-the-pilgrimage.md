# Abraham's charter of the House: purify, proclaim, circle (22:26–33)
**UID:** 202610141685
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 22:26–33 (Ruku 4 of 10 in al-Hajj)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[22:26]` – `[22:33]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The sanctuary gets its constitution: God shows Abraham the site of the House and imposes its first clause — monotheism ("Do not associate anything with Me") — then purification for its three worshipper-postures, a universal proclamation of the Pilgrimage, the purpose clause ("that they may witness benefits"), the rites of arrival (cleansing, vows, circling), and the veneration of sanctities and sacraments as evidence of heart-piety, with shirk exiled by the sky-fall simile and perjury named alongside idols.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4; the heartland of the surah's name. 26–27 charter the place; 28–29 charter the calendar and the circuit; 30–33 charter the animals and the inner law.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[22:26]`** "We showed Abraham the location of the House: “Do not associate anything with Me; and purify My House for those who circle around, and those who stand to pray, and those who kneel and prostrate.”"
  * **Gloss:** The site is divinely shown (awa bayna); the charter is two commands — tawhid first, purity second; the House is reserved for circling, standing (in prayer), and bowing-prostrating bodies.
* **`[22:27]`** "And announce the pilgrimage to humanity. They will come to you on foot, and on every transport. They will come from every distant point."
  * **Gloss:** A proclamation addressed to all humanity, answered by pilgrims walking and riding from every deep pass; the load-beasts named here are classically read as swift, lean camels — but this edition prints "every transport," a deliberate modern-register choice.
* **`[22:28]`** "That they may witness the benefits for themselves, and celebrate the name of Allah during the appointed days, for providing them with the animal livestock. So eat from it, and feed the unfortunate poor."
  * **Gloss:** Purpose clause: benefits witnessed (economics and worship fused), naming of God over the livestock on the appointed days, then the meal rule — eat and feed the destitute; sacrifice is a distribution program.
* **`[22:29]`** "Then let them perform their acts of cleansing, and fulfill their vows, and circle around the Ancient House."
  * **Gloss:** The post-rite itinerary: removing dirt/stubble (haqq al-tayur = "acts of cleansing"), paying vows, tawaf of the Ancient House (al-Bayt al-ʿAtiq — the title unique to these two verses in the corpus).
* **`[22:30]`** "All that. Whoever venerates the sanctities of Allah—it is good for him with his Lord. All livestock are permitted to you, except what is recited to you. So stay away from the abomination of idols, and stay away from perjury."
  * **Gloss:** Veneration of God's sanctities (ḥurumāt) is good with the Lord; the food-law restates the permitted-with-exception-clause (echo of 5:1's diet law), then two abstentions: idol-rijs and lying witness (qawl al-zoor).
* **`[22:31]`** "Being true to Allah, without associating anything with Him. Whoever associates anything with Allah—it is as though he has fallen from the sky, and is snatched by the birds, or is swept away by the wind to a distant abyss."
  * **Gloss:** The posture is hunafa' — upright/true; shirk then gets the surah's most cinematic simile: a fall from the sky, seized by birds or blown to an abyss — total loss of grip, direction, and recovery.
* **`[22:32]`** "So it is. Whoever venerates the sacraments of Allah—it is from the piety of the hearts."
  * **Gloss:** The same veneration-verb now on the sha'a'ir (sacraments/ritual symbols) — and its source is located: taqwa of the heart; external reverence is an index of internal piety.
* **`[22:33]`** "In them are benefits for you until a certain time. Then their place is by the Ancient House."
  * **Gloss:** The sacrificial animals carry provisional utility (riding/yield) up to the term; their terminal point is the Ancient House — the animals belong to the sanctuary's geography.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`tawaf / "circle around"`** `[22:26]`:
  * **Rendering:** "those who circle around" (and "circle around the Ancient House" 22:29).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:125]` "those who circle around it" — same verb in the Baqarah charter; `[2:158]` "circulating between them" (Safa and Marwa) — the sub-circuit inside the greater one.
* **`adhan / "announce"`** `[22:27]`:
  * **Rendering:** "announce the pilgrimage to humanity."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:3]` "a proclamation from Allah and His Messenger to the people on the day of the Greater Pilgrimage" — same noun (adhan) welded to the Hajj; `[14:52]` "This is a proclamation for mankind"; `[3:138]` — the word-form for every public warning.
* **`al-Bayt al-ʿAtiq` (the Ancient House)** `[22:29]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the Ancient House" (twice: 29, 33).
  * **Elsewhere:** unique in the corpus; the cognate honorifics are `[5:97]` "the Kaabah, the Sacred House" and `[5:2]` "the Sacred House" — three titles for one site across the corpus.
* **`shaʿaʿir / "sacraments"`** `[22:32]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the sacraments of Allah" (22:32); same root is rendered "emblems of Allah" at `[22:36]` and "the rites of Allah" at `[2:158]` — one word-family, three English renderings (observable drift).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:2]` "Do not violate Allah's sacraments" — the negative duty matching 22:32's positive veneration.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:125]` — "Sanctify My House for those who circle around it, and those who seclude themselves in it, and those who kneel and prostrate" — the charter twin of `[22:26]`, with "seclude" for 22:26's "stand to pray" (rendering drift on the same triad).
* **Parallel:** `[2:128]` — Abraham's own prayer "show us our rites" (manasik) is answered by the rite-charter here and at `[22:34]`.
* **Parallel:** `[9:3]` — the Proclamation of the Greater Pilgrimage is 22:27's adhan carried into the ninth surah's legal moment.
* **Parallel:** `[5:97]` — the Kaabah as "a sanctuary for the people" is the juridical form of the 22:25–29 parenthesis.
* **Within surah:** `[22:32]` "piety of the hearts" pre-proves `[22:37]` "What reaches Him is the righteousness from you" — the interiority thesis stated, then proved at the altar.
