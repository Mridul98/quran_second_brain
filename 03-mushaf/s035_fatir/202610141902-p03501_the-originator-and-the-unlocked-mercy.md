# The Originator, the unlocked mercy, and the provider question (35:1–7)
**UID:** 202610141902
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 35:1–7 (Ruku 1 of 5 in Fatir)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[35:1]` – `[35:7]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's creedal overture: praise to the Originator whose creative sovereignty reaches even the angel-wings and the on/off switch of mercy — then a direct three-part interrogation of the whole of humanity (remember, ask, return), followed by consolation to the rejected messenger and the surah's first sorting of the two classes.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 5; opens the surah with a hamd formula that answers `[34:1]` (Saba's praise over owned things) with praise over the originating act itself. The "O people!" interrogations here (3, 5) recur at 35:15 — three of the surah's addresses, verified by `rg "O people"` in `corpus/s035_fatir.md` = 3 hits.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[35:1]`** "Praise be to Allah, Originator of the heavens and the earth, Maker of the angels messengers with wings—double, triple, and quadruple. He adds to creation as He wills. Allah is Able to do all things."
  * **Gloss:** The task's candidate quote verifies verbatim. *Fatir* — the surah's name-bearer — is paired with the fixed phrase "Originator of the heavens and the earth"; the angels become *ruslan* (messengers) with 2/3/4 wings, and "He adds to creation as He wills" leaves the addition open. Source: classical tafsir (al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on 35:1) reads "adds" either as adding to the angels' wings beyond these counts or to creatures generally — both reported, neither decided by the text; agent labeling — verify in print. The verse is a tawhid anchor first (unmatched Originator); its kalam relevance is the angel-messenger mode of revelation, whose mechanism-verse is `[42:51]`.
* **`[35:2]`** "Whatever mercy Allah unfolds for the people, none can withhold it. And if He withholds it, none can release it thereafter. He is the Exalted in Power, Full of Wisdom."
  * **Gloss:** The task's "nothing can withhold what He sends down... nothing can send back His mercy" ✓ in substance, but the edition's order is unfold→withhold, not send down→send back. Mercy's faucet is single-handed; the closing names (Power arranging, Wisdom placing) frame the flow as governance, not caprice.
* **`[35:3]`** "O people! Remember Allah’s blessings upon you. Is there a creator other than Allah who provides for you from the heaven and the earth? There is no god but He. So how are you misled?"
  * **Gloss:** The tawhid interrogation ✓ verbatim. Three moves: remember the favors (a shukr command), a rhetorical question collapsing creator and provider into one address, then the confession-formula and the charge: "So how are you misled?" (the task's version omitted this closing line).
* **`[35:4]`** "If they reject you, messengers before you were also rejected. To Allah all matters are returned."
  * **Gloss:** Consolation by precedent — rejection is the messengers' sunna, not a refutation — then the eschatological bracket: "To Allah all matters are returned." The task's remembered "Allah decides the affair" is not a corpus phrase anywhere; this verse is Fatir's nearest anchor (cf. `[13:2]`, `[32:5]` — He manages/regulates all affairs).
* **`[35:5]`** "O people! The promise of Allah is true; so let not the lowly life seduce you, and let not the Tempter tempt you away from Allah."
  * **Gloss:** The second "O people!": the verbatim twin of `[31:33]` ("The promise of Allah is true. Therefore, do not let this life deceive you, nor let illusions deceive you regarding Allah"). "The Tempter" (*al-gharrur*) — a corpus-unique rendering at this verse; the same word is "illusions" in 31:33.
* **`[35:6]`** "Satan is an enemy to you, so treat him as an enemy. He only invites his gang to be among the inmates of the Inferno."
  * **Gloss:** The one enmity worth accepting is declared and licensed ("treat him as an enemy"); the family word for his party is *hizb* — "his gang" here, "partisans of Satan" at `[58:19]`, "his gang" again at `[96:17]`. His invitation's destination is the Blaze (*sa'ir*), "Inferno" in this edition (cf. `[102:6]`, `[34:12]`).
* **`[35:7]`** "Those who disbelieve will suffer a harsh punishment, but those who believe and do righteous deeds will have forgiveness and a great reward."
  * **Gloss:** The first sorting of humanity closes the overture; "forgiveness and a great reward" is a verbatim pair-twin at `[5:9]`, and `[67:12]` attaches it to those who "fear their Lord in secret" — the class 35:18 will warn.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fatir` (Originator)** `[35:1]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Originator of the heavens and the earth"; the same English phrase renders *badi'* at `[2:117]` and `[6:101]`, while *fatir* elsewhere is "Originator" at `[6:14]`, `[42:11]` and "Maker of the heavens and the earth" at `[14:10]` — observable translation merging and drift (`rg "Originator of the heavens" corpus/`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[14:10]` — the messengers' sermon opens on the same epithet ("Is there any doubt about Allah, Maker of the heavens and the earth?"), the provider-theme twin of 35:3.
* **`rahma` (mercy)** `[35:2]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Whatever mercy Allah unfolds" — mercy as anything good released to people.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:107]` near-twin, with drift: "none can repel His grace" — same single/faucet logic; `[6:147]` "Your Lord is Possessor of infinite mercy" — the ownership frame.
* **`al-gharrur` ("the Tempter")** `[35:5]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the Tempter tempt you" — the deceiver-about-God idiom; the same root's noun closes `[3:185]` as "enjoyment of delusion." Verified: "Tempter" occurs only at 35:5 in this corpus.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[31:33]` "nor let illusions deceive you regarding Allah" — the same Arabic word, rendered "illusions"; the delusion-commodity returns at `[35:40]` ("nothing but delusions").
* **`hizb` ("gang"/"partisans")** `[35:6]`:
  * **Rendering:** "his gang" — the party of Satan, recruited by invitation, destined for the Inferno.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[58:19]` "the partisans of Satan... Satan's partisans who are the losers"; `[96:17]` "Let him call on his gang"; `[26:54]`, `[27:48]` — the word's range from army to crew.
