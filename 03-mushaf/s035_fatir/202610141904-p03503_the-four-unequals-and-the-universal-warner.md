# The needy ones, the four unequals, and the warner who came to every community (35:15–26)
**UID:** 202610141904
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 35:15–26 (Ruku 3 of 5 in Fatir)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[35:15]` – `[35:26]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The human position stated flat: we are the needy, Allah is the Rich — no burden transfers, no kinsman carries, purification profits only its own agent; the four unequals (blind/seeing, darkness/light, shade/heat, living/dead) sort the hearers, because hearing is Allah's to give — and the messenger's job is only warning, a job Allah himself universalized: no community but a warner has passed through it, all of whom brought clear proofs, Psalms, and an Enlightening Scripture — and were seized.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 5; the pivot between the evidence block (8–14) and the two-economies drama (27–37). Its "O people!" (15) completes the surah's triple address (3, 5, 15) — verified count of 3 in the corpus file.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[35:15]`** "O people! It is you who are the poor, in need of Allah; while Allah is the Rich, the Praiseworthy."
  * **Gloss:** The ontological ledger: *antum al-fuqara'* — human poverty defined as need, not destitution — against "the Rich, the Praiseworthy" (*al-Ghaniyy al-Hamid*), near-verbatim twin of `[47:38]` "Allah is the Rich, while you are the needy." The same divine pair recurs as "the Independent, the Praiseworthy" at `[57:24]` and `[64:6]` — observable Rich/Independent drift on one word (*ghaniyy*).
* **`[35:16]`** "If He wills, He can do away with you, and produce a new creation."
  * **Gloss:** The poverty clause's sanction: replacement is in His hand — the thought-twin is `[29:19]` "have they not seen how Allah originates the creation, and then reproduces it?"
* **`[35:17]`** "And that would not be difficult for Allah."
  * **Gloss:** The "not difficult/easy for Allah" refrain (cf. `[22:70]`, `[4:30]`, `[19:21]` "It is easy for Me") — the second appearance of the formula inside ten verses (35:11, 17).
* **`[35:18]`** "No burdened soul can carry the burden of another. Even if one weighted down calls for help with its burden, nothing can be lifted from it, even if they were related. You are to warn those who fear their Lord inwardly, and perform the prayer. He who purifies himself purifies himself for his own good. To Allah is the ultimate return."
  * **Gloss:** Four compressed rulings: the non-transferability axiom (verbatim family: `[6:164]`, `[17:15]`, `[39:7]`, `[53:38]`); the kin-clause (twin of `[31:33]` "no parent can avail his child, nor can a child avail his parent"); the addressee-filter — warn those who "fear their Lord inwardly" and pray (twin `[21:49]` "fear their Lord in private", and `[67:12]` pairs secret fear with 35:7's "forgiveness and a great reward"); and the self-benefit formula the task looked for here: Fatir's own is 35:18 ("purifies himself for his own good"), while "whoever is grateful is grateful for his own soul; my Lord is Independent, Generous" is verbatim at `[27:40]` and `[31:12]` — not in this surah (ledger correction, see index §1). "To Allah is the ultimate return" closes the bracket begun at 35:4.
* **`[35:19]`** "Not equal are the blind and the seeing."
  * **Gloss:** Parable 1 of 4 — the formula's home verses: `[6:50]` "Are the blind and the seeing alike?" and `[13:16]` "Are the blind and the seeing equal?" (13:19 extends it to the one who *knows* what was revealed vs the blind — the real verse behind the task's "He who is ingrafted into his Lord?", a phrase that occurs nowhere in the corpus).
* **`[35:20]`** "Nor are the darkness and the light."
  * **Gloss:** Parable 2 — the darkness/light pairing is set-doctrinal at `[6:1]` ("Who created the heavens and the earth, and made the darkness and the light" — the sibling hamd opening) and interrogative at `[13:16]` ("Or are darkness and light equal?"); `[2:17]` stages the same seizure of light for the hypocrites' parable.
* **`[35:21]`** "Nor are the shade and the torrid heat."
  * **Gloss:** Parable 3 — *al-hurur* (the scorching heat) vs *al-zill* (the shade) — the only pairing of these two words in the Quran at this set (lexical note — agent labeling; verify); this edition's "torrid heat" is a corpus-unique rendering (`rg "torrid"` → 35:21 alone).
* **`[35:22]`** "Nor are equal the living and the dead. Allah causes whomever He wills to hear, but you cannot make those in the graves hear."
  * **Gloss:** Parable 4 climaxes the set and names the real asymmetry: living/dead, i.e., hearers and non-hearers. "Allah causes whomever He wills to hear" ✓ (task verified); the incapacity-clause twins `[27:80]` / `[30:52]` ("You cannot make the dead hear") and `[27:81]` ("You can make no one listen, except those who believe in Our verses"); "those in the graves" (*fi-l-qubur*) — the graves as addressable locations recur at `[22:7]`, `[54:7]`, `[100:9]`.
* **`[35:23]`** "You are only a warner."
  * **Gloss:** The job description — verbatim twin `[13:7]` "You are only a warner, and to every community is a guide."
* **`[35:24]`** "We sent you with the truth; a bearer of good news, and a warner. There is no community but a warner has passed through it."
  * **Gloss:** The pair (bashir/nadhir) verbatim-twins `[2:119]` "bringing good news, and giving warnings"; the universality axiom ("a warner has passed through every community") — corpus-unique phrasing — is doctrinally doubled by `[16:36]` "To every community We sent a messenger," and it is the twin of the previous surah's claims at `[34:28]`/`[34:34]` (flagged in the s034 index).
* **`[35:25]`** "If they disbelieve you, those before them also disbelieved. Their messengers came to them with the clear proofs, with the Psalms, and with the Enlightening Scripture."
  * **Gloss:** The precedent (25a re-voices 35:4) plus a revelation-trilogy: *bayyinat* (clear proofs), *zubur* (Psalms — cf. `[4:163]` "We gave David the Psalms"; `[21:105]` "We have written in the Psalms"), and *kitab munir* — the Enlightening Scripture, the same adjective as `[44:2]` "By the Enlightening [Book]" and `[31:20]` "an enlightening Scripture" (case-drift observable).
* **`[35:26]`** "Then I seized those who disbelieved—so how was My rejection?"
  * **Gloss:** The first-person rebuke formula (*kayfa nakir*) — twins `[22:44]`, `[34:45]`, `[67:18]` — closing the ruku on the seizure precedent that 35:37 and 35:42–44 will re-invoke.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`faqir/faqara` (poor, in need)** `[35:15]`:
  * **Rendering:** "It is you who are the poor, in need of Allah" — the gloss "in need of" is the edition's own expansion, observable in the line.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:60]` "Charities are for the poor, and the destitute" — the same *fuqara'* for recipients of alms; 35:15 universalizes the label onto all of humanity, making material poverty a metaphor for the creature-condition; `[47:38]` "you are the needy" — twin verse, drift (poor/needy).
* **`ghaniyy` (the Rich/Independent)** `[35:15]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the Rich, the Praiseworthy."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[57:24]`, `[64:6]` "the Independent, the Praiseworthy"; `[39:7]` "Allah is Independent of you" — one attribute, two renderings, all paired with praise or its near-cognate; the task's "Independent, Generous" collocation is `[27:40]`'s, not this surah's.
* **`zill/hurur` set — the four unequals** `[35:19–22]`:
  * **Rendering:** four antitheses in ascending concreteness (sight, light, temperature, life).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:50]`, `[13:16]`, `[13:19]` — the blind/seeing as the standard parable-frame for revealed-knowing vs unbelief; the shade/heat and living/dead pairings are Fatir's extension (corpus-unique as a four-set).
* **`qubur` (graves)** `[35:22]`:
  * **Rendering:** "those in the graves hear" — the dead as residents, not corpses.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[22:7]` "Allah will resurrect those in the graves"; `[54:7]` "emerge from the graves, as if they were swarming locusts"; `[100:9]` "the contents of the graves are scattered around."
* **`mundhir` (warner)** `[35:23–24]`:
  * **Rendering:** "You are only a warner... There is no community but a warner has passed through it."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:7]` verbatim first clause; `[2:119]` the good-news/warning pair; `[16:36]` every-community messenger; `[34:34]` "no warner to any town, without its affluent saying..." (the sociological reflex on every warner's arrival).
