Itani renders *yawm at-taghabun* "the Day of Mutual Exchange"; the popular "Day of Mutual Loss and Gain" is a classical gloss, not this translation's text. *Source: translator's term choice (observable in corpus); classical gloss — verify.*
* **Central theme:** The surah starts from the cosmos — everything praises Allah, and He knows what hearts hold — then confronts the reader with the record of destroyed unbelieving towns and the oath-bound certainty of resurrection. Ruku 2 turns inward: disasters run only by Allah's leave, belief guides the heart, and the trial arrives through the nearest people — wives, children, possessions — closing on the counter-economy of the good loan and the name "Appreciative," bracketed by a final doxology (64:18).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Doxology (64:1):** kingdom, praise, and total capability ascribed to Allah before any address to humans — the surah's standing frame.
* **Sorting and omniscience (64:2–4):** "Some of you are unbelievers, and some of you are believers"; human design — "He designed you, and designed you well" — and the final return. concealment, disclosure, and hearts are all known.
* **Case file from history (64:5–7):** the news of those who disbelieved before; their refusal of human messengers ("Are human beings going to guide us?"); the oath "by my Lord, you will be resurrected."
* **The command and the Day (64:8–10):** believe in Allah, His Messenger, and "the Light which We sent down"; 64:9 stacks three Day-labels — Gathering, the Day of Gathering, the Day of Mutual Exchange — then the two verdicts.
* **Pivot to providence (64:11–13):** no disaster except by Allah's leave; belief → heart-guidance; obey; the shahada-and-trust clause "in Allah let the believers put their trust."
* **Household as battlefield and blessèd site (64:14–15):** wives and children as "enemies," then the harder saying: possessions and children "are a test."
* **Closing economics (64:16–18):** give for your own good; protection from one's stinginess; the good loan that is multiplied and forgiven; the names — Appreciative, Forbearing, Knower of the Unseen and the Seen.
