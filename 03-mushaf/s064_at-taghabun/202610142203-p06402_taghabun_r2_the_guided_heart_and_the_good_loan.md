# The guided heart, the household trial, and the good loan (64:11–18)
**UID:** 202610142203
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 64:11–18 (Ruku 2 of 2 in at-Taghabun)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[64:11]` – `[64:18]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Providence is rekeyed from history to biography: no disaster occurs except by Allah's leave, and belief converts disaster into heart-guidance; the surah then names the real battlefield — wives, children, possessions as trial and (possibly) enemy — and answers it with a counter-economy: give for your own good, lend Allah, and let Him multiply, until the final doxology of two names seals what the first verse opened.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 2; the inward pivot. 64:1–10 sorted the world into two eternal dwellings; 64:11–18 sorts the believer's *present* — heart, household, wallet. The closing names (64:18) bracket the opening doxology (64:1).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[64:11]`** "No disaster occurs except by Allah’s leave. Whoever believes in Allah, He guides his heart. Allah is Aware of everything."
  * **Gloss:** The theodicy line: *misiba* happens only bi-idhnillah; the promised reward for belief is not the removal of the blow but the guidance of the heart under it — belief addressed at 64:8 now cashes out inwardly.
* **`[64:12]`** "So obey Allah, and obey the Messenger. But if you turn away—it is only incumbent on Our Messenger to deliver the clear message."
  * **Gloss:** Obedience re-paired as in 5:92-style formulas; the Messenger's office is bounded — delivery, not coercion (the same limitation ruku 1's 64:7 oath had pressed).
* **`[64:13]`** "Allah, there is no god but He; and in Allah let the believers put their trust."
  * **Gloss:** Shahada plus tawakkul in one breath; trust is given its exclusive object exactly as the household section begins to demand it. The verbatim twin `[3:122]` "in Allah let the believers put their trust" is battlefield speech — trust here is likewise under pressure.
* **`[64:14]`** "O you who believe! Among your wives and your children are enemies to you, so beware of them. But if you pardon, and overlook, and forgive—Allah is Forgiver and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The audacious verse: enmity is located *inside* the household — partitive "among," not total (*min*; observable: "Among your wives and your children are enemies"); the counter-clause trades vigilance for pardon, and closes with the forgiveness-names ruku 1 never used for these people. No occasion is stated in the text; classical reports attach individual cases (e.g., a man whose family barred his migration) — *Source: classical asbab reports — verify.*
* **`[64:15]`** "Your possessions and your children are a test, but with Allah is a splendid reward."
  * **Gloss:** The generalization of 64:14 — trial (*balā'*) language shared verbatim in structure with `[8:28]` "your possessions and your children are a test"; and against both, "a splendid reward" is the counter-weight the verse hands over.
* **`[64:16]`** "So be conscious of Allah as much as you can, and listen, and obey, and give for your own good. He who is protected from his stinginess—these are the prosperous."
  * **Gloss:** Taqwa scaled to capacity ("as much as you can"); the four imperatives end in *nafaqa* — spending framed as self-interest, not loss; the avarice-prosperity formula is shared with `[59:9]` (rendering drift noted below).
* **`[64:17]`** "If you lend Allah a good loan, He will multiply it for you, and will forgive you. Allah is Appreciative and Forbearing."
  * **Gloss:** The divine business model in one sentence: loan → multiplication → forgiveness; the names *Shakur* ("Appreciative") and *Halim* ("Forbearing") say He registers the gift and does not rush the payer. The k-infraq note already cites this verse.
* **`[64:18]`** "The Knower of the Unseen and the Seen, the Almighty, the Wise."
  * **Gloss:** Final doxology: knowledge (the pair), might, wisdom — the surah that opened with everything praising Him closes by naming what praise is grounded in.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`bi-idhnillah`** (64:11 "except by Allah's leave"): every disaster inside the permit.
  * **Rendering:** "except by Allah's leave" — same phrase Itani uses wherever *idhn* gates an event.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:49]` "I heal the blind and the leprous, and I revive the dead, by Allah’s leave" — miracles gated by the same permit; `[9:51]` "Nothing will happen to us except what Allah has ordained for us" — the qadar-twin phrasing ("ordained," not "leave"). *rg-verified: "by Allah’s leave" occurs in s002 ×2, s003 ×2, s004, s008, s010, s035, s064.*
* **`shuh`** (64:16 "stinginess"): contracted self-guarding with money.
  * **Rendering:** "stinginess" in 64:16; "natural greed" in the formula-twin 59:9. 4:37 "stingy... exhort people to stinginess" for the communal form.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[59:9]` "Whoever is protected from his natural greed—it is they who are the successful" — same protection-prosperity sentence; drift: 64:16 "He who is protected from his stinginess—these are the prosperous."
* **`qard hasan`** (64:17 "a good loan"): money moved toward God as an open transaction.
  * **Rendering:** "a good loan" here — the family is rendered four ways across the corpus: `[2:245]` "a generous loan"; `[57:11]` "a loan of goodness"; `[57:18]` "a loan of righteousness"; `[73:20]` "a generous loan". *Source: translator's term choices, rg-verified fragments.*
  * **Elsewhere:** the family is four-plus — 2:245, 57:11, 57:18, 64:17, 73:20 — k-infraq already maps it.
* **`tawakkul`** (64:13 "put their trust"): entrusting the affair after obedience.
  * **Rendering:** "in Allah let the believers put their trust."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:122]` verbatim fragment; `[8:61]`, `[33:3]` "put your trust in Allah"; `[65:3]` "Whoever relies on Allah" + "He will suffice him" — the same hinge inside the next surah (rg-verified).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within surah:** 64:11's heart-guidance answers 64:4 "Allah knows what is within the hearts" — the Knower guides the known.
* **Twin:** `[59:9]` — the protection-from-avarice formula; and `[8:28]` — the possessions-and-children test formula: at-Taghabun localizes what two Medinan surahs state generically.
* **Twin:** `[63:9]` "Let neither your possessions nor your children distract you from the remembrance of Allah... are the losers" — one surah earlier, the same household with the same verdict-key; 64:14–15 is its intensification (distraction → possible enmity).
* **Forward (next surah):** 64:12's obey-the-Messenger and 64:13's trust feed `[65:2–3]`'s way-out provision and `[65:11]`'s believing-and-acting formula.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (consensus; *Source: classical type-lists — verify*); 64:14's enmity "Among your wives and your children" has no stated text-occasion; transmitted cases (a convert's family hindering hijrah; Umar's report) are asbab literature — *Source: classical reports — verify.*
* **Reading caution:** 64:14 is partitive — it locates enemies *among* wives and children ("Among your wives and your children are enemies to you"), never asserts "all wives and children are enemies"; the gloss must keep *min* in view. *Source: translator's rendering (observable in corpus).*
