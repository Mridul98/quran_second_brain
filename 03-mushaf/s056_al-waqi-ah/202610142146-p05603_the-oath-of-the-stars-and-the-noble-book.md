# The oath at the stars, the noble Book, the soul at the throat — final glorification (56:75–96)
**UID:** 202610142146
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 56:75–96 (Ruku 3 of 3 in al-Waqi'ah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[56:75]` – `[56:96]` (22 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** God swears by "the locations of the stars" that this is a noble Quran in a well-protected Book — then the surah turns the same class-map onto the dying person at the throat ("We are nearer to it than you are"): three death-bed verdicts rerun the three classes, "This is the certain truth," and the second glorification closes the proof.
* **Placement:** The surah's seal: the Book's self-claim (its deepest kalam text, index §4) answers the audits' God-claims; the death-bed rerun (83–94) converts the eschatology of ruku 1–2 into immediate biography; 96 matches 74's tasbih, bracketing the whole.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[56:75]`** "I swear by the locations of the stars." **`[56:76]`** "It is an oath, if you only knew, that is tremendous." — **Gloss:** the oath-object is not the stars but their *locations* (corpus-unique word); the parenthetical "if you only knew" measures the oath's weight by the hearer's ignorance.
* **`[56:77]`** "It is a noble Quran." **`[56:78]`** "In a well-protected Book." — **Gloss:** the swearer's claim: the Quran's own nobility, lodged "in a well-protected Book" (the task's "hidden/well-guarded Book" — this edition prints "well-protected"; twin `[85:22]` "In a Preserved Tablet").
* **`[56:79]`** "None can grasp it except the purified." — **Gloss:** the access clause — translator's choice "grasp" for the classical "touch" reading (see §3; Source: classical tafsir on 56:79 — verify in print); "the purified" marks whose hands/habitus this Book belongs to.
* **`[56:80]`** "A revelation from the Lord of the Worlds." — **Gloss:** the chain-of-custody stamp: not authored below — verbatim-phrase twin of `[26:192]` and `[69:43]` (the kalam note's own citation).
* **`[56:81]`** "Is it this discourse that you take so lightly?" **`[56:82]`** "And you make it your livelihood to deny it?" — **Gloss:** the rebuke, the Book self-naming as "this discourse"; 82 renders *ridq* idiom as "livelihood" — denial becomes their daily bread; these two ayahs are the surah's Medinan-classed exception couple (index §1).
* **`[56:83]`** "So when it has reached the throat." **`[56:84]`** "As you are looking on." **`[56:85]`** "We are nearer to it than you are, but you do not see." — **Gloss:** the death-bed scene: the soul at the throat, bystanders watching, and the nearness-audit — God nearer than the witnesses, unseen; cf. `[50:16]` "nearer to him than his jugular vein", `[40:18]`/`[33:10]` "hearts at/reached the throats".
* **`[56:86]`** "If you are not held to account." **`[56:87]`** "Then bring it back, if you are truthful." — **Gloss:** the reductio: if unaccountable, fetch the departing soul back — a challenge with the 2:23 family's condition ("if you are truthful"); "held to account" is corpus-unique here.
* **`[56:88]`** "But if he is one of those brought near." **`[56:89]`** "Then happiness, and flowers, and Garden of Delights." — **Gloss:** verdict 1 reruns class 1 — "those brought near" = "the nearest" of `[56:11]`; *rayḥān* rendered "flowers" here, "fragrant plants" at `[55:12]` (index §6 pair).
* **`[56:90]`** "And if he is one of those on the Right." **`[56:91]`** "Then, “Peace upon you,” from those on the Right." — **Gloss:** verdict 2: the Right greeted *by their own* — the peace-word of `[56:26]` re-voiced as address (book's inner curly quotes as-is).
* **`[56:92]`** "But if he is one of the deniers, the mistaken." **`[56:93]`** "Then a welcome of Inferno." **`[56:94]`** "And burning in Hell." — **Gloss:** verdict 3: class 3 is re-named, not re-labeled — "the deniers, the mistaken" stands in where "those on the Left" (9/41) was expected (the classical text reads the Left's name here; Source: classical reading of 56:92 — verify in print); welcome = Inferno — *nuzul* again rendered "welcome" (against "hospitality" at 56), the irony answered by the Right's greeting one line above.
* **`[56:95]`** "This is the certain truth." — **Gloss:** the verdict tag on all three reruns — phrase corpus-unique (rg: sole site).
* **`[56:96]`** "So glorify the Name of your Lord, the Magnificent" — **Gloss:** SECOND TASBIH: the bracket closes (74 "your Great Lord" → 96 "the Magnificent"); the line lacks its terminal period in the corpus — quoted as-is, per the artifacts note (book typography regime, not a fix).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`mawāqiʿ` — "the locations of the stars"**: the oath-object.
  * **Rendering:** "locations" — corpus-unique word; the task's "positions of the stars" is other translations' English (index §6).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[81:15]` "I swear by the galaxies." — the sibling star-oath; oaths by cosmic anchors at `[70:40]`, `[75:1–2]`, `[90:1]`, `[84:16]`, `[69:38]`; the Quran's star-worship inversion is what the rain-audit (`[56:69]`) already dismantled.
* **`karīm / makkūn` — "a noble Quran… in a well-protected Book"**: the self-claim cluster.
  * **Rendering:** "well-protected" (Itani's choice for the guarded/hidden register; "hidden/kept/guarded Book" are other editions' English — index §6), paired with "noble" for *karīm* — compare 85's variant rendering: "a Glorious Quran… In a Preserved Tablet" (`[85:21]`–`[85:22]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:192–193]` and `[69:43]` "a revelation from the Lord of the Worlds" — verbatim-phrase twins of 80; `[81:19]`/`[69:40]` "the speech of a noble messenger" — "noble" travels to the bearer; `[52:2]` "a Book inscribed" — the same oath→book structure.
* **`yamass/yasl — "None can grasp it except the purified"`**: the access clause (56:79).
  * **Rendering:** the edition prints "grasp," not the classical "touch" — flagged as translator's choice (index §6: "none **touch**" → "None can **grasp**"); the classical reading (none touch it save the purified — angels, or ritually pure believers; basis for the fiqh rule on touching the mushaf) is Source: classical tafsir on 56:79 — verify in print.
  * **Elsewhere:** "grasp" is Itani's verb for failed comprehension/reach: `[6:103]` "No vision can grasp Him", `[29:43]` "none grasps them except the learned", `[2:255]` "they cannot grasp any of His knowledge"; "the purified" recurs of the scrolls' pages and bearers: `[80:14]` "Exalted and purified" on `[80:13]` "honorable pages… by the hands of scribes" (`[80:16]` "Noble and devoted") — the nearest in-corpus gloss of who "the purified" are; cf. also "purified spouses" `[3:15]`, `[4:57]` (root drift toward ritual/moral purity).
* **`al-ḥaqq al-yaqīn` — "This is the certain truth" (`[56:95]`)**: the seal of verification.
  * **Rendering:** corpus-unique phrase (rg: one hit in the entire corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** the "Inferno/Hell" double-punishment idiom of 93–94 is the surah's own worst currency, answering "hospitality" (56); "happiness, and flowers" (89) — *raḥ* / *rayḥān* — pairs with `[13:29]` "happiness and a beautiful return" and, for the flower-word, `[55:12]` "fragrant plants".
