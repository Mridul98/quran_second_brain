# The Night of Decree: descent, command, peace (97:1–5)
**UID:** 202610142324
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 97:1–5 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Qadr)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[97:1]` – `[97:5]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The Quran's own arrival-night in five lines: God testifies in the first-person plural ("We sent it down"), the text then asks — with its grandest magnification formula — what the night even is, answers "better than a thousand months," reports the night's traffic (angels and the Spirit descending by their Lord's leave with every command), and gives its weather in one word: "Peace it is; until the rise of dawn."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1; the batch's closing verse-unit and 96's twin: there the command "Read!" and the pen, here the descent and the night. k-kalam already anchors this verse in its night-descent cluster (cites 97:1, 44:2–3, 2:185 — rg-verified), making this the batch's one genuine two-way link.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[97:1]`** "We sent it down on the Night of Decree."
  * **Gloss:** *Anzalnāhu* — the Book's autobiography in six words: agent (We, plural of majesty), act (sent down), object (it — the Quran, from the surrounding cluster), time (a named night). "Night of Decree" occurs nowhere else in the corpus (rg-verified); the verb-family drifts across the twin passages — 97:1 "sent it down" vs `[44:3]` "We have revealed it" vs `[2:185]` "the Quran was revealed" (one Arabic verb-family, three Englishers — translator's term choices, observable in corpus).
* **`[97:2]`** "But what will convey to you what the Night of Decree is?"
  * **Gloss:** The *mā adrāka* amazement-formula — it "conveys" (*yadrīka*): the questioner tells the audience that their instruments of knowing fall short of the referent. The same device doubles at `[82:17–18]` for the Day of Justice (rg-verified); here it doubles the night's name itself against 97:1.
* **`[97:3]`** "The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months."
  * **Gloss:** Worth-language, not clock-language: the night outweighs 1,000 months — an amount no human lifetime holds (83 years is later arithmetic, not in the text; *Source: agent note — transmitted calculations, verify*). "a thousand months" is corpus-unique (rg-verified); the stretched-time cousins `[22:47]`, `[32:5]` count "a thousand YEARS" of divine/day-time (rg-verified) — kin imagery, different units, don't conflate.
* **`[97:4]`** "In it descend the angels and the Spirit, by the leave of their Lord, with every command."
  * **Gloss:** The night's traffic report: plural descent (*yannahabu*), two ranks — "the angels and the Spirit" — the Spirit-with-angels pairing matching `[26:193]` "The Honest Spirit came down with it" and `[16:102]` "The Holy Spirit has brought it down from your Lord" (rg-verified; k-kalam's carrier-list). "By the leave of their Lord" — the permission-formula of `[2:97]`, `[7:58]`, `[34:12]` (rg-verified): even the night's traffic runs on leave. "With every command" (*bi-kulli amr*) — one line from `[44:4]` "In it is distinguished every wise command" (rg-verified): the two night-passages describe the same decree-night from opposite sides — settling (44) and descending (97).
* **`[97:5]`** "Peace it is; until the rise of dawn."
  * **Gloss:** Predicate first: "Peace" (*salām*) — the night's whole nature, not one feature among others; the same word-family as the Paradise-greeting `[56:26]` "the greeting: 'Peace, peace'" (rg-verified). Boundary: "until the rise of dawn" (*ḥattā maṭlaʿ al-fajr*) — the night ends at daybreak, and dawn is where the Quran is "witnessed" (`[17:78]` — rg-verified). Safety until light: a night with no share for chaos.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`anzala (sent down)`**: the revelation-verb, here with the night as its timestamp.
  * **Rendering:** "We sent it down" (97:1) — active, divine plural; cf. 44:3 "We have revealed it," 2:185 passive "was revealed" (rg-verified drift).
  * **Elsewhere:** k-kalam's audio-visual self-description — the book "presents itself as speech arriving" (`[42:51]`, `[42:52]` — cited there, rg-verified).
* **`qadr (decree/measure)`**: named only as the night here.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:8]` "everything is by measure"; `[44:5]` "A decree from Us" (rg-verified) — the root holds destiny-and-proportion together; the night is both.
* **`al-rūḥ (the Spirit)`**: 97:4's senior traveler.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:193]` "The Honest Spirit"; `[16:102]` "The Holy Spirit"; `[2:97]` Gabriel named at 2:97's opening (rg-verified) — the carrier-triad of k-kalam; Itani's epithets (Honest/Holy) are term choices worth noting.
* **`bi-idhn (by the leave)`**: 97:4's permission clause.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:97]` "by Allah's leave", `[7:58]`, `[34:12]` (rg-verified) — every descent is a commissioned descent.
* **`salām (peace)`**: 97:5's predicate-of-the-night.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[56:26]` "Peace, peace"; cf. the greeting-forms across the Paradise scenes (rg-verified) — the night preaches the vocabulary of the Garden.
