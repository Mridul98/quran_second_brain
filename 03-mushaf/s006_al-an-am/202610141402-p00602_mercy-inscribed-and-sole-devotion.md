# Sovereignty, inscribed mercy, and the sole witness (6:11–20)
**UID:** 202610141402
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 6:11–20 (Ruku 2 of 20 in al-An’am)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[6:11]` – `[6:20]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A chain of “Say” commands hands the heavens, the earth, and night and day to Allah alone — who has “inscribed for Himself mercy” even as He gathers to a doubtful-less Resurrection — until the ruku culminates in the testimony question (“What thing is more solemn in testimony?”), where the Quran declares its own universal address and the shahada of One God is set against “your idolatry.”
* **Placement:** Ruku 2; the first sustained “Say…” block of the surah. It converts ruku 1’s creation argument into devotional exclusivity (6:14) and plants two motifs the surah will keep: mercy self-inscribed (6:12 → 6:54) and “those who lost their souls” (6:12 → 6:20 → 6:26).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[6:11]`** "Say, “Travel the earth and observe the final fate of the deniers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The command that makes history a laboratory — observation (iʿtibār) as worship.
* **`[6:12]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "Say, “To whom belongs what is in the heavens and the earth?” Say, “To Allah.”"
  * "He has inscribed for Himself mercy. He will gather you to the Day of Resurrection, in which there is no doubt."
  * "Those who lost their souls do not believe."
  * **Gloss:** The sovereignty Q&A is answered before the audience can speak; mercy becomes a self-obligation written by God Himself — even the Gathering is announced as mercy’s venue, not mercy’s exception.
* **`[6:13]`** "To Him belongs whatever rests in the night and the day. He is the Hearing, the Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Time itself is the property listed; the Hearing-Knowing seal grounds the witness language of 6:19.
* **`[6:14]`** "Say, “Shall I take for myself a protector other than Allah, Originator of the heavens and the earth, and He feeds and is not fed?” Say, “I am instructed to be the first of those who submit.” And do not be among the idolaters."
  * **Gloss:** Tawhid stated as dependency in both directions: He feeds, is not fed; submission (islām) is named before Islam’s formal legislation.
* **`[6:15]`** "Say, “I fear, should I defy my Lord, the punishment of a tremendous Day.”"
  * **Gloss:** Even the messenger’s fear is the argument’s form — no one stands outside the Day.
* **`[6:16]`** "Whoever is spared on that Day—He had mercy on him. That is the clear victory."
  * **Gloss:** Mercy and Day re-joined: victory = being spared, echoing the inscribed mercy of 6:12.
* **`[6:17]`** "If Allah touches you with adversity, none can remove it except He. And if He touches you with good—He is Capable of everything."
  * **Gloss:** Removal and giving have one address — the intercession economy of the idolaters loses its purpose.
* **`[6:18]`** "He is the Supreme over His servants. He is the Wise, the Expert."
  * **Gloss:** Qahhār — sovereignty over, not beside; Wisdom paired with microscopic precision (khabīr).
* **`[6:19]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "Say, “What thing is more solemn in testimony?” Say, “Allah is Witness between you and me."
  * "This Quran was revealed to me, that I may warn you with it, and whomever it may reach."
  * "Do you indeed testify that there are other gods with Allah?” Say, “I myself do not testify.” Say, “He is but One God, and I am innocent of your idolatry.”"
  * **Gloss:** The courtroom turns: God’s testimony outranks all oaths; the verse makes the Quran a self-declared address to “whomever it may reach” — a universal readership — before the One-God formula.
* **`[6:20]`** "Those to whom We have given the Book recognize it as they recognize their own children; but those who have lost their souls do not believe."
  * **Gloss:** Recognition exists — and is not enough; the ruku closes on its own opening category (6:12): those who lost their souls (observable bracket).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Travel the earth`** (sīrū fī al-arḍ): the observation command.
  * **Rendering:** “Travel the earth and observe the final fate of the deniers” `[6:11]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:137]` “So travel the earth and note the fate of the deniers” — near-verbatim twin; `[16:36]` “So travel through the earth, and see what the fate of the deniers was” (same verse also links 6:42’s “communities before you”); `[29:20]` “Roam the earth, and observe how He originated the creation” — here the destination is origin, not fate.
* **`inscribed for Himself mercy`** (kataba ʿalayhi nafsa hu rahma):
  * **Rendering:** “inscribed” `[6:12]`; at `[6:54]` the same construction renders “prescribed mercy for Himself” — verb drift within one surah (observable corpus fact; the shared Arabic idiom is classical — *Source: agent inference — verify*).
  * **Elsewhere:** no other occurrence in the corpus (verified by `rg`): the pair 6:12/6:54 is a within-surah mercy bracket.
* **`Originator of the heavens and the earth`** (Fāṭir):
  * **Rendering:** “Originator” at `[6:14]`, again at `[6:101]` (within-surah, later: “how can He have a son…”), and as the opening of Fatir: `[35:1]` “Praise be to Allah, Originator of the heavens and the earth.”
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:117]` “Originator of the heavens and the earth” — same English for bidʿ, not fuṭr (Source: agent inference — verify), a translation-overlap worth knowing when tracing the term.
* **`the Supreme over His servants`** (al-Qahhār):
  * **Rendering:** “Supreme” `[6:18]`; “Conqueror” `[6:61]`; “the One, the Irresistible” `[13:16]` — one attribute, three English costumes (Arabic identity: classical reading — *Source: agent inference — verify*).
  * **Elsewhere:** the Wisdom-Expert couplet frames it: `[6:18]`, `[6:73]`, `[6:103]`, `[34:1]`, `[67:14]` (all verified via `rg "the Expert"`).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical consensus; order 55); the Book-recognizers of 6:20 are classically read as Jews and Christians whose scriptures describe the prophet’s attributes (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir on 6:20 — verify*) — the plain Quran.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** none specific in the corpus; 6:19’s “whomever it may reach” is the surah’s own universalization of a Meccan address (observable).
