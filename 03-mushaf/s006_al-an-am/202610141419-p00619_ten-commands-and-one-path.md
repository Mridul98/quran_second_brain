# The ten commands and the one path (6:151–154)
**UID:** 202610141419
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 6:151–154 (Ruku 19 of 20 in al-An'am)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[6:151]` – `[6:154]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's moral testament is delivered in one sitting: six prohibitions on God, parents, children, indecency, blood — then four on orphan-wealth, scales, speech, covenant — and the whole is gathered into a road: "This is My path, straight, so follow it," with Moses' Scripture cited as the precedent of the same gift.
* **Placement:** The covenant climax the whole second half has been building toward: every forbidden thing here has appeared earlier in the surah (associating 6:100/136, child-killing 6:137/140, the Name on meat 6:118–121, the wombs 6:139, injustice 6:131); 6:153 is Fatihah's answered petition.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[6:151]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "Say, “Come, let me tell you what your Lord has forbidden you: that you associate nothing with Him; that you honor your parents;"
  * "that you do not kill your children because of poverty—We provide for you and for them; that you do not come near indecencies, whether outward or inward;"
  * "and that you do not kill the soul which Allah has sanctified—except in the course of justice. All this He has enjoined upon you, so that you may understand.”"
  * **Gloss:** The invitation verb ("Come") frames a forbid-list that is actually a protect-list: creed, parents, children, chastity, life — each item answering a Meccan practice indicted in ruku 16; the provision-clause interrupts the child-command mid-sentence.
* **`[6:152]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "And do not come near the property of the orphan, except with the best intentions, until he reaches maturity."
  * "And give full weight and full measure, equitably. We do not burden any soul beyond its capacity."
  * "And when you speak, be fair, even if it concerns a close relative. And fulfill your covenant with Allah. All this He has enjoined upon you, so that you may take heed."
  * **Gloss:** The social half: orphan-wealth approached only in the best way, commerce weighed with equity (with the capacity-clause tucked in as mercy), testimony fair even against kin, covenants kept — the five verbs of the market and the courtroom.
* **`[6:153]`** "This is My path, straight, so follow it. And do not follow the other paths, lest they divert you from His path. All this He has enjoined upon you, that you may refrain from wrongdoing."
  * **Gloss:** The list becomes a road — God's first-person path — with exactly one instruction about the alternatives: don't take them; the purpose-clause ("that you may refrain") is the surah's own taqwa-wording.
* **`[6:154]`** "Then We gave Moses the Scripture, perfect for the righteous, and explaining everything clearly, and a beacon, and mercy, that they may believe in the encounter with their Lord."
  * **Gloss:** Precedent: the same gift-pattern — book, completion, detail, light, mercy, and the encounter as its aim (echoing 6:92's twin credential and 6:91's denied Moses-chain).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"My path":**
  * **Rendering:** "This is My path, straight" `[6:153]` — the first-person path-claim is a corpus hapax (rg-verified: "My path" occurs only here).
  * **Elsewhere:** the answer to `[1:6]` "Guide us to the straight path"; and to the petition's plural counter-paths, 6:153's "the other paths" is the surah's own version of 1:7's two failed groups (Source: agent structural reading — verify). Within the surah: `[6:126]` "the straight path of your Lord" and `[6:161]` "My Lord has guided me to a straight path" bracket it.
* **"the soul which Allah has sanctified":**
  * **Rendering:** `[6:151]` — "sanctified" (this surah alone; cf. `[27:91]` "sanctified" for submitted beings, rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[17:33]` "the soul which Allah has made sacred, except in the course of justice" — near-verbatim twin including the justice-exception; `[25:68]` — the same pairing of no-god-and-no-killing in one verse.
* **"covenant with Allah":**
  * **Rendering:** "fulfill your covenant with Allah" `[6:152]` — the only covenant-fulfillment command inside the testament.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:91]` "Fulfill Allah's covenant when you make a covenant" — same verb, scaled to daily oaths; within the surah, 6:152's clause re-opens the Mosaic pledge-list of `[2:83]` (parents, orphans, kin — the shared vocabulary; Source: classical covenant reading — verify).
* **The purpose-trio ("so that you may…"):**
  * **Rendering:** 6:151 "so that you may understand," 6:152 "so that you may take heed," 6:153 "that you may refrain from wrongdoing" — three graded ends for three law-blocks (observable).
  * **Elsewhere:** the third is the English face of taqwa-family wording in this surah (cf. `[6:51]` "perhaps they will grow in piety"); the trio is the surah's own pedagogy note: law aimed at minds, memory, and restraint.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical consensus); the testament is the surah's own summary of what 6:145's closed-list doctrine was protecting.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the structural comparison of 6:151–153 with the Decalogue-tradition is agent inference — verify (the corpus nowhere numbers them); classical commentators call these "the commandments" collectively without a fixed count (observable disagreement in the verse-divisions of the lists).
