# Liars before the Fire (6:21–30)
**UID:** 202610141403
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 6:21–30 (Ruku 3 of 20 in al-An’am)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[6:21]` – `[6:30]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku runs one verdict through three scenes: fabrication of lies against Allah is the greatest wrong (6:21); at the Gathering the idols fail to show and the oath “we were not idolaters” is exposed as self-deception (6:22–24); in this world the listeners already have covers on their hearts and call the revelation “myths of the ancients” (6:25–26); and at the Fire they wish to be sent back — with the damning caveat that even sent back, they would revert (6:27–29), answered by the Lord’s single question: “Is this not real?” (6:30).
* **Placement:** Ruku 3; the surah’s first Day-scene trilogy. Its soul-loss vocabulary picks up directly from 6:12/6:20 (“they ruin only their own souls,” 6:26), and its lie-fabrication charge becomes the surah’s recurring polemic (6:93, 6:144).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[6:21]`** "Who does greater wrong than someone who fabricates lies against Allah, or denies His revelations? The wrongdoers will not succeed."
  * **Gloss:** Two crimes, one verdict: forging revelation and rejecting it are mirror-image fabrications.
* **`[6:22]`** "On the Day when We gather them all together, then say to the idolaters, “Where are your idols, those you used to claim?”"
  * **Gloss:** The absent-gods interrogation — the courtroom scene ruku 2 announced.
* **`[6:23]`** "Then their only argument will be to say, “By Allah, our Lord, we were not idolaters.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their last move is an oath against their own history — eloquence reduced to perjury.
* **`[6:24]`** "Look how they lied to themselves. And what they invented deserted them."
  * **Gloss:** The lie turns inward; the invented partners simply stop showing up.
* **`[6:25]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "Among them are those who listen to you; but We place covers over their hearts, to prevent them from understanding it, and heaviness in their ears."
  * "Even if they see every sign, they will not believe in it. Until, when they come to you, to argue with you, those who disbelieve will say, “These are nothing but myths of the ancients.”"
  * **Gloss:** The failure to hear is diagnosed theologically (We place the covers — divine passive of hardening) and then performed socially: argument as slogan.
* **`[6:26]`** "They keep others from it, and avoid it themselves; but they ruin only their own souls, and they do not realize."
  * **Gloss:** Gatekeeping against the Quran that is also self-harm — the third “lost souls” occurrence in fifteen verses (6:12, 6:20, 6:26).
* **`[6:27]`** "If only you could see, when they are made to stand before the Fire; they will say, “If only we could be sent back, and not reject the revelations of our Lord, and be among the faithful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual wish — “if only you could see” as the surah’s cinematic device.
* **`[6:28]`** "What they used to conceal before will become clear to them. And even if they were sent back, they would revert to what they were forbidden. They are liars."
  * **Gloss:** The wish is voided by a character verdict: return would not reform — “they are liars” names the habit, not the moment.
* **`[6:29]`** "And they say, “There is nothing but our life in this world, and we will not be resurrected.”"
  * **Gloss:** The materialist creed stated plainly — the ruku’s opponent finally gives his own line.
* **`[6:30]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "If only you could see, when they are stationed before their Lord. He will say, “Is this not real?” They will say, “Yes indeed, by our Lord.”"
  * "He will say, “Then taste the torment for having disbelieved.”"
  * **Gloss:** One question, one confession, one sentence — the scene in which their “no resurrection” testifies itself away.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fabricates lies against Allah`** (kadhib ʿalā Allāh):
  * **Rendering:** “fabricates lies against Allah” `[6:21]`; the same charge family recurs in this surah as “invents falsehood against Allah” `[6:93]`, “fabricating lies against Him” `[6:138]`, “invents lies and attributes them to Allah” `[6:144]` (all verified) — the surah’s central polemic verb.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:37]`, `[10:17]`, `[11:18]`, `[29:68]` carry the same greatest-wrong formula (refs verified via `rg`).
* **`myths of the ancients`** (asāṭīr al-awwalīn):
  * **Rendering:** “These are nothing but myths of the ancients” `[6:25]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** the exact phrase verified at `[8:31]` and `[68:15]` — the standard Meccan dismissal label.
* **`covers over their hearts`** (ghishāwaʿ / akinnah):
  * **Rendering:** “We place covers over their hearts… and heaviness in their ears” `[6:25]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[18:57]` “We have placed coverings over their hearts, lest they understand it, and heaviness in their ears” — near-verbatim twin (verified); `[17:46]` “We drape veils over their hearts” — same image, third verb, plus “heaviness in their ears”. The corpus shows one hardening idiom in three English dressings.
* **`ruin only their own souls`** (khusr al-nafs):
  * **Rendering:** `[6:26]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:12]` “Those who lost their souls do not believe”; `[6:20]` repeats it verbatim — the ruku-2/ruku-3 spine of self-loss (observable within-surah chain).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical consensus; order 55); the three scenes (Gathering, argument tents, Fire) belong to the surah’s Day-drama genre rather than to any reported occasion.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 6:25’s class of listeners is classically read as polytheist auditors (and, in some reports, as hypocrites of a later period) — the corpus itself names no group (*Source: classical dispute on 6:25 — verify*). The reading that hardening is God’s action “to prevent them from understanding” is the plain text; its compatibility with their own refusal (6:26, 6:28) is a classical tension left unresolved here (Source: agent observation — verify).
