# The argument of Abraham (6:71–82)
**UID:** 202610141409
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 6:71–82 (Ruku 9 of 20 in al-An’am)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[6:71]` – `[6:82]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah’s creed-sermon: useless idols — what “can neither benefit us nor harm us” — are answered by the God who says “Be,” it will be, whose sovereignty rises with the Trumpet; then Abraham is given as the argument made flesh, from his father Azar to the astronomy of the setting lights — star, moon, sun, each dethroned by its own setting — until the courtroom question lands: which side is more entitled to security? The ruku answers with a definition: those who believe and do not obscure faith with wrongdoing — security, and guidance.
* **Placement:** Ruku 9; the first Abraham narrative of the surah and the payoff of 6:19 (“I am innocent of your idolatry,” reused verbatim at 6:78) and of the darkness vocabulary of 6:59/6:63. Ruku 10 opens on the demonstrative: “That was Our argument which We gave to Abraham.”

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[6:71]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "Say, “Shall we invoke besides Allah something that can neither benefit us nor harm us, and turn back on our heels after Allah has guided us; like someone seduced by the devils and confused on earth, who has friends calling him to guidance: 'Come to us'?"
  * "Say, “The guidance of Allah is the guidance, and we are commanded to surrender to the Lord of the Universe.”"
  * **Gloss:** The idol defined by zero effect, apostasy by a seduction-parable — the lost man with well-meaning callers — then the exclusive clause: “The guidance of Allah is the guidance,” the article as a theological claim. (Typography: the inner quote prints straight single marks — quoted as printed.)
* **`[6:72]`** "“And to perform the prayers, and to revere Him; it is to Him that you will be gathered.”"
  * **Gloss:** Surrender cashes out in prayer and reverencing — against the 6:12 Gathering announced in ruku 2.
* **`[6:73]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "It is He who created the heavens and the earth in truth. On the Day when He says: “Be,” it will be. His saying is the truth, and His is the sovereignty on the Day when the trumpet is blown."
  * "The Knower of secrets and declarations. He is the Wise, the Expert."
  * **Gloss:** Creation “in truth,” saying “truth,” sovereignty at the Trumpet: the same kun that made cosmos will remount it; the Wise-Expert couplet (6:18) seals.
* **`[6:74]`** "Abraham said to his father Azar, “Do you take idols for gods? I see that you and your people are in evident error.”"
  * **Gloss:** The argument descends into one household; “evident error” (ḍalāl mubīn) is the surah’s diagnosis word.
* **`[6:75]`** "Thus We showed Abraham the empire of the heavens and the earth, that he might be one of those with certainty."
  * **Gloss:** Vision as gift: the malakūt — “empire,” a corpus-unique rendering — shown so that certainty (yaqīn) may grow.
* **`[6:76]`** "When the night fell over him, he saw a planet. He said, “This is my lord.” But when it set, he said, “I do not love those that set.”"
  * **Gloss:** The sermon’s first act: a provisional “my lord” — classically read as pedagogical irony (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir — verify*) — undone by setting; love refuses the transient.
* **`[6:77]`** "Then, when he saw the moon rising, he said, “This is my lord.” But when it set, he said, “If my Lord does not guide me, I will be one of the erring people.”"
  * **Gloss:** The pitch rises — and so does the prophet’s self-protection: he invokes Allah’s guidance against error, the ruku’s guidance motif in a prayer.
* **`[6:78]`** "Then, when he saw the sun rising, he said, “This is my lord, this is bigger.” But when it set, he said, “O my people, I am innocent of your idolatry."
  * **Gloss:** The biggest light sets last, and the sermon turns address; (the quotation runs unclosed into 6:79 — book typography, quoted as printed.)
* **`[6:79]`** "I have directed my attention towards Him Who created the heavens and the earth—a monotheist—and I am not of the idolaters.”"
  * **Gloss:** Face turned to the Creator-only — ḥanīf as “a monotheist,” the surah’s own word for Abraham at 6:161 (“the creed of Abraham the Monotheist”).
* **`[6:80]`** "And his people argued with him. He said, “Do you argue with me about Allah, when He has guided me? I do not fear what you associate with Him, unless my Lord wills it. My Lord comprehends all things in knowledge. Will you not reconsider?"
  * **Gloss:** Argument against guidance is category-error; fear is delegated to will, and knowledge out-encircles every idol (quote unclosed as printed).
* **`[6:81]`** "And why should I fear those you associate with Him, and you do not fear associating others with Allah for which He sent down to you no authority? Which side is more entitled to security, if you are aware?”"
  * **Gloss:** The reductio: they fear nothing while arming gods with zero sent-down authority (sulṭān) — the security question reframes the whole ruku.
* **`[6:82]`** "Those who believe, and do not obscure their faith with wrongdoing—those will have security, and they are guided."
  * **Gloss:** Verdict: security (amn) and guidance to faith un-mixed with ẓulm — classically glossed as shirk (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir on 6:82, pairing it with 31:13 — verify*).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Be`** (kun):
  * **Rendering:** “On the Day when He says: “Be,” it will be” `[6:73]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:117]` “Whenever He decrees a thing, He says to it, ‘Be,’ and it becomes”; `[16:40]` “When We intend for something to happen, We say to it, ‘Be,’ and it becomes”; `[36:82]` “and it comes to be” — one creative formula, three tails (verified).
* **`the trumpet`** (būq/nafkh):
  * **Rendering:** “the Day when the trumpet is blown” `[6:73]` (lowercase in corpus, verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[18:99]` “the Trumpet will be blown”; `[20:102]` “On the Day when the Trumpet is blown”; `[39:68]` “the Trumpet will be sounded” — casing and verb drift within the edition (observable corpus fact).
* **`a monotheist`** (ḥanīf):
  * **Rendering:** “a monotheist” `[6:79]`; “the Monotheist” for Abraham at `[6:161]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:67]` “Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian, but he was a Monotheist, a Muslim”; `[10:105]` “dedicate yourself to the true religion—a monotheist—and never be of the polytheists”; `[16:123]` “Follow the religion of Abraham, the Monotheist” — the creed-above-communities claim, twin to 6:79 (all verified).
* **`wrongdoing`** (ẓulm):
  * **Rendering:** “do not obscure their faith with wrongdoing” `[6:82]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[31:13]` “idolatry is a terrible wrong” — the classical key that reads 6:82’s ẓulm as shirk (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir — verify*); the surah’s own ẓulm chain: 6:52 (“one of the unjust”), 6:58 (“Allah is well aware of the unjust”).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical consensus; order 55); the Abraham scene is the surah’s home argument — creed through cosmos, the same move as ruku 1’s praise and ruku 7’s keys.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** “Azar” appears once in the corpus (6:74, verified via `rg`) — classically disputed as father’s name vs. uncle vs. idol-name, since transmitted genealogies name Abraham’s father differently (*Source: classical dispute on 6:74 — verify*); the “planet” (6:76) is classically read as a wandering star, not the fixed stars of 6:97 (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir — verify*).
