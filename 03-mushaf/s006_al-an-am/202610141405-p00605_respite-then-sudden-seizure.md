# Respite, then sudden seizure (6:42–50)
**UID:** 202610141405
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 6:42–50 (Ruku 5 of 20 in al-An’am)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[6:42]` – `[6:50]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A divine sociology in one arc: affliction is sent to humble, but hearts harden and Satan decorates the deeds; when the reminder is disregarded, Allah opens “the gates of all things” — prosperity as trap — and seizes suddenly; the pattern closes with praise (6:45), two “Have you considered?” interrogations (hearing/sight sealed; punishment sudden or gradual), and the messenger’s fourfold disclaimer: no treasuries, no knowledge of the future, not an angel — “I only follow what is inspired to me.”
* **Placement:** Ruku 5; the harvest of ruku 4’s theodicy (6:34’s “they endured rejection and persecution until Our help came to them” now shown from the other side — communities that did not endure). 6:45’s praise line rhymes with the surah’s own opening (6:1), bookending the destruction motif of 6:6.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[6:42]`** "We sent messengers to communities before you, and We afflicted them with suffering and hardship, that they may humble themselves."
  * **Gloss:** The purpose clause first: baʾsāʾ wa ḍarrāʾ exist to bend the knee (taḍarruʿ).
* **`[6:43]`** "If only, when Our calamity came upon them, they humbled themselves. But their hearts hardened, and Satan made their deeds appear good to them."
  * **Gloss:** “If only” (law) as lament; the three-step failure: no humility, hard hearts, decorated deeds.
* **`[6:44]`** "Then, when they disregarded what they were reminded of, We opened for them the gates of all things. Until, when they delighted in what they were given, We seized them suddenly; and at once, they were in despair."
  * **Gloss:** The istidrāj move: answered prayers become the ambush; “suddenly” ties back to 6:31’s sudden Hour.
* **`[6:45]`** "Thus the last remnant of the people who did wrong was cut off. And praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds."
  * **Gloss:** Extinction of the remnant — and the destruction report ends in praise, the surah’s own opening word.
* **`[6:46]`** "Say, “Have you considered? If Allah took away your hearing and your sight, and set a seal on your hearts, what god other than Allah would restore them to you?” Note how We explain the revelations in various ways, yet they still turn away."
  * **Gloss:** The senses and the heart as repossessable loans; only the Restorer can be invoked — idol-relief is unimaginable.
* **`[6:47]`** "Say, “Have you considered? if Allah's punishment descended on you suddenly or gradually, would any be destroyed except the wrongdoing people?”"
  * **Gloss:** Sudden or slow, the destination is narrowed by the text itself: only the wrongdoers.
* **`[6:48]`** "We sent the messengers only as bearers of good news and as warners. Those who believe and reform have nothing to fear, nor shall they grieve."
  * **Gloss:** The messenger’s office, reduced to two functions; the fear/grief formula is the Quran’s standard assurance coinage.
* **`[6:49]`** "But as for those who reject Our revelations, torment will afflict them because of their defiance."
  * **Gloss:** The twin half of 6:48: warners accepted or rejected, the outcome splits by response, not by lineage.
* **`[6:50]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "Say, “I do not say to you that I possess the treasuries of Allah, nor do I know the future, nor do I say to you that I am an angel. I only follow what is inspired to me.”"
  * "Say, “Are the blind and the seeing alike? Do you not think?”"
  * **Gloss:** The fourfold disclaimer: the prophet renounces exactly the grandeur the ruku 1 crowd demanded (6:7–9) — medium, wealth, foreknowledge, angel-nature — and reframes the argument as a blindness test.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`bearers of good news and as warners`** (mubashshirūn wa mundhirūn):
  * **Rendering:** `[6:48]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:24]` “We sent you with the truth; a bearer of good news, and a warner. There is no community but a warner has passed through it” — the same pair plus the umam claim of 6:38/6:42; `[2:119]` “bringing good news, and giving warnings”; `[48:8]` “a witness, and a bearer of good news, and a warner” (all verified).
* **`the treasuries of Allah`** (khazāʾin / ʿind Allāh):
  * **Rendering:** “I possess the treasuries of Allah” `[6:50]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[11:31]` — Noah’s identical disclaimer “I do not say to you that I possess the treasures of Allah, nor do I know the future” — a near-verbatim prophet-formula twin (“treasures” for the same English slot — observable drift); `[17:100]` “the treasuries of my Lord’s mercy”; `[63:7]` “To Allah belong the treasures of the heavens and the earth.”
* **`the future`** (= the unseen, ghayb, per classical reading — *Source: agent inference — verify*):
  * **Rendering:** “nor do I know the future” `[6:50]`; `[11:31]` likewise; `[27:65]` “No one in the heavens or on earth knows the future except Allah.”
  * **Elsewhere:** five verses later the surah says “With Him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them except He” `[6:59]` — “future” and “unseen” as two renderings of one semantic field inside a single surah (observable corpus fact).
* **`made their deeds appear good`** (zayyana):
  * **Rendering:** “Satan made their deeds appear good to them” `[6:43]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[27:24]` “Satan made their conduct appear good to them”; `[16:63]` “Satan made their deeds appear alluring to them”; `[6:122]` within-surah: “Thus the doings of disbelievers are made to appear good to them” — the decoration-verb runs as a surah motif.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Twin formula:** `[11:31]` — Noah’s disclaimer is 6:50’s in another prophet’s mouth — the “I only follow” office belongs to the whole messenger chain (cf. `[10:15]` “I only follow what is revealed to me”).
* **Twin narrative:** `[7:94–95]` — misery “so that they may humble themselves,” prosperity substituted, “Then We seized them suddenly, while they were unaware” — al-Aʿrāf’s version of 6:42–44 (verified near-verbatim of both purpose and seizure clauses).
* **Within surah:** `[6:45]` “And praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds” ↔ `[6:1]` — praise bookends the destruction argument first raised in 6:6.
* **Within surah:** `[6:46]` seal on hearts ↔ `[6:25]` covers over hearts — the hardening idiom escalates from drapery to seal (and `[2:74]` gives the rock simile).
* **Parallel assurance:** `[5:69]` — “they have nothing to fear, nor shall they grieve” — the 6:48 formula’s sister in the covenant-keeping list.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical consensus; order 55); the ruku reads as a sociology of Meccan prosperity — rain and rivers (6:6) and “gates of all things” (6:44) belong to the same trade imagery.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 6:44’s opening of “the gates of all things” is classically read as istidrāj — respite that confirms delusion (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir on 6:44 — verify*). The corpus itself offers no occasion note (observable).
