# Do not drive away the lowly callers (6:51–55)
**UID:** 202610141406
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 6:51–55 (Ruku 6 of 20 in al-An’am)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[6:51]` – `[6:55]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah’s social-piety core: the Quran’s real audience is those who fear the Gathering without protector or intercessor — and precisely those people, the morning-and-evening callers of no worldly rank, must not be driven away, because favor is Allah’s to distribute and He is “aware of the appreciative”; the ruku then flips the verdict into a greeting: peace to the believers, for whoever did wrong in ignorance and repented sits inside the mercy Allah prescribed for Himself.
* **Placement:** Ruku 6 — the shortest ruku in the surah’s first half, and its ethics hinge. It answers ruku 5’s disclaimer (6:50 “nor do I know the future”) socially: if the prophet owns no treasuries, seating by rank is a counterfeit economy. The mercy bracket (6:12 “inscribed” → 6:54 “prescribed”) closes here.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[6:51]`** "And warn with it those who fear to be gathered before their Lord—they have no protector or intercessor apart from him—perhaps they will grow in piety."
  * **Gloss:** “With it” = the Quran (from 6:50’s inspiration clause); the warned are those already trembling at the Gathering — and the lowercase “him” toward Allah is book typography, quoted as printed.
* **`[6:52]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "And do not drive away those who call upon their Lord, morning and evening, seeking His attention."
  * "You are not accountable for them in any way, nor are they accountable for you in any way. If you drive them away, you would be one of the unjust."
  * **Gloss:** A command to the Prophet with a mutual-acquisition clause: no transfer of reward between classes — expulsion is not pruning but injustice (ẓulm).
* **`[6:53]`** "Thus We try some of them by means of others, that they may say, “Are these the ones whom Allah has favored from among us?” Is Allah not aware of the appreciative?"
  * **Gloss:** The elite sneer is quoted, then answered: favor is a trial instrument, and Allah’s awareness — not their eyesight — settles who is favored.
* **`[6:54]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "When those who believe in Our revelations come to you, say, “Peace be upon you, your Lord has prescribed mercy for Himself."
  * "Whoever among you does wrong out of ignorance, and then repents afterwards and reforms—He is Forgiving and Merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The greeting doubles as doctrine: salām spoken to the lowly becomes the announcement of the self-prescribed mercy — with a defined repentance loop: ignorance → wrong → repent → reform.
* **`[6:55]`** "Thus We explain the revelations, and expose the path of the unrighteous."
  * **Gloss:** The explanation refrain returns (as at 6:46, 6:65) — detail given, paths laid bare, choice re-opened.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`morning and evening`** (ghadātan wa ʿashiyyan):
  * **Rendering:** “call upon their Lord, morning and evening, seeking His attention” `[6:52]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[18:28]` “content yourself with those who pray to their Lord morning and evening, desiring His Presence” — the near-verbatim twin (same scene, same social rebuke; “attention” vs. “Presence” for the divine face is observable rendering drift).
* **`protector or intercessor`** (waliyy/shafīʿ):
  * **Rendering:** “no protector or intercessor apart from him” `[6:51]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:254]` “nor friendship, nor intercession” (the Day-economy clause); `[32:4]` “Apart from Him, you have no master and no intercessor”; `[40:18]` “no intimate friend, and no intercessor to be obeyed”; `[10:18]` — the idolaters’ claim “These are our intercessors” is the fiction 6:51 denies (all verified).
* **`prescribed mercy for Himself`** — see ruku 2’s “inscribed” (6:12): the two-verse bracket across ruku 2–6 is the surah’s mercy spine (observable; single verb in Arabic per classical reading — *Source: agent inference — verify*).
* **`Peace be upon you`** (salām):
  * **Rendering:** spoken by the Prophet to arriving believers `[6:54]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:32]` angels’ death-hour greeting “Peace be upon you; enter Paradise”; `[39:73]` paradise keepers’ greeting — 6:54 gives salām already in this world, anticipating the entry greeting (verified lines).
* **`repents afterwards and reforms`** (tāba wa aṣlaḥa):
  * **Rendering:** `[6:54]`, qualified by “out of ignorance” (jahlā).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:39]` “But whoever repents after his crime, and reforms, Allah will accept his repentance” — the twin repent-reform formula in the covenant surah (verified).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Scene twin:** `[18:28]` — the Cave’s version of the same rebuke, extended: “do not obey him whose heart We have made heedless… he follows his own desires” — which 6:56 will echo in the Prophet’s own words.
* **Prophet-formula:** `[11:31]` — Noah: “nor do I say of those who are despicable in your eyes that Allah will never give them any good” — the rank-blind sneer answered by a messenger centuries earlier (verified).
* **Rank and trial:** `[43:32]` — “We elevated some of them in rank above others… But your Lord’s mercy is better than what they amass” — degrees as trial, answered from mercy (6:53 ↔ 43:32).
* **Within surah:** `[6:51]` “fear to be gathered” ↔ `[6:12]` “He will gather you to the Day of Resurrection” — the Gathering announced in ruku 2 is now the filter of audience; `[6:54]` ↔ `[6:12]` the mercy inscription bracket closes.
* **Refrain:** `[6:55]` ↔ `[6:46]` ↔ `[6:65]` — the Thus-explain / Note-how-we-explain pairing (verified all three) — the surah marks its hinge-points with this clause.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical consensus; order 55). The ruku’s social contrast — rank-holders vs. the morning-and-evening callers — is classically read as Quraysh notables demanding a separate seating from humble believers (*Source: classical transmitted reports on 6:52 — verify*; the reports naming Bilal, Suḥayb, ʿAmmār and Khubbāb are in the tafsīr literature, not the corpus).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 6:54’s “does wrong out of ignorance” is classically treated as the broad door for pre-repentance sins (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir on 6:54 — verify*).
