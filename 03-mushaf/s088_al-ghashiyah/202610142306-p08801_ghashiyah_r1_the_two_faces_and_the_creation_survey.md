# The Two Faces, the Camels, and the Bounded Reminder (88:1–26)
**UID:** 202610142306
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 88:1–26 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Ghashiyah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[88:1]` – `[88:26]` (26 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A news-report of the Overwhelming Day opens two face-cameras — shamed, laboring, exhausted faces fed by a flaming spring on thorns that "neither nourishes, nor satisfies hunger," versus joyful, satisfied faces in a lofty garden with "no nonsense" heard — then grounds the whole contrast in a four-item creation-survey (camels, sky, mountains, earth), and finally defines the messenger's office: remind without control, because "To Us is their return. Then upon Us rests their reckoning."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1; the batch's sensory center — 87's two-life trade and 86's disclosure Day arrive here as faces, drinks, foods, and furnishings; the survey hands creation-wonder to 89's oaths and 91's cosmos.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[88:1]`** "Has there come to you the news of the overwhelming?"
  * **Gloss:** The question-formula news of a Day that "overwhelms" — same formula-verb at `[85:17]` (rg-verified); the noun-form "the overwhelming" pairs only with `[69:5]`, where it renders Thamood's annihilating blast — "As for Thamood, they were annihilated by the Overwhelming" — a different Arabic word on standard lexical notes (88: ghashiyah; 69:5: tāghiyah — agent inference, verify), and the collision is worth keeping in mind when the batch's dossier cites `[69:5]`. The verb elsewhere overwhelms the sea-bound, the humiliated, and schemers `[20:78]`, `[70:44]`, `[4:88]`, `[35:43]` (rg-verified).
* **`[88:2]`** "Faces on that Day will be shamed."
  * **Gloss:** Column one opens: faces marked by shame (khashi'a) — the Day's public record begins with humiliation; "Faces on that Day" idiom shared with batch neighbors `[75:22]`, `[80:38]`, `[80:40]` (rg-verified).
* **`[88:3]`** "Laboring and exhausted."
  * **Gloss:** The face's condition: laboring ('āmila) and exhausted (nāsiba) — effort without yield; the same sa'y-root returns at 88:9 inverted ("Satisfied with their endeavor").
* **`[88:4]`** "Roasting in a scorching Fire."
  * **Gloss:** The destination named: roasting (taslā) in a scorching Fire (nār hāmiya) — heat as the environment of the shamed face.
* **`[88:5]`** "Given to drink from a flaming spring."
  * **Gloss:** The reverse-provision: drink that burns — a flaming spring ('ayn āniya); the antithesis of column two's flowing spring (88:12).
* **`[88:6]`** "They will have no food except thorns."
  * **Gloss:** The reverse-provision continues: food reduced to thorns (darī'); "thorns" occurs in the corpus only here and at `[111:5]` "a rope of thorns" (rg-verified); the bitter-shrub kin is `[34:16]` "thorny shrubs".
* **`[88:7]`** "That neither nourishes, nor satisfies hunger."
  * **Gloss:** The double failure: neither nutrition nor satiety — food that fails both functions; the reverse-provision audit complete.
* **`[88:8]`** "Faces on that Day will be joyful."
  * **Gloss:** Column two opens: the same face-word reversed — joyful (nā'ima); the Day's public record now shows radiance.
* **`[88:9]`** "Satisfied with their endeavor."
  * **Gloss:** Labor transfigured, not erased: satisfied (rāḍiya) with their own endeavor (sa'y) — the same effort-root as 88:3's "Laboring" but inverted; the righteous are content with what they worked for.
* **`[88:10]`** "In a lofty Garden."
  * **Gloss:** The setting named: an elevated garden ('āliya) — height as the spatial counterpart to the shamed faces' descent, the vertical axis of the two columns.
* **`[88:11]`** "In it you will hear no nonsense."
  * **Gloss:** The soundscape purified: no laghw — the idle-speech word shared with `[19:62]` and `[56:25]` (rg-verified); silence-as-blessing, the auditory counterpart to visual radiance.
* **`[88:12]`** "In it is a flowing spring."
  * **Gloss:** Water provided without labor: a running spring ('ayn jāriya) inside the garden — the antithesis of column one's flaming spring (88:5).
* **`[88:13]`** "In it are raised beds."
  * **Gloss:** Furniture elevated: raised seating (surur marfū'a) — posture as honor, the bodily counterpart to the joyful face.
* **`[88:14]`** "And cups set in place."
  * **Gloss:** Drink-vessels positioned and ready: the preparedness of bliss, nothing fetched or earned in the moment.
* **`[88:15]`** "And cushions set in rows."
  * **Gloss:** Textile order: cushions arranged (namāriq masfūfa) — the aesthetic of alignment, bliss as geometry rather than chaos.
* **`[88:16]`** "And carpets spread around."
  * **Gloss:** The garden's floor-covering: carpets "spread around" with the surah's own spread-word for the earth (88:20) — floor-covering answered by earth-covering (Source: observable English-rendering echo in this edition; root-level identity — agent inference, verify).
* **`[88:17]`** "Do they not look at the camels—how they are created?"
  * **Gloss:** The survey opens with nearest livestock: camels — otherwise literal-livestock at `[6:144]`, simile-sparks at `[77:33]`, and water-turns in the she-camel dossier `[7:73]`–`[7:77]` (rg-verified): the animal that is the Hejaz's whole economy becomes the first sign.
* **`[88:18]`** "And at the sky—how it is raised?"
  * **Gloss:** The second survey-item: the vault's elevation as a sign — "raised" (rafa'a) is the same verb-family as the beds "raised" at 88:13, linking garden-furniture to cosmic architecture.
* **`[88:19]`** "And at the mountains—how they are installed?"
  * **Gloss:** The third survey-item: mountains as stabilizers "installed" (nusbāt) — anchoring-function as sign, the earth's own furniture matching the garden's.
* **`[88:20]`** "And at the earth—how it is spread out?"
  * **Gloss:** The fourth survey-item: earth spread out — the same earth-spread word recurs at `[50:7]`, `[79:30]`, `[91:6]` (rg-verified); the carpet-spread of 88:16 answered by earth-spread here.
* **`[88:21]`** "So remind. You are only a reminder."
  * **Gloss:** The office-charter, first clause: reminder yes — the bounded mission; near-twin `[50:45]` "you are not a dictator over them. So remind…" (rg-verified).
* **`[88:22]`** "You have no control over them."
  * **Gloss:** The office-charter, second clause: controller no — the muṣayṭir-form is corpus-paired only with `[52:37]` "Or are they the controllers?"; the guardian-disclaimers `[10:108]`, `[6:104]`, `[11:86]` complete the family (rg-verified).
* **`[88:23]`** "But whoever turns away and disbelieves."
  * **Gloss:** The turn (tawalla) is the surah's own verb for column-one behavior — rejection named as the condition for what follows.
* **`[88:24]`** "Allah will punish him with the greatest punishment."
  * **Gloss:** "The greatest punishment" — the superlative that matches 87:12's "Gigantic Fire" in rank-language (Source: observable renderings); divine punishment reserved for the turner-away.
* **`[88:25]`** "To Us is their return."
  * **Gloss:** The reserved close, first clause: return as first-person-plural divine property — kin to `[50:43]` "to Us is the destination" (rg-verified).
* **`[88:26]`** "Then upon Us rests their reckoning."
  * **Gloss:** The reserved close, second clause: account also divine — no judge/force clauses exist in this rendering; the close is return-reckoning alone (see index §5b).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`al-Ghashiyah` (the Overwhelming)**: the Day that comes uninvited and covers everything.
  * **Rendering:** "the news of the overwhelming" (88:1) — event-name, lowercase.
  * **Elsewhere:** the verb-uses: `[4:88]` "Allah Himself has overwhelmed them", `[20:78]` "the sea overwhelmed them", `[70:44]` "overwhelmed by humiliation", `[54:10]` "I am overwhelmed, so help me" (rg-verified); the capital-twin form `[69:5]` "the Overwhelming" is Thamood's blast-word, not this Day-word (lexical mapping — verify).
* **`Faces` (wujūh)**: the day's public record.
  * **Rendering:** "shamed. Laboring and exhausted" vs "joyful. Satisfied with their endeavor" (88:2–3, 8–9).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[75:22]` "Faces on that Day will be radiant"; `[80:38]`/`[80:40]` radiant-and-dust — the batch's face-camera idiom (rg-verified).
* **`endeavor` (sa'y)**: one's own labor/stroke.
  * **Rendering:** "Satisfied with their endeavor" (88:9) — the word appears once in s088 (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** within the surah only: 88:3's "Laboring and exhausted" and 88:9's "Satisfied with their endeavor" form the labor-inversion pair — same effort, opposite ends (Source: observable rendering contrast; agent inference — verify).
* **`control` (muṣayṭir)**: taskmaster, overlord.
  * **Rendering:** "You have no control over them" (88:22).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[52:37]` "Or are they the controllers?"; `[50:45]` "not a dictator"; guardian-disclaimers `[10:108]`, `[6:104]`, `[11:86]` (rg-verified).
