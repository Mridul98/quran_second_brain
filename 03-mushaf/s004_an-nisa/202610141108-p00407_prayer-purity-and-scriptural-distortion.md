# Know what you say: prayer purity and the people who displace words (4:43–50)
**UID:** 202610141108
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:43–50 (Ruku 7 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:43]` – `[4:50]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The first "O you who believe!" of the surah's second half legislates presence itself: no prayer in intoxication "so that you know what you say," purification for every state with clean sand when water fails — and then the camera pans to those given a share of the Book, who buy error, displace words from their contexts, and answer "We hear and we disobey," until the surah states the unforgivable: association with Allah — and the matching vanity: claiming purity for oneself, when "Allah purifies whom He wills."
* **Placement:** The pivot from household law (ruku 1–6) to communal discipline (ruku 7–15): worship-state law opens the section; the Scriptural-corruption motif here becomes the full covenant-breach narrative of ruku 22 (4:153–162); 4:48 is the first of the surah's two near-verbatim shirk-verdicts (its doublet is 4:116, ruku 18).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:43]`** "O you who believe! Do not approach the prayer while you are drunk, so that you know what you say;"
  * **Gloss:** The prohibition rides on a stated purpose — knowing what you say; prayer as lucid speech (the kalam-motif applied to the worshipper's side).
* **`[4:43]`** "nor after sexual orgasm—unless you are travelling—until you have bathed."
  * **Gloss:** Ritual impurity addressed with the travel exception embedded mid-clause.
* **`[4:43]`** "If you are sick, or traveling, or one of you comes from the toilet, or you have had intercourse with women, and cannot find water, find clean sand and wipe your faces and your hands with it."
  * **Gloss:** The tayammum rule: four excuses, one substitute, two body-parts — mercy-engineering for the weak (cf. 4:28's anthropology).
* **`[4:43]`** "Allah is Pardoning and Forgiving."
  * **Gloss:** The purity-law closes with the pardon-names — the refrain-family that fences every concession.
* **`[4:44]`** "Have you not considered those who were given a share of the Book? They buy error, and wish you would lose the way."
  * **Gloss:** The "have you not considered" (a-lam ta'ram?) device opens here — it recurs exactly four times in this surah (`[4:44]`, `[4:49]`, `[4:51]`, `[4:77]`, plus "Have you not observed" at `[4:60]` — verified count in corpus), each time unmasking a claim to superiority; the purchase-idiom ("buy error") is the 2:16 trade-of-guidance family.
* **`[4:45]`** "But Allah knows your enemies best. Allah is sufficient as a Protector, and Allah is sufficient as a Supporter."
  * **Gloss:** The kafayya ("sufficient") double — the surah's signature trust-formula (cf. 4:6 "Reckoner", 4:45, 4:81 "Guardian enough", 4:132 "Manager", 4:166 "witness").
* **`[4:46]`** "Among the Jews are some who take words out of context, and say, “We hear and we disobey”, and “Hear without listening”, and “Observe us,” twisting with their tongues and slandering the religion."
  * **Gloss:** Four abuses catalogued: displacement of terms, the counter-slogan to 2:285's "We hear and we obey", the greeting warped (ra'ina-family), tongue-twist as religion-slander.
* **`[4:46]`** "Had they said, “We hear and we obey”, and “Listen”, and “Give us your attention,” it would have been better for them, and more upright."
  * **Gloss:** The corrective counterfactual — speech is rewritable; intent shows in word-choice (a speech-ethics the kalam-note's grammar shares).
* **`[4:46]`** "But Allah has cursed them for their disbelief; they do not believe except a little."
  * **Gloss:** The curse-refrain enters the surah (lay'ana family — reused 4:47, 4:52, 4:118; cf. 2:88/5:60/5:78, verified).
* **`[4:47]`** "O you who were given the Book! Believe in what We sent down, confirming what you have, before We obliterate faces and turn them inside out, or curse them as We cursed the Sabbath-breakers."
  * **Gloss:** The address, then the ultimatum in the precedent-form: the Sabbath-breakers as the known disaster-case (2:65's apes curse, verified).
* **`[4:47]`** "The command of Allah is always done."
  * **Gloss:** The five-word close: divine command as event, not petition.
* **`[4:48]`** "Allah does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives anything less than that to whomever He wills."
  * **Gloss:** The unforgivable line, drawn once — and here (ruku 7) then again at 4:116 (ruku 18) with the surah's own wording nearly identical (observable doublet).
* **`[4:48]`** "Whoever associates anything with Allah has devised a monstrous sin."
  * **Gloss:** Shirk re-named as a fabrication ("devised") and a "monstrous" (great) sin — the same "serious/great sin" ladder as 4:2.
* **`[4:49]`** "Have you not considered those who claim purity for themselves? Rather, Allah purifies whom He wills, and they will not be wronged a whit."
  * **Gloss:** Self-sanctification annulled: purity is Allah's distributive act; "a whit" — the atom-minimum of wrong held back even from the impure-claimers (4:124's same idiom, ruku 18).
* **`[4:50]`** "See how they devise lies against Allah. That alone is an outright sin."
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's last link: the devise/lie vocabulary (iftira') — lies-about-God as sin visible enough to need no further proof.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sukr` / "drunk... so that you know what you say"**: prayer barred in the confused state.
  * **Classical framing:** this is read in the sources as an interim stage of the intoxicants legislation (2:219 → 4:43 → 5:90 order debated; Ibn Kathir and the abrogation literature) — Source: classical tafsir/naskh discussion — verify sequence claims before repeating them.
* **`sa'id` / "clean sand"**: the wipe-substitute for water.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:6]` — the same "clean sand" (verified) in al-Ma'idah's fuller ablution verse — two-way dependence between this surah's and al-Ma'idah's purity law (the s005 index will hold the other half).
* **`badala`/`harafa` / "take words out of context" / "twisting"**: displacement and tongue-twist as Scriptural malpractice.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:93]` — "They said, “We hear and we disobey”" (verified verbatim twin, with the calf's heart-idiom) — 4:46 quotes Baqarah's counter-slogan; `[2:104]` "say not Ra'ina" family of speech-warping (the greeting-clause's context; verify rendering before citing word-for-word).
* **`shirk` / "association"**: 4:48; and the "wronged a whit" measure (naqlah/qitmur-family) shared with `[4:124]` and, in sign-contexts, `[27:12]`, `[35:27]` (verified matches).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:33]` "they ascribe associates to Allah" (verified) — the same ascribe/associate vocabulary as the watchful-God clause.
