# Prayer shortened, prayer in ranks, prayer at set times (4:101–104)
**UID:** 202610141116
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:101–104 (Ruku 15 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:101]` – `[4:104]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The campaign keeps its prayer: shorten it in travel when fear is real; when you lead in enemy sight, stand with you one group holding their weapons, let them prostrate and fall back, and let the second group come — precautions and weapons not set down except where rain or sickness excuses; then, when the prayer is done, remember Allah standing, sitting, on your sides — and when security returns, perform the prayer, for "the prayer is obligatory for believers at specific times"; so do not falter in pursuit: they ache as you ache, but you expect from Allah what they cannot expect.
* **Placement:** The liturgical unit inside the mobilization band (ruku 10–15): it closes the travel-excitation family begun at 4:43 (same excuses: sickness, travel — observable pairing) and reasserts the surah's rule that hardship licenses form, never abandons address.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:101]`** "When you travel in the land, there is no blame on you for shortening the prayers, if you fear that the disbelievers may harm you."
  * **Gloss:** The qasr license with two conditions stated (travel, fear); "no blame" — relief framed as exoneration, not abandonment.
* **`[4:101]`** "The disbelievers are your manifest enemies."
  * **Gloss:** The six-word warrant for the fear-condition; the enmity-phrase runs in variants across the corpus — "an open enemy" (`[2:168]`, `[17:53]`), "an outright enemy" (`[2:208]`), "an open enemy" for Satan (`[43:62]`) (all verified) — here it is the disbelievers declared "manifest enemies", the family's one collective usage.
* **`[4:102]`** "When you are among them, and you stand to lead them in prayer, let a group of them stand with you, and let them hold their weapons."
  * **Gloss:** The prayer of fear begins inside a live formation: leader, first group, weapons in hand — worship as unit management.
* **`[4:102]`** "Then, when they have done their prostrations, let them withdraw to the rear, and let another group, that have not prayed yet, come forward and pray with you; and let them take their precautions and their weapons."
  * **Gloss:** The rotation: prostrations (sujud, the same word used of the believers' marks at 48:29, verified) as the exchange-tick; the second group joins mid-prayer — the mechanics of rak'a-division left to the practice (Source: classical fiqh of salat al-khawf reconstructs the details — verify).
* **`[4:102]`** "Those who disbelieve would like you to neglect your weapons and your equipment, so they can attack you in a single assault."
  * **Gloss:** The enemy's read of attention-slippage — the reason discipline is folded into the rite.
* **`[4:102]`** "You commit no error, if you are hampered by rain or are sick, by putting down your weapons; but take precautions."
  * **Gloss:** Weather and illness relax the arms-clause only, never the caution-clause; "Allah has prepared for the disbelievers a demeaning punishment."
* **`[4:103]`** "When you have completed the prayer, remember Allah, standing, or sitting, or on your sides."
  * **Gloss:** Dhikr beyond posture — the three-position triad is Ali 'Imran's portrait of the devotees (3:191 "remember Allah while standing, and sitting, and on their sides," verified); battle or bed, the remembrance holds.
* **`[4:103]`** "And when you feel secure, perform the prayer."
  * **Gloss:** Security restores the full form; license was never the norm.
* **`[4:103]`** "The prayer is obligatory for believers at specific times."
  * **Gloss:** The time-boundarity claim (mawqud — this translation's phrase, corpus-unique here, verified): prayer is a debt with dates, enforceable even mid-fear.
* **`[4:104]`** "And do not falter in the pursuit of the enemy."
  * **Gloss:** From prayer to pursuit without a seam: the same body that stood rotated now advances.
* **`[4:104]`** "If you are aching, they are aching as you are aching, but you expect from Allah what they cannot expect."
  * **Gloss:** Pain parity, hope asymmetry — the community's only material edge is expectation; echoes 3:139–140's wound-parity argument ("a similar wound has afflicted the others" — verified 3:140).
* **`[4:104]`** "Allah is Knowledgeable and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The knowledge-refrain closes the campaign's liturgy (and mirrors 4:11's family).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`qasr` / "shortening"**: the travel-relief noun.
  * **Elsewhere:** the verb appears at `[35:11]` in a lifespan sense ("shorten/lengthen days," verified as the only other match) — in law-vocabulary the term is confined to 4:101 in this translation (observable); what is shortened (one rak'a? the standing?) is not in the text — Source: classical fiqh specification (the four-to-two of the pray-er) — verify.
* **`salat al-khawf` / "the prayer of fear"** (descriptive name of 4:102): the only Quranic enactment-scene of it; its details (one rak'a each? both?) are classical fiqh divergences — Source: tafsir/fiqh references (Ibn Kathir lists the Companion variants) — verify.
* **`dhikr` / "remember Allah"**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:191]` (the posture-triad, verified), `[62:10]` (post-prayer dispersal), `[8:45]` ("be steadfast... Allah is with the steadfast" — battle-dhikr, already cited in the sabr note) — remembrance is the corpus's continuity-verb across worship states.
* **`mawqud` / "at specific times"** (4:103): the prayer's appointment.
  * **Elsewhere:** hapax in the corpus (verified) — the one Quranic time-appointment noun for the rite; its classical gloss (awqat al-fard) is tafsir — verify.
