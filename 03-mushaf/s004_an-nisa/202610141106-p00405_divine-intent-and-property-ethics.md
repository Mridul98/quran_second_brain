# Allah intends to make things clear — and to lighten (4:26–33)
**UID:** 202610141106
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:26–33 (Ruku 5 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:26]` – `[4:33]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The law-band pauses to state its own purpose: Allah *intends* — three times (yuridu) — to make things clear, guide you in the ways of those before you, redeem you, and lighten your burden, "for the human being was created weak"; against that intent stand "those who follow their desires," and the chapter of intent closes with the property constitution: no illicit consumption, trade by mutual consent, no self-killing, no coveting what others were given, and shares owed even to contractual partners.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4's prohibition table gets its meta-comment here; 4:29–31 then generalize 4:2/4:10's orphan-wealth ban into the community's whole economic ethic before 4:32–33 re-close the inheritance-and-agreement cycle.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:26]`** "Allah intends to make things clear to you, and to guide you in the ways of those before you, and to redeem you."
  * **Gloss:** Three verbs of intent: clarity (bayana — the revelation-speech word), following the prior prophets' ways, and redemption — the law's teleology stated, not assumed.
* **`[4:26]`** "Allah is Most Knowing, Most Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The knowledge-refrain that closes every share-table now closes the intent-table.
* **`[4:27]`** "Allah intends to redeem you, but those who follow their desires want you to turn away utterly."
  * **Gloss:** The collision is re-described as competing wills: divine tawba (redemption) vs. desire-following deviation — "utterly" (a great deviation).
* **`[4:28]`** "Allah intends to lighten your burden, for the human being was created weak."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's anthropology in one line — concessions (4:3's ceiling, 4:25's graded path, 4:31's remission) are engineering for a weak creature, not loopholes.
* **`[4:29]`** "O you who believe! Do not consume each other’s wealth illicitly, but trade by mutual consent."
  * **Gloss:** The orphan-specific ban (4:2, 4:10) becomes the universal commercial rule; the exception clause names consent as the line between eating and trading.
* **`[4:29]`** "And do not kill yourselves, for Allah is Merciful towards you."
  * **Gloss:** Self-destruction (literal and juridical — the same verb covers killing one another: "do not kill yourselves" reads as one body's mutual harm) banned under the mercy-names.
* **`[4:30]`** "Whoever does that, out of hostility and wrongdoing, We will cast him into a Fire. And that would be easy for Allah."
  * **Gloss:** The two motives (hostility, injustice) trigger the threat; "easy for Allah" — the same divine-ease formula the surah will reuse at 4:169 for the rejecters.
* **`[4:31]`** "If you avoid the worst of what you are forbidden, We will remit your sins, and admit you by a Gate of Honor."
  * **Gloss:** The proportional promise: major-sin avoidance covers the rest; "Gate of Honor" (bab 'izz) — a honor-gate, this edition's hapax (verified via rg).
* **`[4:32]`** "Do not covet what Allah has given to some of you in preference to others."
  * **Gloss:** The prohibition internalized: preferment (fadl) is Allah's allocation; envy of it is outlawed before any act.
* **`[4:32]`** "For men is a share of what they have earned, and for women is a share of what they have earned."
  * **Gloss:** Parallel earnings-shares answer the coveting impulse in both directions — the share-doctrine of 4:7 now extended to acquisition.
* **`[4:32]`** "And ask Allah of his bounty. Allah has knowledge of everything."
  * **Gloss:** If allocation is Allah's, petition is the lawful alternative to coveting; knowledge-refrain again.
* **`[4:33]`** "To everyone We have assigned beneficiaries in what is left by parents and relatives."
  * **Gloss:** The inheritance table re-summoned as a divine assignment (waratha) — "We" the revealer speaking.
* **`[4:33]`** "Those with whom you have made an agreement, give them their share. Allah is Witness over all things."
  * **Gloss:** Contractual partners join the heirs-list in the same sentence; the closing "Witness over all things" fences the whole property cycle.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`yuridu` / "intends"** (4:26, 27, 28 — three consecutive divine-intent statements):
  * **Rendering:** "Allah intends to make things clear... to redeem... to lighten" — the same verb for the desire-followers' counter-intent ("want you to turn away utterly," 4:27): will against will.
  * **Elsewhere:** the "make things clear" half of the intent-verb (bayyana family) is the kalam-note's own vocabulary — see `[20:113]`, `[5:89]`; here the family appears with the subject "Allah intends" (observable).
* **`batil` / "illicitly"**: consumption of wealth on false terms.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:188]` — "do not consume one another’s wealth by unjust means... to consume part of other people’s wealth illicitly, while you know" (verified); `[9:34]` — "many of the rabbis and priests consume people's wealth illicitly" (verified) — the twin of 4:161's same phrase for the usury-eaters.
* **`taradi` / "mutual consent"**: the market's theological minimum.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:233]` — nursing weaning "by mutual consent" (verified) — the one other place this translation uses the phrase; contract and family both run on agreement, not force.
* **`tijara` / "trade"**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[61:10]` — "Shall I inform you of a trade that will save you from a painful torment?" (verified) — a counter-image: the surah's commercial exception (4:29) and as-Saff's spiritual trade use the same noun; also `[2:16]`, `[35:29]` for the trade-of-revelation metaphorics.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical consensus); commercial and testamentary law for a trading caravan-town and a treaty community.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** none specific transmitted for 4:29; "do not kill yourselves" is classically cited by jurists for both suicide and mutual homicide (Ibn Kathir on 4:29) — Source: classical tafsir, agent condensation — verify.
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the text orders lawful exchange to rest on mutual consent, forbids illicit consumption and self/destruction of life, forbids covetousness, and commands paying contractual shares; the "worst of what you are forbidden" (4:31) is classically read as the kabir/major sins with the rest covered by avoidance — Source: classical tafsir consensus phrasing (e.g., Ibn Kathir on 4:31 citing the "if you avoid the great sins" ayah 22:31) — verify.
