# Two factions, three treaties, and one clear authorization (4:88–91)
**UID:** 202610141113
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:88–91 (Ruku 12 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:88]` – `[4:91]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The community is rebuked for splitting into two factions over the hypocrites when Allah has already "overwhelmed them on account of what they did" — and with the rebuke comes the classification matrix of enemies: those who would make you equal unbelievers (no alliance, force if they turn); those who cling to a treaty people or refuse to fight either side (peace accepted, "no excuse for you against them"); and the double-security opportunists who re-plunge into discord when tempted — against these alone "We have given you clear authorization."
* **Placement:** The juridical sorting after the doctrine-band (ruku 11) and before the blood-law (ruku 13): the surah converts a factional argument into a three-class rule of engagement — the text's own answer to "what is the matter with you, divided."

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:88]`** "What is the matter with you, divided into two factions regarding the hypocrites, when Allah Himself has overwhelmed them on account of what they did?"
  * **Gloss:** The faction-fight over nifaq is itself diagnosed as confusion: the verdict on them is already executed by their own acts (observable causative grammar: "on account of what they did").
* **`[4:88]`** "Do you want to guide those whom Allah has led astray? Whomever Allah leads astray—you will never find for him a way."
  * **Gloss:** The guidance-note's hardest property stated plainly: guidance is not a possession to be applied to the one Allah has abandoned to his own deviation — the refrain's twin sits at 4:143 (within-surah, verified by reading).
* **`[4:89]`** "They would love to see you disbelieve, just as they disbelieve, so you would become equal."
  * **Gloss:** The first class: the envy of equality-in-unbelief — the mirror of 4:54's envy-of-grace.
* **`[4:89]`** "So do not befriend any of them, unless they emigrate in the way of Allah. If they turn away, seize them and execute them wherever you may find them; and do not take from among them allies or supporters."
  * **Gloss:** The loyalty-law (no awliya) with the emigration-condition — the same hijra test that ruku 14 will legislate as duty; "seize and execute" appears here and at 4:91, once per class (observable structure).
* **`[4:90]`** "Except those who join people with whom you have a treaty, or those who come to you reluctant to fight you or fight their own people."
  * **Gloss:** Two exemptions ride on existing treaty-committed peoples and on wariness toward both sides — neutrality as a protected class, contingent on its sincerity.
* **`[4:90]`** "Had Allah willed, He would have given them power over you, and they would have fought you. If they withdraw from you, and do not fight you, and offer you peace, then Allah assigns no excuse for you against them."
  * **Gloss:** The sovereignty counterfactual (power was always Allah's to give); the threefold peace-test — withdraw, non-fight, offer peace — and the closing prohibition: no legal grounds against them ("no excuse" unique to this verse, verified).
* **`[4:91]`** "You will find others who want security from you, and security from their own people. But whenever they are tempted into civil discord, they plunge into it."
  * **Gloss:** The third class: double-security-seekers whose equilibrium breaks under temptation to fitna — "plunge" (iham) as their signature act.
* **`[4:91]`** "So if they do not withdraw from you, nor offer you peace, nor restrain their hands, seize them and execute them wherever you find them. Against these, We have given you clear authorization."
  * **Gloss:** The failed triple-test inverted clause-by-clause yields the authorization (sultan mubin) — the only "clear authorization" for offensive action in this band (verified phrase-pair with 55:33's "authority" to pass the heavens).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`shiya` / "factions"** (4:88):
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:65]`, `[19:37]`, `[43:65]` (verified) — sects and parties of the prior communities: faction as a recurring disease of revealed communities, named again here; cf. 3:103/3:105 in Ali 'Imran's fragmentation band (already documented in the s003 build).
* **`mithaq` / "treaty"** (4:90): the international covenant.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:4]` — "the polytheists with whom you made a treaty... give them... four months"; `[9:7]`, `[8:72]`, `[8:56]` (verified) — treaty obligations across the surah-war-law; within-surah `[4:92]` — the treaty-people whose believer-victims get full compensation — the same instrument governs blood-price (see ruku 13).
* **`fitna` / "civil discord"** (4:91):
  * **Rendering:** "civil discord" here; elsewhere the family is rendered "discord" — `[8:25]` "beware of discord which does not afflict the wrongdoers among you exclusively", `[9:47]`, `[2:176]`, `[22:53]` (verified) — always social fracture, never private test, in these uses.
* **`sultan mubin` / "clear authorization"** (4:91): enforceable warrant.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[55:33]` — "you will not pass through except with authority/authorization (sultan)" (verified); `[4:153]` — "We gave Moses a clear authority" (same phrase, ruku 22 — verified) — the sultan idiom spans argument, warrant, and permission (observable across this surah and ar-Rahman).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the classes presuppose the Madinan treaty-field (partial alignments with clans under covenant) — observable in the text's own categories.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 4:88 classically tied to the post-Uhud split over the hypocrites (were they to be fought or spared?) in the tafsir corpora — Source: classical asbab (Ibn Kathir family), agent condensation — verify; 4:90–91 tied to specific clans under treaty with the Prophet (reports naming Khuzafa/Kinana-type alignments) — verify before naming.
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the text states the three engagement-classes and their tests (treaty-membership, withdrawal, peace-offer, restraint of hands) and the authorization conditioned on their failure. Classical fiqh's codification of treaty-status (dhimma, hudna, aman) is extrapolation from these categories — Source: classical fiqh reference.
