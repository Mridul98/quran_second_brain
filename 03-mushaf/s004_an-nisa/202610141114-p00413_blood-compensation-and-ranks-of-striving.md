# Blood, compensation, and the unequal equals (4:92–96)
**UID:** 202610141114
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:92–96 (Ruku 13 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:92]` – `[4:96]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A believer may never kill a believer except by mistake — and even the mistake has a liturgy: free a believing slave, hand compensation to the family unless they remit it as charity, with the victim's treaty-status and the killer's means (two consecutive months of fasting) specifying the ladder; deliberate killing earns Hell, anger, curse, and terrible punishment; then the field-rule: when you journey, investigate, and do not call the man who offers you peace "no believer" — "you yourselves were like this before"; and the ruku ends on the ledger of effort: the sitting-down and the strivers are not equal — Allah has promised goodness to both, and favors the strivers with degrees, forgiveness, and mercy.
* **Placement:** The blood-law follows immediately on the engagement-classes of ruku 12 (treaty categories are carried verbatim into 4:92's compensation matrix — observable); 4:94's investigation-command is 4:83's news-verification turned into a battlefield rule; 4:95–96 close the mobilization arc (ruku 10–13) with the striver/laggard valuation whose first draft was 4:72–74.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:92]`** "Never should a believer kill another believer, unless by error."
  * **Gloss:** The absolute with its single exception — the wording frames the community as bodies one cannot touch even accidentally without consequence.
* **`[4:92]`** "Anyone who kills a believer by error must set free a believing slave, and pay compensation to the victim’s family, unless they remit it as charity."
  * **Gloss:** Two duties, the second waivable by the heirs: manumission and blood-compensation (diya, rendered "compensation"); remission reclassified as charity (sadaqa) — the infaq-grammar inside criminal law.
* **`[4:92]`** "If the victim belonged to a people who are hostile to you, but is a believer, then the compensation is to free a believing slave."
  * **Gloss:** Hostile-people believer: manumission only — no family payment channel; faith outranks tribal ledger.
* **`[4:92]`** "If he belonged to a people with whom you have a treaty, then compensation should be handed over to his family, and a believing slave set free."
  * **Gloss:** Treaty-people believer: both duties run — the treaty of 4:90 buys the victim's family their full claim (observable carry-over).
* **`[4:92]`** "Anyone who lacks the means must fast for two consecutive months, by way of repentance to Allah. Allah is All-Knowing, Most Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The poverty-substitute: the identical fast-liturgy of the zihar-expiation (`[58:4]`, verified) — expiation (tahira/repentance-direction) travels across crimes.
* **`[4:93]`** "Whoever kills a believer deliberately, the penalty for him is Hell, where he will remain forever. And Allah will be angry with him, and will curse him, and will prepare for him a terrible punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The staccato verdict — the curse-refrain (4:46/52/118) now aimed at one act; four threats stacked, one for each clause-break (observable rhetoric).
* **`[4:94]`** "O you who believe! When you journey in the way of Allah, investigate, and do not say to him who offers you peace, “You are not a believer,” aspiring for the goods of this world."
  * **Gloss:** The salam-protection: outward peace-gesture is legally decisive; motive-policing ("aspiring for the goods") named as the error.
* **`[4:94]`** "With Allah are abundant riches. You yourselves were like this before, and Allah bestowed favor on you; so investigate."
  * **Gloss:** Memory as argument: the community's own pre-guidance past ("you were like this before") forbids now reading hearts; the doubled command — investigate twice in one ayah (observable emphasis).
* **`[4:94]`** "Allah is well aware of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The khabir-refrain that closes most of the surah's family-law units closes the blood-law too.
* **`[4:95]`** "Not equal are the inactive among the believers—except the disabled—and the strivers in the cause of Allah with their possessions and their persons."
  * **Gloss:** The inequality clause with its exemption built into the sentence (the disabled excused — "disabled" is a corpus-hapax here, verified); striving priced in property and self.
* **`[4:95]`** "Allah prefers the strivers with their possessions and their persons above the inactive, by a degree. But Allah has promised goodness to both."
  * **Gloss:** Preference and its ceiling "by a degree," then the balance-line: the promise covers the sitting too — no salvation withheld for rank's sake.
* **`[4:95]`** "Yet Allah favors the strivers, over the inactive, with a great reward."
  * **Gloss:** The favor restated with its own economy: ajr azim.
* **`[4:96]`** "Degrees from Him, and forgiveness, and mercy. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The reward unpacked into three (degrees, maghfira, rahma) and sealed with the names — the surah's closing-refrain device at work inside the mobilization band.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`diyah`/compensation**: the payment to the victim's family.
  * **Rendering:** translated "compensation" here; the same English covers ajr ("sublime compensation" 4:40, "great compensation" 4:74/96-family) and the dowry-return provision of `[2:236]`/`[60:10]` (verified) — one English word across three Arabic institutions (observable drift).
* **`ittiqal`/setting-free a "believing slave"**: manumission as expiation.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[90:13]` — "The freeing of a slave" as the steep-road act (verified); the faith-qualification "believing slave" occurs only at 4:92 in the whole corpus (verified: rg hits `[4:92]` only).
* **`siyam` / "two consecutive months"**: the fasting substitute.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[58:4]` — "must fast for two consecutive months" (verified, identical wording) — the two kaffarat-laws share one phrase, so the surah's blood-law and al-Mujadilah's marriage-law are one expiation family (observable).
* **`qa'id` / "the inactive" + `mujahid` / "strivers"**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:20]` — "Those who believe, and emigrate, and strive in Allah's path with their possessions and their persons, are of a higher rank" (verified — same "possessions and persons" pairing); `[61:11]` — "strive in the cause of Allah with your possessions and yourselves" (verified) — the striving-idiom's two other classical sites use the identical body-property binomial.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan blood-law within the campaign band; the treaty-victim case presupposes the Madinan alignment map of ruku 12 (observable).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** classical reports attach 4:92–94 to accidental killings during campaigns (e.g., a believer slain among enemy ranks after a salam-gesture misread — reports in the tafsir corpora, Ibn Kathir/al-Qurtubi families) — Source: classical asbab, agent condensation — verify the specific names before repeating; 4:95 classically tied to the sitting-down companions at Hudaybiyyah-type departures and the "except the disabled" clause to blind/crippled excusals (verified content of clause itself; reports — verify).
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the text orders: no intentional killing of a believer; the three-tier mistake-liturgy (slave, blood-price by victim's political status, fasting substitute); the field-investigation rule protecting the peace-offerer; a degrees-hierarchy between strivers and excused sitters, with the promise kept for both. Qisas (retribution) for the deliberate killing, and whether 4:93 admits repentance's effect in this world, are fiqh debates on top of the wording — Source: classical tafsir/fiqh reference (the well-known Mu'tazila/Baghawī-type arguments on 4:93's "remains forever") — verify attributions.
