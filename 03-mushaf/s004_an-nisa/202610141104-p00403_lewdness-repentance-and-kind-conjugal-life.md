# Four witnesses, the repentance valve, and the solid commitment (4:15–22)
**UID:** 202610141104
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:15–22 (Ruku 3 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:15]` – `[4:22]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** From estates to bodies and bonds: lewdness requires four witnesses of your own; the pair-punishment yields the moment repentance and reform appear, because "Allah is Redeemer, Full of Mercy" — while the repentance valve is open for the ignorant-and-quick and shut at the deathbed profession; then the conjugal property shield: no inheriting women against their will, no coercing to claw back the gift — she "has received from you a solid commitment" — and the fathers' widows are permanently out of reach.
* **Placement:** Closes the first marriage-and-property cycle (ruku 1–3) before the eligibility list of ruku 4; the repentance verses (4:17–18) install the mercy-grammar the surah will reuse at 4:64, 4:110, and 4:146 — an intra-surah arc the surah keeps returning to whenever it threatens.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:15]`** "Those of your women who commit lewdness, you must have four witnesses against them, from among you."
  * **Gloss:** Prosecution starts at four — the evidentiary ceiling placed before any penalty is mentioned.
* **`[4:15]`** "If they testify, confine them to the homes until death claims them, or Allah makes a way for them."
  * **Gloss:** The stated sanction is house-confinement, time-bounded by death or by an opening "way" from Allah — the classical reading identifies that "way" as the later revelation of the public-punishment law of 24:2 (Source: classical tafsir/abrogation discussion, Ibn Kathir and the jurists on 4:15 — agent summary, verify).
* **`[4:16]`** "If two men among you commit it, punish them both."
  * **Gloss:** The paired case: the text orders a punishment whose form is not specified in the ayah — neutrally, the wording itself leaves the penalty type open.
* **`[4:16]`** "But if they repent and reform, leave them alone. Allah is Redeemer, Full of Mercy."
  * **Gloss:** The valve: repent-and-reform terminates the pursuit; the refrain names Allah tawwab — "Redeemer" here, the same root rendered "Relenting" at 4:64 and 2:37 (translator variation, observable).
* **`[4:17]`** "Repentance is available from Allah for those who commit evil out of ignorance, and then repent soon after. These—Allah will relent towards them. Allah is Knowing and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The conditions: evil committed "out of ignorance" (jahl — the stated motive is ignorance, not malice) and "soon after" — with the divine promise stated twice ("relent towards them").
* **`[4:18]`** "But repentance is not available for those who commit evils, until when death approaches one of them, he says, “Now I repent,” nor for those who die as disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** The valve's outer limits: the deathbed declaration and unrepented disbelief — "commit evils" plural, a life-pattern, versus 4:17's single "evil."
* **`[4:18]`** "These—We have prepared for them a painful torment."
  * **Gloss:** The threat-form; the divine "We" of revelation-speech takes over for the verdict (cf. kalam note's channel-grammar).
* **`[4:19]`** "O you who believe! It is not permitted for you to inherit women against their will. And do not coerce them in order to take away some of what you had given them, unless they commit a proven adultery."
  * **Gloss:** Women are heirs, not estates — the pre-Islamic claim of a guardian over a widow's person and dower is annulled; the one exception clause rides on the same four-witness evidence standard.
* **`[4:19]`** "And live with them in kindness. If you dislike them, it may be that you dislike something in which Allah has placed much good."
  * **Gloss:** The positive command for cohabitation ("in kindness" — the same phrase used of parental gentleness at [31:15], verified), framed as epistemic humility about where Allah hides "much good."
* **`[4:20]`** "If you wish to replace one wife with another, and you have given one of them a fortune, take nothing back from it. Would you take it back fraudulently and sinfully?"
  * **Gloss:** Even the suitor-swapping husband is bound: the "fortune" (qintar) stays; the rhetorical question names the recovery scheme "fraudulent and sinful."
* **`[4:21]`** "And how can you take it back, when you have been intimate with one another, and they have received from you a solid commitment?"
  * **Gloss:** Two reasons bar clawback: mutual intimacy, and the dower recast as a *mithaq ghalizah* — "a solid commitment," the exact technical phrase for the prophets' covenant at 33:7 (observable term-echo).
* **`[4:22]`** "Do not marry women whom your fathers married, except what is already past. That is improper, indecent, and a bad custom."
  * **Gloss:** The levirate-style takeover of a father's widow is abolished with a triple moral indictment; "except what is already past" opens every prohibition-list of this surah's amnesty formula.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fahisha` / "lewdness"**: obscene conduct; the surah's umbrella charge.
  * **Rendering:** "lewdness" (4:15); "proven adultery" for the 4:19 exception (fahisha with the witnesses-clause attached).
  * **Elsewhere:** the same word carries the people of Lot's accusation — `[29:29]`, `[27:54]`, `[7:80]` — and `[17:32]` commands "do not go near lewdness" (the near/proven framing matches 4:15's evidence bar; all refs verified via rg).
* **`four witnesses`**: the testimony number imported into the accusation itself.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[24:4]` — accusers of chaste women must "bring four witnesses" or receive eighty lashes and rejected testimony; `[24:13]` — "why did they not bring four witnesses?" — the identical evidentiary engine, in Nur's law-band (verified).
* **`tawba` / repentance — and its two verbs**: the 4:17 definition (ignorance → soon) and the 4:18 exclusions; the divine counterpart root t-w-b rendered "Redeemer" at `[4:16]` and `[9:118]`, "Relenting" at `[2:37]` and `[4:64]` (verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** the whole surah's mercy-arc: `[4:64]` (the wrongdoer who comes and asks forgiveness), `[4:110]` ("will find Allah Forgiving and Merciful"), `[4:146]` ("Except those who repent, and reform, and hold fast to Allah").
* **`mithaq ghalizah` / "a solid commitment"** (4:21): the dower as heavy covenant.
  * **Rendering:** here "a solid commitment"; the same Arabic phrase rendered "a solemn pledge" at `[33:7]` (the prophets' covenant) and inside this surah at `[4:154]` (Israel's covenant) — one term, two English robes (observable; verified).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical consensus) — a community-court passage: procedure, penalties, and property in marriage are legislated, not parabolized.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 4:19 is classically reported against the practice whereby a man whose kinsman died claimed his widow as an inheritance (or held her until she returned the dower), in reports collected by Ibn Kathir/al-Qurtubi on 4:19 — Source: classical asbab, agent condensation — verify.
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the ayahs order: four-witness procedure for the women's case; a paired punishment subject to repentance-and-reform; the repentance window with its two stated exclusions; no involuntary inheritance of women; no coercion to reclaim gifts; kindness as cohabitation standard; the fathers' widows forbidden. The forms and gradations of the punishments (confinement's scope, the "punish them both" penalty, the 24:2 relation) are *fiqh* extrapolations — Source: classical tafsir/fiqh references for any such specification.
