# No good in most secret counsels — and the path that has a name (4:113–115)
**UID:** 202610141118
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:113–115 (Ruku 17 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:113]` – `[4:115]` (3 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The traitors' lobbying only misleads themselves — but for Allah's grace and mercy, a faction would have managed to mislead the community, whose protective endowment is named: the Book and wisdom, and what you did not know taught to you; most private counsels contain no good, except the one whose content is charity, kindness, or reconciliation — done "seeking Allah's approval" — and the unit closes on the rupture clause: whoever splits from the Messenger after the guidance has become clear, and follows other than the path of the believers, is left to the direction he chose, and burned in it.
* **Placement:** The three-ayah pivot between the advocacy-ban (ruku 16) and the shirk-verdict (ruku 18): it converts one courtroom case into community policy (speech in private, loyalty in public), and 4:115 is the surah's fullest single statement of the guidance-forfeiture mechanic that the guidance-note lists as property (4) — "the road can be walked off."

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:113]`** "Were it not for Allah’s grace towards you, and His mercy, a faction of them would have managed to mislead you."
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual of survival — the same grace-bracket as 4:83 (verified phrase-form); "mislead you" occurs elsewhere only at `[71:27]` (Noah's complaint — verified).
* **`[4:113]`** "But they only mislead themselves, and they cannot harm you in any way."
  * **Gloss:** The boomerang law (cf. 3:69 in the s003 build — same grammar): the misguidance intended for others returns to sender.
* **`[4:113]`** "Allah has revealed to you the Scripture and wisdom, and has taught you what you did not know."
  * **Gloss:** The protective gift listed: Book + wisdom + unknown-knowledge — exactly what the traitors' lobbying tried to override in 4:105.
* **`[4:113]`** "Allah’s goodness towards you is great."
  * **Gloss:** Fadl/fadwa refrain — "goodness" fadlan kabira: the surah's abundance-vocabulary closing a grace-counterfactual.
* **`[4:114]`** "There is no good in much of their private counsels, except for him who advocates charity, or kindness, or reconciliation between people."
  * **Gloss:** The najwa audit: private speech is presumptively bad, excepted only on three items — sadaqah, ma'ruf (kindness), islah (reconciliation) — the same islah of 4:35/4:128 and the ma'ruf of 4:19 (all within-surah, verified by reading); "private counsels" is a corpus-hapax here (verified).
* **`[4:114]`** "Whoever does that, seeking Allah’s approval, We will give him a great compensation."
  * **Gloss:** The intent-clause: ibtigha — "seeking Allah's approval" — the exact phrase of `[2:207]` "sells himself seeking Allah's approval" (verified) and the 59:8/48:29 family — and the wage is "a great compensation" (ajran azima): the identical reward-phrase of the fighter at 4:74, now promised to the mediator (observable pairing within one surah's mobilization band).
* **`[4:115]`** "Whoever makes a breach with the Messenger, after the guidance has become clear to him, and follows other than the path of the believers, We will direct him in the direction he has chosen, and commit him to Hell— what a terrible destination!"
  * **Gloss:** The rupture-verse: shiqaq — "makes a breach" — the identical word used of the marital breach at 4:35 (verified: the corpus's only two "breach" hits are 4:35 and 4:115): domestic and communal schism share one anatomy; the chosen-direction irony (tawallā: God's turn matches the man's turn); "path of the believers" is a corpus-hapax (verified) — the road becomes definitional precisely in the verse that describes leaving it.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`najwa` / "private counsels"** (4:114):
  * **Elsewhere:** `[58:8]` — "prohibited from conspiring secretly... they conspire to commit sin" (verified) and `[58:10]` (same band); the najwa-institution is legislated twice in the corpus, here by exception (the three goods) and in al-Mujadilah by prohibition — one subject, two legal temperatures (observable; classical fiqh of najwa cites both — verify).
* **`ibtigha` / "seeking Allah's approval"**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:207]` (verified), `[59:8]`, `[67:18]` — the seeking-verb is always aimed at the divine face or pleasure (all verified by phrase-match scan); this verse supplies the intent-test for the counsels' three goods.
* **`islah` / "reconciliation"**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:35]` (arbiter's purpose), `[2:226]`, `[2:228]`, `[49:9–10]`, `[3:103]` (all verified); the family spans household, marital, communal, and inter-communal repair — 4:114 makes the reconciler one of the three permitted secret-speakers.
* **`shiqaq` / "makes a breach"** (4:115):
  * **Elsewhere:** exactly one other "breach" in the corpus — `[4:35]` "a breach between the two" (verified) — the surah's word for splitting is shared by marriage and community (observable pairing; classical usage: shiqaq as doctrinal schism derives from this verse's form — e.g., Kharijite-category discussions — Source: classical tafsir/fiqh — verify).
