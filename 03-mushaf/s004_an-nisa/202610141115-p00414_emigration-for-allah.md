# Was Allah's earth not vast? (4:97–100)
**UID:** 202610141115
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:97–100 (Ruku 14 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:97]` – `[4:100]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** At death, the angels' single question to those who "wronged themselves" — "What was the matter with you?" — extracts the excuse "We were oppressed in the land," and the reply is geography itself: "Was Allah's earth not vast enough for you to emigrate in it?"; only the genuinely unable — no means, no way out — are excused, and even for them the verdict is suspended in hope ("Allah may well pardon them"); while whoever emigrates for Allah finds on earth "many places of refuge, and plentitude," and if death overtakes him mid-journey, his compensation falls on Allah.
* **Placement:** The emigration answer to the mobilization band: 4:66 had asked what the hypocrites would refuse to do ("Leave your homes") — here leaving homes is legislated as the alternative to self-wronging; 4:97's "wronged themselves" is 4:64's phrase reused (verified), tying repentance-theory to migration-theory in two words.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:97]`** "While the angels are removing the souls of those who have wronged themselves, they will say, “What was the matter with you?”"
  * **Gloss:** The death-scene interrogated: the self-wrongers ("wronged themselves" — same phrase as 4:64's repentance-invite, verified) are asked for their case — a trial staged inside the dying.
* **`[4:97]`** "They will say, “We were oppressed in the land.” They will say, “Was Allah’s earth not vast enough for you to emigrate in it?”"
  * **Gloss:** The defense (oppression) meets the prosecution's geography — vastness as divine provision of exit; "not vast" is a corpus-hapax (verified).
* **`[4:97]`** "These—their refuge is Hell. What a wretched retreat!"
  * **Gloss:** Refuge (maswa) irony: the one who refused every muhajjar gets a "retreat" that is Hell.
* **`[4:98]`** "Except for the weak among men, and women, and children who have no means to act, and no means to find a way out."
  * **Gloss:** The exemption mirrors 4:75's crying class — men, women, children (verified same triad); the double "no means" defines ability, not intention.
* **`[4:99]`** "These—Allah may well pardon them. Allah is Pardoning and Forgiving."
  * **Gloss:** The rare suspension-form ("may well" — tafwīḍ to divine mercy): a ruling withheld out of justice to the unable; the pardon-names close.
* **`[4:100]`** "Anyone who emigrates for the sake of Allah will find on earth many places of refuge, and plentitude."
  * **Gloss:** The positive promise: mobility + abundance — the earth that was vast enough for exit is wide enough for provision.
* **`[4:100]`** "Anyone who leaves his home, emigrating to Allah and His Messenger, and then is overtaken by death, his compensation falls on Allah."
  * **Gloss:** The mid-journey death: the intention ("to Allah and His Messenger") completes the act; the wage becomes a debt on Allah Himself (observable: "falls on Allah" — huqqahu 'ala Allah).
* **`[4:100]`** "Allah is Forgiver, Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The refrain-pair closes the movement that began with Hell-as-refuge.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`zalama nafsahu` / "wronged themselves"**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:64]` (same surah — the wronged-self sinner invited to seek forgiveness), `[14:45]`, `[11:101]`, `[34:19]` (verified) — in the Pharaoh-peoples, self-wronging is the settled verdict; 4:97 imports that verdict to the stay-behind believer, no battle needed.
* **`mustad'af / qahr` / "oppressed"**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[28:5]` — Pharaoh "oppressing a group of the people" (verified); `[34:31–33]` — the oppressed ("the weak") disavowing the proud in the Fire (verified); `[7:137]` (verified) — the oppressed inheritance of the East and West — the word carries political, not merely personal, weight in the corpus, and 4:97's excuse is precisely political.
* **`hijra` / "emigrate"**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:41]` — "Those who emigrate for Allah's sake after being persecuted, We will settle them in a good place in this world; but the reward of the Hereafter is greater" (verified — the exact twin of 4:100's two-tier promise); `[9:20]`, `[8:72/74/75]`, `[59:9]`, `[3:195]` (verified) — emigration as the ranked deed in the striving-band (link to ruku 13).
* **`muhajjar / mataraf` / "many places of refuge, and plentitude"** (4:100):
  * **Elsewhere:** the refuge-idiom (`ma'adha`) runs to Allah and caves — `[16:98]`, `[44:20]`, `[19:18]`, `[113:1–2]` (verified) — but 4:100's refuge is a place on the map: the surah relocates refuge from asylum to migration (agent synthesis of verified occurrences).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; classically read against believers stranded in Meccan territory unable to practice (the "oppressed in the land" defense fitting the pre-conquest situation) — Source: classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir on 4:97 cites the Mecca stay-behind reports; the famous "I am innocent of every Muslim who stays among the polytheists" hadith is the tradition's anchor) — verify wording/attribution before repeating.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 4:98–99 classically tied to the weak converts left behind (e.g., reports about Walid ibn al-Walid and the mustad'afun of Mecca — Ibn Jarir/Ibn Kathir families) — agent condensation — verify.
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the text makes emigration the duty of those able to leave oppression, exempts the unable with divine hope of pardon, and assigns the migrant's unfinished journey to Allah's own ledger. The categories of lands (war/peace/treaty) built atop this by jurists are *fiqh* extrapolation — Source: classical fiqh reference.
