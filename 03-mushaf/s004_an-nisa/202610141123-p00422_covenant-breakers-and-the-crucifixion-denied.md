# The book from the sky, the raised Mount, and the killing that was not (4:153–162)
**UID:** 202610141123
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:153–162 (Ruku 22 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:153]` – `[4:162]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The challenge-verse frames everything: they demand a book dropped from the sky, though their fathers asked Moses for the sight of Allah outright and were struck, took the calf, had the Mount raised over their covenant, and swore a solemn pledge — and the ledger of breaches is itemized (covenant violation, denial, prophets killed unjustly, "Our minds are closed," faithlessness, the monstrous slander against Mary) up to the surah's counter-history: they said "We have killed the Messiah" — they did not kill him, nor crucify him, it only appeared so; rather Allah raised him up to Himself; and the sanction-list (barred good foods, usury taken, wealth eaten dishonestly) meets its counterweight: those firmly rooted in knowledge, the believers, the prayer-keepers, the charity-givers — an immense reward.
* **Placement:** The People-of-the-Book mirror of the surah — the same covenant-breach liturgy as 4:46–47 (ruku 7) and 2:51–93 (Baqarah), with the crucifixion-denial at its center; 4:154's "solemn pledge" is the mithaq-ghalizah phrase that welded 4:21's marriage to Sinai, and here the triangle closes: dower-covenant, prophets'-covenant, Sinai-pledge (all verified occurrences: 4:21, 33:7, 4:154).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:153]`** "The People of the Scripture challenge you to bring down to them a book from the sky. They had asked Moses for something even greater."
  * **Gloss:** Demand-mongering as precedent-inheritance: the written-from-heaven challenge (cf. their own "Ra'ina"-style tests; the sky-book request appears uniquely here in this phrasing — verified "from the sky" scan hits mostly rain-verses).
* **`[4:153]`** "They said, “Show us Allah plainly.” The thunderbolt struck them for their wickedness."
  * **Gloss:** The Sinai-ask (2:55: "unless we see Allah plainly... the thunderbolt struck you" — both clauses verified verbatim twins) — an-Nisa quotes the event in third person, Baqarah in second.
* **`[4:153]`** "Then they took the calf for worship, even after the clear proofs had come to them. Yet We pardoned that, and We gave Moses a clear authority."
  * **Gloss:** The calf (the corpus tells it at `[2:51/2:52/2:54/2:93]`, `[7:148]`, `[20:88]` — verified) after proofs, pardoned anyway — grace inside the breach-report — with "a clear authority" (sultan mubin, the warrant-word's second robe, verified).
* **`[4:154]`** "And We raised the Mount above them in accordance with their covenant, and We said to them, “Enter the gate humbly”, and We said to them, “Do not violate the Sabbath”, and We received from them a solemn pledge."
  * **Gloss:** Three covenant commands quoted (gate, Sabbath — cf. 2:58/4:47, 2:65/7:163, verified) under the suspended Mount (7:171's "suspended" verb — verified in ruku 19), sealed by the mithaq ghalizah: "a solemn pledge" (33:7's identical rendering, verified).
* **`[4:155]`** "But for their violation of their covenant, and their denial of Allah’s revelations, and their killing of the prophets unjustly, and their saying, “Our minds are closed.”"
  * **Gloss:** The breach-list begins (violation = 2:100's "toss it aside", verified); "Our minds are closed" — the self-sealing confession, answered next clause; the killing-of-prophets charge is the surah's recurring indictment (2:61/2:87/3:21/3:112 family — the motif documented in the s002 build; verify individual wording before quoting).
* **`[4:155]`** "In fact, Allah has sealed them for their disbelief, so they do not believe, except for a few."
  * **Gloss:** The seal — 2:7's heart-seal formula reused (verified) as covenant consequence, not arbitrary foreclosure (the guidance-note's forfeiture-vs-prerogative tension lives here).
* **`[4:156]`** "And for their faithlessness, and their saying against Mary a monstrous slander."
  * **Gloss:** The sixth charge: the slander of Mary (the "monstrous" (azim) idiom again, cf. 4:48's "monstrous sin", verified); "faithlessness" continues kufr's second English robe in this surah (observable drift).
* **`[4:157]`** "And for their saying, “We have killed the Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, the Messenger of Allah.”"
  * **Gloss:** The claim quoted in their own words, its full titulary intact — Messiah, son of Mary, Messenger of Allah (all three honorifics separately standard in the corpus: 3:45, 2:87/2:253, verified).
* **`[4:157]`** "In fact, they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but it appeared to them as if they did."
  * **Gloss:** The double denial + shubbiha clause: "crucify" elsewhere appears only as Pharaoh's threat (`[7:124]`, `[20:71]`, `[26:49]`, verified) — the word's four sites: three tyrants, one denied execution (observable; the substitution/speculation reports are classical tafsir material, Tabari/Ibn Kathir ranges — Source: classical tafsir — verify before repeating any scenario).
* **`[4:157]`** "Indeed, those who differ about him are in doubt about it. They have no knowledge of it, except the following of assumptions. Certainly, they did not kill him."
  * **Gloss:** The epistemic verdict: disagreement = doubt = assumption-following (10:36's "follow nothing but assumptions" — the exact twin phrase, verified); the sentence re-asserts, twice-negative: "Certainly, they did not kill him."
* **`[4:158]`** "Rather, Allah raised him up to Himself. Allah is Mighty and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-claim in one verb (rafa'a — Allah as the actor and the destination "to Himself"); the Agent noun absent — deliberate (agent observation — verify classical readings on the divine name used).
* **`[4:159]`** "There is none from the People of the Scripture but will believe in him before his death, and on the Day of Resurrection he will be a witness against them."
  * **Gloss:** The hardest pronoun in this surah: whose death, whose belief — classical tafsir splits between "before the death of every Person-of-the-Book" and "before Jesus' second descent" (both lines transmitted; Ibn Kathir collects the readings on 4:159) — Source: classical divergent tafsir, agent condensation — verify; the verse is flagged as a candidate for a future fleeting note (not created in this build).
* **`[4:160]`** "Due to wrongdoing on the part of the Jews, We forbade them good things that used to be lawful for them; and for deterring many from Allah’s path."
  * **Gloss:** The food-bar as sanction (6:146 details "everything with claws" and fats — verified twin) — lawful-things-forbidden as punishment-history; cf. 3:50, where Jesus comes "to make lawful for you some of what was forbidden" (verified).
* **`[4:161]`** "And for their taking usury, although they were forbidden it; and for their consuming people's wealth dishonestly."
  * **Gloss:** The economic charges: riba (3:130's prohibition-verb reused — verified) and akl al-mal bil-batil (2:188/4:29's illicit-eating phrase — verified) — the usury-and-eating pair that the infaq-note lists as one theology (link per task flag).
* **`[4:161]`** "We have prepared for the faithless among them a painful torment."
  * **Gloss:** The exception-clause's mirror: punishment for "the faithless among them" — the surah keeps refusing collective verdicts (compare 4:153's "Yet We pardoned that" and 4:162's "but those among them...").
* **`[4:162]`** "But those among them firmly rooted in knowledge, and the believers, believe in what was revealed to you, and in what was revealed before you; and the observers of prayers, and the givers of charity, and the believers in Allah and the Last Day—upon these We will bestow an immense reward."
  * **Gloss:** The counterweight class: the rasikhun (3:7's hermeneutic party — verified same phrase) now defined socially: they believe across revelations, keep prayer and zakat — and receive "an immense reward" (the kafayya-and-wage grammar of 4:40 in the dossier's closing balance).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`mithaq ghalizah` / "solemn pledge"** (4:154): Sinai's heavy contract-word.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:7]` (verified — the prophets' solemn pledge) and `[4:21]` as "solid commitment" (verified) — one Arabic phrase, two English robes, three covenants (marriage, prophecy, Sinai) in one lexical weld (observable — the k-covenant note's three-layer map gains its word-evidence here).
* **`salba` / "crucified" (4:157)**: the corpus's only non-Pharaonic use of the verb (verified: all sites 7:124, 20:71, 26:49, 4:157) — crucifixion-language belongs to tyranny-speech in this book, and the surah denies the believers ever completed it (observable + agent synthesis — verify).
* **`rafa'a` / "raised him up"** (4:158): divine elevation — the phrase "raised him up" occurs only here in the corpus (verified); Ali 'Imran's twin is `[3:55]` — "I am terminating your life, and raising you to Me" (verified) — in both sites Allah is the actor and "to Me/Himself" the destination; the mutawaffa clause of 3:55 (translation: "terminating your life") is the classical hinge for how the two verses are sequenced — Source: classical tafsir divergence on 3:55/4:158 — verify.
* **`rasikhun fi-l-ilm` / "firmly rooted in knowledge"** (4:162):
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:7]` (verified — the sound-hearted of the hermeneutic verse) — the same class the surah's own 4:83 "those who can draw conclusions" mirrors from the other side (observable pairing, verified phrase sites).
* **`zann` / "assumptions"** (4:157): knowledge-free conviction.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:36]` — "they follow nothing but assumptions" exact-phrase twin (verified) — the zann-clause is the epistemology of the crucifixion-dispute: difference itself diagnosed as non-knowledge (agent synthesis of the verse's own sequence).
