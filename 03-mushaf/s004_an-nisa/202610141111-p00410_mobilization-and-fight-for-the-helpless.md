# Mobilize for Allah and for the helpless (4:71–76)
**UID:** 202610141111
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:71–76 (Ruku 10 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:71]` – `[4:76]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The command to take precautions and mobilize is followed by a portrait of the laggard — who reads disaster as personal favor and bounty as missed profit — and then by the trading verse: whoever sells this life for the Hereafter and fights, whether killed or victorious, gets a great compensation; and the surah asks the real question: why would you not fight for Allah *and for the helpless men, women, and children* whose prayer is quoted verbatim — "Our Lord, deliver us from this town whose people are oppressive" — since believer and disbeliever both fight, but only one side's ally is weak by strategy.
* **Placement:** The mobilization band (ruku 10–11) opens; the laggard 4:72–73 is the behavioral twin of the oath-spinner 4:62, and 4:75's quoted prayer is the surah's most explicit statement of why the community's force exists — for the mustad'afun, the same class as the "weak children" (4:9) and the "helpless children" of 4:127 (verified same English word).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:71]`** "O you who believe! Take your precautions, and mobilize in groups, or mobilize altogether."
  * **Gloss:** Military discipline as faith-register: watchfulness first, then dispatch — detachments or the whole body; no combat theology yet, pure logistics framed as obedience.
* **`[4:72]`** "Among you is he who lags behind. Then, when a calamity befalls you, he says, “Allah has favored me, that I was not martyred with them.”"
  * **Gloss:** The laggard's first tell: he theologizes his absence — calamity on others is read as personal divine favor.
* **`[4:73]`** "But when some bounty from Allah comes to you, he says—as if no affection existed between you and him—“If only I had been with them, I would have achieved a great victory.”"
  * **Gloss:** The same man re-runs the arithmetic at victory and finds he lost a share — "as if no affection existed": the contract of brotherhood is invisible to him.
* **`[4:74]`** "Let those who sell the life of this world for the Hereafter fight in the cause of Allah."
  * **Gloss:** The transaction idiom: sale of the lower life for the higher — the commercial vocabulary the surah has been policing since 4:29/4:44 now turned positive.
* **`[4:74]`** "Whoever fights in the cause of Allah, and then is killed, or achieves victory, We will grant him a great compensation."
  * **Gloss:** Both outcomes paid identically — death and triumph are one wage; the "We will grant" is revelation-voice commitment (kalam-grammar).
* **`[4:75]`** "And why would you not fight in the cause of Allah, and the helpless men, and women, and children, cry out, “Our Lord, deliver us from this town whose people are oppressive, and appoint for us from Your Presence a Protector, and appoint for us from Your Presence a Victor.”"
  * **Gloss:** The moral engine stated twice: for Allah, and for the class of the powerless whose prayer names both deliverance (Protector/wali, Victor/nasir — "from Your Presence" x2 — the same pair just pronounced sufficient at 4:45).
* **`[4:76]`** "Those who believe fight in the cause of Allah, while those who disbelieve fight in the cause of Evil. So fight the allies of the Devil. Surely the strategy of the Devil is weak."
  * **Gloss:** The battle-line is drawn by cause, not casualty; and the enemy's strategy (kayd) is declared structurally weak — the surah's answer to the laggard's fear calculus of 4:77 (next ruku).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`shara` / "sell the life of this world"**: the trade idiom (4:74).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:111]` — "Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and their properties in exchange for Paradise. They fight in Allah's way, and they kill and get killed" (verified) — the divine purchase side of the believer's sale; and `[2:16]` "bartered error for guidance" — the bad trade of 2:8–20's portrait (verified line earlier in build).
* **`mustad'af` / "the helpless"** (4:75): the structurally powerless.
  * **Rendering/elsewhere:** "helpless children" at `[4:127]` (verified) — the same class inside the surah's women-ruling; `[18:43]` has the adjective in another register (verified match) — the mustad'af-category of 4:97–99 (excused emigrators) is this verse's own list extended (ruku 14).
* **`taghut` / "the cause of Evil"** (4:76): the third English robe of the same institution this surah wears — "evil powers" (4:51), "Satanic sources" (4:60), "the cause of Evil" here (observable drift, verified occurrences).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:256]` — "Whoever renounces evil and believes in Allah has grasped the firmest handhold" (verified) — the rejection-word of Baqarah's handholding verse names the same object.
* **`kayd` / "strategy"** (4:76): "Surely the strategy of the Devil is weak" — the Quran's term for plotted force measured and found weak.
  * **Elsewhere:** the word is unique in this translation here (verified); the surah's neighboring plot-vocabularies are "conspire" — `[4:81]` (same surah) and `[58:8]` (verified) — and Satan's "promises... delusions" at `[4:120]` (same surah, ruku 18). Root-assignment (kayd) per classical lexicons — agent claim, verify in Lane/Lisan.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the mobilization verses sit in the fighting-legislation band of the surah (71–96), read classically against Uhud/Hudaybiyyah-era hypocrisy (Source: classical tafsir general framing for 4:71–77 — verify occasion reports before individual asbab).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 4:72–73 classically attached to the hypocrites absent at battles (Ibn Kathir family); 4:75's prayer classically identified with the oppressed believers left in Mecca, "this town" = Mecca (Ibn Kathir on 4:75) — Source: classical tafsir, agent condensation — verify.
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the text orders precaution and mobilization, states equal wage for killed-or-victorious, identifies the oppressed as a cause of fighting, and assigns disbelief's allies to the Devil's weak strategy. Rules of engagement, exempt classes, and treaty mechanics appear later in the surah (4:90–95); nothing here legislates them — Source: agent observation of scope.
