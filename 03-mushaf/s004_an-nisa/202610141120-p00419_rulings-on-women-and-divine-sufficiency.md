# The ruling about women, reconciliation's preference, and who owns the heavens (4:127–134)
**UID:** 202610141120
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:127–134 (Ruku 19 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:127]` – `[4:134]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The second "They ask you for a ruling" opens — this time about women (4:127, ringing all the way back to ruku 1's orphan-fairness command: the Book itself states the case of widow-girls you withhold from and desire to marry) — and the rulings that follow are the psychology of marriage made visible: reconciliation is best though souls are avaricious; perfect equity between wives is impossible by candid confession, so do not tip entirely and leave her suspended; if they separate, Allah enriches each from His abundance — and the ruku then detonates the frame: to Allah belongs everything in the heavens and everything on earth (said twice more), the taqwa charge shared with every previous Scripture-people, replacement of the whole community if He wills — so whoever desires only this world's reward should know both rewards are with Allah.
* **Placement:** The second women-cycle's pivot between the creed-ruku (18) and the justice-ruku (20); the "ask you for a ruling" phrase occurs exactly twice in the corpus — here and at 4:176 (verified) — bookending the entire surah with two questions about the same vulnerable classes.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:127]`** "They ask you for a ruling about women. Say, “Allah gives you a ruling about them, and so does what is stated to you in the Book about widowed women from whom you withhold what is decreed for them, yet you desire to marry them, and about helpless children: that you should treat the orphans fairly.”"
  * **Gloss:** The ruling is pointed back to revealed text itself — "what is stated in the Book" (read classically as ruku 1–3's own orphan/widow verses — agent identification from the internal echo, verify) — and the abuse named is double: withholding what is theirs while wanting them. "helpless children" resumes 4:75's mustad'af class (verified).
* **`[4:127]`** "Whatever good you do, Allah knows it."
  * **Gloss:** The knowledge-refrain, one breath long, as the unit's only audit.
* **`[4:128]`** "If a woman fears maltreatment or desertion from her husband, there is no fault in them if they reconcile their differences, for reconciliation is best."
  * **Gloss:** Maltreatment (shuqúq) or withdrawal (i'rad) anticipated; the parties' own settlement is lawful ("no fault") — and the maxim "reconciliation is best" re-prices the arbiter-hope of 4:35 (observable pair).
* **`[4:128]`** "Souls are prone to avarice; yet if you do what is good, and practice piety—Allah is Cognizant of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** Anthropology again (cf. 4:28 "created weak"): selfishness (shuhh) is the baseline; goodness and taqwa are the achieved exceptions — the closing refrain uses "Cognizant," another robe of the knowledge-names (observable drift).
* **`[4:129]`** "You will not be able to treat women with equal fairness, no matter how much you desire it."
  * **Gloss:** The candid impossibility clause: the very fairness 4:3 legislated as the condition of polygyny is declared humanly unreachable in its full measure — the law addresses acts, the heart is excused (Source: classical reading of 4:129 as inclinations vs. division of shares — e.g., Ibn Kathir citing the Companions' split on what is divided — verify).
* **`[4:129]`** "But do not be so biased as to leave another suspended."
  * **Gloss:** The limit-line: lean wholly and she hangs — mu'allaqa, "suspended," the same English the corpus uses of the mountain held over them at `[7:171]` (verified) — a half-wife is a woman held over an abyss.
* **`[4:129]`** "If you make amends, and act righteously—Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** Amend-and-act-form closes with the two names the surah's marriage band keeps returning to.
* **`[4:130]`** "And if they separate, Allah will enrich each from His abundance."
  * **Gloss:** The exit is not the cliff: both spouses' provision passes to a wider source (ghinya) — the ruku's economy-of-hope line.
* **`[4:130]`** "Allah is Bounteous and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** "Bounteous" (Ghani) — the self-sufficient Rich-name, which the surah will now say out loud twice in the refrain-verses (observable).
* **`[4:131]`** "To Allah belongs everything in the heavens and everything on earth. We have instructed those who were given the Book before you, and you, to be conscious of Allah."
  * **Gloss:** Ownership-assertion + the cross-generational taqwa charge — the same instruction as 4:43's band; the covenant-note's "renegotiated with each group" pattern stated in one clause (justified link).
* **`[4:131]`** "But if you refuse—to Allah belongs everything in the heavens and everything on earth. Allah is in no need, Praiseworthy."
  * **Gloss:** The refusal is cosmetic against total ownership; "in no need, Praiseworthy" — the Ghani/Hamid pair, same as `[64:6]` and `[14:8]` (verified).
* **`[4:132]`** "To Allah belongs everything in the heavens and everything on earth. Allah suffices as Manager."
  * **Gloss:** The ownership-line a third time in two ayahs (observable repetition-rhetoric), closing with the wakil-word — "Manager" — 4:109's "representative" in office here (drift pair, verified occurrences).
* **`[4:133]`** "If He wills, He can do away with you, O people, and bring others. Allah is Able to do that."
  * **Gloss:** The replacement threat addressed "O people" (the third of the surah's four direct communal address-changes — 4:1/4:133/4:170/4:174, observable): the community is contingent, its Lord is not.
* **`[4:134]`** "Whoever desires the reward of this world—with Allah is the reward of this world and the next."
  * **Gloss:** The wish-economy answered without the 4:123 negation — both rewards are simply *with Allah*, so short-term desire is not denial but shrinkage.
* **`[4:134]`** "Allah is All-Hearing, All-Seeing."
  * **Gloss:** The hearing-seeing pair returns (4:58's names) to fence the whole ruku (verified repetition by reading).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`istafta` / "ask you for a ruling"**: the fatwa-request formula — exactly two sites in the corpus, `[4:127]` and `[4:176]` (verified), both in this surah, both about the vulnerable: the bookend the surah builds around itself.
* **`islah` / "reconciliation" (4:128)**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:35]` (the arbiters' aim), `[4:114]` (the permitted counsel), `[49:9–10]` (believed-brothers' settlement), `[2:226/228]` (spousal band) (all verified) — an-Nisa uses the word for the marriage repair the community must pay for with counsel, arbitration, and patience.
* **`mu'allaq / 'allaqa` / "suspended" (4:129)**:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:171]` — "We suspended the mountain over them, as if it was an umbrella" (verified) — in both uses, suspension is not levitation but threat held in the air.
* **`shuhh` / "avarice" (4:128)**: "Souls are prone to avarice" — the noun's only corpus site (verified hapax).
* **Ghani/Hamid refrain (4:130–132)**: "Bounteous" / "in no need" — `[64:6]`, `[14:8]` (verified) — the self-sufficiency names appear in the surah precisely when human provision-talk peaks (observable placement).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the widow-orphan situation of the class is the band's running subject since 4:3 (observable).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 4:127 is tied in the reports to the orphan-girl whose guardian withheld her dower or kept her unmarried for her property (the Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib account explicitly names 4:127 with 4:3 — al-Bukhari family) — Source: classical asbab — verify wording.
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the text orders: no withholding of decreed shares from widow-girls; permission (not obligation) of marital compromise without judge; confession of practical impossibility of full emotional equity; ban on total leaning that leaves a spouse in legal limbo; and the exit-enrichment promise. The "equal fairness" object (rotational days? inclination?) and the limits of the compromise (renouncing dower for stay?) are classical divergences — Source: tafsir/fiqh references (Ibn Kathir collects both on 4:128–129) — verify.
