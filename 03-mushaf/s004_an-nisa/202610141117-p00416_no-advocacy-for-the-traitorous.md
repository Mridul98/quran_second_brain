# Judge by the truth; never advocate for the treacherous (4:105–112)
**UID:** 202610141117
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:105–112 (Ruku 16 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:105]` – `[4:112]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The Scripture was revealed *with the truth* so that judgment tracks what Allah shows — and so the advocate's chair is closed: do not plead for the traitors, do not argue for those who deceive themselves, since they hide from people but cannot hide from Allah who is present at their night-plots; the Day's question — "who will argue with Allah on their behalf?" — frames the two mercy-verses that answer every other threat (whoever asks forgiveness will find Allah Forgiving; whoever earns a sin earns it against himself), and the unit ends on the heaviest clause: to shoulder your own fault onto an innocent person is "a slander and a clear sin."
* **Placement:** The courtroom ruku inside the hypocrite-dossier: 4:105's judging mandate is the application of 4:58 (the trust/justice charter, verified pairing), and the frame-forging of 4:105–107 is classically attached to one specific perjury case (the asbab below) — the only place in the surah where "advocacy" itself is criminalized.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:105]`** "We have revealed to you the Scripture, with the truth, so that you judge between people in accordance with what Allah has shown you."
  * **Gloss:** Revelation-speech as judicial warrant: the book's truth-mode (anzala bi-l-haqq) feeds a verdict-function; "what Allah has shown you" makes judgment receptive, not inventive (kalam-grammar applied to law).
* **`[4:105]`** "And do not be an advocate for the traitors."
  * **Gloss:** Khayanat — treason as a habit of persons; pleading for them is barred before any facts are cited (the case is named only in the reports below — observable).
* **`[4:106]`** "And ask Allah for forgiveness. Allah is Forgiver and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** A one-line turn toward the Prophet himself: even the near-miss of advocacy needs istighfar — the repentance-refrain inside a prohibition.
* **`[4:107]`** "And do not argue on behalf of those who deceive themselves. Allah does not love the deceitful sinner."
  * **Gloss:** The second ban generalizes: self-deceivers are the real clients no advocate should take; "Allah does not love the khawwan athim" — a double-suffix phrase unique here (the twin idiom "every ungrateful traitor" at 22:38, verified).
* **`[4:108]`** "They hide from the people, but they cannot hide from Allah. He is with them, as they plot by night with words He does not approve."
  * **Gloss:** The surveillance reversal (their hiding vs. His presence) — nearly verbatim twin: `[43:80]` "do they think that We cannot hear their secrets and their conspiracies? Yes... Our messengers are by them, writing down" (verified); "cannot hide" is a corpus hapax here (verified).
* **`[4:109]`** "There you are, arguing on their behalf in the present life, but who will argue with Allah on their behalf on the Day of Resurrection? Or who will be their representative?"
  * **Gloss:** The two-question unmasking; "representative" (wakil — the same word the surah renders "Manager" at 4:132, observable drift) asks: who holds that retainer in the next court?
* **`[4:110]`** "Whoever commits evil, or wrongs his soul, then implores Allah for forgiveness, will find Allah Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The open door, phrased with 4:64's "wrongs his soul" (verified same-phrase pair): the dossier's threats never close the repentance arc.
* **`[4:111]`** "And Whoever earns a sin, earns it against himself. Allah is Aware and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** Kasb — sin's economics: every earning lands on the earner's own account (cf. 52:21-family "every soul for what it earned" — not re-verified here; agent note).
* **`[4:112]`** "And whoever commits a mistake, or a sin, and then blames it on an innocent person, has taken a slander and a clear sin."
  * **Gloss:** Frame-up named as its own crime (buhtan): the "innocent" (bari) vocabulary is Abraham's disavowal-word — `[6:78]`, `[43:26]` "I am innocent of..." (verified) — shifting it to the victim makes the slander clear (mubin).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`kha'in/khayana` / "traitors" + "deceive themselves"**: the kh-w-n family in two robes.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[22:38]` "Allah does not love any ungrateful traitor" (verified — the exact "does not love" twin); `[8:27]` — "do not betray Allah and the Messenger, and betray your trusts knowingly" (verified) — the same verb welds treason (khayanah) to the trusts (amanat) of 4:58, completing this surah's chain: trusts betrayed → treason → no advocacy; within-surah 4:107's intensive "deceitful sinner (khawwan)" is a corpus-hapax (verified pattern).
* **`najwa`-adjacent "plot by night" (bayt)**: night-counsel.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[58:8]` (prohibited from "conspiring secretly" — verified), `[43:80]` (secrets/conspiracies written down — verified) — the plot-vocabulary is consistently nocturnal, and consistently recorded.
* **`wakil` / "representative"** (4:109): agency, legal and cosmic.
  * **Rendering drift:** same root rendered "Manager" at `[4:132]` ("Allah suffices as Manager") and "Trustee"-family verbs elsewhere in the corpus (agent claim — verify the 6:102/39:62 "Guardian/Trustee" occurrences before repeating); within this surah the drift is between a courtroom officer (4:109) and a household officer (4:132) — observable.
* **`kasb` / "earns"**: the earning-verb for sin (4:111) — the same verb renders "earned" for shares at `[4:32]` (verified by reading): the surah's acquisition-word does double duty, wages and guilt alike.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; a community dispute with a real perjury-and-theft case behind it (see asbab).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 4:105–112 are classically attached to the case of Ta'ima (or Utba) ibn Abiraq, who stole armor and whose clan pleaded for him before the Prophet, pressing an innocent Jew to bear the blame (al-Bukhari-family reports; narrations also in the tafsir corpora — Ibn Kathir on 4:105–108) — Source: classical asbab; wording of names differs across reports — verify before repeating specifics. Note that the verses generalize the case at 4:107 and 4:110-112: the text itself lifts the ruling above the incident (observable).
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the text orders: adjudicate by revealed guidance; never advocate for known treachery; no transferring of sin to the innocent (named a clear sin); and keeps forgiveness open to any actual wrongdoer who asks. The procedures of evidence and witness-disqualification derived here are *fiqh* — Source: classical tafsir/fiqh reference.
