# One soul, orphans' wealth, and the fairness ceiling (4:1–10)
**UID:** 202610141102
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:1–10 (Ruku 1 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:1]` – `[4:10]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's constitution is founded on a single act of creation — one soul and its mate, from which "many men and women" were propagated — and every following duty (guard orphan property, do not eat it, marry fairly within a ceiling of four, give dowries as gifts, test wards for maturity, witness the handover, share inheritance with women and children alike, give at the distribution, fear Allah for the weak children you leave behind) is the social consequence of that common origin, under the closing gaze: "Allah is Watchful over you."
* **Placement:** The opening ruku of the family-wealth complex (ruku 1–5). 4:1's one-soul anthropology sets the frame the whole surah legislates within; the "serious sin"/"fire into their bellies" brackets (4:2, 4:10) enclose the orphan-wealth theme that returns at 4:127 (ruku 19) — a ring the surah itself marks (observable).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:1]`** "O people! Fear your Lord, who created you from a single soul, and created from it its mate, and propagated from them many men and women."
  * **Gloss:** A universal "O people!" — the whole surah's particular law addressed first to all humanity, grounded in one origin-pair.
* **`[4:1]`** "And revere Allah whom you ask about, and the parents. Surely, Allah is Watchful over you."
  * **Gloss:** Two reverences: Allah "whom you ask about" (the formula-name of pre-Islamic oaths — Source: classical tafsir on the phrase, Ibn Kathir/al-Qurtubi; verify) and the parents; the refrain "Watchful over you" installs the surveillance that makes guardianship law possible.
* **`[4:2]`** "And give orphans their properties, and do not substitute the bad for the good."
  * **Gloss:** Positive command (hand over) then negative (no swap of rotten for sound in the guardian's ledger).
* **`[4:2]`** "And do not consume their properties by combining them with yours, for that would be a serious sin."
  * **Gloss:** Commingling is named — the mixing that makes theft invisible — graded "a serious sin" (a large sin, kabir-type wording).
* **`[4:3]`** "If you fear you cannot act fairly towards the orphans—then marry the women you like— two, or three, or four."
  * **Gloss:** The fairness anxiety about orphans is the stated occasion of the polygyny ceiling; the "if... then" order keeps fairness as the condition, four as the limit, not the norm.
* **`[4:3]`** "But if you fear you will not be fair, then one, or what you already have. That makes it more likely that you avoid bias."
  * **Gloss:** The monogamy default: one — or what you already have (existing bonds); the verse states its own rationale ("more likely").
* **`[4:4]`** "Give women their dowries graciously. But if they willingly forego some of it, then consume it with enjoyment and pleasure."
  * **Gloss:** The dower is a gift ("graciously"); only the wife's willing release legitimizes keeping any of it — the only route back into her property.
* **`[4:5]`** "Do not give the immature your money which Allah has assigned to you for support."
  * **Gloss:** Financial majority is not age alone — the immature (the light-handed) are not handed capital.
* **`[4:5]`** "But provide for them from it, and clothe them, and speak to them with kind words."
  * **Gloss:** Withholding the principal comes with three positive duties: maintenance, clothing, and *kind speech* — guardianship is not merely solvent but courteous.
* **`[4:6]`** "Test the orphans until they reach the age of marriage. If you find them to be mature enough, hand over their properties to them."
  * **Gloss:** A competency test (`rushd`, rendered "mature enough") at the marriage-age checkpoint converts wardship into release.
* **`[4:6]`** "And do not consume it extravagantly or hastily before they grow up. The rich shall not charge any wage, but the poor may charge fairly."
  * **Gloss:** Two clocks of abuse (waste, premature consumption) and one honest one: the needy guardian's fair fee; gratuitous service for the rich.
* **`[4:6]`** "When you hand over their properties to them, have it witnessed for them. Allah suffices as a Reckoner."
  * **Gloss:** Legal witnesses, and if none — "Allah suffices as a Reckoner" (hisab): the audit that never closes.
* **`[4:7]`** "Men receive a share of what their parents and relatives leave, and women receive a share of what their parents and relatives leave; be it little or much—a legal share."
  * **Gloss:** Against the practice that excluded women and small children, the text asserts a share for both sexes, "be it little or much," called *fard* — "a legal share" (enforced entitlement).
* **`[4:8]`** "If the distribution is attended by the relatives, and the orphans, and the needy, give them something out of it, and speak to them kindly."
  * **Gloss:** The un-listed bystanders at the estate — kin, orphans, needy — get a courtesy portion and kind words (Source: classical tafsir reads this as recommendation, not forced share, e.g. Ibn Kathir on 4:8 — verify the fiqh spread).
* **`[4:9]`** "Those who are concerned about the fate of their weak children, in case they leave them behind, should fear Allah, and speak appropriate words."
  * **Gloss:** The empathy command: as you would want your own orphaned children treated, treat the orphans in your charge — "appropriate words" included.
* **`[4:10]`** "Those who consume the wealth of orphans illicitly consume only fire into their bellies; and they will roast in a Blaze."
  * **Gloss:** The bracket closes: illicit consumption (the same word as 2:188's eaten wealth) becomes eschatological fire — the warning form the surah repeats for every property breach.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`nafs` / "single soul" (`wahida`)**: one origin-self, "its mate" (`zawjaha`) created from it.
  * **Rendering:** "a single soul... its mate"; the same creation-claim is rendered "a single person" at `[7:189]` and "one person" at `[39:6]` (both verified via rg), and "as a single soul" for universal resurrection at `[31:28]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:189]`, `[39:6]`, `[31:28]` — the one-soul motif always grounds either spousal tranquility or the unity of the human family; here it grounds *law*.
* **`yatim` / "orphans"**: minors without paternal support; the surah's first protected class.
  * **Rendering:** "orphans" consistently (4:2, 3, 6, 8).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:83]` (the covenant clause "and the orphan" in the same list), `[6:152]` ("come near the orphan's wealth... until he reaches maturity" — the exact twin rule), `[2:220]`, `[93:9]`, `[107:2]` (the orphan-deterred and orphan-repulsed as the unbeliever's portrait).
* **`sadaqat` / "dowries"**: the wife's property-gift (mahr/sadaqah).
  * **Rendering:** "dowries" here (4:4), "dowry" at `[4:24]`, but "recompense" at `[4:25]` — one institution, three English words (translator variation, observable).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:236]`, `[2:237]` — the same "dowry" term in the Baqarah divorce band the rulings here assume.
* **`muqit` / "Watchful over you"** (4:1 close; also "Reckoner" 4:6, "Witness over all things" 4:33).
  * **Rendering:** "Surely, Allah is Watchful over you."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:33]` "Is He who is watchful over the deeds of every soul?" and `[33:52]` — the English watchfulness-word used as a divine-audit refrain (translation-level echo verified; the underlying Arabic differs from raqib-family — Source: agent inference — verify root assignment).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical consensus). The guardianship-orphanage situation is Medinan: the surah legislates for a community absorbing war orphans and widow-remarriages (observable from the rulings' content; Source: consensus reading of the legal matter).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 4:3 is classically linked to reports about guardians inclined to marry attractive/wealthy orphan wards without dealing fairly (transmitted via Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib's account about orphan girls, collected by al-Bukhari; echoed in Ibn Kathir on 4:3), and 4:3–4 are paired in the same reports with 4:127's question about "widowed women from whom you withhold what is decreed." Source: classical asbab reports, agent condensation — verify hadith wording and chains.
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the text orders (1) transfer of orphan property at competency, (2) a ban on commingling and on wage abuse, (3) a ceiling of four wives with a monogamy default under fairness fear, (4) dowry as irrevocable gift absent the wife's consent, (5) inheritance shares for both sexes (detailed in ruku 2). Any expansion (e.g., who counts as mahram-guardian, court supervision mechanisms) is classical fiqh, not the ayah's wording — Source: classical tafsir/fiqh reference where cited.
