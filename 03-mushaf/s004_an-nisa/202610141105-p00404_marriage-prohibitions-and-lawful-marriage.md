# The prohibition list, the married women, and the way for the unable (4:23–25)
**UID:** 202610141105
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 4:23–25 (Ruku 4 of 24 in an-Nisa')
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[4:23]` – `[4:25]` (3 ayahs — three long ones; the surah's densest eligibility law sits in its shortest ruku)
* **Thesis:** The marriage market gets its boundaries in one breath: fourteen categories forbidden — blood kin, foster kin, in-laws, the two-sisters-simultaneous — then "all married women, except those you rightfully possess," then the open field beyond these limits for lawful pursuit with gifts, and finally the graded concession for the man who cannot afford a free believing woman: her believing ward, half the free woman's punishment for the same crime, and the closing preference — "But to practice self-restraint is better for you."
* **Placement:** The hinge of the marriage cycle (ruku 1–5): after the conjugal duties of ruku 3, the eligibility map; before 4:26's meta-statement of divine intent ("Allah intends to make things clear to you"), which reads like the preface written for exactly this kind of table.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[4:23]`** "Forbidden for you are your mothers, your daughters, your sisters, your paternal aunts, your maternal aunts, your brother's daughters, your sister's daughters,"
  * **Gloss:** The blood list opens — three ascendant/collateral classes in seven named relations.
* **`[4:23]`** "your fostermothers who nursed you, your sisters through nursing,"
  * **Gloss:** Foster kin enter by the lactation bond — nursing creates mahramhood (the extent of the milk-bond is classical fiqh, not the wording here — Source: classical tafsir/fiqh reference, e.g. the reports on five breastfeeder; verify).
* **`[4:23]`** "your wives' mothers, and your stepdaughters in your guardianship— born of wives you have gone into—but if you have not gone into them, there is no blame on you."
  * **Gloss:** Affinity: the mother-in-law is absolute; the step-daughter's bar triggers on consummation of the mother's marriage — the text states its own conditionality.
* **`[4:23]`** "And the wives of your genetic sons, and marrying two sisters simultaneously. Except what is past. Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The ex-daughter-in-law and the sororal pair close the list; the amnesty formula "Except what is past" (identical frame to 4:22) seals it with the mercy-names.
* **`[4:24]`** "And all married women, except those you rightfully possess. This is Allah’s decree, binding upon you."
  * **Gloss:** The married-women ban with one stated exception of possession-status; the whole table labeled a binding decree (kurub? the wording: "Allah's decree" — observable).
* **`[4:24]`** "Permitted for you are those that lie outside these limits, provided you seek them in legal marriage, with gifts from your property, seeking wedlock, not prostitution."
  * **Gloss:** The open field beyond the limits, with the intent-clause: legal marriage, property-backed gifts, wedlock not illicit companionship.
* **`[4:24]`** "If you wish to enjoy them, then give them their dowry—a legal obligation. You commit no error by agreeing to any change to the dowry. Allah is All-Knowing, Most Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The dowry obligation fixed; mutual renegotiation of amount permitted — "no error"; knowledge-refrain closes.
* **`[4:25]`** "If any of you lack the means to marry free believing women, he may marry one of the believing maids under your control. Allah is well aware of your faith. You are from one another."
  * **Gloss:** The concession for the means-poor; the two clauses "Allah is well aware of your faith. You are from one another" re-level master and ward as one believing body (a theological aside inside a ruling — observable rhetoric).
* **`[4:25]`** "Marry them with the permission of their guardians, and give them their recompense fairly—to be protected—neither committing adultery, nor taking secret lovers."
  * **Gloss:** Guardian's permission, fair "recompense" (the same mahr institution, third English rendering in this band), chastity status defined: neither open nor hidden illicit partnership.
* **`[4:25]`** "When they are married, if they commit adultery, their punishment shall be half that of free women. That is for those among you who fear falling into decadence."
  * **Gloss:** The graded penalty stated in-text; the license's purpose clause: for those who fear slipping — concession as prophylactic, not privilege.
* **`[4:25]`** "But to practice self-restraint is better for you. Allah is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's final judgment: the concession is lawful, restraint superior — the same "That makes it more likely" pedagogy as 4:3's ceiling.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`mahram` list / "Forbidden for you"**: the relation-table (blood, wet-nurse, affinity, simultaneous).
  * **Rendering:** catalog style, no categories named — the relations themselves are the law.
  * **Elsewhere:** the only other full-block of prohibited relations is in the Bible's own holiness code sense — within this Quran, no parallel list (verified: rg "Forbidden for you are your" hits s004 only); the "mothers... daughters" pairing as ethical unit appears in covenant recitations `[2:83]` (parents/kin clause) instead.
* **`rida` / fostermothers ("who nursed you")**: the lactation bond as kinship.
  * **Elsewhere:** the word "foster" recurs only as an unrelated verb at `[10:81]` "Allah does not foster the efforts of the corrupt" (verified) — the institution's legal vocabulary is confined to this ayah in this translation; the nursing law's details (duration, count) are fiqh — Source: classical tafsir/fiqh reference.
* **`ma malakat aymanukum` / "those you rightfully possess"**: the possession-status exception.
  * **Elsewhere:** the same institution's household-word "maids" returns at `[4:25]` and at `[24:32]` "marry the unmarried among you... and your righteous maids" (verified); `[24:31]` "or what their right hands possess" (verified via rg "right hands") — the phrase-family is small and stable.
* **`mahr / sadaqat` again**: "dowry" (4:24 ×2), "recompense" (4:25), "gifts from your property" (4:24) — one institution, three renderings within three ayahs (observable; cf. `[2:236]` "dowry" in the Baqarah band, verified).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical consensus); eligibility and punishment tables are the community's court-law register.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** none specific transmitted for the list itself; "Except what is past" is classically read as amnesty for pre-Islamic practice (the same formula as 4:22) — Source: consensus of classical tafsir on the phrase.
* **Legal content, neutrally stated:** the ayahs order the fourteen-category prohibition, the married-women ban with its possession exception, marriage-with-gifts as the lawful mode, dowry obligation with freedom of mutual adjustment, the unable man's path to a believing ward with guardian permission and half-graded penalty, and the superiority of restraint. Details the schools derive (milk-bond extent, "gone into" definition, guardians' authority conditions, penalty arithmetic) are *fiqh* — Source: classical tafsir/fiqh reference.
