# Noon. By the Pen: the vindicated character and the owners of the garden (68:1–33)
**UID:** 202610142215
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 68:1–33 (Ruku 1 of 2 in al-Qalam)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[68:1]` – `[68:33]` (33 ayahs — the longest ruku-1 in the brain's Hanafi table for this cluster; globals 498)
* **Thesis:** God swears by the instrument of inscription — the Pen and what they write — that the accused messenger is sane, endlessly rewarded, and on "a great moral character" (68:1–7); the denier dossier (68:8–16) then receives its mirror parable: owners of a garden who vow the harvest with no reservation, bar the poor at dawn, and wake to nothing — their confession arc (glorify → blame → "we were domineering → turning to our Lord") being the ruku's actual sermon (68:17–33).
* **Placement:** The surah's oath-half; ruku 2 (68:34–52) supplies the verdict scenes the parable previews ("Such is the punishment; but the punishment of the Hereafter is greater," 68:33).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[68:1]`** "Noon. By the pen, and by what they inscribe." — lone letter-name (see Muqatta'at fleeting note, which quotes this line verbatim) + the Pen oath: the book swears by its own medium; "what they inscribe" (ma yasthurun) keeps the writers anonymous — *Source: agent inference — verify against classical lists of oath addressees.*
* **`[68:2]`** "By the grace of your Lord, you are not insane." — the charge answered under oath; "by the grace of your Lord" (fa-bi-ni'mati) ties vindication to ni'ma — a shukr-frame. Task-flag: "possessed" rejected; corpus = "insane".
* **`[68:3]`** "In fact, you will have a reward that will never end." — the counter-verdict: the insulted life has a non-terminating wage (minna — "unfailing" in the task's wording; Itani: "will never end").
* **`[68:4]`** "And you are of a great moral character." — khuluq azim; the Prophet's ethics swore-in as divine testimony; "moral character" is 68:4-only in the corpus (rg-counted).
* **`[68:5]`** "You will see, and they will see." — the wager inverted: sight as the medium of resolution (echoes 67:3–4's "look again").
* **`[68:6]`** "Which of you is the afflicted." — the word they shouted (68:51 "He is crazy!" — the madman's twin) becomes the trial's open question.
* **`[68:7]`** "Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path, and He knows best the well-guided." — divine omniscience closes the madness-case; the guidance-family vocabulary (strayed/guided — cf. k-guidance) is explicit.
* **`[68:8]`** "So do not obey the deniers." — the ruku's pivot to ethics: obedience refused to kadh-dhibun (those pronouncing lies about the Book).
* **`[68:9]`** "They would like you to compromise, so they would compromise." — tuddhinu: the pressure for softening; the conditional mirror ("so they would") shows negotiation as their method — *Source: classical reading takes tudhina as "softening religion" (din-idiom) — verify against lexicon; corpus shows only the rendering.*
* **`[68:10]`** "And do not obey any vile swearer." — hallaf: habitual swearer (the oath-abuser right after the oath-surah — observable irony).
* **`[68:11]`** "Backbiter, spreader of slander." — ghuybah/namim: speech-crimes against others' absence and between parties.
* **`[68:12]`** "Preventer of good, transgressor, sinner." — mannu' al-khayr: the same withholding verb-root as 68:24's plot (obstructing good).
* **`[68:13]`** "Rude and fake besides." — ghaliz + zaghil: coarseness plus counterfeit claim — two adjectives Itani compresses.
* **`[68:14]`** "Just because he has money and children." — the credential-illusion: wealth/children as the grounds of authority (cf. the s063 dossier logic 63:7's economics).
* **`[68:15]`** "When Our Verses are recited to him, he says, “Myths of the ancients!”" — the kalam-denial line; exact phrase-twins `[6:25]`, `[8:31]`; family variants `[16:24]`, `[83:13]`, `[27:68]`, `[25:5]`, `[46:17]` (all rg-verified).
* **`[68:16]`** "We will brand him on the muzzle." — khatm ala al-anf: the nose-muzzle branding — reversal of a face that read itself as credential.
* **`[68:17]`** "We tested them, as We tested the owners of the garden, when they vowed to harvest it in the morning." — the parable's setup in one breath: the divine test (yabtalunahum) explicitly compared — "as We tested" — making the garden the surah's own case-law.
* **`[68:18]`** "Without any reservation." — la yastathnuna: the istithna omitted — the task's "do not say God-willing" clause lives here in negative form; its positive home is `[18:23–24]` (rg-verified).
* **`[68:19]`** "But a calamity from your Lord went around it while they slept." — taifu: a rounder going the perimeter at night; the gift they planned to hoard is flanked.
* **`[68:20]`** "And in the morning it was as if picked." — as if mowed/harvested — the loss looks exactly like their plan, executed by another hand.
* **`[68:21]`** "In the morning, they called to one another." — the dawn conference: urgency after loss, still in the planning register.
* **`[68:22]`** "“Go early to your plantation, if you are going to harvest.”" — "plantation" (zar'in) slips in even as they speak in conditional doubt.
* **`[68:23]`** "So off they went, murmuring to one another." — yafurtun: the hush of a conspiracy they believe invisible (cf. 67:13 — speech hidden from no One).
* **`[68:24]`** "“No poor person is to enter it upon you today.”" — THE crime named at the center: the gate barred to miskīn — the harvest-share of `[51:19]`/`[70:24–25]` refused in advance (rg-verified twins; k-infraq thesis).
* **`[68:25]`** "And early they went, resolved in intent." — sabihin mudghimin: early and "domineering" — the same word their confession will reuse (68:31, rg-internal).
* **`[68:26]`** "But when they saw it, they said, “We were wrong." — sight after the hush: the first admission, a sentence left to continue.
* **`[68:27]`** "We are now deprived.”" — mahrumun: the harvest was always theirs as trust; now the line-item reads zero.
* **`[68:28]`** "The most reasonable of them said, “Did I not say to you, ‘if only you would glorify?’”" — aqsaduhum + tasbih: the inner dissent existed; glorifying God = acknowledging the Giver before claiming the gift.
* **`[68:29]`** "They said, “Glory to our Lord—We were indeed in the wrong.”" — the confession proper: subhana rabbina, in the same breath as the wrongness.
* **`[68:30]`** "Then they turned to one another, blaming one another." — yatālawlamun: mutual blame — same root as 75:2's oath "the blaming soul" (rg-verified: corpus has "blaming" only in 68:30 and 75:2).
* **`[68:31]`** "They said, “Woe to us—we were indeed domineering." — ilayna... kudhum: the word of 68:25's intent now named as the crime — the hush read back as arrogance.
* **`[68:32]`** "Perhaps our Lord will give us a better substitute for it. We are turning to our Lord.”" — the exit: raja + turnaround; the parable ends in repentance, not ruin — unlike ruku 2's gate-closers.
* **`[68:33]`** "Such is the punishment; but the punishment of the Hereafter is greater, if they only knew." — the generalizing keystone: the garden as sample; "if they only knew" closes the oath-half on withheld knowledge.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`qalam` (Pen) / `yasthurun` (inscribe):** 68:1 — the oath's addressees; inscription as the medium the surah stakes its vindication on. **Elsewhere:** `[52:2–3]` "And a Book inscribed. In a published scroll"; `[83:20–21]` the record "in Elliyyeen... a numerical book... witnessed by those brought near" (rg-verified) — the k-kalam inscription family.
* **`yastathni` (make reservation/say God-willing):** 68:18 (negative). **Elsewhere:** `[18:24]` "Without saying, 'If Allah wills'" (rg-verified) — the only explicit istithna-command in the corpus.
* **`mudghim/domineering` & `lawwama/blaming`:** 68:25/31 and 68:30 — the parable's own word-loop; 68:30's "blaming" is one of only two "blaming" lines in the corpus (`[75:2]` the other — rg-counted).
