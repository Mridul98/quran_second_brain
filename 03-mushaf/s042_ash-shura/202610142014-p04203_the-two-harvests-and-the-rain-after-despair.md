# The two harvests, the obliterated false, and the rain after despair (42:20–29)
**UID:** 202610142014
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 42:20–29 (Ruku 3 of 5 in ash-Shura)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[42:20]` – `[42:29]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku puts capital where the heart is — two harvests, one increase and one forfeiture (20) — then clears the forgery charge against the messenger by showing God's own litigation style: He obliterates the false and confirms the true by His Words (24), accepts repentance and answers prayer (25–26), rations provision precisely to keep earth from chaos (27), and signs the whole argument with the rain that falls after despair and the creatures who can be gathered at will (28–29).
* **Placement:** The surah's practical-theology pivot: ruku 2 said who judges and who prescribed; this ruku shows the Judge's economics (harvest, provision), His courtroom habits (false vs. true), and His mercy's weather (rain after despair).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[42:20]`** "Whoever desires the harvest of the Hereafter, We increase for him his harvest; and whoever desires the harvest of this world, We give him thereof, and he has no share of the Hereafter."
  * **Gloss:** The cultivation metaphor turned eschatological: desire one harvest, be given it — the world's harvest is real but terminal; the other compounds.
* **`[42:21]`** "Or is it that they have partners who litigate for them religious laws never authorized by Allah? Were it not for the conclusive decision, it would have been settled between them. The wicked will have a painful punishment."
  * **Gloss:** Shirk exposed as legislation-by-committee: partners issuing religious law without divine mandate — the judgment deferred only by the conclusive decision.
* **`[42:22]`** "You will see the unjust terrified of what they have earned, and it will befall them. As for those who believe and do good deeds, they will be in the Meadows of the Gardens; they will have whatever they please in the presence of their Lord; that is the supreme blessing."
  * **Gloss:** The census of fear and delight — "whatever they please in the presence of their Lord": the presence itself is the garden.
* **`[42:23]`** "That is the good news Allah gives to His servants who believe and do good deeds. Say, “I ask of you no wage for it, except affection among the near of kin.” Whoever does a good deed, We will increase its goodness for him. Allah is Forgiving and Appreciative."
  * **Gloss:** The message is free — wage refused; the sole excepted "wage" is kin-affection (classically disputed: love of the Prophet's family vs. natural kin-bonds; *Source: classical exegetical disagreement — verify in print*); closed by the Ghafir-name pair, Forgiving + Appreciative.
* **`[42:24]`** "Or do they say, “He forged a lie about Allah.” If Allah so willed, He could have sealed your heart. But Allah obliterates the false, and confirm the true by His Words. He knows what is in the hearts."
  * **Gloss:** The forgery charge answered: an unsealed heart is proof of assignment, not fabrication — and God's Words run a subtraction of falsehood and an addition of truth (corpus "and confirm the true" is the book's own uninflected verb — quoted as-is, sic per the artifacts note).
* **`[42:25]`** "It is He who accepts the repentance of His worshipers, and remits the sins, and knows what you do."
  * **Gloss:** Repentance is not merely permitted but *accepted* — the twin of `[40:3]`'s name-string "Accepter of repentance."
* **`[42:26]`** "And He answers those who believe and do good deeds, and He increases them of His grace. But the disbelievers will suffer a terrible punishment."
  * **Gloss:** Answered prayer — the Ghafir promise "Pray to Me, and I will respond to you" (`[40:60]`) restated as fact; grace adds the increase.
* **`[42:27]`** "If Allah were to increase the provision to His servants, they would transgress on earth; but He sends down in precise measure whatever He wills. Surely, regarding His servants, He is Expert and Observant."
  * **Gloss:** The rationing theodicy: abundance unchecked becomes aggression; provision is metered by a Knowing One (verbal twin `[15:21]` "We send it down only in precise measure").
* **`[42:28]`** "It is He who brings down the rain after they have despaired, and unfolds His mercy. He is the Guardian, the Praised."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's mercy-icon: rain arriving exactly at the end of hope, spreading mercy like a scroll ("the Praised" — the same Hamid claim as `[31:12]` "Allah is Sufficient and Praiseworthy").
* **`[42:29]`** "And of His signs are the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the creatures He has spread throughout them; and He is Able to gather them at will."
  * **Gloss:** Scattering is never out of hand: the One who spreads can gather — resurrection argued from meteorology and biology in one breath.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **ḥarth (harvest/tilth):** `[42:20]` "the harvest of the Hereafter… the harvest of this world."
  * **Rendering:** "harvest" — everywhere else in the corpus the word is agrarian-literal (`[3:117]` a frost striking "the harvest of a people"; `[18:33]` gardens yielding "their harvest"; `[6:141]` "the day of its harvest") — 42:20 alone cultivates it eschatologically (rg-verified).
* **Ghafūr + Shakūr:** `[42:23]` "Allah is Forgiving and Appreciative."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:30]` "He is Forgiving and Appreciative." — the verbatim twin pair; k-shukr (`202610141600`) cites 42:33 but not this formula — flagged at index. "Appreciative" is Itani's shakūr rendering when divine.
* **qadar (precise measure):** `[42:27]` "He sends down in precise measure whatever He wills."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[15:21]` "We send it down only in precise measure" — the only other such line (rg-verified); the pair supplies the book's rationing grammar against 42:12's spread-and-reduce.
* **waly + ḥamd:** `[42:28]` "He is the Guardian, the Praised."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[31:12]` "…Allah is Sufficient and Praiseworthy" — the Praised-name's twin; guardianship (*wali*) answers the surah's running protector-question (42:6, 8, 9, 44).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical lists); the wage-refusal (23) and forgery-charge (24) sit in the Meccan polemic where the messenger's credibility is the trial in progress. *Source: classical lists + corpus internal claims.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** "affection among the near of kin" (23) is classically read two ways — love of the Prophet's family (transmitted in both Sunni and Shi'i exegetical corpora) or the general natural duty of kin-love; the corpus itself decides neither. *Source: classical disagreement — verify in print; agent records both, prefers none.*
