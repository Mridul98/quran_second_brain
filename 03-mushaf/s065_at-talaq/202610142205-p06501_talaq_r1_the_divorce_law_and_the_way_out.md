# The divorce law and the taqwa that finds a way out (65:1–7)
**UID:** 202610142205
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 65:1–7 (Ruku 1 of 2 in at-Talaq)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[65:1]` – `[65:7]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Marriage's end is choreographed to the calendar — purity-timing, counted terms, witnesses, residence, maintenance, nursing wages — and through the anxious machinery runs a triple promise to whoever fears Allah: a way out from nowhere expected, ease, remitted sins and amplified reward, because the God who legislates "has set a measure to all things."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 2; the legal half of at-Talaq and the practical sequel to at-Taghabun's household warnings (64:14–16). Its refrain-theology hands off to ruku 2's cosmology.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[65:1]`** "O Prophet! If any of you divorce women, divorce them during their period of purity, and calculate their term. And be pious before Allah, your Lord. And do not evict them from their homes, nor shall they leave, unless they have committed a proven adultery. These are the limits of Allah—whoever oversteps Allah’s limits has wronged his own soul. You never know; Allah may afterwards bring about a new situation."
  * **Gloss:** The command fixes timing (the purity phase, not the menstrual one) and makes counting a duty. Eviction is banned in both directions — he may not expel, she may not leave — except on the "proven" ground, the same evidentiary adjective `[33:30]` uses for the Prophet's wives ("a proven indecency"). The limits formula lands the jeopardy clause, then the statute leaves its own window open: "Allah may afterwards bring about a new situation" — classically read as reconciliation turning back or a pregnancy surfacing. *Source: classical tafsir on 65:1 — verify.*
* **`[65:2]`** "Once they have reached their term, either retain them honorably, or separate from them honorably. And call to witness two just people from among you, and give upright testimony for Allah. By that is exhorted whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day. And whoever fears Allah—He will make a way out for him."
  * **Gloss:** The fork at term's end repeats `[2:229]` ("honorable retention, or setting free kindly"); two just witnesses turn private endings into public record "for Allah." The exhortation clause is the twin of `[2:232]`'s "Thereby is advised whoever among you believes in Allah and the Last Day." And the verse breaks mid-register into promise: the first refrain — a way out.
* **`[65:3]`** "And will provide for him from where he never expected. Whoever relies on Allah— He will suffice him. Allah will accomplish His purpose. Allah has set a measure to all things."
  * **Gloss:** Unlooked-for provision, then the trust-hinge: whoever relies is sufficed — at-Talaq's payoff of 64:13's "in Allah let the believers put their trust" (cf. `[33:3]` "Allah is enough as a trustee," `[8:61]`). The four clipped sentences climb from biography to cosmos: His purpose accomplished, a measure set to all things — why the term can be counted is why anything can.
* **`[65:4]`** "As for those of your women who have reached menopause, if you have any doubts, their term shall be three months—and also for those who have not menstruated. As for those who are pregnant, their term shall be until they have delivered. Whoever fears Allah—He will make things easy for him."
  * **Gloss:** Where the standard count (the three periods of `[2:228]`) cannot run, the law still numbers: three months, three months, delivery. The second refrain turns the counting into ease.
* **`[65:5]`** "This is the ordinance of Allah, which He sent down to you. Whoever fears Allah—He will remit his sins, and will amplify his reward."
  * **Gloss:** The chapter self-identifies: an ordinance "sent down" — revelation-language applied to case-law. "Ordinance(s)" recurs only at `[58:4]` ("These are the ordinances of Allah"), the other Medinan ruling-pass. The third refrain opens the ledger — sins remitted, reward amplified; the "amplify" verb is `[2:245]`'s loan-mathematics, the same grammar 64:17 just used.
* **`[65:6]`** "Allow them to reside where you reside, according to your means, and do not harass them in order to make things difficult for them. If they are pregnant, spend on them until they give birth. And if they nurse your infant, give them their payment. And conduct your relation in amity. But if you disagree, then let another woman nurse him."
  * **Gloss:** Post-split economics: housing at your level with a means-cap; harassment named as coercion-by-discomfort; maintenance through pregnancy; nursing paid as labor (cf. `[2:233]`'s duty-pairing and hired nursing-mothers); amity as the operating rule; and a market fallback when amity fails — "let another woman nurse him."
* **`[65:7]`** "The wealthy shall spend according to his means; and he whose resources are restricted shall spend according to what Allah has given him. Allah never burdens a soul beyond what He has given it. Allah will bring ease after hardship."
  * **Gloss:** Spending indexed to actual endowment, neither above nor below; the capacity formula of `[2:286]` ("Allah does not burden any soul beyond its capacity") restated fiscally; and the sunniest close on any statute in the corpus: "Allah will bring ease after hardship" — the prosaic cousin of `[94:5–6]`.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`'iddah` ("term"):** the waiting-period as a counted object.
  * **Rendering:** "their term" (65:1, 2, 4) with "calculate their term" — the transliteration never appears; the counting verb is visible in the translation.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:228]` "Divorced women shall wait by themselves for three periods"; `[2:231]`/`[2:232]` "when they have reached their term" — the same end-of-term phrase; `[46:15]` "His bearing and weaning takes thirty months" — counting applied to gestation itself.
* **`makhraj` ("way out"):** the exit opened, not engineered.
  * **Rendering:** "He will make a way out for him" (65:2), completed by provision "from where he never expected" (65:3).
  * **Elsewhere:** rg-verified — "way out" occurs only four times corpus-wide, elsewhere only as asked-for or denied: `[4:98]` the weak have "no means to find a way out"; `[40:11]` from the doom, "Is there any way out?"; `[42:46]` "Whomever Allah leaves astray has no way out." 65:2 is the only place it is promised — to the fearer.
* **`taqwa` refrain:** the surah's engine.
  * **Rendering:** four lines, four renderings inside 12 ayahs: "be pious before Allah" (65:1), "fears Allah" (65:2, 4, 5), "beware of Allah" (65:10). *Source: translator's term choice (observable in corpus); rg-verified.*
  * **Elsewhere:** `[57:28]` "Fear Allah, and believe in His Messenger" with double-portion, light, forgiveness — same fear→benefit grammar; 64:16 "be conscious of Allah as much as you can" — capacity-scaled taqwa, the twin of 65:7's capacity-scaled spending.
* **`qadr/burden` (measure and load):**
  * **Rendering:** "Allah has set a measure to all things" (65:3); "Allah never burdens a soul beyond what He has given it" (65:7).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[54:49]` "Everything We created is precisely measured"; `[2:286]`, `[2:233]` — the burden formula in its prayer- and nursing-versions. The divorce law quotes the creation law.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (*Source: classical type-lists — verify*); continuous with the Medinan divorce legislation of 2, 4, 33 — at-Talaq is their procedural restatement with the promise-refrain added.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** a transmitted report attaches the opening to a specific premature divorce (a man who divorced during menstruation; the timing command read as correction) — *Source: classical asbab report (Ibn Umar narratives, Bukhari/Muslim) — verify.* The text names no one: like the Baqarah cluster, the ruling stands general.
