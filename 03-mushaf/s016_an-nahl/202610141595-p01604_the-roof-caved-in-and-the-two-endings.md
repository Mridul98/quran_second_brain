# The roof caved in — and the two endings at the soul's taking (16:26–34)
**UID:** 202610141595
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 16:26–34 (Ruku 4 of 16 in an-Nahl)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[16:26]` – `[16:34]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** History and death do the arguing: the schemers before them had their structures pulled from the foundations and the roof fall in from where they did not perceive; at the Resurrection Allah files the one-question case ("Where are My associates?"), the angels take the wrongdoers' souls while they plead "We did no wrong," and the gates of Hell open for the arrogant; then the same pair is replayed for the pious — they answer "Goodness," enter Gardens of Perpetuity, and are taken in a wholesome state with "Peace be upon you."
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 cashes ruku 3's verdict (16:25 "Evil is what they carry") in this-world example (26) and death-scene drama (27–34); its question-pair (16:24/16:30) is the surah's first explicit sorting of the two receptions.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[16:26]`** "Those before them also schemed, but Allah took their structures from the foundations, and the roof caved in on them. The punishment came at them from where they did not perceive."
  * **Gloss:** Architecture as the image of makr: demolition from below, collapse from above, arrival from an unperceived vector — the surah's three-axis seizure.
* **`[16:27]`** "Then, on the Day of Resurrection, He will disgrace them, and say, “Where are My associates for whose sake you used to dispute?” Those who were given knowledge will say, “Today shame and misery are upon the disbelievers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The partners are not asked — they are absent; the "given knowledge" speak the verdict. The lawsuit is reduced to a roll-call that fails.
* **`[16:28]`** "Those wronging their souls while the angels are taking them away—they will propose peace: “We did no wrong.” Yes you did. Allah is aware of what you used to do.”"
  * **Gloss:** Deathbed plea bargain refused; the corpus line ends with a stray closing quote [sic]. "Wronging their own souls" — the surah's recurring self-injury idiom (cf. 16:33, 16:118).
* **`[16:29]`** "Enter the gates of Hell, to dwell therein forever. Miserable is the residence of the arrogant."
  * **Gloss:** Verdict word returns: arrogant (16:22–23) named as the residents; "gates" matches the Garden's later multiplicity.
* **`[16:30]`** "And it will be said to those who maintained piety, “What has your Lord revealed?” They will say, “Goodness.” To those who do good in this world is goodness, and the Home of the Hereafter is even better. How wonderful is the residence of the pious."
  * **Gloss:** The twin of 16:24's sneer — same question, opposite answer ("Goodness"), and a three-tier goodness: revealed, worldly, otherworldly ("even better").
* **`[16:31]`** "The Gardens of Perpetuity, which they will enter, beneath which rivers flow, where they will have whatever they desire. Thus Allah rewards the pious."
  * **Gloss:** Full-stop eschatology: permanence, rivers, desire-gratification; "thus" closes the argument by reward-grammar.
* **`[16:32]`** "Those who are in a wholesome state when the angels take them—will say, “Peace be upon you; enter Paradise, for what you used to do.”"
  * **Gloss:** The good death (tayyib) mirrored against 16:28's violent taking; the corpus line's subject slips ("will say" — [sic]). The greeting the arrogant begged (salm, 16:28) is spoken to the pious in full.
* **`[16:33]`** "Are they but waiting for the angels to come to them, or for the command of your Lord to arrive? Those before them did likewise. Allah did not wrong them, but they used to wrong their own souls."
  * **Gloss:** The opening verse's "command" (16:1) named again as what is arriving; the theodicy line: zero divine wrong, total self-wrong.
* **`[16:34]`** "So the evils of their deeds assailed them, and what they used to ridicule engulfed them."
  * **Gloss:** Two verbs of encirclement — the ridiculed threat (16:1 "do not rush it") now surrounds the mocker.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`the angels taking them away (tawaffi)`** `[16:28`, `16:32]`:
  * **Rendering:** "while the angels are taking them away" / "when the angels take them."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:97]` "While the angels are removing the souls of those who have wronged themselves... 'We were oppressed in the land'" — same scene with its excuse-line; `[39:42]` "Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that have not died during their sleep"; `[32:11]` the angel of death "will reclaim you."
* **`propose peace (salma)`** `[16:28]`:
  * **Rendering:** "they will propose peace" — they attempt to buy terms at the moment of seizure.
  * **Elsewhere:** the same root answers them only as the greeting of the saved: `[16:32]` "Peace be upon you"; `[25:75]` "will be greeted therein with greetings and peace" — the saved are spoken to in the very register (salam) the doomed merely "propose" (salma) at seizure. *Source: observable corpus pairing; the salma/salam wordplay noted in classical tafsir — verify.*
* **`wronging their own souls`** `[16:28`, `16:33`, `16:118]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:97]` (above); `[11:101]` "We did not wrong them, but they wronged themselves" — verbatim twin of 16:33's theodicy line, with 16:33's own "gods... availed them nothing" echoed at `[11:101]`'s second sentence; `[7:23]` "Our Lord, we have done wrong to ourselves" — Adam's pair naming the self-wrong that becomes tawba, against 16:28's denial of it.
* **`Gardens of Perpetuity (janat 'adn)`** `[16:31]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:76]` "The Gardens of Perpetuity... That is the reward for him who purifies himself" — and the same phrase in ar-Ra'd's covenant-keepers verse `[13:23]` "Everlasting Gardens."
