# One God, and the "legends of the ancients" retort (16:22–25)
**UID:** 202610141594
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 16:22–25 (Ruku 3 of 16 in an-Nahl)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[16:22]` – `[16:25]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The inventory's conclusion is a creed sentence — "Your God is one God" — followed by its pathology (denial- hearts, arrogance) and its stock reply ("Legends of the ancients"); the ruku ends with a cargo image answering 16:7's pack-animals: they will carry their own loads complete, plus some of the loads of those they misguided — "Evil is what they carry."
* **Placement:** The hinge between the provision-cosmos (ruku 1–2) and the schemers' fate (ruku 4): tawhid stated, its rejection profiled, its rejection priced.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[16:22]`** "Your God is one God. As for those who do not believe in the Hereafter, their hearts are in denial, and they are arrogant."
  * **Gloss:** One sentence of doctrine, one of symptom: disbelief in the Hereafter is diagnosed as a heart-state (inkar) plus posture (takabbur) — not an intellectual position.
* **`[16:23]`** "Without a doubt, Allah knows what they conceal and what they reveal. He does not like the arrogant."
  * **Gloss:** The 16:19 omniscience-clause restated for this pair; the moral verdict is aesthetic: Allah "does not like" (la yuhibb) the arrogant.
* **`[16:24]`** "And when it is said to them, “What has your Lord sent down?” They say, “Legends of the ancients.”"
  * **Gloss:** The recurring interrogation of the Meccan surahs; the answer is asatir al-awwalin — the same dismissal recorded at `[25:5]`, `[23:83]`, `[27:68]`, `[46:17]`, `[83:13]`.
* **`[16:25]`** "So let them carry their loads complete on the Day of Resurrection, and some of the loads of those they misguided without knowledge. Evil is what they carry."
  * **Gloss:** Pack-animals were God's mercy (16:7); the leaders of misguidance become beasts of burden for their own sin plus a share of their followers' — a measured "some," and the verdict "Evil is what they carry" (bi'sa).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`one God`** `[16:22]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Your God is one God" — the same sentence-form as `[2:163]` "Your God is one God. There is no god but He, the Benevolent, the Compassionate."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:4]` "Your God is indeed One"; `[6:19]` "He is but One God" — the phrase clusters where the corpus wants an unambiguous unity confession.
* **`asatir (legends/tales of the ancients)`** `[16:24]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Legends of the ancients"; elsewhere Itani uses "Tales of the ancients" (`[25:5]`, `[46:17]`) for the same word — an observable rendering drift, same slur.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:5]` adds the fabrication mechanics ("he wrote them down; they are dictated to him morning and evening"); `[23:83]`/`[27:68]` attach it to the resurrection promise; `[83:13]` makes it the reflex "when Our revelations are recited."
* **`loads (vizr/haml)`** `[16:25]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[29:13]` "They will carry their own loads, and other loads with their own. And they will be questioned on the Day of Resurrection concerning what they used to fabricate" — the twin verse; `[6:31]` "And they will carry their burdens on their backs—evil is what they carry" — the identical bi'sa verdict; the shared-guilt idea is bounded by `[6:164]` "no soul bears the burdens of another": the leader carries a share for the misguidance-role, not the follower's heart. *Source: the qualification is the standard classical resolution of 16:25/29:13 with 6:164 — agent inference, verify.*
* **`arrogant (mutakabbir)`** `[16:22–23`, `16:29]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:29]` "Miserable is the residence of the arrogant" closes the next ruku on the same word — the surah's own definition of the hell-door; cf. `[40:56]` "there is nothing in their hearts but the feeling of greatness."

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:163]` — identical creed-sentence in the Medinan longest surah: the Meccan kernel the community will keep reciting.
* **Parallel:** `[29:13]` — the loads-twin (see Key Terms).
* **Parallel:** `[25:5]` — the fullest version of the asatir accusation (dictation theory), directly relevant to the kalam dossier.
* **Within surah:** the question "What has your Lord sent down/revealed?" is a built-in pair: 16:24 (the arrogant's sneer) vs. `[16:30]` (the pious' answer "Goodness") — the surah grades the same question by the two receptions; "their hearts are in denial" (16:22) pre-loads `[16:23]`'s conceal/reveal omniscience and `[16:108]`'s sealed hearts.
