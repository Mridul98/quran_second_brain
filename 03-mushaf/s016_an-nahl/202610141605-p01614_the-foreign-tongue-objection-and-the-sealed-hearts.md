# "A human teaches him" — the foreign tongue, the sealed hearts, and the emigrants' return (16:101–110)
**UID:** 202610141605
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 16:101–110 (Ruku 14 of 16 in an-Nahl)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[16:101]` – `[16:110]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The revelation-integrity ruku: verse-substitution draws the "impostor" charge (101), answered by the Holy Spirit's truthful descent (102) and the corpus's most quotable linguistic rebuttal — "the tongue of him they allude to is foreign, while this is a clear Arabic tongue" (103); then the fabrication-logic inverted (only the faithless fabricate, 105); the apostasy clause with its compulsion exception (106–109: wrath, sealing, heedlessness, loss); and the rescue parenthesis for the persecuted emigrants who struggled and persevered — "your Lord thereafter is Forgiving and Merciful" (110).
* **Placement:** The surah's kalam-forensic center: it answers the attack on revelation's authorship (101–105) before diagnosing the heart-disease that makes the attack persuasive (106–109), then re-arms the community's honorific category (the emigrants, echoing 16:41–42).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[16:101]`** "When We substitute a verse in place of another verse—and Allah knows best what He reveals—they say, “You are an impostor.” But most of them do not know."
  * **Gloss:** Tabdil (substitution) as the trigger-charge; the parenthesis asserts divine omniscience over the disclosure-policy; the accusation "You are an impostor" is the audience's standing verdict on the messenger (cf. 2:87's plural form).
* **`[16:102]`** "Say, “The Holy Spirit has brought it down from your Lord, truthfully, in order to stabilize those who believe, and as guidance and good news for those who submit.”"
  * **Gloss:** The delivery-chain answer: Ruh al-Qudus, from the Lord, bi-l-haqq, with three functions — stabilization (stability of the believer), guidance, glad-tidings.
* **`[16:103]`** "We are well aware that they say, “It is a human being who is teaching him.” But the tongue of him they allude to is foreign, while this is a clear Arabic tongue."
  * **Gloss:** The plagiarism claim and the philological rebuttal: the alleged human teacher's lisan is a'jam (non-Arab/foreign); the Quran's is lisan 'arabi mubin — the surah's own text-quality argument.
* **`[16:104]`** "Those who do not believe in Allah’s revelations—Allah will not guide them, and for them is a painful punishment."
  * **Gloss:** Guidance foreclosed by refusal — the 16:37 principle re-asserted at the level of individual destiny.
* **`[16:105]`** "It is those who do not believe in Allah’s revelations who fabricate falsehood. These are the liars."
  * **Gloss:** The imposture charge turned back: fabrication (iftira') belongs to the faithless — an explicit authorship-forensics verse.
* **`[16:106]`** "Whoever renounces faith in Allah after having believed—except for someone who is compelled, while his heart rests securely in faith—but whoever willingly opens up his heart to disbelief—upon them falls wrath from Allah, and for them is a tremendous torment."
  * **Gloss:** The apostasy statute with its interiority-clause: compulsion (ikrah) exempts where the heart stays at rest in faith; the sin is opening the chest to kufr willingly.
* **`[16:107]`** "That is because they have preferred the worldly life to the Hereafter, and because Allah does not guide the people who refuse."
  * **Gloss:** The diagnosis: preference (istithar) — the same economic verb family as 16:95's trade; refusal as a settled posture.
* **`[16:108]`** "It is they whom Allah has sealed their hearts, and their hearing, and their sight. It is they who are the heedless."
  * **Gloss:** The sealing-triad of 2:7 re-applied; the ghafilun label — heedlessness as the final self-description.
* **`[16:109]`** "There is no doubt that in the Hereafter they will be the losers."
  * **Gloss:** The loser-verdict formula; `[39:63]` "those who blaspheme against the revelations of Allah—it is they who are the losers" is the Zumar twin in the same adjacent-surah idiom.
* **`[16:110]`** "But then your Lord—for those who emigrated after being persecuted, then struggled and persevered—your Lord thereafter is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The parenthesis that saves the frightened: hijra→persecution→struggle→sabr; "thereafter" (min ba'd) is repeated twice — divine character as the last word for the community that had known apostasy-pressure.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`substitute / tabdil`** `[16:101]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:106]` "We never nullify a verse, nor cause it to be forgotten, unless We bring one better than it, or similar to it" — the doctrine-side twin (nasikh-mansukh vocabulary); `[10:15]` "Bring a Quran other than this, or change it" — the demand-side twin. *Source: classical abrogation-doctrine mapping — verify print.*
* **`foreign / clear Arabic (a'jam / 'arabi mubin)`** `[16:103]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:195]` "In a clear Arabic tongue" (verbatim formula in the Ash-Shu'ara' sequence: 26:193 "The Honest Spirit came down with it" — the same delivery-line as 16:102); `[43:3]` "We made it an Arabic Quran, so that you may understand"; `[39:28]` "An Arabic Quran, without any defect"; `[41:44]` "Had We made it a Quran in a foreign language, they would have said, 'If only its verses were made clear.' Non-Arabic and an Arab?" — the same objection-and-rebuttal pair staged rhetorically.
* **`compelled (ukriha)`** `[16:106]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:256]` "There shall be no compulsion in religion" — the creed-side law; `[2:173]`/`[6:145]` "compelled by necessity" — the dietary-side exception (same grammar of exemption-for-coerced-bodies, cf. 16:115); the classical report ties 16:106 to Ammar ibn Yasir tortured while his heart "rested in faith". *Source: transmitted sirah/tafsir report (Ibn Ishaq-type) — agent labeling, verify.*
* **`sealed (khatama)`** `[16:108]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:7]` "Allah has set a seal on their hearts and on their hearing, and over their vision is a veil" — verbatim-triad twin; `[4:155]` "Allah has sealed them for their disbelief, so they do not believe, except for a few"; `[6:46]` "set a seal on your hearts" — the sealing is always the consequence of prior refusal (cf. 16:107).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan; the apostasy-clause (106–110) is classically attached to the pressure on early believers (Ammar-type torture reports; 'Ubaidullah-type defection reports vary) — the exception-clause (106b) is the surah's pastoral valve. *Source: transmitted occasion-reports in classical commentaries — agent labeling; reports conflict; verify.*
* **Note on 16:103:** the alleged human teacher is unnamed in the corpus ("him they allude to"); tafsir reports name a non-Arab (Roman/Egyptian) craftsman or a freed slave — the "sheep-seller" detail some readers carry from popular retellings does not appear in the corpus wording here. *Source: transmitted identifications differ (Ibn 'Abbas/Mujahid-type reports) — agent labeling, verify in print; treat the "foreign tongue" argument as the corpus-fact.*
