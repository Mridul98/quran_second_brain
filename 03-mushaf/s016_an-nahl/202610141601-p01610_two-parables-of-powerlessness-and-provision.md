# Unequal livelihood, and two men who cannot equal the giver (16:71–76)
**UID:** 202610141601
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 16:71–76 (Ruku 10 of 16 in an-Nahl)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[16:71]` – `[16:76]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** An argument from household economics: Allah favors some with more livelihood than others, yet none of the favored would hand their property to their own servants as full partners (71) — so why treat His creatures as partners in His dominion? The family inventory (mates, children, grandchildren, good things, 72) makes idols, who possess no provision at all (73), a category error; two mathal close it — the bonded slave who owns nothing vs. the man given plentiful provision who gives secretly and openly (75), and the mute burden vs. the man who commands justice on a straight path (76).
* **Placement:** The surah's parable-chapter on power and provision: ruku 9's gifts (milk, honey) become ruku 10's question — who actually holds and circulates wealth — and its answer pre-loads the covenant block's economics (16:95–97).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[16:71]`** "Allah has favored some of you over others in livelihood. Those who are favored would not give their properties to their servants, to the extent of making them partners in it. Will they then renounce Allah’s blessings?"
  * **Gloss:** Inequality of rizq is a fact; the analogy bites: you will not equate yourself with your own dependants in your wealth — then do not equate the created with the Creator in His.
* **`[16:72]`** "Allah has given you mates from among yourselves; and has produced for you, from your mates, children and grandchildren; and has provided you with good things. Will they then believe in falsehood, and refuse Allah’s favors?"
  * **Gloss:** The kinship-and-table inventory (zawj, banin, banun, rizq min al-tayyib) — the daily grain of gratitude — then the double charge: belief in falsehood (batil) and refusal (kufr) of favors.
* **`[16:73]`** "And yet they serve besides Allah what possesses no provisions for them in the heavens, nor on earth, nor are they capable."
  * **Gloss:** Idols fail the wealth-test: no rizq, no ownership, no power (maqidin) — worship addressed to the un-creditable.
* **`[16:74]`** "So do not cite the examples for Allah. Allah knows, and you do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The mathal discipline: comparisons to Allah are forbidden — and the very next two verses are parables about humans, not about God. The line polices analogy itself.
* **`[16:75]`** "Allah cites the example of a bonded slave, who has no power over anything; and someone to whom We have given plentiful provision, from which he gives secretly and openly. Are they equal in comparison? All praise belongs to Allah, but most of them do not know."
  * **Gloss:** Parable one (wealth): the owned vs. the giver — equality collapses; the giver's two channels (secret/open) are the infaq signature; "All praise belongs to Allah" re-grounds 1:2.
* **`[16:76]`** "And Allah cites the example of two men: one of them dumb, unable to do anything, and is a burden on his master; whichever way he directs him, he achieves nothing good. Is he equal to him who commands justice, and is on a straight path?"
  * **Gloss:** Parable two (speech-and-direction): the mute deadweight vs. the just-guide — the idol-parable in human dress, ending on the straight-path idiom.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`livelihood / provision (rizq)`** `[16:71–73]`:
  * **Rendering:** "favored some of you over others in livelihood"; "possesses no provisions."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[43:32]` "It is We who have allocated their livelihood in this life, and We elevated some of them in rank above others" — divine allocation, not merit or accident; `[30:28]` "do you make your servants full partners in the wealth We have given you?" — the same household-analogy verse in Ar-Rum.
* **`partners (shuraka) in wealth`** `[16:71`, cf. `16:75–76]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:29]` "Allah cites the example of a man shared by partners at odds, and a man belonging exclusively to one man. Are they equal in status?" — the Zumar twin of this parable-family (plural-master vs. single-master); `[30:28]` (as above). The three verses are the corpus's economic parable cluster.
* **`gives secretly and openly`** `[16:75]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:274]` "Those who spend their wealth by night and day, privately and publicly"; `[13:22]` "spend from Our provisions to them, secretly and openly" — the covenant-keepers' channel-list; cf. [p01303](../s013_ar-ra-d/202610141564-p01303_the-covenant-keepers-and-the-covenant-breakers.md).
* **`cite examples for Allah (mathal)`** `[16:74–76]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:60]` "Allah sets the Highest Example"; `[16:112]` "Allah cites the example of a town" — an-Nahl is the surah most dense in the masal verb; 16:74 bans the example-claim about God's own being while 16:75–76 show the licensed use (parables about the human condition). *Source: classical distinction between tamsil al-mukhayal (similitude) and tanzih — agent inference, verify.*
