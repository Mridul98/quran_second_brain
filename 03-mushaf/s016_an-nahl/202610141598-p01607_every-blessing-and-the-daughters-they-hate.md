# Every blessing from Allah — and the daughters they hate (16:51–60)
**UID:** 202610141598
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 16:51–60 (Ruku 7 of 16 in an-Nahl)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[16:51]` – `[16:60]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The psychology of ingratitude is dissected: one God, sole owner, sole object of fear (51–52); blessing→harm→groaning→lifting→partnering→ingratitude, a six-beat cycle (53–55); then the allocation of the unknown (56) reaches its ugliest form — attributing daughters to Allah while keeping for themselves what they desire (57), dramatized in the shadow-play of a man receiving the news of a girl infant (58–59), and labeled: the disbelievers "set a bad example," while Allah "sets the Highest Example" (60).
* **Placement:** The ruku where the surah's gratitude-audit turns into a gender-and-projection case study: the same minds that make the un-creators gods (16:20) also assign God the offspring they despise.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[16:51]`** "Allah has said: “Do not take two gods; He is only One God; so fear only Me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The confession compressed to its practical verb: fear is singular because ownership is singular.
* **`[16:52]`** "To Him belongs everything in the heavens and the earth; and to Him obedience is due always. Do you, then, fear anyone other than Allah?"
  * **Gloss:** Ownership-argument for exclusive devotion — qunut ("obedience is due always") as the owed posture.
* **`[16:53]`** "Whatever blessing you have is from Allah. And when harm touches you, it is to Him that you groan."
  * **Gloss:** The cycle's beats 1–2: all ni'ma is His; distress drives even the polytheist back to Him alone (jarthama — the cry of distress).
* **`[16:54]`** "But when He lifts the harm from you, some of you associate others with their Lord."
  * **Gloss:** Beat 3–4: relief is repaid with shirk — the surah's core ingratitude mechanism (cf. 30:33's verbatim twin).
* **`[16:55]`** "To show ingratitude for what We have given them. Enjoy yourselves. You will soon know."
  * **Gloss:** Beat 5: kufr al-ni'ma by name; then the sarcastic permission — "Enjoy yourselves. You will soon know" — a deferred-verdict clause.
* **`[16:56]`** "And they allocate, to something they do not know, a share of what We have provided for them. By Allah, you will be questioned about what you have been inventing."
  * **Gloss:** The idol-share of provision (the 6:136 allotment rite); the audit verb: you will be questioned about your inventions.
* **`[16:57]`** "And they attribute to Allah daughters—exalted is He—and for themselves what they desire."
  * **Gloss:** The projection exposed: to God the least-preferred gender; to themselves what they choose; the tanzih interjection ("exalted is He") is the surah's reflex.
* **`[16:58]`** "And when one of them is given news of a female infant, his face darkens, and he chokes with grief."
  * **Gloss:** The somatic close-up: blackened face, suppressed rage (kazim) — the shame is physical before it is argued.
* **`[16:59]`** "He hides from the people because of the bad news given to him. Shall he keep it in humiliation, or bury it in the dust? Evil is the decision they make."
  * **Gloss:** The two options of the wa'd-era — degrade her, or kill her; the verdict lands on the decision, not the news.
* **`[16:60]`** "Those who do not believe in the Hereafter set a bad example, while Allah sets the Highest Example. He is the Mighty, the Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's example-theory in one line: their mathal al-suu (the daughters-attribution) vs. His mathal al-a'la — the comparison between how they speak of God and how He speaks of Himself.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`groan (yarthamuna)`** `[16:53]`:
  * **Rendering:** "it is to Him that you groan" — the cry under affliction.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[30:33]` "When affliction touches the people, they call on their Lord, turning to Him in repentance. But then, when He gives them a taste of His mercy, some of them attribute partners to their Lord" — the identical cycle in Yunus's words; the two surahs share this diagnostic.
* **`daughters / female infant (bint, untha, maulud)`** `[16:57–59]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:149]` "Ask them, ‘Are the daughters for your Lord, while for them the sons?’"; `[43:16]` "Or has He chosen for Himself daughters from what He creates, and favored you with sons?"; `[6:100]` "they invented for Him sons and daughters, without any knowledge"; `[81:8–9]` "When the girl, buried alive, is asked: For what crime was she killed?" — the live-burial the 16:59 option answers to; `[16:58]`'s grief at the birth is the same world 81 indicts.
* **`the Highest Example (al-mathal al-a'la)`** `[16:60]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Allah sets the Highest Example."
  * **Elsewhere:** unique wording in this corpus (`rg "Highest Example"` — only 16:60); the mathal-family is this surah's native tongue (16:75–76, 112). *Source: the phrase's classical use as God's self-description in creation-theology — agent inference, verify.*
* **`ingratitude (kufr of blessing)`** `[16:55`, `cf. 16:83]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[14:7]` "If you give thanks, I will grant you increase; but if you are ungrateful, My punishment is severe" — the law behind the cycle; `[16:83]` "They recognize Allah's blessing, but then deny it" — the surah's own summary verdict.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan; the wa'd (female-infant) practice is pre-Islamic Arabian, and the passage reads as a portrait of the audience, not a law-report. *Source: classical historical notices + the verses' own present-tense phrasing — verify.*
