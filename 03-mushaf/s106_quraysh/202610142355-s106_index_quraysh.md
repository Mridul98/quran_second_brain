## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Meccan per the classical type-lists; the corpus carries no classification data (header fields only — rg-verified). Revelation order ≈ 21 in the transmitted nuzul tables (list-dependent) — *transmitted tables — verify*.
* **Size:** 4 ayahs, 1 ruku (ruku_map global 548 — verified). Four lines, one sentence in Arabic (the corpus renders 106:1–2 as a favor-fragment completed by 106:3–4's command and reasons).
* **Title:** Quraysh — the corpus header "QURAISH" tracks `[106:1]` "For the security of Quraish." — among the very few surahs whose title-word is a named human group attested inside the book (its nearest counterpart: Saba/Sheba in 34, which k-shukr reads as the ingratitude-case; the word Quraish itself occurs only inside s106's own file — rg-verified; cf. 105 whose "people" are named only by their hardware).
* **Central theme:** A favor-audit with a one-verb response. Two blessings are recited — the protected caravan-cycle (winter and summer journeys, vv1–2) and the fed-and-feared-nothing condition (v4) — and the single imperative between them is the audit's answer: "Let them worship the Lord of this House" (v3). The House whose Lord ran the Elephant-affair next door is now the address of gratitude: security is not self-generated; it is attributed, and attribution is worship.
* **Reading hint:** 105/106 are the build's clearest structural twins: destruction averted at the House → provision secured under the House's Lord. Some early recensions transmitted the two as one surah without an intervening basmala (classical reports — verify); this edition prints its standard basmala header before each (observable in corpus), so each counts separately here per the 6236/556 framework.

## 3. Structural Arc
* **vv1–2 — the recital (movement):** "For the security of Quraish. / Their security during winter and summer journeys." — the double "security" (Itani renders the one Arabic favor-word īlāf twice: a possessed condition and a traveled one — observable repetition).
* **v3 — the response (pivot):** "Let them worship the Lord of this House." — the hortative "let them" (fa-yaʿbudū) is the surah's only imperative move; the House gets the demonstrative "this" — the same House 105's expedition failed to reach (adjacency reading; *Source: agent inference — verify*).
* **v4 — the reattribution (reason):** "Who has fed them against hunger, and has secured them against fear." — the two nouns of the recital (journeys = livelihood; security = safety) return as the Lord's two acts: feeding and fear-removal. Frame closes; nothing belongs to the tribe itself.
* **Bracket position:** ruku 548; the favor-pole of the 104–108 cluster — 105's "how your Lord dealt with" (defense) becomes 106's "the Lord of this House… has fed… has secured" (provision) (*Source: structural observation on the pair's renderings*).
* **Verse-length symmetry note:** 106:1–2 and 106:3–4 pair as recital-command / command-reason — the favor is mentioned twice (security, provision-safety) and the response once; the surah deliberately weighs blessings heavier than the obligation they generate. (*Source: structural observation on corpus renderings — verify.*)

## 5b. Batch-Return Verification Flags (this index)
* **Hint renderings corrected (all rg-verified against `corpus/s106_quraysh.md`):** "winter/summer journey pact" → "**For the security of Quraish. / Their security during winter and summer journeys.**" — the corpus word is "security" (twice), not "pact"; the classical reading of īlāf as a traded pact/contract (Quraysh's caravan treaties) is commentary, not translation (*Source: classical lexicon/reports on 106:1 — verify before preaching the treaty-reading*). "this Lord of the House" → "**the Lord of this House**" (this modifies House). "fed them from hunger and secured them from fear" → "**Who has fed them against hunger, and has secured them against fear.**" — "against," not "from."
* **Formula twin verified:** `[27:91]` "I was commanded to worship the Lord of this town, who has sanctified it" — the one other "Lord of this [sanctuary-word]" construction in the corpus (rg-verified): 106:3's imperative shares the shaper's formula (worship tied to a localized sacred site, with sanctification as the ground).
* **Adjacency/structural twin with 105 — verified as design, not data:** the mushaf sequence 105→106 and the demonstrative "this House" (106:3) make the pair a single argument (defense-then-provision); no verse of 106 restates 105's event. Classical reports of joint recension (no separate basmala, counted as one surah in some codices) are outside the corpus — *Source: transmitted reports — verify*; this edition's header basmalas are observable either way (see fleeting note on basmala numbering).
* **Unique-name audit:** "Quraish" occurs only inside s106's own file (106:1 + its header — rg-verified); "winter" 1 site (106:2 only); the "journeys" plural outside 106 occurs once — `[34:19]` (all rg-verified) — the two journey-sites are the grateful/ingrate pair cited under k-shukr above.
* **"This House" is unique (rg-verified):** every other House-reference in the corpus runs "the House"/"My House"/"the Sacred House"/"the Kaabah" (2:125, 2:127, 3:97, 5:97, 22:26, 14:37) — 106:3's demonstrative "this" is the one time the text points at the House as present-to-the-eye, which is exactly what the 105 adjacency loads (the House the birds defended).
* **"Against" rendering note (edition-observation):** 106:4 runs "fed them **against** hunger… secured them **against** fear" — the unusual "against" (for protective deliverance) is Itani's signature here (rg-verified); do not "correct" the quote to the more common "from hunger/from fear" wording of other translations.
