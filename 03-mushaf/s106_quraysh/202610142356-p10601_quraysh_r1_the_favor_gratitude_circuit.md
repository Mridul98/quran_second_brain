# The Favor–Gratitude Circuit: Caravans, House, Food, Safety (106:1–4)
**UID:** 202610142356
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 106:1–4 (Ruku 1 of 1 in Quraysh)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[106:1]` – `[106:4]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Four lines run the book's oldest circuit — favor recited, response commanded, favor re-attributed: Quraysh's celebrated winter/summer caravan security and its fed, fearless condition are handed over to "the Lord of this House," and the only adequate answer to a protected life is worship of the Protector. The surah is k-shukr's niʿma→shukr program in miniature — and 105 next door is its negative proof.
* **Placement:** Ruku 548 of 556; the favor-pole of the 104–108 cluster. As 105 shows the House's Lord *defending* it, 106 shows him *feeding and securing* its neighborhood — the pair is one argument in two movements: do not trade security for amnesia.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[106:1]`** "For the security of Quraish."
  * **Gloss:** A fragment, not a sentence — "for/because of the īlāf (security, bonded habit) of Quraysh": the surah begins mid-gratitude, naming the tribe (the book's one direct tribal self-designation in this form — nearest proper-name-of-a-people is "Sheba's homeland" `[34:15]` — rg-verified) as the beneficiary of a condition it experiences as normal.
* **`[106:2]`** "Their security during winter and summer journeys."
  * **Gloss:** The same favor-word repeated with its itinerary: the two caravan seasons (corpus-unique pairing — "winter" appears nowhere else, rg-verified) — trading journeys that, by contemporary norms, should have been fatal: banditry, hunger, exposure. Security is named twice because it is the whole platform the command will stand on.
* **`[106:3]`** "Let them worship the Lord of this House."
  * **Gloss:** The pivot and only imperative: the response to carried goods is carried *up* — worship (ʿibādah, "yaʿbudū") directed not to the House but to its Lord, localized by the demonstrative "this" — the sanctuary in front of their eyes (and, per the adjacency, the one 105's expedition failed to touch).
* **`[106:4]`** "Who has fed them against hunger, and has secured them against fear."
  * **Gloss:** The reasons clause closes the circuit: two verbs, two negatives removed — bread from hunger, safety from dread. The tribe's two boast-conditions (full bellies, walkable roads) are re-attributed in the same breath that asked for worship: gratitude here is not a feeling but a re-addressing of causation.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`īlāf` (rendered "security" ×2)**: bonded habit, confederated safety — here the caravan-secure condition itself.
  * **Rendering:** "the security of Quraish… their security during…" — Itani's choice, the word doubled in one Arabic sentence (observable). Classical lexicon/reports read the favor as the *pact-system* of trade journeys (the hint's "pact") — *Source: classical commentary on 106:1–2 — verify before preaching the treaty-reading as translation.*
  * **Elsewhere:** the security cluster is the sanctuary strand, all rg-verified: `[2:125]` "a sanctuary", `[3:97]` "Whoever enters it attains security", `[5:97]` "a sanctuary for the people", `[28:57]` "Safe Sanctuary", `[29:67]` "Secure Sanctuary", `[95:3]` "this safe land" — 106:1–2 is the tribal-scale echo of a doctrine the corpus states at city- and oath-scale.
* **`riḥlat` (journeys)**: the winter/summer caravan-cycle.
  * **Rendering:** "winter and summer journeys" (106:2).
  * **Elsewhere:** journeys-as-divine-favor-and-audit at `[34:19]` — Saba's people ask "lengthen the distances of our journeys" and are scattered for ingratitude (rg-verified; k-shukr cites this verse). The two journey-sites in the corpus form the pair: 106 = journeys under a commanded gratitude; 34:19 = journeys destroyed by a refused one (*Source: agent inference on the two-site distribution — verify*).
* **`Rabb hādhā al-bayt` (Lord of this House)**: the worship-address.
  * **Rendering:** "the Lord of this House" (106:3).
  * **Elsewhere:** the formula-twin `[27:91]` "I was commanded to worship the Lord of this town, who has sanctified it, and to Whom everything belongs" (rg-verified — the only other "Lord of this [sanctuary-word]" construction); the House dossier: `[2:125]` "We made the House a focal point for the people", `[2:127]` Abraham "raises the foundations of the House", `[22:26]` "purify My House", `[3:97]` "Pilgrimage to the House is a duty", `[5:97]` "the Kaabah, the Sacred House", `[14:37]` "by Your Sacred House" — 106:3's "this House" sits the command on covenant ground.
* **`aṭʿama/t-jaʿala… ʿamnan` (fed… secured)**: the two acts of v4.
  * **Rendering:** "has fed them against hunger, and has secured them against fear."
  * **Elsewhere:** Abraham's prayer answered in these very two items — `[2:126]` "make this a peaceful land, and provide its people with fruits", `[14:37]` "provide them with fruits, that they may be thankful", `[28:57]` "all kinds of fruits, as provision from Ourselves" — provision-and-peace as the answered-prayer bundle (rg-verified; *Source: agent inference that 106:4 markets 14:37's prayer — verify*). Feeding-the-hungered is also k-infraq's floor (`[93:8]` "found you in need, and enriched you"; `[76:8]` feed "the poor, and the orphan").

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan per classical type-lists (corpus carries none — rg-verified); order ≈ 21 — *transmitted tables — verify*. The address "Quraysh" makes the audience a named tribe — the surah speaks *to* the beneficiaries, not about strangers (observable: "Quraysh" is named in third person while the command (106:3 "Let them worship") also addresses them in third person — an audit delivered to its subjects about themselves).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the two-season itinerary (Yemen in winter, Syria/Sham in summer) and Quraysh's confederated safe-conduct arrangements come from the classical commentaries and sira apparatus — no schedule, route, or treaty is stated in the text. *Source: classical transmitted reports — verify before teaching the itinerary as scripture.*
