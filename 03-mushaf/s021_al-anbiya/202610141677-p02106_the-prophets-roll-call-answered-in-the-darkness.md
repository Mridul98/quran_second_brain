# The prophets' roll-call — answered in the darkness (21:76–93)
**UID:** 202610141677
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 21:76–93 (Ruku 6 of 7 in al-Anbiya)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[21:76]` – `[21:93]` (18 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The roll-call proper, in eleven "And..." units: Noah's ancient call answered; David and Solomon given judgment, wisdom, mountains and birds, shields, wind, and curbed devils; Job answered at the superlative of mercy; Ishmael, Enoch and Ezekiel inscribed among the steadfast; Jonah's darkness-du'a — "There is no god but You! Glory to You! I was one of the wrongdoers!" — answered with the surah's own theorem "Thus We save the faithful"; Zechariah's secluded call answered with John; the virgin guarded, breathed upon, made a sign with her son — and then the ecclesiology snap: "This community of yours is one community... But they splintered."
* **Placement:** Ruku 6 of 7; the proof-of-concept for `[21:9]` ("We saved them together with whomever We willed") and the reservoir the closing ruku draws on (Gog and Magog; the fuel verdict; the return of 21:93).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[21:76]`** "And Noah, when he called before. So We answered him, and delivered him and his family from the great disaster."
  * **Gloss:** "When he called before" — before everything; the call is counted as the whole story; cf. `[37:75]` "And Noah called out to Us, and We are the Best of responders."
* **`[21:77]`** "And We supported him against the people who rejected Our signs. They were an evil people, so We drowned them all."
  * **Gloss:** The answer has two clauses — support and drowning. Task anchor corrected: 21:77 says "We drowned them all"; the "no dweller" wording is Noah's own imprecation at `[71:26]` ("do not leave of the unbelievers a single dweller on earth") — its execution is here, not its text.
* **`[21:78]`** "And David and Solomon, when they gave judgment in the case of the field, when some people’s sheep wandered therein by night; and We were witnesses to their judgment."
  * **Gloss:** The only telling of the sheep-in-the-field case in the corpus — no parallel narrative verse; God's witnessing frames the judgment as performed under observation.
* **`[21:79]`** "And so We made Solomon understand it, and to each We gave wisdom and knowledge. And We subjected the mountains along with David to sing Our praises, and the birds as well—surely We did."
  * **Gloss:** Understanding given to one, wisdom to both; the liturgy of nature — twins at `[34:10]` ("O mountains, and birds: echo with him") and `[38:19]`; cf. `[27:15]`.
* **`[21:80]`** "And We taught him the making of shields for you, to protect you from your violence. Are you, then, appreciative?"
  * **Gloss:** Technology as mercy (↔ `[34:11]` "Make coats of armor"); the ruku's shukr-check — "Are you, then, appreciative?" is the gratitude-audit question of this surah.
* **`[21:81]`** "And to Solomon the stormy wind, blowing at His command towards the land that We have blessed. We are aware of everything."
  * **Gloss:** The wind's route re-anchors the blessed land of `[21:71]`; cf. `[34:12]` for the month-journey wind.
* **`[21:82]`** "And of the devils were some that dived for him, and performed other, lesser tasks. But We kept them restrained." — **Gloss:** the deep-sea laborers; the closing clause ("We kept them restrained") is the safety-pin in Solomon's legend; cf. `[34:12]` sprites under command.
* **`[21:83]`** "And Job, when he cried out to his Lord: “Great harm has afflicted me, and you are the Most Merciful of the merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The complaint plus the superlative; the same du'a-formula at `[12:92]` and `[7:151]` ("Most Merciful of the merciful"); `[38:41]` is the variant telling ("Satan has afflicted me with hardship and pain").
* **`[21:84]`** "So We answered him, lifted his suffering, and restored his family to him, and their like with them—a mercy from Us, and a reminder for the worshipers." — **Gloss:** restoration doubled; "a reminder for the worshipers" ↔ `[21:48]` "a reminder for the righteous" — the reminder has an address-list.
* **`[21:85]`** "And Ishmael, and Enoch, and Ezekiel; each was one of the steadfast."
  * **Gloss:** Three names in one breath; "the steadfast" (sabr) as their whole biography — cf. `[38:48]` "all are among the outstanding" (rendering drift) and the wage-promise `[39:10]` "The steadfast will be paid their wages in full, without reckoning."
* **`[21:86]`** "And We admitted them into Our mercy. They were among the righteous." — **Gloss:** the 21:75 clause generalized to a trio; "admitted into mercy" + "the righteous" — the ruku's refrain pair.
* **`[21:87]`** "And Jonah, when he stormed out in fury, thinking We had no power over him. But then He cried out in the darkness, “There is no god but You! Glory to You! I was one of the wrongdoers!”"
  * **Gloss:** The su'a — storming out — then the darkness, then the three-part du'a: negation (no god but You), tanzih (Glory to You), self-indictment (I was of the wrongdoers) — tawhid spoken as the exit from distress; note the edition's capital "He cried out" [sic] for Jonah. Cf. the same triad's parts at `[2:32]`, `[3:191]` ("Glory to You"), `[7:23]` ("we have done wrong to ourselves").
* **`[21:88]`** "So We answered him, and saved him from the affliction. Thus We save the faithful."
  * **Gloss:** The theorem stated: "Thus We save the faithful" — the exact mirror of `[21:29]`'s "Thus We reward the wrongdoers" and of `[21:15]`'s ending; the mechanism behind it: `[37:143–144]` ("Had he not been one of those who praised...") and the contrast-case `[68:48]` ("the Fellow of the Fish who cried out in despair").
* **`[21:89]`** "And Zechariah, when he called out to his Lord, “My Lord, do not leave me alone, even though you are the Best of heirs.”"
  * **Gloss:** The heirlessness complaint; "Best of heirs" is unique in the corpus; the call's secrecy is `[19:3]` ("a call in seclusion") — the task's "secret du'a" is verified in Maryam's telling, not 21:89's own words.
* **`[21:90]`** "So We answered him, and gave him John. And We cured his wife for him. They used to vie in doing righteous deeds, and used to call on Us in love and awe, and they used to humble themselves to Us."
  * **Gloss:** Answer in one verse (cf. `[19:7]` "We give you good news of a son, whose name is John"); the household's habit: vying in good deeds (`[23:61]` "they race towards goodness"), calling in raghbah/rahbah, humility — the piety-portrait that 21:49 named.
* **`[21:91]`** "And she who guarded her virginity. We breathed into her of Our spirit, and made her and her son a sign to the world."
  * **Gloss:** Mary without her name in this telling; the same breath-phrase as `[66:12]` ("who guarded her womb... We breathed into her of Our Spirit") — one root (faraj), two renderings, observable drift; "a sign to the world" makes mother-and-son a public datum, not a lineage claim (see ‘Isa note).
* **`[21:92]`** "This community of yours is one community, and I am your Lord, so worship Me."
  * **Gloss:** The ecclesiology verse: umma wahda under one Lord; near-verbatim twin `[23:52]` ("This nation of yours is one nation" — community/nation drift), with `[2:213]` and `[42:8]` as the backdrop.
* **`[21:93]`** "But they splintered themselves into factions. They will all return to Us."
  * **Gloss:** The fracture (↔ `[23:53]` "they tore themselves into sects; each party happy with what they have"); and the roll-call's last word — return, which ruku 7 will spend on judgment.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`the darkness` (ẓulumāt)** `[21:87]`:
  * **Rendering:** "He cried out in the darkness" (singular in Itani; the Arabic is plural-darknesses, classically glossed belly/night/sea). *Source: classical tafsir on 21:87 (reports collected by Ibn Kathir) — agent labeling; verify.*
  * **Elsewhere:** the du'a's components recur at `[2:32]`/`[3:191]` (subhanaka), `[7:23]` (wronged-self confession); its effect at `[10:98]` (the only town whose belief profited) and `[37:143–144]` (the praise that shortened the detention).
* **`the steadfast`** `[21:85]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:10]` "The steadfast will be paid their wages in full, without reckoning"; the Sabr note's central term; 21:83–84 (Job) is its enactment.
* **`Enoch / Ezekiel` (translator's identifications)** `[21:85]`:
  * **Rendering:** Idris appears as "Enoch" (cf. `[19:56]` "And mention in the Scripture Enoch") and Dhul-Kifl as "Ezekiel" (cf. `[38:48]` "Ishmael, Elisha, and Ezekiel") — consistent across the corpus; the identifications themselves belong to transmitted exegetical reports. *Source: classical identification reports (Ibn Kathir cites the Dhul-Kifl/Ezekiel and Idris/Enoch notices) — agent labeling; verify.*
* **`one community` (umma)** `[21:92]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[23:52]` (near-verbatim, "nation"), `[2:213]` "Humanity used to be one community", `[42:8]`/`[11:118]` "Had your Lord willed, He could have made... one community, but they continue to differ" — unity willed, division lived, return deferred to God.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan — the roll-call belongs to the surah's late-Meccan consolation mode: every answered call argues that this mockery (ruku 1, 3) will end the way theirs did.
* **Narrative context:** the field/sheep judgment (21:78) has no narrative parallel elsewhere in the corpus (observable: `rg "wandered therein"` — single hit); classical readings (damage-compensation law given to Solomon) are legal-expansion from transmitted reports. *Source: classical tafsir on 21:78 — agent labeling; verify.* "Thinking We had no power over him" classically = supposing God would not constrain/handle him strictly — *Source: standard reading; agent labeling; verify.*
