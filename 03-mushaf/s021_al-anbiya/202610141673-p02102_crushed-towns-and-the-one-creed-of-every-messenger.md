# Crushed towns, no amusement — and the one creed of every messenger (21:11–29)
**UID:** 202610141673
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 21:11–29 (Ruku 2 of 7 in al-Anbiya)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[21:11]` – `[21:29]` (19 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku builds the surah's creedal spine: history shows luxury towns reduced to "silent ashes" once they sense divine might (11–15); creation is not an amusement, and truth crushes falsehood (16–18); the heavenly court worships untired and is barred from rivalry (19–20, 26–29); so earth-born gods who cannot even resurrect are absurd (21–24) — and the single creed every messenger ever carried is stated as the answer to all of it: "There is no god but I, so worship Me" (25).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 7; the argument-unit between the opening indictment (ruku 1) and the cosmological signs (ruku 3). Verse 25 is the surah's thesis; verses 22 and 24 carry its two interrogation-moves ("If there were..." / "Bring your proof").

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[21:11]`** "How many a guilty town have We crushed, and established thereafter another people?"
  * **Gloss:** History as precedent: the pattern raaba → crush → replace; cf. `[18:59]` ("We destroyed them when they committed injustices").
* **`[21:12]`** "Then, when they sensed Our might, they started running away from it." — * **Gloss:** Faith at sight of force is exactly the non-faith 21:6 already disqualified — flight, not repentance.
* **`[21:13]`** "Do not run, but come back to your luxuries, and to your homes, that you may be questioned." — * **Gloss:** Ironic imperative: return to your delicacies — the luxuries will not shelter you from the interrogation ("that you may be questioned").
* **`[21:14]`** "They said, “Woe to us; we were unfair.”"
  * **Gloss:** The first of the surah's three woe-confessions (here 21:14, at `[21:46]`, at `[21:97]`) — delayed truth-telling as the sound of the destroyed.
* **`[21:15]`** "This continued to be their cry, until We made them silent ashes."
  * **Gloss:** "Silent ashes" (unique wording): the cry repeated until the mouth itself ends; the antithesis to the untired praise of 21:20.
* **`[21:16]`** "We did not create the sky and the earth and what is between them for amusement."
  * **Gloss:** The cosmological denial behind the moral diagnosis: if creation is not la'ib, attention is owed; note singular "the sky" in 16 vs. "the heavens" in 19 (edition typography).
* **`[21:17]`** "If We wanted amusement, We could have found it within Us, were We to do so." — * **Gloss:** A reductio that refuses the premise's projection: divine self-sufficiency forecloses play-creation.
* **`[21:18]`** "In fact, We hurl the truth against falsehood, and it crushes it, so it vanishes. Woe unto you, for what you describe."
  * **Gloss:** The epistemic law of the ruku: truth is a projectile; "what you describe" (the accusations of ruku 1) is the falsehood being struck.
* **`[21:19]`** "To Him belongs everyone in the heavens and the earth. Those near Him are not too proud to worship Him, nor do they waver."
  * **Gloss:** Universal ownership + angelic worship without hubris or fatigue: the heavenly court as the mirror-image of the heedless towns.
* **`[21:20]`** "They praise night and day, without ever tiring."
  * **Gloss:** Near-verbatim twin `[41:38]` ("those in the presence of your Lord praise Him night and day, and without ever tiring").
* **`[21:21]`** "Or have they taken to themselves gods from the earth who resurrect?"
  * **Gloss:** The absurdity stated once: gods taken *from the earth* that could raise the dead — the resurrection-capability is the test of divinity (cf. `[7:191]` gods "who create nothing, and are themselves created").
* **`[21:22]`** "If there were in them gods other than Allah, they would have gone to ruin. So glory be to Allah, Lord of the Throne, beyond what they allege."
  * **Gloss:** The argument from order: plural divinity implies ruin; the twin argument at `[23:91]`; "Lord of the Throne" ↔ `[27:26]`, `[81:20]`.
* **`[21:23]`** "He will not be questioned about what He does, but they will be questioned."
  * **Gloss:** Asymmetric accountability — His sovereignty uninterrogated, every other soul answerable; the reversal of 21:13's ironic "come back... that you may be questioned."
* **`[21:24]`** "Or have they taken, besides Him, other gods? Say, “Bring your proof. This is a message for those with me, and a message of those before me.” But most of them do not know the truth, so they turn away."
  * **Gloss:** The demand for burhan (↔ `[2:111]` "Produce your proof, if you are truthful") and the unity-of-revelation claim: one message across all prior books — a kalam claim, not a polemic boast.
* **`[21:25]`** "We never sent a messenger before you without inspiring him that: “There is no god but I, so worship Me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The thesis verse: every mission, one creed — the universal-line reading of prophet-cycle (cf. `[16:2]` "there is no god but Me, and fear Me"; `[37:150]` shows the *negative* creed on the angels).
* **`[21:26]`** "And they say, “The Most Merciful has taken to himself a son.” Be He glorified; they are but honored servants."
  * **Gloss:** The son-charge (↔ `[19:88]` "The Most Merciful has begotten a son"; `[43:81]` reductio "If the Most Gracious had a son...") answered with tanzih and the angels' status: honored 'ibad, not lineage.
* **`[21:27]`** "They never speak before He has spoken, and they only act on His command."
  * **Gloss:** Angelic protocol: no speech ahead of God's speech, action only by command — intercession as permission, not prerogative.
* **`[21:28]`** "He knows what is before them, and what is behind them; and they do not intercede except for him whom He approves; and they tremble in awe of Him."
  * **Gloss:** The Throne Verse's clause recurs (↔ `[2:255]` "Who is he that can intercede with Him except with His permission"; `[53:26]` intercession avails nothing until permission) — even the court fears.
* **`[21:29]`** "And whoever of them says, “I am a god besides Him,” We will reward him with Hell. Thus We reward the wrongdoers."
  * **Gloss:** The court's own boundary clause: divinity-claims are answered in hell; "Thus We reward the wrongdoers" is the surah's refrain-formula (↔ `[21:100]`-block, `[21:15]`).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`la'ib` (amusement)** `[21:16–17]`:
  * **Rendering:** "for amusement"; second-person drift: "If We wanted amusement."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[44:38]` "We did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them to play" — near-verbatim twin; `[38:27]` "in vain" (different root — batil — thematic twin, lexical cousin); the task's suggested `[46:30]` is the jinn-Scripture scene, not a twin (verified: `rg "^\[46:30\]"`).
* **`Lord of the Throne` (Rabb al-'arsh)** `[21:22]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[27:26]` "the Lord of the Sublime Throne"; `[81:20]` "eminent with the Lord of the Throne"; `[2:255]` "His Throne extends over the heavens and the earth" (21:28's twin).
* **`burhan` (proof)** `[21:24]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Say, 'Bring your proof.'"
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:111]` "Say, 'Produce your proof, if you are truthful'" — the same disputational formula; the quranic demand runs one direction: proof is owed to tawhid, not to signs.
* **`There is no god but I, so worship Me`** `[21:25]`:
  * **Rendering:** the creed as direct speech of wahy to every messenger.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:2]` (creed + fear); `[21:87]` (Jonah speaks it from inside the fish); `[21:108]` (this surah's closing restatement); the first-person pair "no god but I" is 21:25 only (`rg "no god but I" corpus/` — single hit); the third-person confession "There is no god except Allah" is what the proud refuse at `[37:35]`, and Shuaib's version "no god other than Him" stands at `[11:84]` — one creed, three grammatical persons.
