# Abraham's integrity — shattered idols, the cool fire, and the blessed land (21:51–75)
**UID:** 202610141676
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 21:51–75 (Ruku 5 of 7 in al-Anbiya)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[21:51]` – `[21:75]` (25 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's longest ruku is the Ibrahim exemplar: a man given his integrity "formerly" by a God who says "We knew him well" confronts a statuary cult, exploits the idols' silence in a staged trial ("Ask them, if they can speak"), hears his prosecutors' own verdict ("You yourselves are the wrongdoers"), survives the pyre when the fire is ordered to become "coolness and safety," and is carried with Lot to the blessed land — where the rescued become "leaders, guiding by Our command," praying and giving charity.
* **Placement:** Ruku 5 of 7; the first and largest movement of the roll-call. It runs 21:9's salvation-formula ("We saved them... destroyed the extravagant") as narrative, and pre-echoes ruku 6's pattern of answered calls; 21:98 ("You and what you worship besides Allah are fuel for Hell") is the surah's own verdict back on the 21:68 verdict.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[21:51]`** "We gave Abraham his integrity formerly, and We knew him well." — * **Gloss:** Election stated as prior knowledge: hanf/integrity was *given* ("We gave"), not achieved — the ruku's ground note.
* **`[21:52]`** "When he said to his father and his people, “What are these statues to which you are devoted?”" — * **Gloss:** The question first, not the sermon: devotion ("you are devoted") is the object of interrogation; cf. `[7:138]` ("devoted to some statues").
* **`[21:53]`** "They said, “We found our parents worshiping them.”"
  * **Gloss:** The ancestral-defense — identical move at `[37:69]` ("They had found their parents astray").
* **`[21:54]`** "He said, “You and your parents are in evident error.”"
  * **Gloss:** Ancestry cut from both ends; the error is "evident" — the surah's obsession with seeing (`[21:30]`, `[21:44]`).
* **`[21:55]`** "They said, “Are you telling us the truth, or are you just playing?”"
  * **Gloss:** "Just playing" — the la'ib charge aimed at the prophet (↔ `[21:16]`'s cosmic denial: creation itself is not play).
* **`[21:56]`** "He said, “Your Lord is the Lord of the heavens and the earth, the One who created them, and I bear witness to that." — * **Gloss:** The creed-line of `[21:25]` spoken by Ibrahim: Lord-creator, with personal testimony attached.
* **`[21:57]`** "“By Allah, I will have a plan for your statues after you have gone away.”"
  * **Gloss:** The oath frames the coming act as strategy, not vandalism-by-nature.
* **`[21:58]`** "So he reduced them into pieces, except for their biggest, that they may return to it."
  * **Gloss:** The one spared giant becomes the exhibit — evidence staged for interrogation.
* **`[21:59]`** "They said, “Who did this to our gods? He is certainly one of the wrongdoers.”" — **Gloss:** the cult's self-certification of crime.
* **`[21:60]`** "They said, “We heard a youth mentioning them. He is called Abraham.”" — **Gloss:** "mentioning" — the same dhikr-verb now used against the accusers (they heard him *mention* the gods).
* **`[21:61]`** "They said, “Bring him before the eyes of the people, so that they may witness.”" — **Gloss:** a public trial convened — the courtroom scene the whole surah keeps staging.
* **`[21:62]`** "They said, “Are you the one who did this to our gods, O Abraham?”" — **Gloss:** direct address; the narrative turns to the cross-examination proper.
* **`[21:63]`** "He said, “But it was this biggest of them that did it. Ask them, if they can speak.”"
  * **Gloss:** The famous stratagem: blame transferred to the spared idol, with the demand that gods answer — cf. `[37:91–92]` ("will you not eat? What is it with you, that you do not speak?") and `[46:5]` (idols "will not answer him until the Day of Resurrection").
* **`[21:64]`** "Then they turned to one another, and said, “You yourselves are the wrongdoers.”" — * **Gloss:** The court convicts itself — the worshippers recognize the crime of worshipping.
* **`[21:65]`** "But they reverted to their old ideas: “You certainly know that these do not speak.”" — * **Gloss:** Self-conviction reversed by habit: "these do not speak" is confessed — and worshipped anyway (↔ `[21:3]`'s heedlessness).
* **`[21:66]`** "He said, “Do you worship, instead of Allah, what can neither benefit you in anything, nor harm you?" — * **Gloss:** The benefit/harm test of divinity — `[7:191]` "who create nothing" and `[16:17]` the Creator-vs-nothing pair in applied form.
* **`[21:67]`** "Fie on you, and on what you worship instead of Allah. Do you not understand?”"
  * **Gloss:** "Fie" (uff) — the expression of disgust; note the edition's quote-marks misalign between 66 and 67 (closing dropped in 66, opener missing here — see Index §6); same root at `[17:23]` rendered "a word of disrespect" — observable drift.
* **`[21:68]`** "They said, “Burn him and support your gods, if you are going to act.”" — * **Gloss:** The verdict — and the task-anchor correction: this is the crowd's command to burn, **not** a "fuel for the fire" line; the fuel-sentence is `[21:98]` (cf. `[2:24]`, `[3:10]`).
* **`[21:69]`** "We said, “O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham.”"
  * **Gloss:** The divine speech addressed to an element: fire named, commanded, doubled ("coolness" for sensation, "safety" for effect); `rg "coolness" corpus/` → only here and `[78:24]` ("they will taste therein neither coolness, nor drink") — the miracle-word inverted in the Fire of the deniers.
* **`[21:70]`** "They planned to harm him, but We made them the worst losers." — **Gloss:** plot → reversal, the surah's standard end-morpheme; cf. `[37:98]` (same story-line: "They wished him ill, but We made them the losers").
* **`[21:71]`** "And We delivered him, and Lot, to the land that We blessed for all people." — * **Gloss:** Deliverance is geography too: the blessed land (↔ `[17:1]` "whose precincts We have blessed"; within-surah at `[21:81]`); Lot enters without a story — his ruku comes at 74.
* **`[21:72]`** "And We granted him Isaac and Jacob as a gift; and each We made righteous." — **Gloss:** son and grandson as nafl, each made salah — election issuing in virtue, cf. `[29:27]`.
* **`[21:73]`** "And We made them leaders, guiding by Our command; and We inspired them to do good works, and to observe the prayer, and to give out charity. They were devoted servants to Us."
  * **Gloss:** The prophetic job-description: imaam by divine command (not dynasty), the good/prayer/charity triad (`[2:110]` "give alms" — rendering drift for zakat]), qanit servants; this is `[21:25]`'s creed lived as leadership.
* **`[21:74]`** "And Lot—We gave him judgment and knowledge, and We delivered him from the town that practiced the abominations. They were wicked and perverted people." — * **Gloss:** The same grant-formula ("We gave him") as 21:72; his people's "abominations" match `[29:28]` ("an obscenity not perpetrated before you").
* **`[21:75]`** "And We admitted him into Our mercy; for He was one of the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** Admission into mercy — the rescue's final clause, echoed verbatim-structurally at `[21:86]`; "one of the righteous" closes the ruku on its refrain.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`integrity / hanf`** `[21:51]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We gave Abraham his integrity formerly."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:161]` "My Lord has guided me to a straight path, an upright religion, the creed of Abraham the Monotheist"; `[6:85]`, `[29:27]` "of the upright" / "among the upright"; `[65:11]` "acts with integrity" — one root-family, four renderings.
* **`statues` (sanam)** `[21:52, 57]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:138]` — the only other corpus occurrence of the plural scene ("people who were devoted to some statues of theirs"), including the same "devoted" frame.
* **`coolness and safety`** `[21:69]`:
  * **Rendering:** unique dual in the corpus; as-Saffat's version of the episode (`[37:96–98]`) narrates pyre and reversal **without** the fire-address — observable difference between the two tellings.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[29:24]` "Kill him, or burn him. But Allah saved him from the fire" — the third telling of the pyre; `[78:24]` coolness negated in Hell.
* **`charity (zakat)` / `prayer`** `[21:73]`:
  * **Rendering:** "to observe the prayer, and to give out charity." **Elsewhere:** `[2:110]` "perform the prayer, and give alms" — same pair, drifted word; the cultic core of "guiding by Our command."

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan — the idol-trial is the surah's showcase Meccan polemic; Ibrahim is Mecca's own ancestral claim, here re-claimed for tawhid (cf. `[6:161]`, `[16:120–123]` in this brain's an-Nahl).
* **Narrative context:** the extra details of the pyre (furnace, size of fire, age of the "youth") belong to transmitted exegetical reports, not the verse-text. *Source: classical tafsir reports on 21:68–69 (Ibn Kathir; as-Saffat's `[37:97]` "pyre/furnace") — agent labeling; verify.* The "biggest idol" stratagem is read classically as Ibrahim exposing their inability to act, not as deceit — *Source: standard reading — agent labeling; verify.*
