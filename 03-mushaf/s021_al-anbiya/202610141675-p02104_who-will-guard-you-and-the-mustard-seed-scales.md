# Who guards you by night and by day? — the mustard-seed scales and the blessed message (21:42–50)
**UID:** 202610141675
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 21:42–50 (Ruku 4 of 7 in al-Anbiya)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[21:42]` – `[21:50]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Two interrogation-moves finish the demolition of the gods — who guards you against the Merciful by night and day, and can your rivals even defend themselves? — while long enjoyment gets answered by the land's gradual reduction; then the ruku installs the machinery of the end: a touch of punishment drawing the woe-cry, scales fine enough for a mustard-seed — and anchors all of it in Scripture: the same chain that gave Moses and Aaron the Criterion now delivers "this too is a blessed message that We revealed."
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 7; the hinge between the signs-block (ruku 3) and the prophet-roll-call (ruku 5–6). Verse 50's "This too" explicitly slots the Quran beside the Torah (21:48) before Ibrahim's story opens.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[21:42]`** "Say, “Who guards you against the Merciful by night and by day?” But they turn away from the mention of their Lord."
  * **Gloss:** The hima-question: night-and-day guardianship is exactly what the angelic court does "night and day, without ever tiring" (`[21:20]`) — and what dead idols never did. "The Merciful" is the Quraysh's contested divine name (cf. `[25:60]` "And what is the Merciful?").
* **`[21:43]`** "Or do they have gods who can defend them against Us? They cannot help themselves, nor will they be protected from Us."
  * **Gloss:** Near-verbatim logic of `[7:192]` ("can neither help them, nor help their own selves") — a defense force that needs defending; the same reductio recurs at 21:21 and `[16:17]`.
* **`[21:44]`** "We have given these enjoyments, and their ancestors, until time grew long upon them. Do they not see how We gradually reduce the land from its extremities? Are they then the victors?"
  * **Gloss:** Long enjoyment is not immunity: the land shrinks at its edges — a twin of `[13:41]` ("Do they not see how We deal with the earth, diminishing it at its edges?"). Classically read as the erosion of polytheist territory by the rising community (also: death, loss of fertility). *Source: classical dispute on 21:44 (reports at Ibn Kathir/Tabari) — agent labeling; verify.*
* **`[21:45]`** "Say, “I am warning you through inspiration.” But the deaf cannot hear the call when they are being warned."
  * **Gloss:** The method is wahy only (`[21:7]` restated); the deaf-and-call image is the stock pair — `[30:52]` "nor can you make the deaf hear the call when they turn away," also `[27:80]`, `[43:40]`.
* **`[21:46]`** "And when a breath of your Lord’s punishment touches them, they say, “Woe to us, we were truly wicked.”"
  * **Gloss:** "A breath" (a waft — the phrase is unique in the corpus, `rg "breath of"` → 21:46 only): a mere touch of punishment already extracts the second woe-confession, verbatim-cousin of `[21:14]` ("Woe to us; we were unfair").
* **`[21:47]`** "We will set up the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, so that no soul will suffer the least injustice. And even if it be the weight of a mustard-seed, We will bring it up. Sufficient are We as Reckoners."
  * **Gloss:** The eschatological metrology: scales ("the balances," cf. `[7:8]` "The scales on that Day will be just") + the mustard-seed extreme (`[31:16]` "even if it were the weight of a mustard-seed... Allah will bring it to light") + "Sufficient are We as Reckoners" (`[4:6]` "Allah suffices as a Reckoner").
* **`[21:48]`** "We gave Moses and Aaron the Criterion, and illumination, and a reminder for the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The Scripture-chain, concretely: furqan as at `[2:53]` ("We gave Moses the Scripture and the Criterion") and `[37:117]` ("And We gave them the Clarifying Scripture" — the same Mosaic-Aaronic pair).
* **`[21:49]`** "Those who fear their Lord in private, and are apprehensive of the Hour."
  * **Gloss:** The addressees of 48: private God-consciousness (the phrase "fear their Lord in private" is unique in the corpus) + Hour-anxiety — the taqwa definition the surah will personify in the roll-call.
* **`[21:50]`** "This too is a blessed message that We revealed. Are you going to deny it?"
  * **Gloss:** Near-verbatim twin `[6:92]` ("This too is a Scripture that We revealed— blessed"); the Quran enters the same blessedness-register as the Torah — the kalam claim made by the pronoun "too."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`hima` (guarding)** `[21:42]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Who guards you against the Merciful by night and by day?" — the noun-idea (divine custody) against the gods' zero custody; no external twin uses this verb-frame.
* **`gradually reduce the land`** `[21:44]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We gradually reduce the land from its extremities."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:41]` "We deal with the earth, diminishing it at its edges" — the near-twin; both are Meccan interrogatives of the form "Do they not see how We...?"
* **`mustard-seed / scales / Reckoner`** `[21:47]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[31:16]` (mustard-seed brought to light), `[7:8]` (just scales), `[4:6]` ("Allah suffices as a Reckoner") — the three reckoning-instruments recur together only here.
* **`furqan (Criterion)` / `blessed message`** `[21:48–50]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the Criterion, and illumination, and a reminder for the righteous"; "This too is a blessed message."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:53]`, `[37:117]` (Mosaic-Aaronic grant), `[25:1]` (the Criterion "upon His servant"), `[6:92]` (the "this too is blessed" formula), `[43:44]` (dhikr again, cf. ruku 1).
