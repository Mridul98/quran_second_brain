# The reckoning drawn near — and the messenger questioned (21:1–10)
**UID:** 202610141672
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 21:1–10 (Ruku 1 of 7 in al-Anbiya)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[21:1]` – `[21:10]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens with an eschatological verdict — the reckoning has drawn near and people turn away heedlessly — then stages the Meccan prosecution of the messenger (dream-jumble, fabrication, poetry, sign-demand) and answers it with the human-prophet axiom: God's messengers were inspired men who ate and were mortal, so "ask the people of knowledge"; the unit closes by counter-claiming that this sent-down Book is the listeners' *own* reminder.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 7; the indictment that the whole surah answers. Its "drawn near" (`[21:1]`) is answered verse-for-structure by `[21:97]`'s "The promise of truth has drawn near" — the surah's bracket; its "mortal like you" (`[21:3]`) is answered at `[21:108]` ("Say, 'It is revealed to me that your God is One God'"), the same formula as `[41:6]`.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[21:1]`** "Mankind’s reckoning has drawn near, but they turn away heedlessly."
  * **Gloss:** The account (hisab) is not future tense in the making but already arrived ("has drawn near"); the double disease is ghafla (heedlessness) plus i'rad (turning away) — the surah's opening diagnosis of the human situation.
* **`[21:2]`** "No fresh reminder comes to them from their Lord, but they listen to it playfully."
  * **Gloss:** The reminder is continually renewed ("fresh... from their Lord"), and the failure is not lack of contact but posture: they listen "playfully" — amusement as the opposite of heedfulness, the word la'ib returns at `[21:16–17]`.
* **`[21:3]`** "Their hearts distracted, the wrongdoers confer secretly, “Is this anything but a mortal like you? Will you take to sorcery, with openeyes?”"
  * **Gloss:** The inner council of the wrongdoers: the disqualifier is the messenger's humanity ("a mortal like you"), and accepting revelation is mocked as sorcery taken "with open eyes" — willful self-hypnosis. The quote [sic] "with openeyes" is this edition's extraction artifact.
* **`[21:4]`** "He said, “My Lord knows what is said in the heaven and the earth; and He is the Hearer, the Knower.”"
  * **Gloss:** The prophetic reply bypasses the biographical charge and reasserts divine omniscience — the secret conference is itself overheard; Hearer-Knower brackets the surah's epistemics (echoed at `[21:110]`).
* **`[21:5]`** "And they said, “A jumble of dreams,” and, “He made it up,” and, “He is a poet,” “let him bring us a sign, like those sent to the ancients.”"
  * **Gloss:** The four-charge indictment: confused dreams, forgery, poetry — and the demand for a precedent-sign like the earlier messengers', which verse 6 will turn against them with the logic of history.
* **`[21:6]`** "None of the towns We destroyed before them had believed. Will they, then, believe?"
  * **Gloss:** The rhetorical verdict: sign-demands never produced faith in the destroyed towns, so a new sign will not save these — the only exception in the corpus is Jonah's town (`[10:98]`, see ruku 6), which makes the roll-call a controlled counter-example.
* **`[21:7]`** "We did not send before you except men, whom We inspired. Ask the people of knowledge, if you do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The divine answer to "a mortal like you": the sending pattern has always been inspired men; and the objectors are remitted to inter-faith epistemics — "ask the people of knowledge," the closest thing to a methodology verse in the Quran.
* **`[21:8]`** "We did not make them mere bodies that ate no food, nor were they immortal."
  * **Gloss:** Embodiment and mortality are constitutive, not disqualifying: the sent ones ate (cf. `[25:20]`) and died; immortality was never the messenger's qualification.
* **`[21:9]`** "Then We fulfilled Our promise to them, and We saved them together with whomever We willed, and We destroyed the extravagant."
  * **Gloss:** The salvation formula the roll-call will run nine times: promise fulfilled, the sent ones saved *with whomever We willed*, the isrāf (extravagance) destroyed — election and rescue fused in one line.
* **`[21:10]`** "We have sent down to you a Book, containing your message. Do you not understand?"
  * **Gloss:** Turn to the present audience: this Book is "your message" (dhikrakum) — honor, reminder, and subject all at once; the ruku ends where it began, on their comprehension ("Do you not understand?").

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`hisab` (reckoning)** `[21:1]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Mankind’s reckoning has drawn near."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:6]` "Allah suffices as a Reckoner" ↔ `[21:47]` "Sufficient are We as Reckoners" (near-verbatim); `[14:51]` "Allah is Quick in reckoning"; `[88:26]` "upon Us rests their reckoning" — the account is God's own office.
* **`dhikr` (reminder/message)** `[21:2]`, `[21:10]`:
  * **Rendering:** drifts inside one ruku: "fresh reminder" (2), "your message" (10); the same word returns as "blessed message" `[21:50]` and "the Reminder" `[21:105]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:5]` "No fresh reminder comes to them from the Most Merciful" — near-verbatim twin of 21:2; `[43:44]` "It is a message for you, and for your people" — twin of 21:10; `[38:87]` "a reminder to mankind."
* **`bashar mithlukum` (a mortal like you)** `[21:3]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Is this anything but a mortal like you?"
  * **Elsewhere:** `[41:6]` "Say, 'I am only a human like you; it is inspired in me that your God is One God'" — the self-quote this surah repeats at `[21:108]`; `[54:24]` "one of us, a human being?"; the formula inverts at `[7:194]` — "servants like you" describes the *idols*, not the messenger.
* **`ask the people of knowledge`** `[21:7]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:43]` "So ask the people of knowledge, if you do not know" — verbatim twin inside the same Meccan sequence (an-Nahl is the previous-but-two documented surah in this brain).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (unanimous) — the charge-cluster of 21:3–5 is the standard middle/late-Meccan prosecution scene; no Medinan legal material in the ruku.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the four accusations are treated classically as Quraysh's sayings about the Quran (dream-jumble/fabrication/poetry). *Source: classical tafsir reports on 21:5 as collected by Ibn Kathir — agent labeling; verify in print.* No asbab unit transmitted for 21:1–10. *Source: silence of the asbab genre — agent inference.*
