# Written in the Psalms after the Reminder — mercy to the worlds (21:94–112)
**UID:** 202610141678
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 21:94–112 (Ruku 7 of 7 in al-Anbiya)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[21:94]` – `[21:112]` (19 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The finale runs the endgame ledger: deeds written, the destroyed barred from return, Gog and Magog swarming as the truth-promise draws near — closing the surah's opening bracket with the third woe-confession; then the double separation: worshippers-and-gods as one fuel, wailing unhearied, while the faithful hear not its hissing, and the Supreme Fear does not worry them; heaven folded like a book, creation repeated by a promise God bound on Himself; the decree already written in the Psalms after the Reminder — the righteous inherit the earth — and the surah ends where its creed began: "your God is One God," answered by a final du'a, "My Lord, judge with justice."
* **Placement:** Ruku 7 of 7; the surah's eschatological and missiological resolution. `[21:107]`–`[21:108]` answer the roll-call (one mercy-sending, one creed), and `[21:112]` hands the argument — never settled by proof alone — to God as Judge, mirroring the messengers' own du'as (`[7:89]`, `[34:26]`).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[21:94]`** "Whoever does righteous deeds, and is a believer, his effort will not be denied. We are writing it down for him." — **Gloss:** effort ("sa'y") neither lost nor minimized; the writing-down ↔ `[54:53]` "Everything, small or large, is written down."
* **`[21:95]`** "There is a ban on the town that We had destroyed—that they will not return." — **Gloss:** the crux clause: once destruction is decreed, no return — the wish of `[6:27–28]` ("If only we could be sent back... they would revert") denied as law; its single exception is Jonah's town (`[10:98]`, cf. `[21:87–88]`). *Source: classical dispute on the referent of "return" (to life? to faith?) — agent labeling; verify.*
* **`[21:96]`** "Until, when Gog and Magog are let loose, and they swarm down from every mound." — **Gloss:** the barrier's end, named in the Quran only here and in the Zul-Qarnain story (`[18:94]`); "swarm down from every mound" — the intrusion of chaos as an eschatological timestamp.
* **`[21:97]`** "The promise of truth has drawn near. The eyes of those who disbelieved will stare in horror: “Woe to us. We were oblivious to this. In fact, we were wrongdoers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The bracket closes: "has drawn near" answers `[21:1]`; the third woe-confession completes the trio of 21:14/`[21:46]` — the surah's destroyees all say the same sentence, too late.
* **`[21:98]`** "You and what you worship besides Allah are fuel for Hell. You will descend into it."
  * **Gloss:** Task-anchor corrected: the "fuel" line is here, not `[21:68]`; twins `[2:24]` ("whose fuel is people and stones"), `[3:10]` ("These will be fuel for the Fire"), `[66:6]` — worship-pairs, not individuals, descend.
* **`[21:99]`** "Had these been gods, they would not have descended into it. All will abide in it." — **Gloss:** the reductio of 21:21–22 and `[21:66]` executed at the Day: gods who could not save themselves share the fuel-heap; abode now universal within the Fire.
* **`[21:100]`** "In it they will wail. In it they will not hear." — **Gloss:** the double clause: wailing unheard — either their own cries unanswered or the hiss unnoticed? The syntax keeps both open; cf. `[67:7]` (thrown in, hearing its roaring) and `[20:108]` (hushed voices).
* **`[21:101]`** "As for those who deserved goodness from Us—these will be kept away from it." — **Gloss:** the pre-appointment of goodness: "deserved goodness from Us" precedes their deeds — the roll-call's theology of mercy (21:75/86) as the other half of 21:94's ledger.
* **`[21:102]`** "They will not hear its hissing, and they will forever abide in what their hearts desire." — **Gloss:** the mirrored clause to 21:100 — here the sound of Hell never reaches them; abode becomes desire-fulfillment; `rg "hissing"` — unique in the corpus.
* **`[21:103]`** "The Supreme Fear will not worry them, and the angels will receive them: “This is your Day which you were promised.”"
  * **Gloss:** The greatest terror passes them by; the angelic greeting ↔ `[41:30]` ("the angels will descend upon them: 'Do not fear, and do not grieve, but rejoice in the news of the Garden which you were promised'"); "Supreme Fear" — unique phrasing.
* **`[21:104]`** "On the Day when We fold the heaven, like the folding of a book. Just as We began the first creation, We will repeat it—a promise binding on Us. We will act."
  * **Gloss:** The cosmos rolled: ↔ `[39:67]` ("the heavens will be folded in His right"); re-creation as replay of the first draft (↔ `[10:34]`, `[29:19]`); "a promise binding on Us" — God puts Himself under the vow (cf. `[58:21]` "Allah has written: 'I will certainly prevail'").
* **`[21:105]`** "We have written in the Psalms, after the Reminder, that the earth will be inherited by My righteous servants."
  * **Gloss:** The intertextual citation: scripture quoting older scripture; inheritance of the earth ↔ `[28:5]` (the oppressed "made the inheritors"), `[7:137]`, `[39:74]` "made us inherit the land."
* **`[21:106]`** "Indeed, in this is a message for people who worship." — **Gloss:** the dhikr-word returns (cf. `[21:10]`) — the Book contains "your message" after all; the addressee is narrowed to the worshippers.
* **`[21:107]`** "We did not send you except as mercy to mankind."
  * **Gloss:** The sending (ib'aath) — the same verb as `[21:25]` — is redefined: the prophet-cycle's end is rahma; the phrase stands alone in the corpus (`rg "mercy to mankind"` → 21:107 only).
* **`[21:108]`** "Say, “It is revealed to me that your God is One God. Are you going to submit?”"
  * **Gloss:** The wahy-formula of `[41:6]` ("it is inspired in me that your God is One God") and the creed of `[2:163]` — 21:3's "mortal like you" objection finally answered with the mortal's actual content: submission asked, not proven.
* **`[21:109]`** "But if they turn away, say, “I have informed you sufficiently. Although I do not know whether what you are promised is near or far.”" — **Gloss:** equal disclosure, withheld timetable — near-verbatim twin `[72:25]` ("I do not know whether what you are promised is near..."); the bracket of 21:1 is left, by design, unresolved in human knowledge.
* **`[21:110]`** "He knows what is said openly, and He knows what you conceal." — **Gloss:** the Hearer-Knower of `[21:4]` restated; ↔ `[27:25]` ("knows what you conceal and what you reveal") — the surah's epistemic bookend.
* **`[21:111]`** "“And I do not know whether it is perhaps a trial for you, and an enjoyment for a while.”" — **Gloss:** the respite re-read: long enjoyment (↔ `[21:44]`) may be test, not favor; cf. `[46:9]` "I do not know what will be done with me, or with you" — the prophet's confessed ignorance about outcomes.
* **`[21:112]`** "He said, “My Lord, judge with justice.” And, “Our Lord is the Gracious, Whose help is sought against what you allege.”"
  * **Gloss:** The last verse is a du'a, not a triumph: the judgment petition (↔ `[7:89]` "Our Lord, decide between us... You are the Best of Deciders"; `[34:26]` "He will judge between us equitably") and the turning to the Gracious for help (musta'an) — the Fatihah's "upon You we call for help" (`[1:5]`) as the surah's final word. *Correction of the task note: no "I have been commanded with the word of truth" and no "O servant of mine" occur — see index §1; the servant-address closest to the ending is "My righteous servants," `[21:105]`.*

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fuel (qudus? — 'qamas')`** `[21:98]`:
  * **Rendering:** "You and what you worship besides Allah are fuel for Hell."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:24]` "whose fuel is people and stones"; `[3:10]` "These will be fuel for the Fire"; `[66:6]` "whose fuel is people and stones" — the fuel-term runs the whole shirk-indictment from the Medinan front to this Meccan one.
* **`Psalms (Zabur) / the Reminder (Dhikr)`** `[21:105]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We have written in the Psalms, after the Reminder."
  * **Elsewhere:** "the Psalms" as David's book at `[17:55]` and `[4:163]`; the formula-class at `[3:184]`/`[35:25]` ("the proofs, and the Psalms, and the Illuminating Scripture"); "the Reminder" here classically = the Torah or the Mother of the Book — dispute. *Source: classical tafsir on 21:105 (Ibn Kathir transmits both identifications) — agent labeling; verify.*
* **`inherited by My righteous servants`** `[21:105]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[28:5]`, `[7:137]`, `[39:74]`, `[23:10–11]` ("These are the inheritors. Who will inherit Paradise") — this-world and other-world inheritance fused; the surah's righteous refrain (21:72/75/86) is now the class that inherits.
* **`fold / first creation`** `[21:104]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:67]` ("the heavens will be folded in His right"); `[10:34]`, `[29:19]` — originates-and-repeats; the cosmogony of `[21:30]` (one mass → torn) is matched verse-for-verse by the eschaton (folded → remade).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan — the endgame inventory (Gog and Magog, folded heaven, scales of return) without any state-legislation; the surah's closing is liturgical (du'a), consistent with its late-Meccan consolation profile.
* **Interpretive notes:** "a promise binding on Us" (21:104) is treated classically among the strongest divine self-obligations in the Quran (cf. `[58:21]`); *Source: classical reading (Ibn Kathir on 21:104) — agent labeling; verify.* The closing du'a's addressee shifts between the prophet ("My Lord") and the community ("Our Lord") — observable in the verse's own pronouns.
