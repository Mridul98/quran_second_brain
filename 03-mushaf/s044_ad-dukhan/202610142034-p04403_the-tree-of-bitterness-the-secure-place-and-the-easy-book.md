# The Tree of Bitterness, the secure place, and the Book made easy (44:43–59)
**UID:** 202610142034
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 44:43–59 (Ruku 3 of 3 in ad-Dukhan)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[44:43]` – `[44:59]` (17 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The finale stages both destinations as interrogations: the sinner is dragged to the Tree of Bitterness and taunted "Taste! You who were powerful and noble," while the righteous get gardens, springs, lovely companions, no further death — "the supreme salvation" — whereupon the surah defends its own plain language ("We made it easy in your language") and hands the parties to mutual watching.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 3; the verdict-scene answering ruku 1's smoke day (10–16) and ruku 2's Day of Sorting (40–42). The abandoned gardens of 44:25 are answered gift-for-gift by the gardens of 44:52.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[44:43]`** "The Tree of Bitterness." — **Gloss:** A bare name-verse, opening the menu of Hell; "Tree of Bitterness" is this edition's rendering at all three sites: `[37:62]`, `[56:52]`, here (rg-verified).
* **`[44:44]`** "The food of the sinner." — **Gloss:** The sinner gets a diet matched to his name; "food of the sinner" is corpus-unique (rg: only 44:44).
* **`[44:45]`** "Like molten lead; boiling inside the bellies." — **Gloss:** The scald-metal image; compare the sky itself "like molten brass" at `[70:8]` — the same family of molten things, one rendered "lead," one "brass" (translation variance per the artifacts fleeting note).
* **`[44:46]`** "Like the boiling of seething water." — **Gloss:** The simile re-sets to liquid; "seething" is the edition's scalding-water word also at `[52:6]` ("the seething sea") and `[55:44]` ("a seething bath").
* **`[44:47]`** "Seize him and drag him into the midst of Hell!" — **Gloss:** The imperative of arrest; the divine seize-formula of the precedent-verses ("then I seized them," `[22:44]`; "But I seized them," `[40:5]`) now runs as a standing order.
* **`[44:48]`** "Then pour over his head the suffering of the Inferno!" — **Gloss:** An execution poured, head-first; ledger (from ruku 1): the "lesser punishment before the greater" is `[32:21]`, not this verse — here the punishment comes undiluted.
* **`[44:49]`** "Taste! You who were powerful and noble." — **Gloss:** The taunt inverts 44:25–27: the powerful-and-noble who left gardens, plantations and comforts behind now "taste" only this; honor is quoted back as irony.
* **`[44:50]`** "This is what you used to doubt." — **Gloss:** The doubt of 44:9 ("play around in doubt") has become the exhibited thing itself; cf. the same verdict-grammar at `[45:33]` ("the very thing they ridiculed will haunt them").
* **`[44:51]`** "As for the righteous, they will be in a secure place." — **Gloss:** The counter-scene opens on security; "a secure place" is one of only two corpus sites of the phrase — the other is `[77:21]` (the embryo "lodged in a secure place"), so the Day's "secure place" for the righteous is this ruku's own coinage.
* **`[44:52]`** "Amidst gardens and springs." — **Gloss:** The inventory Pharaoh's people left behind (44:25), now gifted — this edition renders the water-word "fountains" in 44:25 and "springs" in 44:52 (and "springs" in the twin scene `[26:57]`): term-choice drift within one surah. *Source: translator's term choice (observable in corpus).*
* **`[44:53]`** "Dressed in silk and brocade, facing one another." — **Gloss:** Dress and posture; "silk and brocade" verbatim-twin `[18:31]` ("green garments of silk and brocade"), brocade again at `[55:54]`; "facing one another" is the paradise geometry of `[15:47]`, `[56:16]`, `[37:44]`.
* **`[44:54]`** "So it is, and We will wed them to lovely companions." — **Gloss:** "So it is" answers 44:28's "So it was; and We passed it on to another people" — the transfer-formula that moved estates now moves spouses (verifiable by rg at both lines); "lovely companions" verbatim `[56:22]`; rendered "couple them with gorgeous spouses" at `[52:20]` (drift ledger).
* **`[44:55]`** "They will call therein for every kind of fruit, in peace and security." — **Gloss:** The banquet of demand-driven fruit — cf. `[2:25]` "Whenever they are provided with fruit therefrom..."; "every kind of fruit" is corpus-rare (rg); peace-and-security repeats 44:51's root.
* **`[44:56]`** "Therein they will not taste death, beyond the first death; and He will protect them from the torment of Hell." — **Gloss:** Their own creed (44:35, "nothing but our first death") becomes true in the only way it was ever going to: one death, then none — "taste death" is the formula of `[29:57]` and `[21:35]`.
* **`[44:57]`** "A favor from your Lord. That is the supreme salvation." — **Gloss:** The whole scene is grace, not wage; "supreme salvation" is this edition's unique label, member of a dense "supreme" family: "supreme achievement" (`[9:72]`, `[64:9]`, `[40:9]`), "supreme triumph" (`[10:64]`, `[9:111]`, `[37:60]`), "supreme blessing" (`[42:22]`), "supreme success" (`[61:12]`), and its mirror "supreme disgrace" (`[9:63]`) — all rg-verified.
* **`[44:58]`** "We made it easy in your language, so that they may remember." — **Gloss:** The surah's self-defense: the Blessed-Night descent (44:3) has arrived as plain Arabic; verbatim twin `[19:97]` ("We made it easy in your tongue"), and the Qur'an-easy refrain of `[54:17/22/32/40]` ("We made the Quran easy to learn... remember... understand... memorize").
* **`[44:59]`** "So wait and watch. They too are waiting and watching." — **Gloss:** The closing watchword; the "watch—they will soon see" imperative of `[37:175/179]` and the await-the-decision line of `[52:48]` supply its twins; mutual waiting ends the surah exactly where its smoke-day warning (10) began the contest.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`zaqqum` / "Tree of Bitterness"**:
  * **Rendering:** always "the Tree of Bitterness" (`[44:43]`, `[37:62]`, `[56:52]`); the transliteration "zaqqum" appears nowhere in this corpus (rg-verified) — the name is translated, never named.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:63–64]` "We made it an ordeal for the unjust... grows from the bottom of Hell" — the twin description; the classical naming zaqqum: *Source: consensus of classical tafsir; observable term choice in the corpus is the English rendering.*
* **"The first death" (creed-formula)**:
  * **Rendering:** "beyond the first death" (44:56) after "our first death" (44:35) — the English formula returns twice in one surah: denied by the disbelievers, qualified for the blessed.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:59]` (the Garden party's "nothing but our first death"); `[45:24]` (the time-creed "we die and we live") — resurrection-denial formulas across the same Meccan band.
* **"Seething" and molten imagery (the scalding register)**:
  * **Rendering:** "seething water" (44:46), "seething sea" (52:6), "seething bath" (55:44); the molten pair "molten lead" (44:45) / "molten brass" (70:8).
  * **Elsewhere:** the sensory register of Hell in this edition is liquid-metal and seething-water, never fire alone — four sites, rg-verified; no Arabic claim made (translation-level observation only).
* **`supreme` (akram/afzal-class labels)**:
  * **Rendering:** "the supreme salvation" (44:57) — unique.
  * **Elsewhere:** achievement (9:72, 64:9, 40:9), triumph (10:64, 9:111, 37:60), blessing (42:22), success (61:12), disgrace (9:63) — the "supreme ___" verdict-slot is filled six different ways corpus-wide; drift is translator's, the superlative frame is the text's.
