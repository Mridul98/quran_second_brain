# The chosen people, the first death, and the Day of Sorting Out (44:30–42)
**UID:** 202610142033
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 44:30–42 (Ruku 2 of 3 in ad-Dukhan)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[44:30]` – `[44:42]` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Israel's deliverance and knowing election (30–33) stand as the counter-argument to "These people's" one-death creed (34–36): a people who survived Pharaoh and Tubba's annihilated peers cannot have been created "to play" — so the appointed Day of Sorting Out comes, where no friend avails, except those on whom the Mighty, the Merciful has mercy (37–42).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 3; the pivot between the Pharaoh precedent (ruku 1's end) and the two destinations (ruku 3): rescue-history becomes resurrection-argument.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[44:30]`** "And We delivered the Children of Israel from the humiliating persecution." — **Gloss:** The rescue named by its pathos; the "humiliating" punishment formula recurs at `[22:57]` and `[39:40]`; the Pharaonic oppression it recalls: `[28:4]` "He persecuted a group of them, slaughtering their sons, while sparing their daughters."
* **`[44:31]`** "From Pharaoh. He was a transgressing tyrant." — **Gloss:** One man singled out as the regime; *taghut* — the transgressing-tyrant label; the dossier continues ruku 1's 17–29, and the "clan of Pharaoh" reappears at `[40:46]`.
* **`[44:32]`** "And We chose them knowingly over all other people." — **Gloss:** Election stated as deliberate — the adverb "knowingly" is taken in the classical reading as God choosing with foreknowledge (*Source: consensus of classical tafsir on 44:32 — verify in print*); phrase-twins: `[6:86–87]` ("favored each one of them over all other people... We chose them"), `[2:122]`, `[7:144]`, and this surah's neighbor `[45:16]` ("advantage over all other people").
* **`[44:33]`** "And We gave them many signs, in which was an obvious test." — **Gloss:** The gift carries the examination; the same signs-plus-audience grammar in the sibling dossier of 45:3–5; "an obvious test" is corpus-rare in this form (rg: only 44:33).
* **`[44:34]`** "These people say." — **Gloss:** A razor-sharp present-tense turn from ancient Israel to the present audience — the surah's pivot-line (the short sentence stands alone).
* **`[44:35]`** "“There is nothing but our first death, and we will not be resurrected." — **Gloss:** The one-death creed, quoted in their own mouth; near-verbatim twin `[37:59]` ("Except for our first death, and we will not be punished"); answers 44:8's "He gives life and causes death."
* **`[44:36]`** "Bring back our ancestors, if you are truthful.”" — **Gloss:** The falsifiability demand: produce the dead; verbatim twin `[45:25]`; the "ancestors of old" named at 44:8 are precisely the ones they refuse to trust God with.
* **`[44:37]`** "Are they better, or the people of Tubba and those before them? We annihilated them. They were evildoers." — **Gloss:** The comparative rebuke ("Are they better...?" — same coinage pattern as `[54:43]`'s challenge to Quraysh's superiority); Tubba's people appear exactly twice in the corpus, here and `[50:14]`, both in rejected-messenger catalogues.
* **`[44:38]`** "We did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them to play." — **Gloss:** The cosmological premise: creation has no idle author; the trio's twins: `[21:16]` "for amusement" (+ 21:17 "If We wanted amusement, We could have found it within Us") and `[38:27]` "in vain," fourth variant `[46:3]` "except with reason, and for a finite period."
* **`[44:39]`** "We created them only for a specific purpose, but most of them do not know." — **Gloss:** The negative clause flips to a purpose with a term — nearest corpus gloss of the same idea is `[46:3]`'s "finite period"; "but most of them do not know" recurs as the surahs' epistemic refrain (cf. 44:7 "if you know for sure").
* **`[44:40]`** "The Day of Sorting Out is the appointed time for them all." — **Gloss:** *Yawm al-fasl*: the Day that distinguishes — twin rendering `[78:17]` "The Day of Sorting has been appointed"; "the appointed time for them all" gathers every generation into one deadline.
* **`[44:41]`** "The Day when no friend will avail a friend in any way, and they will not be helped." — **Gloss:** The collapse of social credit: the "no friend" Day — twins `[2:254]` ("neither trading, nor friendship, nor intercession"), `[2:48]` ("no soul will avail another... nor will any ransom be taken"), `[31:33]` (no parent for child, no child for parent), and the individual portrait `[69:35]` ("So he has no friend here today").
* **`[44:42]`** "Except for him upon whom Allah has mercy. He is the Mighty, the Merciful." — **Gloss:** The single exception; the ruku closes on the mercy-name exactly as the surah's oath-closed on "As mercy from your Lord" (`[44:6]`), might bracketing mercy (cf. `[57:2]` might-and-kingdom pairing).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`al-mawt al-awwal`** (the first death):
  * **Rendering:** "our first death" (44:35); "beyond the first death" (44:56, ruku 3) — the same phrase used twice in opposite directions: denied by the materialists, survived by the blessed.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:59]` (the Garden party's version), `[45:24]` ("we die and we live") — the creed this ruku refutes is answered internally by 44:56.
* **`yawm al-fasl`** (the Day of Sorting Out):
  * **Rendering:** "The Day of Sorting Out" (44:40); `[78:17]` "The Day of Sorting" — one Arabic name, two English caps (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[78:17]` "The Day of Sorting has been appointed" — rg across the whole corpus: these two lines are the only "Sorting"-Day renderings, a two-site pair.
* **`Tubba'`**:
  * **Rendering:** "the people of Tubba" (44:37); `[50:14]` — both places pair Tubba with rejected messengers.
  * **Elsewhere:** the two corpus sites are both rejected-messenger catalogues; the classical identification of Tubba' with the kings of Himyar (Yemen) lies outside the corpus — *Source: agent inference from classical historiography — verify.*
* **`la'ib / batil / amusement`** (the creation-not-in-play trio):
  * **Rendering:** "to play" (44:38), "for amusement" (21:16), "in vain" (38:27), "with reason, and for a finite period" (46:3).
  * **Elsewhere:** worldly-life definitions stack the same field: `[29:64]` "diversion and play," `[57:20]` "play, and distraction, and glitter," `[47:36]` "play and pastime" — play-language is consistently a denial-lexicon, never applied to God's making except in negation.
