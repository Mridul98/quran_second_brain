# Fight those who attack you — and the messenger who cannot bear your suffering (9:123–129)
**UID:** 202610141486
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 9:123–129 (Ruku 16 of 16 in at-Tawbah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[9:123]` – `[9:129]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The closing ruku narrows the war-law to its final wording ("fight those of the disbelievers who attack you"), shows the chapter itself as the heart's assay (faith increased in the believers, filth added to the sick-hearted), and then — after the surah that began with immunity from the polytheists — ends on the tenderest self-description in the Quran: *a messenger from among yourselves, agonized over your suffering*, closing with the basmala-free believer's formula: "Allah is enough for me...Lord of the Sublime Throne."
* **Placement:** Final ruku; the surah's envelope: from the proclamation of disavowal (9:1) to grief for the disavowed (9:128), law to compassion in the same breath.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[9:123]`** "O you who believe! Fight those of the disbelievers who attack you, and let them find severity in you, and know that Allah is with the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The terminal war-clause: *who attack you* qualifies the object; severity is demanded as deterrence; the taqwa refrain closes, as at 9:36.
* **`[9:124]`** "Whenever a chapter is revealed, some of them say, “Which of you has this increased in faith?” As for those who believe: it increases them in faith, and they rejoice."
  * **Gloss:** The chapter as assay: the question sneered is the question the text answers — revelation's effect is a faith-meter readable by the community.
* **`[9:125]`** "But as for those in whose hearts is sickness: it adds disgrace to their disgrace, and they die as unbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** The sick-hearted counter-reading; "disgrace upon disgrace" — the exposure-grammar of 9:64 completed; death-outside-faith the final loss.
* **`[9:126]`** "Do they not see that they are tested once or twice every year? Yet they do not repent, and they do not learn."
  * **Gloss:** Annual testing (classically the campaigns themselves) with two failed readings: no repentance, no comprehension — the surah's name-word still the expected answer.
* **`[9:127]`** "And whenever a chapter is revealed, they look at one another, “Does anyone see you?” Then they slip away. Allah has diverted their hearts, because they are a people who do not understand."
  * **Gloss:** The theater of avoidance — glances, the whispered check for witnesses, the exit; heart-diversion as consequence of the sneaking (2:9's self-deception idiom).
* **`[9:128]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "There has come to you a messenger from among yourselves, concerned over your suffering, anxious over you."
  * "Towards the believers, he is compassionate and merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The Prophet-portrait summit: *from among yourselves* (insider, same blood, same tongue); *ʿazīz* rendered "concerned over your suffering" — he finds your distress hard; *harīṣ* "anxious over you" — eager for your good; then the two rare mercy-names (*raʾūf raḥīm*) for the believer-facing mode.
* **`[9:129]`** "If they turn away, say, “Allah is enough for me; there is no god except He; in Him I have put my trust; He is the Lord of the Sublime Throne.”"
  * **Gloss:** The surah's final instruction for rejection: a five-part dhikr — sufficiency, tawhid, trust, and the Throne — the same verse-form Solomon hears at `[27:26]`; what began without a basmala ends with a doxology.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"who attack you" (qātala-combat reciprocal):** 9:123's qualifier.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:190]` — "fight in the cause of Allah those who fight you, but do not commit aggression" — the corpus's paired limitation-verse; `[9:13]` — "initiated hostilities against you" — the surah's own earlier charge. The closing command repeats the opening's reciprocity frame.
* **"chapter" (sūrah):** the assay-noun.
  * **Rendering:** "Whenever a chapter is revealed" (9:124, 127) — the frame-word of the fear-verse 9:64.
  * **Elsewhere:** the other corpus "chapter" sites — `[2:23]`, `[10:38]`, `[47:20]` — all test or threaten (verified); see [Kalam note](../../02-knowledge/202610132005-k-kalam_allah_revelation_as_speech.md).
* **"sickness in hearts" (maraḍ):** 9:125's class-marker.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:10]` — "In their hearts is sickness, and Allah has increased their sickness"; `[47:20]` — "those in whose hearts is sickness looking at you with the look of someone fainting at death": the compound-increase grammar of 9:125 is Baqarah's. See [Nifaq note](../../02-knowledge/202610141300-k-nifaq_the_inside_trade.md).
* **"from among yourselves" + "Sublime Throne":** the closing pair.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:164]` — "He raised up among them a messenger from among themselves, who recites to them His revelations, and purifies them" — the exact insider-Messenger formula; `[27:26]` — "Allah — There is no god but He, the Lord of the Sublime Throne" — the identical dhikr spoken by the Queen of Sheba's creed (verified); the Throne itself spans `[2:255]` ("His Throne extends over the heavens and the earth") to 9:129.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (and among the very last, per index); 9:128–129 are classically called the surah's mercy-signature — the two names *raʾūf* and *raḥīm* gathered onto the Messenger's portrait. *Source: classical tafsir (on 9:128) — verify*; the corpus gives no occasion (observable); the identification of the underlying Arabic lexemes (*ʿazīz*, *harīṣ*, *raʾūf*) is standard classical lexicography — *Source: lexicons — verify*.
* **9:123 discipline:** the verse states "those of the disbelievers who attack you" — the restrictive clause is textual, not interpretive; the classical fiqh apparatus around it (offensive-defensive taxonomy) is Source: classical jurisprudence — verify.
* **Basmala:** the surah opened without it; its closing dhikr ("Allah is enough for me...") is the edition's final line of at-Tawbah — belongs to the numbering thread: [f-basmala note](../../01-fleeting/202610132006-f-basmala_numbering_in_this_edition.md).
