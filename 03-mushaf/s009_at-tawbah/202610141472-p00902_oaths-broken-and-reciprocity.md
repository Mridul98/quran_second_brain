# Oaths broken, reciprocity bounded: fight the leaders of disbelief until they desist (9:7–16)
**UID:** 202610141472
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 9:7–16 (Ruku 2 of 16 in at-Tawbah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[9:7]` – `[9:16]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku litigates the treaties: reciprocity holds only while the partners are upright ("As long as they are upright with you, be upright with them"), but the indicts are specific — kinship- and oath-less aggression, trading away revelations — and the combat command that follows is addressed to "the leaders of disbelief" "so that they may desist," with repentance restoring full brotherhood.
* **Placement:** Second ruku; the legal case-file for ruku 1's proclamation. Its closing verse (9:16) pivots the surah from external war to internal testing — who takes allies apart from Allah — feeding the hypocrite dossier of ruku 6–13.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[9:7]`** "How can there be a treaty with the polytheists on the part of Allah and His Messenger, except for those with whom you made a treaty at the Sacred Mosque? As long as they are upright with you, be upright with them. Allah loves the pious."
  * **Gloss:** The exception narrowed and the rule of moral reciprocity stated; "upright" (isteqāmā) binds both sides.
* **`[9:8]`** "How? Whenever they overcome you, they respect neither kinship nor treaty with you. They satisfy you with lip service, but their hearts refuse, and most of them are immoral."
  * **Gloss:** The indictment: victory makes them abandon even kin-claims; mouth-yes, heart-no — the dossier's first mouth–heart split in this surah.
* **`[9:9]`** "They traded away Allah's revelations for a cheap price, so they barred others from His path. How evil is what they did."
  * **Gloss:** The commercial idiom — the nifaq note's "losing trade" — applied to the treaty-breakers.
* **`[9:10]`** "Towards a believer they respect neither kinship nor treaty. These are the transgressors."
  * **Gloss:** 9:8's pair repeated verbatim — the rhetorical refrain marks the type.
* **`[9:11]`** "But if they repent, and perform the prayers, and give the obligatory charity, then they are your brethren in faith. We detail the revelations for a people who know."
  * **Gloss:** The release clause of 9:5 restated as *fraternity*: the same three acts convert enemy into brother.
* **`[9:12]`** "But if they violate their oaths after their pledge, and attack your religion, then fight the leaders of disbelief—they have no faith— so that they may desist."
  * **Gloss:** Counter-condition: oaths violated *after* pledging plus attack on religion; target is "the leaders of disbelief"; purpose clause — "so that they may desist."
* **`[9:13]`** "Will you not fight a people who violated their oaths, and planned to exile the Messenger, and initiated hostilities against you? Do you fear them? It is Allah you should fear, if you are believers."
  * **Gloss:** Three charges recited (oath-violation, the exile plot, beginning hostilities) — the war here is argued as *defensive*; the fear-relocation is the point.
* **`[9:14]`** "Fight them. Allah will punish them at your hands, and humiliate them, and help you against them, and heal the hearts of a believing people."
  * **Gloss:** Combat's stated effects are therapeutic: "heal the hearts" — the revelation itself elsewhere called "healing for what is in the hearts" (`[10:57]`).
* **`[9:15]`** "And He will remove the anger of their hearts. Allah redeems whomever He wills. Allah is Knowledgeable and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** Even the victims' rage is a patient; redemption (tawbah vocabulary) remains divine discretion.
* **`[9:16]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "Or do you think that you will be left alone, without Allah identifying which of you will strive, and take no supporters apart from Allah, His Messenger, and the believers?"
  * "Allah is well Aware of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The pivot: the testing question turns inward — striving (*jihād*) and ally-choice become the audit of the community itself.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ʿahd` (treaty) / `aymān` (oaths) / pledge:** the covenant vocabulary of the whole ruku.
  * **Rendering:** "treaty" `[9:7–10]`; "violate their oaths after their pledge" `[9:12]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[8:56]` treaty-violators "every time"; `[4:90]`, `[4:92]` treaty peoples owed compensation; `[48:10]` "Whoever breaks his pledge breaks it to his own loss."
* **"kinship":** corpus-unique pairing.
  * **Rendering:** "neither kinship nor treaty" only at `[9:8]`, `[9:10]` (all corpus hits, observable).
  * **Elsewhere:** the kin-claim protected elsewhere — `[4:36]` "be good to the parents, and the relatives" — is what 9:8 shows the oath-breakers despising.
* **"traded away" (sharā'):** 9:9's commercial idiom.
  * **Rendering:** "traded away Allah's revelations for a cheap price."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:174]`–`[2:175]` "exchange it for a small price" — the same losing trade; cf. 9:111's counter-transaction. See [Nifaq note](../../02-knowledge/202610141300-k-nifaq_the_inside_trade.md).
* **"brethren in faith":** 9:11's re-admission formula.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[49:10]` "The believers are brothers" — the same fraternity 9:11 grants to former combatants upon the triad of repentance-prayer-alms.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; classically connected to the post-Ḥudaybiyyah treaty system whose collapse the ruku recites — "the treaty at the Sacred Mosque" (9:7) and the exile plot (9:13) are read by classical tafsir as the Quraysh covenant and its violations. *Source: classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir, al-Tabari on 9:7–13) — verify.*
* **Text-claim discipline (9:12–13):** what the text says is a command to fight *named* parties — oath-violators who attacked the religion and initiated hostilities — with a desist-purpose clause; neutral peacetime combat is not what the verse states.
