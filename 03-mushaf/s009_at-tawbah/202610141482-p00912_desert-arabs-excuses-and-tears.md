# Desert-Arabs, excuses, and tears: the exempt weak, the sealed rich, and the Bedouin believers (9:90–99)
**UID:** 202610141482
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 9:90–99 (Ruku 12 of 16 in at-Tawbah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[9:90]` – `[9:99]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The census splits the excuse-market by capacity and heart: no blame on the weak, the sick, and the penniless-true ("in no way can the righteous be blamed"), and no blame — the surah's most cinematic line — on the men who came to be carried, found no mount, and left with eyes overflowing with tears; blame falls instead on the rich exemption-seekers whose hearts are sealed; and the Desert-Arabs get a three-way classification: excuse-makers, those who count giving as a fine, and those who count it as a means to Allah.
* **Placement:** Twelfth ruku; the dossier's fairness section — the text audits need before it condemns refusal.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[9:90]`** "Some of the Desert-Arabs came to make excuses, asking to be granted exemption, while those who were untrue to Allah and His Messenger stayed behind. A painful punishment will afflict those among them who disbelieved."
  * **Gloss:** The tribal delegation arrives with prepared excuses; "those among them who disbelieved" — the punishment hedges to the disbelief subset (observable).
* **`[9:91]`** "There is no blame on the weak, nor on the sick, nor on those who have nothing to give, provided they are true to Allah and His Messenger. In no way can the righteous be blamed. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The three exempt categories with one condition (truthfulness) — incapacity audited by loyalty, not by capability.
* **`[9:92]`** "Nor on those who approach you, wishing to ride with you, and you said, “I have nothing to carry you on.” So they went away, with their eyes overflowing with tears, sorrowing for not finding the means to spend."
  * **Gloss:** The tear-verses: willingness without means — grief as proof of intent; the surah's tenderest camera angle on the camp's edge.
* **`[9:93]`** "But blame is on those who ask you for exemption, although they are rich. They are content to be with those who stay behind. Allah has sealed their hearts, so they do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The real indictment: exemption requested *while rich* — capacity present, will absent; contentment with the stay-behind class is the seal's trigger in this grammar.
* **`[9:94]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "They present excuses to you when you return to them. Say, “Do not offer excuses; we do not trust you; Allah has informed us of you.”"
  * "And Allah will watch your actions, and so will the Messenger; then you will be returned to the Knower of the Invisible and the Visible, and He will inform you of what you used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The post-return scene: excuses pre-scripted, rejected on epistemic grounds — the community is not the judge; the unseen-known God is.
* **`[9:95]`** "They will swear to you by Allah, when you return to them, that you may leave them alone. So leave them alone. They are a disgrace, and their destiny is Hell; a reward for what they used to earn."
  * **Gloss:** Sworn appeasement met with a nominal truce ("leave them alone") whose real sentence runs behind it: disgrace, Hell, earned wage.
* **`[9:96]`** "They will swear to you that you may accept them. But even if you accept them, Allah does not accept the wicked people."
  * **Gloss:** Acceptance's two counters — human pardon possible, divine acceptance closed for *al-fusūq* as profession.
* **`[9:97]`** "The Desert-Arabs are the most steeped in disbelief and hypocrisy, and the most likely to ignore the limits that Allah revealed to His Messenger. Allah is Knowing and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The sociology claim: nomadship's distance from revelation's limits as the surah explains Bedouin nifaq — its most general sentence about a whole class.
* **`[9:98]`** "And among the Desert-Arabs are those who consider their contribution to be a fine. And they wait for a reversal of your fortunes. Upon them will fall the cycle of misfortune. Allah is Hearing and Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** Giving as tax-loss resentment ("a fine" — the same word-group as 9:58's critics), plus waiting for the community's downfall.
* **`[9:99]`** "Yet among the Desert-Arabs are those who believe in Allah and the Last Day, and consider their contribution to be a means towards Allah, and the prayers of the Messenger. Surely it will draw them closer, and Allah will admit them into His mercy. Allah is Forgiving and Compassionate."
  * **Gloss:** The class split completed: the *same* contribution as fine or as near-bring (*qurba*) — the economics of intention decide; "the prayers of the Messenger" — his forgiveness-prayer as a wanted asset (contrast 9:80's refusal for the hardened).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"Desert-Arabs" (aʿrāb):** the class-portrait noun.
  * **Rendering:** `[9:90]`, `[9:97–99]`, `[9:101]`, `[9:120]` — the dossier's tribal ledger.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[48:11]`, `[48:16]` — the lagging Desert-Arabs' excuse formula; `[49:14]` — "The Desert-Arabs say, “We have believed.” Say, “You have not believed; but say, ‘We have submitted’" — the same class in the corpus's bedouin-realism register (verified as a single rendering-cluster).
* **"tears":** 9:92's grief-proof.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:83]` — "you see their eyes overflowing with tears, as they recognize the truth in it" — the corpus's only other "eyes overflowing with tears" construction: tears as the sign of recognized truth, shared verbatim (observable near-twin).
* **"sealed their hearts":** 9:93 (↔ 9:87) — see [Nifaq note](../../02-knowledge/202610141300-k-nifaq_the_inside_trade.md); the formula's archetypal site `[2:7]`, with `[47:16]` as the in-community parallel (verified).
* **"Knower of the Invisible and the Visible" (ʿālim al-ghayb wa-sh-shahāda):** 9:94's court-of-appeal name.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[62:8]` — the identical "returned to the Knower...He will inform you" close; `[32:6]` — the name paired with Power and Mercy.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan/Tabuk horizon; 9:92's tearful penniless are classically named individuals (the seven who wept at the Prophet's "I find nothing to carry you on"); 9:97's generalization about the Desert-Arabs is read by classical commentators as sociological, not ethnic — *Source: classical tafsir (reports on 9:92, commentary on 9:97) — verify*.
* **Text-claim discipline:** 9:91–92 *exempt* the incapable by text; the reading that this ruku harshens only for the *able and content* is observable in its own syntax — the blame verse (9:93) names wealth as the aggravator.
