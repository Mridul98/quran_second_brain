# Immunity proclaimed: the disavowal and its four months of grace (9:1–6)
**UID:** 202610141471
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 9:1–6 (Ruku 1 of 16 in at-Tawbah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[9:1]` – `[9:6]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The only surah that opens without a basmala opens mid-decree: a declaration of immunity (*barā'a*) to the treaty-making polytheists, bounded by a four-month grace and then by three precise exceptions — honored treaties, the repentant pray-and-alms community, and safe-conduct for anyone who merely wants to hear the Word of Allah.
* **Placement:** Opening ruku. It sets the treaty-dissolution frame that ruku 2 (`[9:7–16]`) litigates, and its "Allah loves the righteous" refrain (`[9:4]`) is the surah's taqwa anchor.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[9:1]`** "A declaration of immunity from Allah and His Messenger to the polytheists with whom you had made a treaty."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's first word is a legal notice, not an invocation: *barā'a* — release, disavowal — served on treaty partners.
* **`[9:2]`** "So travel the land for four months, and know that you cannot escape Allah, and that Allah will disgrace the disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** A bounded grace period: four months of free movement before enforcement; "cannot escape" is repeated verbatim at `[9:3]` as the surah's hammer clause.
* **`[9:3]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "And a proclamation from Allah and His Messenger to the people on the day of the Greater Pilgrimage, that Allah has disowned the polytheists, and so did His Messenger."
  * "If you repent, it will be better for you. But if you turn away, know that you cannot escape Allah."
  * "And announce to those who disbelieve a painful punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The announcement is dated — the day of the Greater Pilgrimage (the corpus's only use of this name) — and immediately offers the exit of repentance before the punishment notice.
* **`[9:4]`** "Except for those among the polytheists with whom you had made a treaty, and did not violate any of its terms, nor aided anyone against you. So fulfill the treaty with them to the end of its term. Allah loves the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The first exception: treaties kept in full are honored to term — dissolution is only for violators; the verse ends on the taqwa refrain.
* **`[9:5]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "When the Sacred Months have passed, kill the polytheists wherever you find them. And capture them, and besiege them, and lie in wait for them at every ambush."
  * "But if they repent, and perform the prayers, and pay the alms, then let them go their way."
  * "Allah is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The enforcement clause (the "sword verse" of later nomenclature) is textually sequenced *after* the Sacred Months and *bracketed* by the repentance–prayer–alms exit and a forgiveness-as mercy closing; the corpus itself conditions and releases the command in the same line.
* **`[9:6]`** "And if anyone of the polytheists asks you for protection, give him protection so that he may hear the Word of Allah; then escort him to his place of safety. That is because they are a people who do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The second exception: asylum (*ijāra*) — combat does not silence proclamation; a hostile combatant may hear the Word and be escorted home unharmed.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`barā'a` ("A declaration of immunity"):** release, disavowal of obligation.
  * **Rendering:** "declaration of immunity" `[9:1]`; the verb family "disowned" at `[9:3]` and of Abraham at `[9:114]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** "immunity" occurs in the corpus only here and `[54:43]` ("Or do you have immunity in the scriptures?"); cf. `[2:167]` followers disowning leaders — the root's forensic use.
* **"Sacred Months":** the inviolable truce months.
  * **Rendering:** "Sacred Months" at `[9:5]`; defined at `[9:36]` ("of which four are sacred").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:194]` "The sacred month for the sacred month"; `[5:2]`, `[5:97]` "Sacred Month" — retaliation regulated by the months across the corpus.
* **"Word of Allah":** 9:6's asylum object — hearing before verdict.
  * **Rendering:** "the Word of Allah" here; "the Word of Allah is the Highest" at `[9:40]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:75]` — "when some of them used to hear the Word of Allah" (then distort it): the refusal type that 9:6's hearing-then-escort protects. See [Kalam Allah note](../../02-knowledge/202610132005-k-kalam_allah_revelation_as_speech.md).
* **"repent" (tawbah cluster):** the surah's namesake verb at its first turns.
  * **Rendering:** "If you repent" `[9:3]`, "if they repent" `[9:5]`, `[9:11]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:17]` "Repentance is available from Allah for those who commit evil out of ignorance" — the standing door this surah keeps reopening.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Treaty twin:** `[8:56]` — "Those of them with whom you made a treaty, but they violate their agreement every time" — the violation category 9:4's exception excludes.
* **Combat parallel:** `[2:191]` — "kill them wherever you overtake them, and expel them from where they had expelled you. Oppression is more serious than murder. But do not fight them at the Sacred Mosque, unless they fight you there" — the paired command with its own Sacred-Mosque restriction.
* **Within surah:** `[9:12–13]` — the oaths-violators named as the actual addressees; `[9:36]` — "fight the polytheists collectively, as they fight you collectively" restates 9:5's reciprocity inside the calendar argument.
* **Within surah:** `[9:114]` — Abraham's disavowal of his polytheist father is the precedent-figure of 9:1's *barā'a*.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan, classically among the very last revealed (see index); the proclamation is dated in-text to "the day of the Greater Pilgrimage."
* **Asbab / narrative context:** classical reports place the announcement in the Year 9 delegation season, delivered under Abu Bakr's command and proclaimed by ʿAli at Mina; 9:5's addressees are read in classical tafsir as the oath-breaking polytheists of the prior treaties, not all polytheists as such — and 9:5's scope is the locus of a classical abrogation debate with `[2:190]`. *Source: classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir, al-Tabari on 9:1–5) and transmitted reports — verify.* What the text itself says is narrower than later "sword verse" shorthand: conditions (Sacred Months elapsed), addressees (treaty-making polytheists of 9:1), exceptions (9:4, 9:6), and a full release clause (9:5's center).
* **Basmala:** this surah carries none in this or any Mushaf tradition — see [Basmala is unnumbered in this edition except 1:1](../../01-fleeting/202610132006-f-basmala_numbering_in_this_edition.md).
