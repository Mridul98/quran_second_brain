# Surah 9: REPENTANCE (at-Tawbah)
**UID:** 202610141487

**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Medinan — classically among the very last surahs revealed (revelation order 128–129 in the transmitted classical enumeration, the surah that closes the list). *Source: transmitted classical ordering lists (at-Tawbah standardly placed at the end, sometimes jointly with Yunus as 128–129) — agent labeling per classical lists — verify.*
* **The missing basmala:** this surah carries **no basmala** — here or in any Mushaf tradition; it is the only corpus file with no `Basmala:` header line (`rg -c "^Basmala" corpus/s009_at-tawbah.md` → 0 hits, vs. 1 each in s008/s010; observable corpus fact). Transmitted reports on ʿUthmān's collection describe the pairing of at-Tawbah with al-Anfāl without the separator basmala — a known editorial point, not a gap: the surah opens mid-sentence, with a proclamation of *immunity*. See [Basmala is unnumbered in this edition except 1:1](../../01-fleeting/202610132006-f-basmala_numbering_in_this_edition.md).
* **Size:** 129 ayahs, 16 ruku (Hanafi/Idad-e-Quran partition per `data/ruku_map.json`).
* **Central theme:** The dissolution. The surah executes the end of the polytheists' treaties (9:1–13) — immunity proclaimed, Sacred Months honored, breached oaths answered — and then rebuilds the community on two currencies: **repentance** (its name; 9:3, 5, 11, 104, 112, 117–118) and **wealth rightly spent**, the eight ports of charity (9:60) set against the hoarded gold branded in the Fire (9:34–35). Its longest sustained project is the **hypocrite dossier expanded** (9:42–49, 64–68, 90–97, 101 — see [Nifaq: the inside trade](../../02-knowledge/202610141300-k-nifaq_the_inside_trade.md)), anchored in the **broken-pledge covenant frame** (9:75–77, 9:111) and the trade vocabulary of 9:34–41. The Tabuk reluctant ones (9:81–97) get the dossier's longest case file; and the surah that began by disowning the polytheists ends by describing a Messenger who cannot bear their suffering (9:128–129).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Dissolution and its exceptions (ruku 1–2):** immunity proclaimed with a four-month grace (9:1–3), the honest treaties expressly honored to term (9:4, 7), the Sword context bounded by Sacred Months, asylum, and the exit clause of repentance–prayer–zakat (9:5–6); then the oath-breakers named and fought "so that they may desist" (9:12–13).
* **Who is the community made of (ruku 3–4):** the mosque-attenders defined by belief, prayer, charity, and fear of none but Allah (9:17–19); kinship reordered under loyalty to Allah (9:23–24); Hunayn as the object lesson that numbers save no one and serenity is sent, not earned (9:25–27); the Sanctuary purified and the due tax set for the People of the Book (9:28–29).
* **Creed and calendar (ruku 5–6):** son-claims and clerical lordship corrected under tawhid (9:30–33); the hoarders and the twelve-month order of "the correct religion" (9:34–37); the Tabuk summons "Mobilize, light or heavy" against the love of the near life (9:38–42).
* **The hypocrite dossier, case by case (ruku 7–12):** exemptions requested and granted too soon (9:43–49); laziness in prayer and grudgingness in spending profiled (9:54); the eight recipients of charity answer the withholding hands (9:60); mockery, the cursed party, and Eden's two promises (9:64–72); the reneged pledge penalized with hypocrisy in the heart (9:75–77); the sluggards of the heat and the sealed Desert-Arabs — balanced by the weeping penniless and the honest Bedouin believers (9:81–99).
* **Repentance infrastructure (ruku 13–15):** the Pioneers' pleasure mutual with Allah (9:100); the confessors whose contributions the Prophet is ordered to *receive* — zakat as the machinery of tawbah (9:102–105); the mosque of harm dissected (9:107–110); the Purchase verse (9:111) and the three pardons (9:117–118); "be with the sincere" and the delegation for religious understanding (9:119–122).
* **Closing (ruku 16):** defensive-fight command (9:123); the chapter that splits the room by its effect (9:124–127); the surah ends on the Messenger's grief for the wayward and the basmala-less self-sufficing dhikr: "Allah is enough for me... Lord of the Sublime Throne" (9:128–129).
