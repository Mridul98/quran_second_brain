# The Tabuk sluggards: "Do not venture out in the heat" — and no prayer over them ever (9:81–89)
**UID:** 202610141481
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 9:81–89 (Ruku 11 of 16 in at-Tawbah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[9:81]` – `[9:89]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The expedition's verdict scene: the stay-behinds rejoice at sitting behind the Messenger and hate to strive with wealth and lives, saying "Do not venture out in the heat" — answered by the surah's hottest line: "The Fire of Hell is much hotter"; the rulings that follow are permanent separations (no joint future campaigns, no funeral prayer, no graveside), while the Messenger and the sincere believers get the gardens — the two endings seated at the same campfire.
* **Placement:** Eleventh ruku; the applied verdict of the excuse-business (ruku 7) and the pre-ruku to the Desert-Arab census (ruku 12).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[9:81]`** "Those who stayed behind rejoiced at their staying behind the Messenger of Allah. And they hated to strive with their wealth and their lives in Allah’s way. And they said, “Do not venture out in the heat.” Say, “The Fire of Hell is much hotter, if they only understood.”"
  * **Gloss:** Joy-in-absence, hatred-of-striving, and the weather excuse — met with comparative eschatology: the heat they fled is a fraction of the heat they are walking into.
* **`[9:82]`** "Let them laugh a little, and weep much; in recompense for what they used to earn."
  * **Gloss:** The measured exchange-rate of laughter and weeping; "earn" (*kasab*) — the surplus vocabulary of the whole surah.
* **`[9:83]`** "If Allah brings you back to a party of them, and they ask your permission to go out, say, “You will not go out with me, ever, nor will you ever fight an enemy with me. You were content to sit back the first time, so sit back with those who stay behind.”"
  * **Gloss:** The standing ruling: readmission to the war-line refused forever, sentenced by their own first-time contentment.
* **`[9:84]`** "You are never to pray over anyone of them who dies, nor are you to stand at his graveside. They rejected Allah and His Messenger, and died while they were sinners."
  * **Gloss:** The funeral ban: both intercessory prayer and graveside presence prohibited — the corpus's only "pray over"/"graveside" pair (observable); social death extended past biological death.
* **`[9:85]`** "Do not let their possessions and their children impress you. Allah desires to torment them through them in this world, and their souls expire while they are disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** 9:55's verbatim twin (near-exact: "desires" for "intends") — the success-as-torment refrain doubled for emphasis (observable).
* **`[9:86]`** "When a chapter is revealed, stating: “Believe in Allah and strive with His Messenger,” the prominent among them ask you for exemption. They say, “Allow us to stay with those who stay behind.”"
  * **Gloss:** Revelation as pressure test: the new chapter extracts exemption-requests from the notables (*aqbāb*) — petition to be permanently with the losers.
* **`[9:87]`** "They prefer to be with those who stay behind. Their hearts were sealed, so they do not understand."
  * **Gloss:** Preference named as diagnosis; the seal — 2:7's grammar — now applied inside the community, with "do not understand" as its symptom.
* **`[9:88]`** "But the Messenger and those who believe with him struggle with their possessions and their lives. These have deserved the good things. These are the successful."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-party introduced with the same two currencies — possessions and lives — spent, not hoarded; note "struggle" here vs. "strive" at 9:81 for the same root (rendering drift, observable).
* **`[9:89]`** "Allah has prepared for them gardens beneath which rivers flow, wherein they will abide forever. That is the great victory."
  * **Gloss:** The gardens formula and the victory-word (*fawẓ*) matching 9:72's "supreme achievement" — the two parties' two preparations.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"stayed behind / sit back" (quʿūd cluster):** the ruku's leitmotif verb.
  * **Rendering:** "Those who stayed behind" (9:81, 87), "sit back" (9:83), "stay with those who stay behind" (9:86).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[48:11]` — "The Desert-Arabs who remained behind will say...our belongings and our families have preoccupied us"; `[48:16]` — "Say to the Desert-Arabs who lagged behind" — the Hudaybiyyah-generation precedent of the same sin (see [Nifaq note](../../02-knowledge/202610141300-k-nifaq_the_inside_trade.md)).
* **"heat" (ḥarr):** the excuse and the counter-image.
  * **Rendering:** "Do not venture out in the heat" / "The Fire of Hell is much hotter" — one word doing excuse-work and eschatology-work (observable wordplay of the surah).
* **"pray over" + "graveside":** the funeral ban's two objects.
  * **Elsewhere:** corpus-unique (single hits, `[9:84]` — observable); contrast `[63:5]` — the hypocrites *offered* the Messenger's forgiveness-prayer as cover; here it is barred.
* **"seal" (khatam):** 9:87's diagnosis.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:7]` "Allah has set a seal on their hearts"; `[47:16]` — "whose hearts Allah has sealed" — the seal's corpus grammar: consequence of preference, not pre-creation decree.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the scene is classically the Tabuk expedition (Year 9) — the heat line quoted verbatim from camp complaints about the season's march. *Source: classical tafsir and campaign reports (Ibn Ishaq) — verify*; the corpus itself dates nothing (observable).
* **Text-claim discipline:** 9:83–84 are addressed rulings *to the Prophet about named individuals* ("a party of them...those who stayed behind"), not general commands about dead relatives; the ban's extension by jurists to categories is classical fiqh. *Source: classical jurisprudence — verify.*
