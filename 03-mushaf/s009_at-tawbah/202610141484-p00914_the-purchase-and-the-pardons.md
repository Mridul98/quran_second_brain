# The Purchase: Allah buys the believers' lives — and pardons the hour of difficulty (9:111–118)
**UID:** 202610141484
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 9:111–118 (Ruku 14 of 16 in at-Tawbah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[9:111]` – `[9:118]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's transaction summit: Allah *purchases* the believers' lives and properties, price — Paradise, contract cited across Torah, Gospel, and Quran, and no one more true to promise than He; the buyers' eight-attribute profile follows (9:112); then the exclusions (no forgiveness-prayer for the polytheist dead, Abraham's own exception handled) and the three historical pardons: the Prophet's hour of difficulty, the Emigrants and Supporters, and the three left behind whose earth closed in — tawbah as the surah's last institutional word.
* **Placement:** Fourteenth ruku; the covenant-economy capstone of ruku 1–13's broken-trade anatomy: the one deal that cannot default.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[9:111]`** — four clause-chunks:
  * "Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and their properties in exchange for Paradise."
  * "They fight in Allah’s way, and they kill and get killed."
  * "It is a promise binding on Him in the Torah, and the Gospel, and the Quran. And who is more true to his promise than Allah?"
  * "So rejoice in making such an exchange—that is the supreme triumph."
  * **Gloss:** The divine *shirāʾ* inverted: God is the buyer, lives are the goods, Paradise the coin, His promise the escrow — counter-image of 9:9's "traded away...for a cheap price" and 9:77's penalized pledge; symmetric warfare clause ("kill and get killed"); "rejoice" addressed to the deal itself.
* **`[9:112]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "Those who repent, those who worship, those who praise, those who journey, those who kneel, those who bow down, those who advocate righteousness and forbid evil, and those who keep Allah’s limits—"
  * "give good news to the believers."
  * **Gloss:** The buyers' profile in eight pen-name attributes (repentance first — the surah's namesake opens the list); the limits-keepers end where 9:97's limit-ignorant Bedouin excuse-makers began; the benediction: "give good news."
* **`[9:113]`** "It is not for the Prophet and those who believe to ask forgiveness for the polytheists, even if they are near relatives, after it has become clear to them that they are people of Hellfire."
  * **Gloss:** The intercession boundary drawn on *clarity* as trigger — the prohibition activates "after it has become clear."
* **`[9:114]`** "Abraham asked forgiveness for his father only because of a promise he had made to him. But when it became clear to him that he was an enemy of Allah, he disowned him. Abraham was kind and clement."
  * **Gloss:** The Abraham exception handled: a prior promise, not permanent practice; disavowal follows clarification; and the character note softens the precedent — kind, clement.
* **`[9:115]`** "Allah would never lead a people astray, after He had guided them, until He makes clear to them what they should guard against."
  * **Gloss:** The justice theorem behind 9:113: misguidance only post-clarification — God's straying-penalty runs on notice.
* **`[9:116]`** "To Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth. He gives life, and He causes death. And besides Allah, you have neither protector, nor supporter."
  * **Gloss:** Sovereignty formula (life-and-death clause) — the Purchase's ground: He owns what He buys.
* **`[9:117]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "Allah has redeemed the Prophet, and the Emigrants, and the Supporters—those who followed him in the hour of difficulty—after the hearts of some of them almost swerved."
  * "Then He pardoned them."
  * "He is Kind towards them, Compassionate."
  * **Gloss:** The pardon decree: *tawbah* rendered "redeemed" — the Prophet, Migrants, and Supporters, including the near-swervers; the "hour of difficulty" classically the Tabuk march (and Hunayn's retreat for the Prophet's own redemption).
* **`[9:118]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "Also towards the three who were left behind."
  * "Then, when the earth, as vast as it is, closed in on them, and their very souls closed in on them, and they realized that there was no refuge from Allah, except in Him, He redeemed them, so that they may repent."
  * "Allah is the Redeemer, the Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The three named-un-named: claustrophobia of conscience — the earth and their own souls contracting (9:25's Hunayn-image turned redemptive); refuge-only-in-Him as the discovery; "so that they may repent" — redemption is for repentance, not despite it.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"purchased" (sharā'):** 9:111 — the corpus's only divine-purchase verb.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:207]` — "he who sells himself seeking Allah’s approval"; `[61:10]` — "a trade that will save you from a painful torment"; `[35:29]` — believers "expect a trade that will not fail" — the honest side of the surah's two-economy ledger; see [Infraq note](../../02-knowledge/202610132004-k-infraq_vs_riba_two_economies.md) and [Nifaq note](../../02-knowledge/202610141300-k-nifaq_the_inside_trade.md) §3.
* **"promise binding" (ʿad):** 9:111's escrow.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[48:10]` — "whoever fulfills his covenant with Allah, He will grant him a great reward"; cf. `[9:77]` — the broken-promise penalty from the other side. See [Covenant note](../../02-knowledge/202610132002-k-covenant_mithaq.md).
* **"redeem / Redeemer" (tawbah-verb as divine act):** 9:117–118 renderings ("redeemed", "the Redeemer") — same root as the surah's name, and the verb of `[9:15]` ("Allah redeems whomever He wills") and `[9:27]` ("Allah will relent towards whomever He wills"); the rendering of divine *tawbah* as redemption is the translator's consistent choice (observable in corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:17]` — "These—Allah will relent towards them"; `[25:71]` — "Whoever repents and acts righteously—has inclined towards Allah with repentance"; `[66:8]` — "O you who believe! Repent to Allah with sincere repentance" — the repentance family (verified).
* **"keep Allah's limits":** 9:112's closing attribute.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:229]` — "These are Allah’s limits, so do not transgress them"; `[9:97]` — the Desert-Arabs "most likely to ignore the limits": the same limit-vocabulary, opposite verdicts (observable).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within surah:** `[9:9]`/`[9:77]` ↔ `[9:111]` — cheap-price trades and penalized pledges answered by the trade that cannot default; "the supreme triumph" (*fawẓ*) ties 9:111 to 9:100/9:89 — the victory-word's three sites.
* **Within surah:** `[9:25]` ↔ `[9:118]` — the narrowed land of the retreating army becomes the closing-in earth of the three penitents who come back.
* **Parallel:** `[60:4]` — Abraham's disavowal of his father's polytheism, *with the exception of his forgiveness-promise*, quoted verbatim as the precedent 9:114 narrates — the two surahs cross-cite.
* **Guidance:** `[9:115]` — clarification-before-straying mirrors `[17:15]` "nor do We ever punish until We have sent a messenger" — the notice-doctrine twin.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; 9:117's "hour of difficulty" and 9:118's "three" are classically Tabuk (the three famous absentees — Kaʿb and companions — boycotted until the clouds lifted). *Source: classical tafsir and sahih transmitted reports (on 9:118, Ibn Kathir/al-Tabari) — verify*; the corpus never names them (observable).
* **9:113 discipline:** the text prohibits forgiveness-prayer *after clarification* of enmity; the classical reading scopes it to the deceased-polytheist case of 9:113–114's Abraham-exception. *Source: classical tafsir — verify.*
