# The eight ports of charity — and the prophet who is all ears (9:60–66)
**UID:** 202610141478
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 9:60–66 (Ruku 8 of 16 in at-Tawbah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[9:60]` – `[9:66]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's only pure legislation: the zakat distribution charter with exactly eight named categories — "an obligation from Allah, an all-knowing, most-wise" — set inside a portrait of men whose problem with the Prophet is precisely that he listens: "He is all ears," a slur turned, in 9:61's reply, into his mercy credentials.
* **Placement:** Eighth ruku. 9:60 is the answer to the hoarding of 9:34–35 and the grudging spending of 9:54; the ruku then pivots to the camp's mocking danger (9:61–66).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[9:60]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "Charities are for the poor, and the destitute, and those who administer them, and for reconciling hearts, and for freeing slaves, and for those in debt, and in the path of Allah, and for the traveler in need—"
  * "an obligation from Allah."
  * "Allah is All-Knowing, Most Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The eight ports: two poverty-words (poor, destitute), the collector's wage, diplomacy-payouts, manumission, debt-relief, holy-way expenses, and the stranded traveler; the suffix "in need" on the traveler renders *ibn as-sabīl* closed-circuit; the charter is stamped *farīḍa* — obligation, not suggestion.
* **`[9:61]`** — three clause-chunks:
  * "And among them are those who insult the Prophet, and say, “He is all ears.”"
  * "Say, “He listens for your own good. He believes in Allah, and trusts the believers, and is mercy for those of you who believe.”"
  * "Those who insult the Messenger of Allah will have a painful penalty."
  * **Gloss:** "All ears" — the caricature of an unguarded leader — is re-described as function: he listens *for your good*; his three traits: believes, credits the believers, is a mercy-source.
* **`[9:62]`** "They swear to you by Allah to please you. But it is more proper for them to please Allah and His Messenger, if they are believers."
  * **Gloss:** The people-pleasing oath: the dossier's transactional piety again.
* **`[9:63]`** "Do they not know that whoever opposes Allah and His Messenger, will have the Fire of Hell, abiding in it forever? That is the supreme disgrace."
  * **Gloss:** The *muḥādda* (opposition) verdict — 58:5/58:20's formula in legal register.
* **`[9:64]`** "The hypocrites worry lest a chapter may be revealed about them, informing them of what is in their hearts. Say, “Go on mocking; Allah will bring out what you fear.”"
  * **Gloss:** The fear that a *sūrah* will arrive about them — revelation as the hypocrite's specific dread; the imperative irony: keep mocking, the exposure is automatic.
* **`[9:65]`** "If you ask them, they will say, “We were just joking and playing.” Say, “Were you making jokes about Allah, His revelations, and His Messenger?”"
  * **Gloss:** The apology defense ("joking") met with the three-term indictment: Allah, revelations, Messenger — the mockery's real objects.
* **`[9:66]`** "Do not apologize. You have disbelieved after your belief. If We pardon some of you, We will punish others, because they are guilty."
  * **Gloss:** No excuse accepted; the status-change named (belief→unbelief); divine discretion split within the very group — pardon possible for some, the dossier's final anti-caste note.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"Charities" (ṣadaqāt):** zakat's own name in 9:60 (also `[9:79]`, `[9:103–105]` use "contributions").
  * **Rendering:** "Charities are for..." — the plural noun governing eight datives.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:177]` — the righteous man gives wealth "to near relatives, and orphans, and the needy, and the homeless, and the beggars, and for the freeing of slaves"; `[4:36]` and `[5:96]` — the traveler's protected status — the 9:60 categories are the corpus's own earlier marks assembled.
* **"reconciling hearts" (muʾallafatu qulūbuhum):** the diplomacy-port.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[8:63]` — "He united their hearts. Had you spent everything on earth, you would not have united their hearts" — heart-uniting is divine work the charter budgets for.
* **"all ears":** the insult.
  * **Elsewhere:** corpus-unique (single hit, `[9:61]`, observable).
* **"chapter" (sūrah-singular):** the hypocrites' fear-object.
  * **Rendering:** "a chapter may be revealed about them."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:23]`, `[10:38]` — produce a chapter like it; `[47:20]` — when "a decisive chapter is sent down" and fighting is in it, the sick-heart look faints: the same word as a pressure gauge of faith. ## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; classical reports attach 9:65–66 to a saying uttered during the Tabuk march ("he was just joking") — *Source: classical tafsir (reports on 9:65, Ibn Kathir/al-Tabari) — verify*.
* **9:60 reading:** the eight categories are textually exhaustive in their syntax ("Charities are *for*...") — the limitation to them is the standard juristic inference (*Source: classical fiqh consensus — verify*); the corpus itself offers the list and the *farīḍa* stamp.
