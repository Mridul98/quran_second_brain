# Surah 107: ASSISTANCE (al-Ma'un)

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Meccan per the classical type-lists (the batch's assignment); a minority assignment of this surah to the Medinan period is preserved in some transmitted lists — *Source: transmitted lists — verify*. Corpus carries no classification data (header fields only — rg-verified). Revelation order ≈ 17 (list-dependent) — *transmitted tables — verify*.
* **Size:** 7 ayahs, 1 ruku (ruku_map global 549 — verified). Two halves of four and three lines, welded by a "So": vv1–3 define, vv4–7 indict.
* **Title:** al-Ma'un — the corpus header "ASSISTANCE" tracks `[107:7]` "And withhold the assistance."; the last word names the surah (as in 104, whose title is its first word's root). Classical lexica widen *ma'un* to small, everyday services and things no one withholds except from stinginess — *Source: classical lexicon on 107:7 — verify; Itani's single-word "the assistance" is observable in corpus.*
* **Central theme:** Religion denied is diagnosed socially before it is named cre­dally: the denier of the religion (v1) is identified by what he does to the orphan (v2) and by what he never urges — feeding the poor (v3). Then the "So woe" turns inward to the worship-community: prayer that is heedless (v4–5), performed for audience (v6), and micro-withholding (v7) is the same denial wearing liturgical dress. The surah's argument: there is no orthodoxy without orphan-and-poor audit; ritual is the second test, not the first.
* **Reading hint:** count the pronouns — "him… he… his" (vv1–3) then "those who pray… their prayers… them" (vv4–7): one man sketched, a class pronounced; the woe-line "So woe to those who pray" (107:4) is the corpus's only woe aimed at worshippers as such (rg-verified against the full "Woe" inventory).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **v1 — the interrogation:** "Have you considered him who denies the religion?" — the consideration-formula (same opener as 105:1 and the ~20-site "Have you considered" family, rg-verified) invites a creed-answer and will get a conduct-answer.
* **vv2–3 — the definition by conduct:** "It is he who mistreats the orphan. / And does not encourage the feeding of the poor." — the relative pronoun "It is he who" (dhālika) makes v2–3 the *content* of denial: disbelief located in social behavior, not in a stated disbelief.
* **vv4–5 — the woe at the inside:** "So woe to those who pray. / Those who are heedless of their prayers." — the "So" welds the two halves: the pray-ers belong to the denier's line; heedlessness (sahw/sāhūn) is not unbelief but worship's absence-of-presence.
* **v6 — the audience effect:** "Those who put on the appearance." — the performance clause: their prayer has spectators where it should have God.
* **v7 — the micro-withholding:** "And withhold the assistance." — the ledger closes on small change: the sin-scale drops from denying religion to withholding ma'un — the two ends are equated by the list's own flatness (*Source: structural observation on corpus renderings*).

## 5b. Batch-Return Verification Flags (this index)
* **Hint renderings corrected (all rg-verified against `corpus/s107_al-ma-un.md`):** "the denier-of-Judgment" → "**him who denies the religion**" — Itani renders *dīn* as "religion" here (contrast `[1:4]` "the Day of Judgment" and `[83:11]` "the Day of Reckoning" for cognate dīn-uses; the Day-denial reading of 107:1 is classical exegesis, not this translation's surface). "repulses the orphan" → "**mistreats the orphan**"; "no urging feeding of the poor" → "**does not encourage the feeding of the poor**"; "who are seen-off" → "**Those who put on the appearance.**"; "withhold the small kindnesses" → "**withhold the assistance**" — "small kindnesses" is classical gloss, not Itani.
* **Twin-verse verification — 89/90 (as the hint suspected):** `[89:17–18]` "But you do not honor the orphan. / And you do not urge the feeding of the poor." — 107:2–3's near-verbatim negative twin (honor/encourage drift on the second line; "urge" ≈ "encourage" — rg-verified). `[90:13–16]` the ascent's positive feeding ("the feeding on a day of hunger / An orphan near of kin / Or a destitute in the dust") — the ledger the denier fails (all rg-verified). Also verified: `[93:9–10]` "Therefore, do not mistreat the orphan. / Nor rebuff the seeker." — the imperative form of 107:2's indictment.
* **"Encourage vs urge" drift flagged:** 107:3 "does not encourage the feeding of the poor" vs 89:18 "do not urge the feeding of the poor" — Itani runs two English verbs across the identical Arabic construction (* Source: observable rendering drift — verify against a glossary of the translation's choices*). Quote each site in its own wording; never cross-quote.
* **"Have you considered" family verified:** 107:1 belongs to the corpus's consideration-interrogative cluster — 20+ sites including `[105:1]`, `[89:6]` (as "seen"), `[53:19]`, `[59:11]` (rg-verified) — and the rarer "Have you considered HIM who…" singular-framing subcluster runs exactly four sites: `[2:258]`, `[45:23]`, `[53:33]`, `[107:1]` (rg-verified).
* **"So woe to…" sites verified:** the pattern runs four sites — `[2:79]` (fabricated scripture), `[19:37]`, `[51:60]` (disbelievers), and `[107:4]`. What is unique (full "Woe" inventory grepped) is the *object*: 107:4 is the corpus's only woe pronounced on those who **pray** — and unlike the 77/83 Day-tagged woes against deniers, this woe falls inside the worship-community. 2:79 is the closest sibling: woe at a religious *profession* (scripture-forging), matched to woe at a religious *performance* here.
* **Revelation-order note:** the hint's "Meccan, cluster 104–108" holds per type-lists; 107 is the surah in this cluster whose classification is most disputed in transmitted lists — flagged, not resolved.
