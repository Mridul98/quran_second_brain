# The Double-Repetition Disavowal: Four Denials and the Partition of Ways (109:1–6)
**UID:** 202610142362
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 109:1–6 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Kafirun)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[109:1]` – `[109:6]` (6 ayahs; global ruku 551 of 556 — verified)
* **Thesis:** Answering a worship-swap with pure severance: two mirror-pairs of denial ("I do not worship what you worship / Nor do you worship what I worship" then the same symmetry re-run with "serve") leave both parties exactly where they were, and v6 draws the line as ownership — "You have your way, and I have my way."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1; the FIRST door of the Quran's closing six-surah unit (109–114). The unit's other half (112–114) states the positive creed and the refuge; 109 is its negative pole — whom-not-and-what-not to worship. In the surah-sequence it follows al-Kawthar's "your enemy — he is the cut-off" (108:3): disavowal is the next word.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[109:1]`** "Say, “O disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** The commissioned address — "Say" (qul) opens three of the six closing surahs (109, 112, 113, 114 — the say-formula brackets, rg-verified). The vocative names the category, not persons; maximum distance without a single insult. Itani's quotation marks OPEN here and CLOSE only at 109:6 — the whole surah is one utterance (observable typography).
* **`[109:2]`–`[109:3]`** "I do not worship what you worship. / Nor do you worship what I worship."
  * **Gloss:** Mirror-pair one: A→B then B→A. Not a demand or a threat but a statement of settled fact about present worship; the symmetry gives neither side a rhetorical advantage — it simply reports that no shared act of worship exists (*Source: structural observation on Itani's renderings*).
* **`[109:4]`–`[109:5]`** "Nor do I serve what you serve. / Nor do you serve what I serve."
  * **Gloss:** Mirror-pair two — the same double-refrain device re-run with the verb rendered "serve" instead of "worship." The repetition IS the rhetoric: four denials, two objects, both directions (the surah's "double-repetition" architecture). NOTE (hint correction): Itani does not mark the past/present tense split some classical readings find in 109:4–5; his verbs stay present throughout — any tense-allegory is exegesis above this text (*Source: observable rendering; classical tense reading = transmitted — verify*).
* **`[109:6]`** "You have your way, and I have my way.”"
  * **Gloss:** The partition line — and the corpus's only "your way" site (rg-verified): *din* here is rendered "way", not "religion". Ownership, not curse: each party HAS a way; the sentence closes the surah's quotation marks. (Hint correction: not "to you your religion, to me my religion" in this edition.)

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`'ibadah` (worship/serve)**: one Arabic root (ʿ-b-d) rendered with TWO English verbs inside six verses — "worship" (109:2–3) then "serve" (109:4–5).
  * **Rendering:** drift is translator register, not text; 10:104 renders the same verb-family "serve" in a 109-type disavowal — Itani pairs "serve" with the disavowal-context twice (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[1:5]` "It is You we worship" (positive twin); `[39:64]` "Is it other than Allah you instruct me to worship" (the qul-rebuke near-twin, rg-verified).
* **`din`** ("way" here): the corpus's chameleon noun.
  * **Rendering:** "way" ONLY at 109:6 (unique — rg-verified); "religion" at `[2:256]` "no compulsion in religion", `[5:3]` "perfected your religion", `[10:104]` "doubt about my religion", `[110:2]` "Allah’s religion"; "Judgment" at `[1:4]` "Master of the Day of Judgment" (all rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** the drift is the point — in the partition verse, *din* is softened to the plain-spoken "way"; two verses later in the same unit (110:2) the same word is "religion" (*Source: observable corpus fact + agent inference on pattern — verify*).
* **`kafirun` (disbelievers)**: the surah title and the only vocative.
  * **Rendering:** kept as the category-label "disbelievers"; no names — the inverse strategy of 111:1's named "Abee Lahab" (see s111 passage).
