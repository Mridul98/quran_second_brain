# The banquet and the knives: prison preferred to sin (12:30–35)
**UID:** 202610141545
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:30–35 (Ruku 4 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:30]` – `[12:35]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The private scandal goes public as gossip, the gossip is answered with a banquet where the women lose their hands to his beauty, and Yusuf's response is the surah's sharpest prayer: prison is preferable to the thing they call him to — and God answers by diverting their scheming, at the price of the prison itself.
* **Placement:** The temptation ruku's escalation and turn: 12:25's threatened "imprisonment" arrives as mercy-by-incarceration; ruku 5's cellmates are already booked.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:30):** the rumor spreads — the city narrates what the house suppressed.
* **`[12:30]`** "Some ladies in the city said, “The governor's wife is trying to seduce her servant. She is deeply in love with him. We see she has gone astray.”"
  * **Gloss:** The household's title finally names itself ("the governor's wife" — this edition's rendering of the woman's status); the verdict "gone astray" comes from her own class.
* **Story motion (12:31–32):** the counter-move — a banquet, knives, and hands cut in awe; then the ultimatum.
* **`[12:31]`** "And when she heard of their gossip, she invited them, and prepared for them a banquet, and she gave each one of them a knife. She said, “Come out before them.” And when they saw him, they marveled at him, and cut their hands. They said, “Good Allah, this is not a human, this must be a precious angel.”"
  * **Gloss:** The famous scene: appetite redirected — slicing fruit, slashing hands; their confession ("not a human... a precious angel") proves the case she was charged with, in public. "Good Allah" is this edition's rendering of an exclamation (ʿayya Allah / "my God!") — translator's choice, observable.
* **`[12:32]`** "She said, “Here he is, the one you blamed me for. I did try to seduce him, but he resisted. But if he does not do what I tell him to do, he will be imprisoned, and will be one of the despised.”"
  * **Gloss:** She converts their shared collapse into a threat; "he resisted" (aba — this edition's phrasing) records his refusal in her own mouth.
* **Story motion (12:33–34):** the prayer that chooses the lesser harm, and the God who answers it exactly.
* **`[12:33]`** "He said, “My Lord, prison is more desirable to me than what they call me to. Unless You turn their scheming away from me, I may yield to them, and become one of the ignorant.”"
  * **Gloss:** The surah's ethic of choice: a certain prison over a certain sin — and the honesty of "I may yield": even Yusuf asks for diversion, not for confidence.
* **`[12:34]`** "Thereupon his Lord answered him, and diverted their scheming away from him. He is the Hearer, the Knower."
  * **Gloss:** Answer = the prison comes; the theodicy knot (answered prayer, unwanted outcome) is left for the reader — recovered at 12:100's audit trail. "The Hearer, the Knower" — the answering pair.
* **Story motion (12:35):** the verdict with no evidence needed — they imprison him anyway, and the text names why that is strange.
* **`[12:35]`** "Then it occurred to them, after they had seen the signs, to imprison him for a while."
  * **Gloss:** "After they had seen the signs" (al-ayat — torn shirt, cut hands, the whole dossier): incarceration after proof is the surah's quiet indictment, and its bridge into ruku 5.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`kayd/makr` ("scheming"):** the ruku's running word for the women's pressure.
  * **Rendering:** "a woman's scheme... Your scheming" (12:28), "their scheming" (12:33–34), "their schemes" (12:50), "the scheming of the betrayers" (12:52) — one English idiom carried across several Arabic plot-words; classically *kayd* (12:33's context) vs *makr* (12:5 "plot and scheme") — *Source: agent inference on Arabic lexical pairing — verify.*
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:76]` "Thus We devised a plan for Joseph" — the same strategy-word applied to divine counter-plot; the motif pair (their kayd diverted, His kayd devised) is this surah's providence grammar.
* **"the signs" (ayat):** 12:35's evidence-word.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:105]` "How many a sign in the heavens and the earth do they pass by" — the surah's closing rebuke for the same failure: seeing signs, imprisoning anyway (cf. Pharaoh narratives' "signs" cluster).
* **"the ignorant" (jahiliyya-class noun):** 12:33's feared end-state — "become one of the ignorant."
  * **Elsewhere:** the same pairing logic at `[12:89]` "in your ignorance" (their old crime) — ignorance as the actionable state Yusuf flees.
* **"the Hearer, the Knower":** the closing Name-pair.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:115]` and `[44:6]` verbatim same pair — Itani's fixed rendering of as-Sami' al-'Alim.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within surah (threat → mercy):** `[12:25]` "except imprisonment" → `[12:32]` "he will be imprisoned" → `[12:35]` "to imprison him for a while" — the villain's penalty becomes the saint's address; "a while" (hiyan) is the span ruku 5–6 counts.
