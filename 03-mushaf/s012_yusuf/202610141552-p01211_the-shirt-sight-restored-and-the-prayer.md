# The shirt restores sight, the dream fulfills, and Yusuf prays to depart (12:94–104)
**UID:** 202610141552
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:94–104 (Ruku 11 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:94]` – `[12:104]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The caravan that began the loss (12:19) carries the cure: a father who "senses" Joseph against all mockery, a shirt laid on a face, sight returned, sins met with a deferred forgiveness-prayer — and when the eleven bow, Yusuf reads the dream closed, counts the mercies backward through the pit and the prison, and asks not to stay: "Receive my soul in submission."
* **Placement:** The fulfillment ruku: 12:4's dream pays out at 12:100; then the narrator steps back into the audience (12:102–104) and the surah's frame-argument resumes (ruku 12).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:94–95):** the scent against the consensus of skeptics.
* **`[12:94]`** "As the caravan set out, their father said, “I sense the presence of Joseph, though you may think I am senile.”"
  * **Gloss:** The blind father "senses" (this edition's rendering of finding Joseph's scent/direction — tarafa) what the sighted call dotage; the caravan, once the instrument of loss (12:19), is now the carrier of news.
* **`[12:95]`** "They said, “By Allah, you are still in your old confusion.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their fourth "by Allah" — this time swearing against the truth itself; the surah keeps score on their oaths (12:73, 85, 91, 95).
* **Story motion (12:96–98):** the good-news bearer, the healed eyes, and the request for pardon.
* **`[12:96]`** "Then, when the bearer of good news arrived, he laid it over his face, and he regained his sight. He said, “Did I not say to you that I know from Allah what you do not know?”"
  * **Gloss:** The shirt's third act completes (12:18 forged → 12:25 testimony → 12:93/96 healing); 12:86's claim is now verifiable — the "old confusion" (12:95) was a clairvoyance they could not price.
* **`[12:97]`** "They said, “Father, pray for the forgiveness of our sins; we were indeed at fault.”"
  * **Gloss:** The brothers finally ask what Yusuf already granted (12:92) — now they want the father's prayer, not his silence.
* **`[12:98]`** "He said, “I will ask my Lord to forgive you. He is the Forgiver, the Most Merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** Jacob defers the pardon to God's court and names the court's character — classically he postponed the asking to the last part of the night (Source: classical tafsir reports, e.g., Ibn Kathir on 12:98 — verify).
* **Story motion (12:99–100):** the family enters Egypt; the eleven bow — the dream is booked.
* **`[12:99]`** "Then, when they entered into the presence of Joseph, he embraced his parents, and said, “Enter Egypt, Allah willing, safe and secure.”"
  * **Gloss:** "Allah willing" — the surah's one conditional of human planning, spoken by the man whose plan God planned around (12:76); the "safe and secure" answers the father's 12:13 fear.
* **`[12:100]`** "And he elevated his parents on the throne, and they fell prostrate before him. He said, “Father, this is the fulfillment of my vision of long ago. My Lord has made it come true. He has blessed me, when he released me from prison, and brought you out of the wilderness, after the devil had sown conflict between me and my brothers. My Lord is Most Kind towards whomever He wills. He is the All-knowing, the Most Wise.”"
  * **Gloss:** Prostration before the boy of 12:4 — classically a prostration of greeting/honor, not worship (Source: consensus of classical tafsir, e.g., Ibn Kathir on 12:100 — verify); Yusuf's retrospective: the prison re-counted as blessing, the brothers' conflict re-assigned to the devil ("Satan is man's sworn enemy," 12:5 — now diagnosed as the mechanism), and the whole ledger closed with "Most Kind" (latif — unique corpus rendering here) toward whomever He wills.
* **Story motion (12:101):** the hero at the summit asks for the exit — the surah's prayer of detachment.
* **`[12:101]`** "“My Lord, You have given me some authority, and taught me some interpretation of events. Initiator of the heavens and the earth; You are my Protector in this life and in the Hereafter. Receive my soul in submission, and unite me with the righteous.”"
  * **Gloss:** The inventory matches the promises of 12:6 (authority, ta'wil) and 12:21/56 (establishment); "some... some" — the gifts stated in partitive humility; the petition: death as being "received" (tawaffa) in surrender, and companionship with the righteous — office declined as a final destination.
* **Story motion (12:102–104):** the narrator turns from the story to the hearers.
* **`[12:102]`** "This is news from the past that We reveal to you. You were not present with them when they plotted and agreed on a plan."
  * **Gloss:** The frame-argument (cf. 12:3): the story's accuracy is its source; the Meccan plotters ("agreed on a plan") are quietly compared with the brothers' pit-council (12:5, 15).
* **`[12:103]`** "But most people, for all your eagerness, are not believers."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's "most people" refrain (cf. `[12:21]`, `[12:38]`, `[12:40]`, `[12:68]`) lands on the present audience: eagerness cannot override the majority's stance.
* **`[12:104]`** "You ask them no wage for it. It is only a reminder for all mankind."
  * **Gloss:** The messenger's economics — no wage (cf. 11:51, 38:86, 6:90) — and the book's scope: dhikr for all the worlds, exactly what 12:111 will restate as guidance and mercy.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ta'wil` as "fulfillment":** 12:100 — the word's payoff meaning.
  * **Rendering:** "the fulfillment of my vision" — same ta'wil-family promised at 12:6 ("interpretation of events"): outcomes are the interpretation; cf. `[18:78/82]`.
* **tawaffi "Receive my soul in submission":** 12:101's death petition.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:42]` "Allah takes the souls at the time of their death"; `[3:102]` "do not die except as Muslims" — the same surrender-at-death grammar asked, not assumed; the verb is God's (He "receives"), never the servant's grasp.
* **"news from the past" (ghayb-narration):** 12:102.
  * **Elsewhere:** bracket-pair with `[12:3]` "prior to it, you were of the unaware"; the kalam note's category: revealed content includes un-eyewitnessed history (cf. 42:51's inspiration modes).
* **"Initiator of the heavens and the earth" (badi'):** 12:101.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:46]` the same address-form in a prayer — it pairs with the other half of Yusuf's line: a Creator-title before a death-request.
* **"Most Kind" (latif):** 12:100 — the providence name this surah earns (rendered "Most Kind"; unique corpus occurrence of this superlative form).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan; the turn at 12:102 from story to audience is the surah's own homiletic coda, classically the function of the ghayb-news formula (Source: classical tafsir framing of Yusuf's interruptions).
* **Textual note:** `[12:99]` "Enter Egypt, Allah willing" is the surah's only explicit divine-will conditional on human action; "Egypt" appears elsewhere in the corpus at `[2:61]`, `[10:87]`, `[43:51]` — the Moses/Israel narrative world that Yusuf's "the land" (`[12:21]`) pre-figures. *Source: corpus observation.*
