# Vindication, the soul that commands evil, and the storehouses (12:50–57)
**UID:** 202610141548
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:50–57 (Ruku 7 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:50]` – `[12:57]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Yusuf refuses a pardon he did not earn in the open: make the king re-inquire about the women who cut their hands first — the governor's wife confesses ("It was I who tried to seduce him, and he is telling the truth"), the honest self-indictment follows ("the soul commands evil"), and the cell opens straight into office: "established and secure," in charge of the storehouses.
* **Placement:** The vindication that answers 12:33–35's imprisonment-after-signs; the surah's public exoneration of the temptation arc, then the reward-grammar line (12:57) that ruku 12 will restate.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:50):** the summons arrives — and Yusuf sends it back.
* **`[12:50]`** "The king said, “Bring him to me.” And when the envoy came to him, he said, “Go back to your master, and ask him about the intentions of the women who cut their hands; my Lord is well aware of their schemes.”"
  * **Gloss:** After years of waiting for exactly this summons, he declines to exit before his record is cleared — the vindication delay is now self-imposed; "my Lord is well aware of their schemes" hands the inquiry to the surah's surveillance refrain (12:19, 77).
* **Story motion (12:51–52):** the re-trial of the banquet — the women recant, the accuser confesses.
* **`[12:51]`** "He said, “What was the matter with you, women, when you tried to seduce Joseph?” They said, “Allah forbid! We knew of no evil committed by him.” The governor’s wife then said, “Now the truth is out. It was I who tried to seduce him, and he is telling the truth.”"
  * **Gloss:** The women's defense is pure character ("no evil committed by him"); her confession at the public microphone inverts 12:25's accusation — "Now the truth is out."
* **`[12:52]`** "“This is that he may know that I did not betray him in secret, and that Allah does not guide the scheming of the betrayers.”"
  * **Gloss:** Her motive clause: even confession keeps a witness — she did not betray him behind closed doors; and the narrator's creedal aside: Allah "does not guide the scheming of the betrayers" (khina — the same root family as `[8:58]` "Allah does not like the treacherous").
* **Story motion (12:53):** the surah's most quoted psychology line — every self, including hers, under command.
* **`[12:53]`** "“Yet I do not claim to be innocent. The soul commands evil, except those on whom my Lord has mercy. Truly my Lord is Forgiving and Merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The nafs ammara — "the soul commands evil" (unique wording in this corpus): either her own continuing self-indictment or Yusuf's voice (classically debated; the corpus leaves the speaker line open after 12:52 — *Source: classical tafsir disagreement on the speaker — verify*); innocence is claimed by no one, mercy excepted.
* **Story motion (12:54–55):** the cell becomes the cabinet.
* **`[12:54]`** "The king said, “Bring him to me, and I will reserve him for myself.” And when he spoke to him, he said, “This day you are with us established and secure.”"
  * **Gloss:** "established and secure" (makin, amin — two words the king had forfeited on the women's oath): Yusuf gets rank and immunity in one breath.
* **`[12:55]`** "He said, “Put me in charge of the storehouses of the land; I am honest and knowledgeable.”"
  * **Gloss:** The first act of freedom is a job application with the surah's own credentials (hafiz, 'alim — "honest and knowledgeable") — the man sold as "of little value" (12:20) prices himself in office terms.
* **Story motion (12:56–57):** the narrator re-frames: establishment as mercy-dispensation, and the better reward.
* **`[12:56]`** "And thus We established Joseph in the land, to live therein wherever he wished. We touch with Our mercy whomever We will, and We never waste the reward of the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** Verbatim-frame with 12:21 ("We thus established Joseph in the land") — the ruku's ring; whomever-We-will mercy paired with the no-waste-reward promise (cf. `[7:170]`, `[18:30]`, `[11:115]`).
* **`[12:57]`** "But the reward of the Hereafter is better for those who believe and observed piety."
  * **Gloss:** The palace is not the payoff — the surah's this-world/world-to-come hedge (same grammar as `[6:32]`/`[12:109]` "the Home of the Hereafter is better"), spoken just as Yusuf takes office.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **nafs ammara ("the soul commands evil"):** 12:53's anthropology.
  * **Rendering:** unique wording in the corpus for the self's compulsion to evil; the exception clause is mercy, not willpower.
  * **Elsewhere:** paired with `[12:24]` "We diverted evil... away from him" — what Yusuf did not will, this verse universalizes in the confession; cf. the sabr note's steering-under-pressure (taqwa) as the named alternative.
* **`khina` ("the betrayers" / "I did not betray him"):** 12:52's trust-word.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[8:58]` "Allah does not like the treacherous"; `[25:29]` "Satan has always been a betrayer of man"; `[66:10]` — Noah's and Lot's wives "betrayed them" — betrayal-of-trust, the exact mirror of Yusuf's "honest" self-description at 12:55 (hifz).
* **hifz/`"honest"`, "Guardian":** 12:55 "I am honest and knowledgeable" ↔ 12:64 "Allah is the Best Guardian."
  * **Rendering:** one root (hafiz) rendered "honest" here, "Guardian" there — Yusuf claims the office; Jacob locates the safety — *Source: agent inference on root identity (observable in English drift) — verify.*
* **"established" (makin):** 12:54's court word, 12:21/56's divine word ("We established Joseph").
  * **Elsewhere:** 12:56 deliberately rhymes Yusuf's secure station with God's establishment — the human title and the divine verb share the root.
