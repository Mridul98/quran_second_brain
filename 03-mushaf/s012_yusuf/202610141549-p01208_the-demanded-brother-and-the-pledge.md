# The demanded brother, the pledge before Allah, and the scattered gates (12:58–68)
**UID:** 202610141549
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:58–68 (Ruku 8 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:58]` – `[12:68]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The storehouse official is unrecognized by the very men he once pitied: he fills their measure, demands the full brother as the price of future measure, secretly refunds their payment to bait their return — and Jacob, burned once by "trust us," releases the boy only on a pledge before Allah, with one fatherly precaution he himself will call useless.
* **Placement:** The brothers' second descent arc begins: ruku 2 stole a brother; ruku 8–10 will make them surrender one, then beg him back. The trust-question of `[12:11]` is the hinge verse pair (12:11 ↔ 12:64).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:58–61):** recognition one-way; the demand lands.
* **`[12:58]`** "And Joseph's brothers came, and entered into his presence. He recognized them, but they did not recognize him."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's cruel symmetry — the boy they threw in a hole now runs the grain of an empire and knows their faces; they see an Egyptian minister (Genesis 42:8 parallel — *Source: comparative narrative; agent inference — verify*).
* **`[12:59]`** "When he provided them with their provisions, he said, “Bring me a brother of yours from your father. Do you not see that I fill up the measure, and I am the best of hosts?”"
  * **Gloss:** Hospitality with an invoice attached: measure for measure is his public law; "a brother of yours from your father" — the full-brother clause of `[12:8]` now becomes policy.
* **`[12:60]`** "“But if you do not bring him to me, you will have no measure from me, and you will not come near me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The carrot's other face: no measure, no access — Yusuf's test runs through the same commodity, grain, that bought his slavery.
* **`[12:61]`** "They said, “We will solicit him from his father. We will surely do.”"
  * **Gloss:** Easy promises from the same speakers of `[12:12]` "we will take care of him" — the text remembers every "surely."
* **Story motion (12:62–65):** the refund trap — generosity engineered to reel them back.
* **`[12:62]`** "He said to his servants, “Put their goods in their saddlebags; perhaps they will recognize them when they return to their families, and maybe they will come back.”"
  * **Gloss:** The payment hidden in the sacks: Yusuf's "plan" (see ruku 9's kayd) starts as a kindness with a forecast — "maybe they will come back."
* **`[12:63]`** "When they returned to their father, they said, “O father, we were denied measure, but send our brother with us, and we will obtain measure. We will take care of him.”"
  * **Gloss:** They repeat the 12:12 care-promise verbatim-style ("we will take care of him") — the father has heard this sentence before.
* **`[12:64]`** "He said, “Shall I trust you with him, as I trusted you with his brother before? Allah is the Best Guardian, and He is the Most Merciful of the merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The hinge: `[12:11]`'s question quoted back as an accusation; "his brother" (Joseph) named in the same breath as the new trust — and Jacob transfers guardianship (hafiz, unique superlative here) where the brothers failed it.
* **`[12:65]`** "And when they opened their baggage, they found that their goods were returned to them. They said, “Father, what more do we want? Here are our goods, returned to us. We will provide for our family, and protect our brother, and have an additional camel-load. This is easy commerce.”"
  * **Gloss:** The refund converts hesitation into arithmetic: goods back, family fed, brother protected, one camel-load extra — "easy commerce" is exactly the language a man who sold a brother for coins will answer in.
* **Story motion (12:66–68):** the pledge, the gates, and the father's candid theory of providence.
* **`[12:66]`** "He said, “I will not send him with you, unless you give me a pledge before Allah that you will bring him back to me, unless you get trapped.” And when they gave him their pledge, he said, “Allah is witness to what we say.”"
  * **Gloss:** A mawthiq — "a pledge before Allah" (same English word for Allah's covenant-pledges at `[57:8]`, `[3:187]`) — plus the only escape clause Jacob allows: force majeure, "unless you get trapped"; the contract's counter-signature is not the brothers' honor but God as witness — the pledge is filed where it cannot be lost.
* **`[12:67]`** "And he said, “O my sons, do not enter by one gate, but enter by different gates. I cannot avail you anything against Allah. The decision rests only with Allah. On Him I rely, and on Him let the reliant rely.”"
  * **Gloss:** The scattered-gates precaution (classically: against the evil eye on eleven handsome brothers through one gate — *Source: classical tafsir, e.g., Ibn Kathir on 12:67 — verify*); instantly disclaimed: no strategy "avails against Allah" — and the surah's tawhid refrain from 12:40's mouthpiece, Yusuf, now in Jacob's: "The decision rests only with Allah."
* **`[12:68]`** "And when they entered as their father had instructed them, it did not avail them anything against Allah; it was just a need in the soul of Jacob, which he carried out. He was a person of knowledge inasmuch as We had taught him, but most people do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The text's own verdict on precaution: it changed nothing in the decree yet was a father's need — the line between prudence and presumption, adjudicated inside the surah; "a person of knowledge inasmuch as We had taught him" = the taught-knowledge motif (12:6/21/37) extended to Jacob.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`kayl` ("measure"):** the ruku's commodity-word.
  * **Rendering:** "fill up the measure" (12:59), "no measure from me" (12:60), "denied measure" (12:63), "obtain measure" (12:63), "full measure" (12:88, next arc).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:85]` "Give full measure and weight, and do not cheat people out of their rights" — measure as the standing prophetic economics; the infraq note's honest-scale quadrant.
* **`mawthiq` ("a pledge"):** 12:66's contract word.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[57:8]` "He has received a pledge from you"; `[3:187]` "Allah received a pledge from those who were given the Scripture" — the corpus uses "pledge" for Allah-side covenant and for Jacob's demand: same gravity, human scale.
* **"the Best Guardian" (hafiz):** unique superlative; cf. 12:55 "honest" (same root drift — see ruku 7 §3).
* **"On Him I rely" (tawakkul):** 12:67's credo.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[27:79]` "So rely on Allah. You are upon the clear truth"; `[11:123]` "worship Him, and rely on Him"; `[33:48]` — the command-form family; Jacob's version adds the mutual clause "and on Him let the reliant rely," the root's only such pairing.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan; the father's dual posture — take every precaution, credit none with the outcome (12:67–68) — is the surah's providence thesis in domestic form.
* **Narrative context:** the "brother of yours from your father" is Benjamin; the corpus never names him until "his brother" language (12:69) — *Source: classical identification; the Quran's silences differ from Genesis 42–43's explicit naming — agent inference — verify.*
