# The goblet in the saddlebag: a plan devised for Joseph (12:69–79)
**UID:** 202610141550
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:69–79 (Ruku 9 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:69]` – `[12:79]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Yusuf gets his brother alone, comforts him with the truth ("I am your brother"), then plants the king's goblet in the young man's bag so that a theft accusation — a detention his own office could not legally perform — becomes the brothers' problem to solve; the narrator stops the machinery to name what is happening: "Thus We devised a plan for Joseph."
* **Placement:** The stratagem ruku: the surah's providence vocabulary hits its technical peak (kayd, degrees, knowledge) and the brothers' confession arc (ruku 10) is loaded by this trap.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:69):** the private reunion the public test was built for.
* **`[12:69]`** "And when they entered into the presence of Joseph, he embraced his brother, and said, “I am your brother; do not be saddened by what they used to do.”"
  * **Gloss:** The demanded brother finally held; Yusuf's first gift is identity, his second is absolution in advance ("do not be saddened by what they used to do") — he names the old crime to the victim who never knew its full shape.
* **Story motion (12:70–75):** the plant, the herald, the rigged interrogation — a script the brothers half-agree to.
* **`[12:70]`** "Then, when he provided them with their provisions, he placed the drinking-cup in his brother’s saddlebag. Then an announcer called out, “O people of the caravan, you are thieves.”"
  * **Gloss:** The cup (saqa' — the king's drinking-vessel) enters as state property; the charge is shouted by a herald — public law theater staged over a private plant.
* **`[12:71]`** "They said, as they came towards them, “What are you missing?”"
  * **Gloss:** Their counter-question is confident — men with clean sacks of bought goods.
* **`[12:72]`** "They said, “We are missing the king’s goblet. Whoever brings it will have a camel-load; and I personally guarantee it.”"
  * **Gloss:** Same object, second name ("goblet" — one Arabic word, two renderings in adjacent verses, observable drift); the bounty is the exact size of 12:65's promised "additional camel-load" — the plot pays in their own arithmetic.
* **`[12:73]`** "They said, “By Allah, you know we did not come to cause trouble in the land, and we are not thieves.”"
  * **Gloss:** A sincere oath from men who once faked blood — the text lets their protest be true in this case and untrue in their memory.
* **`[12:74]`** "They said, “What shall be his punishment, if you are lying?”"
  * **Gloss:** The accusers invite the defendants' own law — the trap's hinge turns on jurisdiction, not on evidence.
* **`[12:75]`** "They said, “His punishment, if it is found in his bag: he will belong to you. Thus we penalize the guilty.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their verdict: servitude for theft ("he will belong to you") — classically the law of Jacob's house/brothers' own code, not Egyptian law (Genesis 44 echoes this — *Source: classical tafsir and comparative narrative — verify*); "Thus we penalize the guilty" is their hand-written sentence.
* **Story motion (12:76):** the search is choreographed, the narrator names the author of the choreography.
* **`[12:76]`** "So he began with their bags, before his brother's bag. Then he pulled it out of his brother’s bag. Thus We devised a plan for Joseph; he could not have detained his brother under the king’s law, unless Allah so willed. We elevate by degrees whomever We will; and above every person of knowledge, there is one more learned."
  * **Gloss:** Dramatic misdirection (search the elders first) then the reveal; the divine-planning aside — the king's law could never hold a free man's brother, so the brothers' law was needed; and two gnomic lift-offs: elevation by degrees, and knowledge always outranked.
* **Story motion (12:77–79):** the accusation flung back at Yusuf's own history; his withheld answer; the refusal of the swap.
* **`[12:77]`** "They said, “If he has stolen, a brother of his has stolen before.” But Joseph kept it to himself, and did not reveal it to them. He said, “You are in a worse situation, and Allah is Aware of what you allege.”"
  * **Gloss:** They weaponize his family against him — an allusion to a prior theft (of Esau's garments? of idols? — *Source: classical reports; the corpus deliberately withholds the referent — verify*); "kept it to himself" is Yusuf's silence-as-withholding (kitman — root kha-ta-meem; *Source: classical Arabic lexicon; agent gloss — verify*), and his counter: your case is worse, and God knows what you allege (the 12:19 surveillance refrain answered).
* **`[12:78]`** "They said, “O noble prince, he has a father, a very old man, so take one of us in his place. We see that you are a good person.”"
  * **Gloss:** The substitution plea — the offer of a hostage that they know they must refuse to make — mercy-flattery from men once unmerciful.
* **`[12:79]`** "He said, “Allah forbid that we should arrest anyone except him in whose possession we found our property; for then we would be unjust.”"
  * **Gloss:** The same refuge-word as 12:23 ("Allah forbid"), now deployed for justice: he will not trade the guilty for the innocent — Yusuf's law, not their bargain.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"drinking-cup" / "goblet":** 12:70/72's one object.
  * **Rendering:** "drinking-cup" then "the king's goblet" — same saqa'-word, register drift visible in English.
  * **Elsewhere:** the vessel-word family is mostly this edition's hereafter imagery — `[56:18]` "With cups, pitchers, and sparkling drinks"; `[76:5]` "a cup whose mixture is aroma" — in Yusuf it is an Egyptian state object: one class of vessel, two opposite worlds.
* **`kayd` ("We devised a plan"):** 12:76's theological verb.
  * **Elsewhere:** their "scheming" (12:33–34, 50–52) vs this divine counter-plan (12:76): the surah reserves the noun's honor for God's craft; cf. 12:100's "devil had sown conflict" as the counter-craft.
* **"kept it to himself" (withholding, kitman):** 12:77's silence.
  * **Elsewhere:** Yusuf conceals here what the surah will publicly reveal at 12:90 — withholding and disclosure are his two administrative modes (cf. 12:32 "he resisted," the withheld yes).
* **"the king's law" (din):** 12:76 — the word din rendered "law" once here, while 12:40/12:38 render it "religion"/"faith": one word, two of its English faces, observable in this translation's context choices.
* **"We elevate by degrees whomever We will":** 12:76's gnomic.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:83]` verbatim twin — "We elevate by degrees whomever We will. Your Lord is Wise and Informed"; with `[39:23]`/`[42:52]` "He guides with it whomever He wills" — elevation and guidance share the whomever-He-wills grammar.
