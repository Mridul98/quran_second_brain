# Lessons for the seekers: the plot, the pit, and the cheap sale (12:7–20)
**UID:** 202610141543
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:7–20 (Ruku 2 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:7]` – `[12:20]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The father's warning in `[12:5]` comes true exactly: jealousy is reasoned into attempted murder, softened into exposure, executed with a forged shirt — and the boy is sold out of the story for "a few coins," while the narrator keeps insisting Allah is aware of what they do.
* **Placement:** The first descent of the promised arc (`[12:6]`); the surah's lesson-frame opens here ("lessons for the seekers") and will close at `[12:111]`.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:7):** the camera pulls back — these events are declared a standing lesson, then the zoom dives into the brothers' council.
* **`[12:7]`** "In Joseph and his brothers are lessons for the seekers."
  * **Gloss:** The thesis line of the whole surah; "the seekers" (those who ask) makes reading this story itself an act of seeking.
* **Story motion (12:8–10):** jealousy debates its own crime — from "kill Joseph" to a negotiated pit.
* **`[12:8]`** "When they said, “Joseph and his brother are dearer to our father than we are, although we are a whole group. Our father is obviously in the wrong."
  * **Gloss:** Resentment dressed as arithmetic: a "whole group" outweighing two favorites; "his brother" = the full brother (Benjamin, unnamed in the corpus; *Source: classical identification — verify*).
* **`[12:9]`** "“Kill Joseph, or throw him somewhere in the land, and your father‘s attention will be yours. Afterwards, you will be decent people.”"
  * **Gloss:** The naked transaction of the surah: murder scheduled, then repentance pre-booked — the "afterwards" is the moral comedy.
* **`[12:10]`** "One of them said, “Do not kill Joseph, but throw him into the bottom of the well; some caravan may pick him up—if you must do something.”"
  * **Gloss:** The compromise that looks like mercy and is still the deed; the unnamed "one of them" (classically identified as the eldest or Reuben — *Source: classical tafsir reports; not named in corpus*).
* **Story motion (12:11–14):** the deception of the father — his own wordplay ("trust") thrown back at him.
* **`[12:11]`** "They said, “Father, why do you not trust us with Joseph, although we care for him?”"
  * **Gloss:** The hook of the ruku's great echo: Jacob will ask the identical question of the same men at `[12:64]`.
* **`[12:12]`** "“Send him with us tomorrow, that he may roam and play; we will take care of him.”"
  * **Gloss:** A promise of care that the ruku will immediately violate twice — the wolf rumor and the well.
* **`[12:13]`** "He said, “It worries me that you would take him away. And I fear the wolf may eat him while you are careless of him.”"
  * **Gloss:** The father names the exact cover story they will invent — his fear states their script.
* **`[12:14]`** "They said, “If the wolf ate him, and we are many, we would be good for nothing.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their reply unknowingly confesses: if "we are many" and a boy is lost, they are worthless — they will prove it.
* **Story motion (12:15–18):** the deed and the forged proof; Jacob answers with the surah's first sabr.
* **`[12:15]`** "So they went away with him, and agreed to put him at the bottom of the well. And We inspired him, “You will inform them of this deed of theirs when they are unaware.”"
  * **Gloss:** Divine inspiration to a child in the dark — the promise of 12:111's confrontation embedded at the pit's floor; note the reversal: *they* will be the unaware ones.
* **`[12:16]`** "And they came to their father in the evening weeping."
  * **Gloss:** Staged grief — the performance the rest of the surah keeps auditing.
* **`[12:17]`** "They said, “O father, we went off racing one another, and left Joseph by our belongings; and the wolf ate him. But you will not believe us, even though we are being truthful.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their father's wolf verbatim (12:13) — a lie built from borrowed words, closing with the confession that he won't believe them.
* **`[12:18]`** "And they brought his shirt, with fake blood on it. He said, “Your souls enticed you to do something. But patience is beautiful, and Allah is my Help against what you describe.”"
  * **Gloss:** The forged shirt enters the surah's great prop (it returns at `[12:93]`); Jacob sees through it and coins the ruku's ethic: *sabrun jamil* — "patience is beautiful" (rendered again at `[12:83]` as "Patience is a virtue").
* **Story motion (12:19–20):** the pit becomes a market — the sale that made an Egyptian's household.
* **`[12:19]`** "A caravan passed by, and they sent their water-carrier. He lowered his bucket, and said, “Good news. Here is a boy.” And they hid him as merchandise. But Allah was aware of what they did."
  * **Gloss:** The rescuers become traders in one breath; the narrator's refrain — "Allah was aware" — brackets the crime (`[12:19]`, answered at `[12:77]`).
* **`[12:20]`** "And they sold him for a cheap price—a few coins—they considered him to be of little value."
  * **Gloss:** The valuation the whole surah refutes: what men price cheaply, God establishes in a land (`[12:21]`).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sabr` ("patience is beautiful"):** Jacob's creed here, the surah's spine.
  * **Rendering:** "patience is beautiful" (`[12:18]`) vs "Patience is a virtue" (`[12:83]`) — one Arabic phrase (*sabrun jamil*), two English idioms; drift worth teaching with.
  * **Elsewhere:** the pairing of patience with divine help here ("Allah is my Help") matches the sabr-as-stamina-under-loss quadrant of the Knowledge note (`[2:153]` "seek help through patience and prayer"; `[2:155]`).
* **`qamis` (shirt):** the surah's running evidence object.
  * **Rendering:** "his shirt, with fake blood on it" (`[12:18]`), torn "from behind"/"from the front" (`[12:25–27]`), "this shirt of mine" healing sight (`[12:93]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** the shirt/garment as testimony also at `[7:26]` ("the garment of God-consciousness") — clothing as moral evidence in this translation's idiom.
* **"lessons for the seekers" (`ibrah`):** 12:7's frame-word.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:111]` "a lesson for those who possess intelligence" (frame closure); `[11:120]` "a lesson, and a reminder for the believers"; `[20:113]` "a lesson for them" — the Quran's own word for what narrative is for.
* **"Allah was aware of what they did":** the surveillance refrain.
  * **Elsewhere:** paired verbatim-shaped at `[12:77]` "Allah is Aware of what you allege" and near-twin `[12:19]` ↔ the plot line's frame.

* **Within surah (shirt arc):** `[12:18]` ↔ `[12:25–28]` ↔ `[12:93]` — forged shirt condemns, torn shirt exonerates, returned shirt heals.
