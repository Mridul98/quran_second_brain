# "There is no blame upon you today": patience's final stretch (12:80–93)
**UID:** 202610141551
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:80–93 (Ruku 10 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:80]` – `[12:93]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The brothers' last maneuver — a hostage offer refused — collapses into a fabricated report ("your son has stolen") that recreates the first crime exactly, and Jacob runs the same play against the same players with the same creed: patience, complaint to Allah alone, and a ban on despair; then Yusuf unmasks himself, and the surah's pardon formula lands: "There is no blame upon you today."
* **Placement:** The confession ruku — the surah's sabr thesis (12:18) matures here (12:83), its reward-grammar peaks (12:90), and its shirt-motif turns from forged evidence (12:18) to healing relic (12:93).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:80–82):** despair at the prison-gate of Egypt; the eldest refuses to repeat the old failure.
* **`[12:80]`** "And when they despaired of him, they conferred privately. Their eldest said, “Don’t you know that your father received a pledge from you before Allah, and in the past you failed with regard to Joseph? I will not leave this land until my father permits me, or Allah decides for me; for He is the Best of Deciders.”"
  * **Gloss:** The first despair in the surah's chain (→ 12:87 → 12:110); the eldest (Judah, unnamed here — *Source: classical identification — verify*) stakes the memory of the pledge (12:66) and the old failure — and names God "Best of Deciders" (unique corpus superlative), the providence refrain's latest title.
* **`[12:81]`** "“Go back to your father, and say, ‘Our father, your son has stolen. We testify only to what we know, and we could not have prevented the unforeseen.’”"
  * **Gloss:** A report assembled from half-truths — "we testify only to what we know" is technically Yusuf's own line (12:77) turned inside out.
* **`[12:82]`** "“Ask the town where we were, and the caravan in which we came. We are being truthful.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their witnesses: a town that never noticed them and a caravan that is itself the recurring instrument of this family's plot-lines (12:19; 12:94 departs next ruku).
* **Story motion (12:83–86):** Jacob's answer — the sabr ruku of the patriarch, grief and all.
* **`[12:83]`** "He said, “Rather, your souls have contrived something for you. Patience is a virtue. Perhaps Allah will bring them all back to me. He is the Knowing, the Wise.”"
  * **Gloss:** The 12:18 sentence re-uttered ("your souls..."; "Patience is a virtue" — same Arabic as "patience is beautiful" two ruku and one grief earlier); his hope outruns the evidence: "bring them all back."
* **`[12:84]`** "Then he turned away from them, and said, “O my bitterness for Joseph.” And his eyes turned white from sorrow, and he became depressed."
  * **Gloss:** Patience is not numbness — the surah's honest ledger: sabr first (12:83), then the wound (12:84); sight whitened by grief will be the very sense the shirt restores.
* **`[12:85]`** "They said, “By Allah, you will not stop remembering Joseph, until you have ruined your health, or you have passed away.”"
  * **Gloss:** The same sons who swore "by Allah" (12:73) now swear at their father's grief — remembrance weaponized against the rememberer.
* **`[12:86]`** "He said, “I only complain of my grief and sorrow to Allah, and I know from Allah what you do not know."
  * **Gloss:** Complaint directed vertically — the surah's second "I know from Allah what you do not know" (repeated verbatim at 12:96 when the claim is vindicated).
* **Story motion (12:87–88):** the prohibition of despair and the brothers' final, honest plea.
* **`[12:87]`** "“O my sons, go and inquire about Joseph and his brother, and do not despair of Allah's comfort. None despairs of Allah's comfort except the disbelieving people.”"
  * **Gloss:** Against their despair, a command: inquiry plus hope; "Allah's comfort" (rawh/faraj-family — this edition's unique rendering, cf. `[39:53]` "do not despair of Allah's mercy") — despair re-diagnosed as unbelief, the surah's own 12:80 word paid back.
* **`[12:88]`** "Then, when they entered into his presence, they said, “Mighty governor, adversity has befallen us, and our family. We have brought scant merchandise. But give us full measure, and be charitable towards us—Allah rewards the charitable.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their script is finally true ("adversity has befallen us"); the plea runs on Yusuf's own economy — measure, and then ihsan beyond measure: "Allah rewards the charitable."
* **Story motion (12:89–93):** unmasking, confession, pardon, and the shirt's third act.
* **`[12:89]`** "He said, “Do you realize what you did with Joseph and his brother, in your ignorance?”"
  * **Gloss:** The pit and the demand answered together; "in your ignorance" re-uses Yusuf's 12:33 fear-word — ignorance named as the act's real author.
* **`[12:90]`** "They said, “Is that you, Joseph?” He said, “I am Joseph, and this is my brother. Allah has been gracious to us. He who practices piety and patience—Allah never fails to reward the righteous.”"
  * **Gloss:** The disclosure ruku-opens 12:77's withheld silence; gratitude first ("Allah has been gracious to us" — the 12:6 blessing fulfilled), then the surah's thesis verse: taqwa + sabr as the only two credentials, reward guaranteed.
* **`[12:91]`** "They said, “By Allah, Allah has preferred you over us. We were definitely in the wrong.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their third "by Allah" — now an oath against their own interest; "We were definitely in the wrong" finally matches deed and confession.
* **`[12:92]`** "He said, “There is no blame upon you today. Allah will forgive you. He is the Most Merciful of the merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The pardon formula — no blame today, forgiveness deferred to God, sealed with the mercy-superlative that Yusuf himself used (12:64 is Jacob's; 12:92 his; the same phrase is Moses' in `[7:151]` and Job's in `[21:83]`).
* **`[12:93]`** "“Take this shirt of mine, and lay it over my father’s face, and he will recover his sight. And bring your whole family to me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The shirt's third act: forged (12:18) → torn (12:25) → healing (12:93) — the instrument of the father's blindness reversed by the son's garment.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sabrun jamil`:** the twice-spoken phrase (12:18/12:83).
  * **Rendering:** "patience is beautiful" → "Patience is a virtue" — same Arabic idiom, drift between the ruku-pairs; the surah's two most patient lines are translation variants of one another.
  * **Elsewhere:** the sabr+taqwa pairing of 12:90 is the Knowledge note's central grammar (`[2:45]`, `[2:153]`); reward-by-patience also `[16:96]` "We will reward those who are patient according to the best of their deeds."
* **"Allah's comfort":** 12:87's hope-word.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:53]` "do not despair of Allah's mercy" — the despair-prohibition formula; here Itani renders the object as "comfort" (rawh-family), a gentler register than "mercy" — *Source: agent inference on lexical pairing — verify.*
* **"the charitable" / ihsan:** 12:88's economy.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:195]` "be charitable. Allah loves the charitable"; `[5:93]` "Allah loves the charitable"; `[3:17]` the virtue-list "the patient, and the truthful, and the reverent, and the charitable" — the Quran's reward-grammar for ihsan, exactly Yusuf's court procedure.
* **"no blame upon you":** 12:92's pardon-formula.
  * **Elsewhere:** the phrase normally licenses legal exemptions — `[9:91]` (weak, sick, destitute), `[48:17]` (blind, lame, sick), `[24:29]` (uninhabited houses); at 12:92 it licenses inter-personal pardon inside the law — same words, rarer work.
* **"Best of Deciders":** 12:80 — the judgment-title (al-Hakam family), unique here; sibling of 12:67 "The decision rests only with Allah."

## 6. Context Notes
