# Established in the land: the seduction and the proof of his Lord (12:21–29)
**UID:** 202610141544
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:21–29 (Ruku 3 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:21]` – `[12:29]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The sale turns into installation — "Allah has control over His affairs" — and maturity brings the surah's central test: a shut door, an offered self, and a line the translation renders with startling symmetry, "She desired him, and he desired her, had he not seen the proof of his Lord."
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 ended with a price; this ruku answers it — the cheap boy is housed, taught, and then proven; the shirt motif pivots from forgery (12:18) to evidence.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:21–22):** providence restated as household logistics; the promise of 12:6 quietly paid.
* **`[12:21]`** "The Egyptian who bought him said to his wife, “Take good care of him; he may be useful to us, or we may adopt him as a son.” We thus established Joseph in the land, to teach him the interpretation of events. Allah has control over His affairs, but most people do not know."
  * **Gloss:** Two plots in one verse: a buyer's hope and God's establishment ("in the land" — Egypt unnamed as such here); the ruku's thesis sentence, "Allah has control over His affairs," unique phrasing in the corpus.
* **`[12:22]`** "When he reached his maturity, We gave him wisdom and knowledge. We thus reward the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The same endowment formula as Moses at his maturity (`[28:14]` "We gave him wisdom and knowledge. Thus do We reward the virtuous") — a Quranic coming-of-age template.
* **Story motion (12:23–24):** the temptation and its averted center — the surah's most discussed two lines.
* **`[12:23]`** "She in whose house he was living tried to seduce him. She shut the doors, and said, “I am yours.” He said, “Allah forbid! He is my Lord. He has given me a good home. Sinners never succeed.”"
  * **Gloss:** The refuge-word "Allah forbid" (ma'adha) and his gratitude argument — the master who housed him is God, not the Egyptian; seduction rendered as one closed-doors sentence.
* **`[12:24]`** "She desired him, and he desired her, had he not seen the proof of his Lord. It was thus that We diverted evil and indecency away from him. He was one of Our loyal servants."
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual: desire was reciprocal until the "proof" (burhan) intervened — the text does not sanitize the pull; the deflection is attributed to God ("We diverted"), and Yusuf is named among "Our loyal servants" (mukhlasin).
* **Story motion (12:25–29):** the flight, the torn shirt, the husband's verdict — evidence over accusation.
* **`[12:25]`** "As they raced towards the door, she tore his shirt from behind. At the door, they ran into her husband. She said, “What is the penalty for him who desired to dishonor your wife, except imprisonment or a painful punishment?”"
  * **Gloss:** The tear-direction will do the judging; her counter-charge pre-registers the script — prison named before it happens (12:32–35, 12:35).
* **`[12:26]`** "He said, “It was she who tried to seduce me.” A witness from her household suggested: “If his shirt is torn from the front: then she has told the truth, and he is the liar."
  * **Gloss:** A forensic test proposed mid-dispute — the anonymous "witness from her household" (classically an infant speaking for Yusuf, *Source: classical tafsir reports — verify*; the corpus says only "a witness").
* **`[12:27]`** "But if his shirt is torn from the back: then she has lied, and he is the truthful.”"
  * **Gloss:** Physics as testimony: pursuit direction inverts the accusation.
* **`[12:28]`** "And when he saw that his shirt was torn from the back, he said, “This is a woman's scheme. Your scheming is serious indeed.”"
  * **Gloss:** The husband misreads the verdict as a gendered plot — "a woman's scheme" — and the surah's *kayd/makr* vocabulary begins its drift here.
* **`[12:29]`** "“Joseph, turn away from this. And you, woman, ask forgiveness for your sin; you are indeed in the wrong.”"
  * **Gloss:** The household settles for damage control: silence for him, forgiveness for her — the public exoneration is deferred to ruku 7 (12:51).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`burhan` ("the proof of his Lord"):** 12:24's intervention device.
  * **Rendering:** "the proof" — classically a vision (of Jacob's shirt-image, or of divine command) — *Source: classical tafsir variety (Ibn Kathir reports several) — the corpus gives no content; verify before teaching the content.*
  * **Elsewhere:** "A clear proof has come to you" `[7:85]`; the root's evidentiary sense at `[12:108]` "based on clear knowledge."
* **`mukhlas` ("one of Our loyal servants"):** 12:24's verdict on Yusuf.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[38:83]` — Iblis's carve-out: "Except for your loyal servants among them": the purified-servant class named from both sides of the enmity.
* **"the Egyptian" / "her husband" / "the governor's wife":** the cast's title drift.
  * **Rendering:** the master is "The Egyptian" (12:21), then "her husband" (12:25), then Yusuf is "her servant" (12:29 context, 12:30 "her servant"); classically *al-'Aziz* — the corpus never uses the title (translator's choice, observable).
* **"Allah has control over His affairs":** the ruku's thesis, unique wording in the corpus; the providence refrain recovered at `[12:76]` "unless Allah so willed."
