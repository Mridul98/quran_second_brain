# Signs passed by, the messenger's way, and the closure on lessons (12:105–111)
**UID:** 202610141553
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 12:105–111 (Ruku 12 of 12 in Yusuf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[12:105]` – `[12:111]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The story's witnesses turn to the present audience: people who "pass by" heavens-and-earth signs unattended, believe while associating, and feel secure from the Hour — against them the messenger states his way ("I invite to Allah, based on clear knowledge"), the history of help-through-despair is recited, and the surah seals its own thesis: in their stories is a lesson, this Book a confirmation, a detailed explanation, guidance, and mercy.
* **Placement:** The closing ruku mirrors the opening one: letters-and-Book claims (12:1–3) return as story-and-Book claims (12:111); the narrative's "most people do not know" refrain ends on "people who believe."

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **Story motion (12:105–107):** the audience's diagnosis — signs ignored, shirk smuggled into belief, security from the Hour.
* **`[12:105]`** "How many a sign in the heavens and the earth do they pass by, paying no attention to them?"
  * **Gloss:** The surah's own "signs" (the torn shirt, the cut hands, the dreams, the famine protocol) generalize into cosmic ayat; passing by paying no attention is the Meccan condition — cf. Lot's townspeople who literally passed by the destroyed places daily (`[37:137]`).
* **`[12:106]`** "And most of them do not believe in Allah unless they associate others."
  * **Gloss:** The reframing verse — belief itself is not denied, its mixture is: tawhid (12:39–40's prison sermon) restated as the surah's creed test.
* **`[12:107]`** "Do they feel secure that a covering of Allah’s punishment will not come upon them, or that the Hour will not come upon them suddenly, while they are unaware?"
  * **Gloss:** "while they are unaware" — the story's key word (12:3, 15, 102) turned into eschatology: the pit taught what suddenness means; security is the surah's last named disease.
* **Story motion (12:108):** the messenger's counter-statement — a way, an invitation, clear knowledge, dissociation.
* **`[12:108]`** "Say, “This is my way; I invite to Allah, based on clear knowledge—I and whoever follows me. Glory be to Allah; and I am not of the polytheists.”"
  * **Gloss:** The da'wah manifesto: a road (sabil), a call to Allah and not to a sect, an evidential basis ("clear knowledge" — b-y-r family, rendered elsewhere "clear proof(s)" `[7:85]`, "clear truth" `[27:79]`), followers recruited by invitation only, and the Glory-sentence that closes 12:39–40's argument.
* **Story motion (12:109–110):** the precedent of the messengers — men, towns, roving the earth, and help that arrives at the end of despair.
* **`[12:109]`** "We did not send before you except men, whom We inspired, from the people of the towns. Have they not roamed the earth and seen the consequences for those before them? The Home of the Hereafter is better for those who are righteous. Do you not understand?"
  * **Gloss:** Incarnation-objections answered by history: human messengers from towns (Yusuf was one — sold, jailed, housed), consequences verifiable by travel, and 12:57's comparative restated verbatim — `[6:32]` carries the identical line.
* **`[12:110]`** "Until, when the messengers have despaired, and thought that they were rejected, Our help came to them. We save whomever We will, and Our severity is not averted from the guilty people."
  * **Gloss:** The despair chain's final link (12:80 → 12:87 → here): even messengers reach the end of their rope — and the help-verb is the same one Yusuf's parents will ask for and receive (12:96's "bearer of good news"); salvation is by will, severity by desert.
* **Story motion (12:111):** the colophon — the Book signs its own story.
* **`[12:111]`** "In their stories is a lesson for those who possess intelligence. This is not a fabricated tale, but a confirmation of what came before it, and a detailed explanation of all things, and guidance, and mercy for people who believe."
  * **Gloss:** Four claims in one breath: the stories are ibrah (12:7's frame closed), the Book is not myth (cf. `[34:43]` the disbelievers' own accusation "a fabricated lie" — denied here), it confirms prior scripture (`[3:3]` verbatim-shaped), explains in detail (`[6:114]` "the Book, explained in detail"), and is guidance-and-mercy (the Fatihah petition's two goods, 1:6; cf. `[31:3]`).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"signs... pass by":** 12:105's indifference-word.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:137]` "You pass by them in the morning" (Lot's destroyed towns); internally ↔ `[12:35]` "after they had seen the signs" — the Egyptians saw the dossier and imprisoned anyway: 12:105 indicts the same reflex.
* **"my way" (sabil):** 12:108.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:125]` "Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good advice" — same invitation-grammar, same road-word (sabil); *Source: agent inference on shared Arabic word — verify.*
* **"clear knowledge" (basira):** 12:108's epistemic claim.
  * **Elsewhere:** the b-y-r evidentiary family renders as "a clear proof" `[7:85]` and "the clear truth" `[27:79]` — a preacher who speaks on evidence, not inheritance; pairs with the surah's "but most people do not know" countergroup.
* **`ibrah` ("a lesson") + "those who possess intelligence":** 12:111's closure.
  * **Elsewhere:** 12:7's "lessons for the seekers" (frame pair); `[11:120]` "a lesson, and a reminder for the believers"; `[39:18]` "These are they who possess intellect" — one Arabic word-class, "intelligence"/"intellect" rendering drift; `[39:23]`'s "best of narrations" is the cousin of 12:3's superlative (see ruku 1).
* **"confirmation of what came before it" (musaddiq):** 12:111.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:3]` "He sent down to you the Book with the Truth, confirming what came before it"; `[46:30]` the jinn's "confirming what came before it. It guides to the truth, and to a straight path"; `[35:31]`; the Qurans's self-description as verifier of the prior scriptures, here of the prior *narratives* too (Yusuf as retold Torah material — *Source: agent inference — verify*).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within surah (frame closure):** `[12:111]` ↔ `[12:7]` ↔ `[12:1–3]` — lesson → story → Book: the ruku that opened on revelation closes on revelation's purpose.
* **Within surah (despair chain):** `[12:110]` ↔ `[12:80]`/`[12:87]` — the sons despaired, the father forbade despair, the messengers themselves despaired — and help "came" each time.
* **Within surah (unawareness motif):** `[12:107]` "while they are unaware" ↔ `[12:3]` "you were of the unaware" ↔ `[12:15]` "when they are unaware" — the word's three uses: the narrator's past, the victim's promise, the audience's danger.
* **Verbatim twin:** `[6:32]` — "The Home of the Hereafter is better for those who are righteous. Do you not understand?" matches 12:109's center exactly.
* **Parallel:** `[16:125]` — the da'wah method paired to 12:108's "way"; `[53:23]` — "They follow nothing but assumptions, and what the ego desires, even though guidance has come to them" is 12:106's diagnosis from the other surah.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan; the closing address (12:103–111) matches the surah's opening epistemics — a Meccan audience of "sign-passers" (cf. the destroyed-town motif throughout Meccan surahs, e.g., `[7:4]`).
* **Structural note:** 12:110's "when the messengers have despaired" is classically read as the console of every prophetic community including Muhammad's (Source: classical tafsir framing, e.g., Ibn Kathir on 12:110 — verify).
