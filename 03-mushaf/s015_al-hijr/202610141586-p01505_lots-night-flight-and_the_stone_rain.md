# Lot's night flight and the stone rain: the readable road (15:61–79)
**UID:** 202610141586
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 15:61–79 (Ruku 5 of 6 in al-Hijr)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[15:61]` – `[15:79]` (19 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The second envelope from ruku 4 is executed: unrecognized envoys, a dread night, a crowd arriving "joyfully" for their own destruction, Lot's guest-honor plea ("these are my daughters"), the oath by the Prophet's own life over their drunken blundering — then the Blast at sunrise, the town inverted under stones of baked clay — and the epistemic conclusion: the ruin remains "on an existing road," lessons for those who read signs, a sign for the believers; the Woods join the ledger, and "Both are clearly documented."
* **Placement:** Ruku 5; the surah's execution-and-evidence unit. It converts ruku 1's "They will find out" (15:3) into visible text, and sets the template ruku 6 applies to the Rock (80–84: same morning-strike, same useless acquisitions).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[15:61]`** "And when the envoys came to the family of Lot."
  * **Gloss:** Scene-cut from announcement to arrival; the family named before the flight.
* **`[15:62]`** "He said, “You are a people unknown to me.”"
  * **Gloss:** Lot's alarm reads hospitality as threat — strangers were precisely what his town forbade (see 70); the unknown-guest motif's twin is Abraham's "We are wary of you" (52).
* **`[15:63]`** "They said, “We bring you what they have doubts about.”"
  * **Gloss:** The promised punishment arriving as the thing disputed: doubt cured by event.
* **`[15:64]`** "“We bring you the truth, and we are truthful.”"
  * **Gloss:** A three-fold truth-claim in one line — the envoys' own veracity as warranty; echoes the preserved-Message logic of 15:9.
* **`[15:65]`** "“Travel with your family at the dead of the night, and follow up behind them, and let none of you look back, and proceed as commanded.”"
  * **Gloss:** The exit-liturgy: dark-hour departure, rear-guard position, no looking back, blind obedience — near-verbatim kin `[11:81]` ("during the cover of the night, and let none of you look back").
* **`[15:66]`** "And We informed him of Our decree: the last remnant of these will be uprooted by early morning."
  * **Gloss:** The deadline: root-and-branch ("last remnant... uprooted") before morning; the dawn-motif again at `[54:34]` "We rescued them at dawn."
* **`[15:67]`** "And the people of the town came joyfully."
  * **Gloss:** The horror-detail: they hurried to the guests elated — joy at the edge of their own sentence; ruku 1's "be lulled by hope" (`[15:3]`) cashed out in one adverb.
* **`[15:68]`** "He said, “These are my guests, so do not embarrass me.”"
  * **Gloss:** Guest-honor as the last argument; "embarrass" (ikhzā — shame-visit) names what they're about to do.
* **`[15:69]`** "“And fear Allah, and do not disgrace me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The same plea doubled with taqwa-vocabulary — shame twice, God once.
* **`[15:70]`** "They said, “Did we not forbid you from strangers?”"
  * **Gloss:** The town's prior statute against outsiders — the same root-word as Lot's "unknown" (62): their law made every guest an enemy.
* **`[15:71]`** "He said, “These are my daughters, if you must.”"
  * **Gloss:** The offered daughters (the town's women under his prophetic guardianship, per classical reading — Source: consensus gloss; 11:78's fuller "they are purer for you," verified twin).
* **`[15:72]`** "By your life, they were blundering in their drunkenness."
  * **Gloss:** The Quran's only oath "by your life" (muhammadaka? — Source: standard observation that this oath-formula is unique to this verse — verify): God swears by the Prophet's life while Lot's people stumble drunk — two lives in frame; "blundering" (taraha) = drunken stagger.
* **`[15:73]`** "So the Blast struck them at sunrise."
  * **Gloss:** Execution at the promised hour; the "Blast" (sayha) family: `[11:94]`, `[29:40]`, `[23:41]` (verified).
* **`[15:74]`** "And We turned it upside down, and rained down upon them stones of baked clay."
  * **Gloss:** Inversion plus stone-rain — verbatim twin `[11:82]` "We turned it upside down, and We rained down on it stones of baked clay"; cf. `[105:4]` "rocks of baked clay," `[51:33]` "rocks of clay."
* **`[15:75]`** "Surely in that are lessons for those who read signs."
  * **Gloss:** The evidence-clause: not "signs" alone — "lessons" (plural, ibrah) for a reading class; "read signs" is corpus-unique as a phrase (verified) — the spectators are redefined as readers (Source: agent inference — verify).
* **`[15:76]`** "And it is on an existing road."
  * **Gloss:** The ruin sits on a road travelers still use (the caravan route to the Dead Sea per classical geography — Source: standard note — verify); passing-by twin: `[37:137–138]` "You pass by them in the morning. And at night. Do you not understand?"
* **`[15:77]`** "Surely in that is a sign for the believers."
  * **Gloss:** The standard sign-clause; verbatim kin `[29:44]` "Surely in that is a sign for the believers" (verified).
* **`[15:78]`** "The people of the Woods were also wrongdoers."
  * **Gloss:** Ayka appended to the ledger — the forest-dwellers of the Shu'ayb-adjacent cycle; the name renders elsewhere "the People of the Woods" `[26:176]`, "the Dwellers of the Woods" `[38:13]`, `[50:14]` (verified drift).
* **`[15:79]`** "So We took revenge upon them. Both are clearly documented."
  * **Gloss:** "Both" = Sodom and the Woods (or the two roads — exegetes split; Source: 15:79's referent per Ibn Kathir — both towns' ruins on documented routes — agent labeling — verify); "clearly documented" (mubayyan) — corpus-unique phrasing (verified).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"the Blast" (sayha):** `[15:73]`, reused `[15:83]`.
  * **Rendering:** "the Blast" — Itani's strike-word here; note the drift: the same Thamood-class strike is "the Scream" at `[11:67]` (verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[23:41]` "Then the Blast struck them"; `[29:40]` "Some were struck by the Blast" — a four-mode inventory of the one seizure (also 11:94).
* **"stones of baked clay" (sijjil):** `[15:74]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[11:82]` verbatim; `[105:4]` "rocks of baked clay" (Ababil's stones); `[51:33]` "rocks of clay" — the material's three renderings; baked = kilned by human craft returned as rain (Source: agent image-note — verify).
* **"lessons" / "a sign":** `[15:75–77]`.
  * **Rendering:** "lessons for those who read signs" then "a sign for the believers" — the evidence ladder: reading-class then believing-class.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[29:44]` sign-for-the-believers verbatim; `[51:37]` "We left in it a sign for those who fear the painful punishment" — which re-quotes 15:50's exact threat-phrase (verified pairing: the survivors' lesson is addressed to the fearers of that punishment).
* **"the Woods":** `[15:78]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:176]` "The People of the Woods disbelieved the messengers" (capitalization drift, same verse-type); `[38:13]` and `[50:14]` list them with Thamud and Lot as confederates in rejection.
