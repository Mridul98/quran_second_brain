# Alif, Lam, Ra: the preserved Message under accusation (15:1–15)
**UID:** 202610141582
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 15:1–15 (Ruku 1 of 6 in al-Hijr)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[15:1]` – `[15:15]` (15 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The Book states its own credentials exactly where its audience disputes them: untranslated letters, a clarity-claim, then the surah's centerpiece — "Surely We revealed the Message, and We will surely preserve it" (15:9) — planted between the sneer "you are insane" (15:6) and the escalator demand for angels (15:7–8), with the mockers' future-hardened hearts (15:12–13) and even an opened sky-gate failing to convince (15:14–15).
* **Placement:** The surah's threshold: the kalam claim (revelation with a divine guarantee behind it) against the anthropology of refusal; ruku 2 (16–25) continues the same security motif into the cosmos, and ruku 6 (87, 90–95) returns to the Quran-and-mockers bracket at the close.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[15:1]`** "Alif, Lam, Ra. These are the Verses of the Book; a Quran that makes things clear."
  * **Gloss:** Letter-names numbered as ayah 1, then a double self-description: Verses of the Book, and a Quran that "makes things clear" (see Key Terms; Fleeting note on the letters applies directly).
* **`[15:2]`** "Perhaps those who disbelieve would like to become Muslims."
  * **Gloss:** An oracular aside — the future tense of regret: at doom or at death, the deniers will wish they had submitted (Source: classical reading of 15:2 — wish at the Hour/at Badr per tafsir reports; Ibn Kathir collects the options; agent selection — verify).
* **`[15:3]`** "Leave them to eat, and enjoy, and be lulled by hope. They will find out."
  * **Gloss:** The prophet is told to abandon them to their appetite and their long hope — "They will find out" defers the argument to experience.
* **`[15:4]`** "We have never destroyed a town unless it had a set time."
  * **Gloss:** The law that disciplines all destruction narratives in this surah: no wreck happens before its deadline.
* **`[15:5]`** "No nation can bring its time forward, nor can they delay it."
  * **Gloss:** The two-sided fixity of that deadline — the same formula-twin at `[7:34]`, `[10:49]`, `[16:61]`.
* **`[15:6]`** "And they said, “O you who received the message, you are insane.”"
  * **Gloss:** The accusation scene opens: their own address ("you upon whom the Reminder is sent down") concedes the phenomenon while rejecting the man; the pairing "not insane — by the grace of your Lord" answers it at `[68:2]`.
* **`[15:7]`** "Why do you not bring us the angels, if you are truthful?”"
  * **Gloss:** The spectacle-demand: angels as verification. Corpus-unique wording here; the same demand at `[6:8]`.
* **`[15:8]`** "We do not send the angels down except with reason, and they will not be held back."
  * **Gloss:** Angels come "with truth/measure" (bi'l-haqq) — i.e., on errand, not on demand — and when sent, the case is closed, no reprieve (cf. `[6:8]` "the matter would have been settled, and they would not have been reprieved").
* **`[15:9]`** "Surely We revealed the Message, and We will surely preserve it."
  * **Gloss:** The centerpiece, both halves doubled by lam-oaths in the Arabic (classical note; agent flag): revelation and its guard belong to the same "We" — the book's integrity is a divine undertaking, not a human achievement.
* **`[15:10]`** "We sent others before you, to the former communities."
  * **Gloss:** The precedent defense: you are not an anomaly — the office has history.
* **`[15:11]`** "But no messenger came to them, but they ridiculed him."
  * **Gloss:** Near-verbatim twin at `[43:7]`; ridicule is the constant of the office, so mockery refutes nothing.
* **`[15:12]`** "Thus We slip it into the hearts of the guilty."
  * **Gloss:** The grammar is severe: the derision ("it") is slipped by God Himself into criminal hearts — the hardening is a divine verb over their chosen contempt (Source: classical reading — Ibn Kathir on 15:12 ties it to God's sealing of persistent rejecters, cf. `[2:7]`; agent labeling — verify).
* **`[15:13]`** "They do not believe in it, though the ways of the ancients have passed away."
  * **Gloss:** Stance fixed past evidence: they won't believe, and the ancients' destroyed "ways" (sunnah) have gone by as precedent unlearned.
* **`[15:14]`** "Even if We opened for them a gateway into the sky, and they began to ascend through it."
  * **Gloss:** The reductio of the spectacle-demand: grant the exact miracle (a sky-gate, ascension — the demand of `[17:93]`)…
* **`[15:15]`** "They would still say, “Our eyes are hallucinating; in fact, we are people bewitched.”"
  * **Gloss:** …and refusal would still self-medicalize the vision: intoxication, not evidence. The bewitched-cluster: `[26:153]`, `[26:185]`, `[17:47]`, `[23:89]`.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **Muqatta'at (disconnected letters):** `[15:1]`.
  * **Rendering:** "Alif, Lam, Ra." — transliterated, untranslated, numbered as ayah 1.
  * **Elsewhere:** the ALR cluster is exactly five surahs — `[10:1]`, `[11:1]`, `[12:1]`, `[14:1]`, `[15:1]` (verified) — each followed by a Book/revelation claim: the 12:1 twin "These are the Verses of the Clear Book," 11:1's perfected Scripture, 14:1's revealed Scripture.
* **`dhikr` (the Message):** `[15:9]` "Surely We revealed the Message."
  * **Rendering:** "the Message" here; Itani's dominant rendering of the same word elsewhere is "the Reminder" (`[20:124]`, `[21:105]`, `[43:5]`, `[65:10]`, `[81:27]`); "Message" recurs for dhikr at `[25:18]`, `[25:29]` (verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[41:41–42]` "Those who reject the Reminder when it has come to them—it is an invincible Book. Falsehood cannot approach it, from before it or behind it" — the preservation guarantee of 15:9 spelled out (observable corpus pairing; the dhikr=Quran identification per consensus tafsir).
* **"makes things clear":** `[15:1]`.
  * **Rendering:** "a Quran that makes things clear"; the same root-family gives 12:1's "the Clear Book."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[27:1]` "a book that makes things clear"; `[43:2]` "By the Book that makes things clear" (verified) — clarity is the Book's self-advertised property against the opacity-accusations of 6–15.
* **"set time" (ajal ma'dud/maloom):** `[15:4–5]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:34]`, `[10:49]`, `[16:61]` — "appointed time… cannot delay it by one hour, nor advance it"; `[18:59]` towns destroyed "at an appointed time" — the law that governs every destruction report in ruku 4–6.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Kalam link:** `[15:9]` ↔ `[41:41–42]` — the twin of the preservation claim; feeds the [Kalam note](../../02-knowledge/202610132005-k-kalam_allah_revelation_as_speech.md)'s thesis that the book's apologetic is about its own delivery and keeping (that note does not yet cite 15:9 — gap reported to orchestrator, 2026-10-14).
* **Judgment on the 2:2 pairing (assignment):** `[2:1–2]` — same opening grammar (letters → Book), but the claims are on different axes: 2:2 addresses the reader ("in which there is no doubt," a guide for the righteous), while 15:9 addresses God's side of the contract (He revealed it, He guards it). Link them as complementary poles of one claim, not as duplicates.
* **Angels-demand cluster:** `[15:7–8]` ↔ `[6:8–9]` — "Had We sent down an angel, the matter would have been settled"; `[2:210]` — waiting for Allah "in the shadows of the clouds, together with the angels" is the same misunderstood demand; `[17:92–93]` — "bring Allah and the angels before us… unless you bring down for us a book that we can read."
* **Sky-gate reductio:** `[15:14–15]` ↔ `[17:93]` (ascension demand with its own refusal logic) and `[78:19]` "the sky is opened up, and becomes gateways" — opened gates belong to the Day, not to argument.
* **Within surah:** `[15:11]` ridicule-law is ruku 6's `[15:95]` "We are enough for you against the mockers"; `[15:12–13]`'s slipped hearts pre-load the hardening seen in Sodom's "came joyfully" (`[15:67]`) and the Rock's carved complacency (`[15:82]`).
