# Precedence-envy, the parents prayer, and the squandered account (46:11–20)
**UID:** 202610142053
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 46:11–20 (Ruku 2 of 4 in al-Ahqaf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[46:11]` – `[46:20]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku audits two rival claims to rank: the enviers who cannot bear that goodness arrived to others first (11), and the sneering son who cannot bear that his parents' God raises the dead (17) — against which stands the grateful forty-year prayer of `[46:15]`, the surah's moral center, whose account is settled at the Fire in `[46:20]`: every good "squandered and enjoyed" comes back as shame.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 4; the two-books panel (Moses's model and mercy, this confirming Arabic Book, 12) answers the evidence-demand of `[46:4]`, and the parents passage (15–17) sits exactly mid-surah — the gratitude test between the cosmic and the eschatological arguments.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[46:11]`** "Those who disbelieve say to those who believe, “If it were anything good, they would not have preceded us to it.” And since they were not guided by it, they will say, “This is an ancient lie.”"
  * **Gloss:** Precedence-envy diagnosed: grace received by others is taken as evidence of its worthlessness; refusal to be guided produces the "ancient lie" label (corpus-unique phrase, rg-verified). Ledger: the task's proposed quote ("has this come upon them?... Allah knows best what the doers of good do") is not this edition's 46:11 — the knows-best clause occurs nowhere in s046 (rg-verified).
* **`[46:12]`** "And before it was the Book of Moses, a model and a mercy. And this is a confirming Book, in the Arabic language, to warn those who do wrong—and good news for the doers of good."
  * **Gloss:** The twin-book panel: Moses's Book "a model and a mercy" ("model" is corpus-unique here, rg-verified; cf. `[28:43]` "an illumination... guidance, and mercy"); *this* Book confirms, warns, and gladdens — its Arabicity answering the foreign-Scripture demand of `[41:44]`.
* **`[46:13]`** "Those who say, “Our Lord is Allah,” then lead a righteous life—they have nothing to fear, nor shall they grieve."
  * **Gloss:** The no-fear formula — near-twin `[41:30]` ("Surely, those who say: 'Our Lord is Allah,' and then go straight, the angels will descend upon them"); faith plus upright continuation, not a single utterance.
* **`[46:14]`** "These are the inhabitants of Paradise, where they will dwell forever—a reward for what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The permanence clause; recompense keyed to *what they used to do* — the verb that will recur at 19 and 20.
* **`[46:15]`** "We have enjoined upon man kindness to his parents. His mother carried him with difficulty, and delivered him with difficulty. His bearing and weaning takes thirty months. Until, when he has attained his maturity, and has reached forty years, he says, “Lord, enable me to appreciate the blessings You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents, and to act with righteousness, pleasing You. And improve my children for me. I have sincerely repented to You, and I am of those who have surrendered.”"
  * **Gloss:** THE PARENTS VERSE — the duty (kindness), the mother's double difficulty, thirty months, then the forty-year prayer with four petitions: gratitude for blessings on me *and my parents*, righteous conduct, improved offspring, repentance and surrender. Ledger: the task's quoted wording ("hardship upon hardship," "weaning is two years," "to Me is the return") belongs to Luqman, `[31:14]`–`[31:15]`; this edition distinguishes them — 46:15 says "with difficulty... thirty months," and its closing is "I am of those who have surrendered," not "to Me is the return." The shukr verb here is rendered "appreciate" (Itani's consistent drift, cf. `[31:12]`). Backlink: [Shukr note](../../02-knowledge/202610141600-k-shukr_gratitude_audit.md) (§7).
* **`[46:16]`** "Those are they from whom We accept the best of their deeds, and We overlook their misdeeds, among the dwellers of Paradise— the promise of truth which they are promised."
  * **Gloss:** The divine accounting: the *best* accepted, the misdeeds overlooked — "the promise of truth," the phrase also at `[14:22]` (Satan's concession) and `[21:97]` (its arrival).
* **`[46:17]`** "As for him who says to his parents, “Enough of you! Are you promising me that I will be raised up, when generations have passed away before me?” While they cry for Allah’s help, “Woe to you! Believe! The promise of Allah is true!” But he says, “These are nothing but tales of the ancients.”"
  * **Gloss:** The counter-son staged beat-for-beat against the grateful son: "Enough of you!" (corpus-unique rendering of the parents-rebuff; cf. the prohibition of even a word of disrespect, `[17:23]`), resurrection mocked, parents' appeal to Allah's help, and the same "ancient" label as 46:11 — "tales of the ancients" — envy and disbelief share one vocabulary.
* **`[46:18]`** "Those are they upon whom the sentence is justified, among the communities that have passed away before them, of jinn and humans. They are truly losers."
  * **Gloss:** The sentence becomes binding — promise-fulfilment grammar standing in for covenant language (no mithaq-form in s046, rg-verified); "of jinn and humans" plants the counter-audience that arrives at 29.
* **`[46:19]`** "There are degrees for everyone, according to what they have done, and He will repay them for their works in full, and they will not be wronged."
  * **Gloss:** The answer to 46:11's envy: rank (degrees) is divine payroll by works, full and unwronged — twin `[6:132]` "They all have ranks according to what they did."
* **`[46:20]`** "On the Day when the faithless will be paraded before the Fire: “You have squandered your good in your worldly life, and you took pleasure in them. So today you are being repaid with the torment of shame, because of your unjust arrogance on earth, and because you used to sin.”"
  * **Gloss:** THE ENJOYMENT-ACCOUNT ✓ (task theme, with corrections): wording is "squandered your good... took pleasure" — spending without gratitude, repaid as "torment of shame" (twin `[41:16]` "the punishment of shame"; presentation scene cf. `[51:13]`); three causes stack: arrogance, injustice, sin. "the faithless" — third kufr rendering in one surah (3 "blasphemers," 7/11 "disbelieve," 34 "disbelieved").

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ifk qadim` / asatir (the "ancient" label)**: disbelief's verdict on revelation.
  * **Rendering:** "an ancient lie" (46:11, corpus-unique) and "tales of the ancients" (46:17).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[23:83]`, `[27:68]` "legends of the ancients"; `[68:15]` "Myths of the ancients!" — one topos, three Itani renderings (rg-verified); the family spans the whole Meccan corpus.
* **`shukr` (gratitude)**: acknowledge-and-spend; here fused with parent-kindness.
  * **Rendering:** "enable me to appreciate the blessings" (46:15) — shukr verb as "appreciate," niʿma as "blessings."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[31:12]` "Whoever is appreciative—is appreciative for the benefit of his own soul" (same drift); `[31:14]` "So give thanks to Me, and to your parents. To Me is the destination" — the twin passage standardly renders the verb "give thanks"; canonical note linked in §7.
* **`wasiyyah` (enjoined kindness)**: "We have enjoined upon man kindness to his parents" (46:15).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[17:23]` welds parent-kindness to exclusive worship and bans the minimal insult ("a word of disrespect") — exactly the rudeness performed in 46:17; 46:15's mother-double-hardship pairs with `[31:14]` ("hardship upon hardship," rg-verified wording there).
* **`darajat` (degrees)**: 46:19 "There are degrees for everyone, according to what they have done."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:132]` "They all have ranks according to what they did" — the verbatim-shape twin; degrees are God's answer to precedence-envy, not its confirmation.
