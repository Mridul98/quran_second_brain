# Townships around you, the jinn who listened, and the messengers of resolve (46:27–35)
**UID:** 202610142055
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 46:27–35 (Ruku 4 of 4 in al-Ahqaf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[46:27]` – `[46:35]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's closing gambit reverses the audiences: the hearers' own landscape testifies (27–28), then a dispatched band of jinn — non-human listeners — attend the recitation, go home warning their people, and model the exact response the human opponents refuse (29–32); with the tiredless Creator proven able to raise the dead (33) and the Fire-scene re-staged (34), the book ends by commanding patience *by the measure of the messengers of resolve* (35).
* **Placement:** Final ruku; gathers the surah's threads — the mute gods (4–6) resurface as abandoned intercessors (28), the warnings ignored (3) are heard by jinn (29), and the impatience of Aad (22, 24) is answered by the patience-command and the hour-compression of the promised Day (35).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[46:27]`** "We have destroyed many townships around you, and diversified the signs, so that they may return."
  * **Gloss:** The geography of evidence: *around you* — the destroyed settlements as the hearers' own horizon (cf. `[6:92]`, the Book warning "the Mother of Cities and all around it"); "diversified the signs" restates the surah's method; the aim is return, not annihilation.
* **`[46:28]`** "Why then did the idols, whom they worshiped as means of nearness to Allah, not help them? In fact, they abandoned them. It was their lie, a fabrication of their own making."
  * **Gloss:** The intercession audit: gods taken as *wasilah* ("means of nearness"; the only other corpus site is the command to "seek the means of approach," `[5:35]`) become absent exactly when needed — "they abandoned them"; the religion is dissolved into its own fabrication. Completes the 4–6 audit of the mute gods.
* **`[46:29]`** "Recall when We dispatched towards you a number of jinn, to listen to the Quran. When they came in its presence, they said, “Pay attention!” Then, when it was concluded, they rushed to their people, warning them."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-audience: jinn *dispatched* (divine initiative), organized around the recitation ("Pay attention!" — an audience disciplining itself), and their first act after hearing is mission — "rushed to their people, warning them." The human opponents ignore warnings *received*; these listeners go distribute them. Paired surah: 72 (al-Jinn).
* **`[46:30]`** "They said, “O our people, we have heard a Scripture, sent down after Moses, confirming what came before it. It guides to the truth, and to a straight path."
  * **Gloss:** The jinn sermon, in the surah's own vocabulary: "sent down" (echoing `[46:2]`), after-Moses and confirming (answering `[46:12]`), truth-and-straight-path guidance. Their *O our people* mirrors every messenger's speech in this book (46:21, 31). Book typography: the quotation opens here without closing, running to `[46:31]` — quote as-is, never normalize (cf. s040's class-2 artifact note).
* **`[46:31]`** "O our people! Answer the caller to Allah, and believe in Him; and He will forgive you your sins, and will save you from a painful punishment.”"
  * **Gloss:** The petition: *answer the caller* (da'i — "Your Lord has said, 'Pray to Me, and I will respond to you,'" `[40:60]`; and see `[3:193]`, the humans' past-tense confession "we have heard a caller calling to the faith... and we have believed" — the line the task's "we answered the caller and believed" actually recalls); belief paired with forgiveness and rescue, the Ha-Meem salvation formula.
* **`[46:32]`** "He who does not answer the caller to Allah will not escape on earth, and has no protectors besides Him. Those are in obvious error."
  * **Gloss:** The negative option: no flight on earth, no rival protectors (tawhid restated as the loneliness of the refuser), error made "obvious" — the same verdict-word as 46:28's "their lie."
* **`[46:33]`** "Do they not realize that Allah, who created the heavens and the earth, and was never tired by creating them, is Able to revive the dead? Yes indeed; He is Capable of everything."
  * **Gloss:** The resurrection argument from creation-without-fatigue — verbatim-shape twin `[50:38]` "We created the heavens and the earth and what is between them in six days, and no fatigue touched Us"; a fortiori: if the greater act cost nothing, the lesser is "Capable of everything."
* **`[46:34]`** "On the Day when those who disbelieved are presented to the Fire: “Is this not real?” They will say, “Yes, indeed, by our Lord.” He will say, “Then taste the suffering for having disbelieved.”"
  * **Gloss:** The second exposure-scene, tighter than the first: a three-part interrogation — the question, the sworn admission ("by our Lord" — truth finally spoken by disbelievers), and the sentence "taste the suffering." Restages `[46:20]` (cf. `[51:13]`); kufr's fourth rendering ("those who disbelieved," where 3 had "blasphemers," 20 "the faithless").
* **`[46:35]`** "So be patient, as the messengers with resolve were patient, and do not be hasty regarding them. On the Day when they witness what they are promised, it will seem as if they had lasted only for an hour of a day. A proclamation: Will any be destroyed except the sinful people?"
  * **Gloss:** THE SABR CLOSE ✓: patience commanded with a *model* — "the messengers with resolve" (ulu-l-'azm, corpus-unique rendering, rg-verified; cf. `[42:43]`, patience-and-forgiveness as "a sign of real resolve"); the paired prohibition answers Aad's "bring us what you threaten" (22) and "what you were impatient for" (24); the hour-compression twin of `[10:45]` ("as if they had tarried only one hour of a day") and `[79:46]`; the closing cry is the verbatim-shape twin of `[6:47]` "would any be destroyed except the wrongdoing people?" — the Ha-Meem family ends on patience, not panic.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`jinn`**: the second audience.
  * **Rendering:** 46:29 "a number of jinn, to listen to the Quran"; 46:18 "communities... of jinn and humans."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[72:1]` — "a band of jinn listened in... 'We have heard a wondrous Quran'" — the paired surah; "wondrous" belongs to 72's wording, not 46:30 (ledger, see index §1); the 46 account supplies the dispatch-and-return frame 72 narrates first-person.
* **`da'i (the caller)`**: 46:31–32 "the caller to Allah."
  * **Rendering:** the recitation itself personified as summons — the jinn heard *words* and answer a *caller*.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:186]` — "I answer the call of the caller when he calls on Me. So let them answer Me" (the verb pair, verified); `[3:193]` — "we have heard a caller calling to the faith... and we have believed" — the human rhyme of the jinn scene.
* **`wasilah (means of nearness)`**: 46:28 "worshiped as means of nearness to Allah."
  * **Rendering:** the idols as approach-brokers, exposed as absconding fabrications.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:35]` — "seek the means of approach to Him... so that you may succeed" — the only corpus twin; there a command, here its counterfeit (rg-verified pairing).
* **`ulu-l-'azm (messengers with resolve)`**: 46:35, corpus-unique.
  * **Rendering:** "the messengers with resolve were patient" — resolve defined *by* patience.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[42:43]` — "whoever endures patiently and forgives—that is a sign of real resolve" (same root, Ha-Meem twin); the traditional roster of the resolve-messengers is transmitted, not Quranic (*Source: classical tradition — verify in print*).
