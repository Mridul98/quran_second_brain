# The testimony of the liars: oaths, timber, and the expulsion boast (63:1–8)
**UID:** 202610142191
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 63:1–8 (Ruku 1 of 2 in al-Munafiqun)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[63:1]` – `[63:8]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The class named in the surah's title performs its own indictment: it testifies correctly about the Messenger and is thereby exposed (63:1 — Allah validates the testimony *and* brands the witnesses liars), converts oaths into cover (63:2), runs the believe-then-disbelieve mechanism to its terminal seal (63:3), and keeps a full political profile — impressive bodies, panicked hearts, a refused forgiveness, a defunding plot, and an expulsion boast about the City (63:4–8) — while "power belongs to Allah, and His Messenger, and the believers."
* **Placement:** Opening ruku and the k-nifaq note's anchor dossier (the note cites 63:1–3, 63:7, and 63:9–10 — backlinks verified); ruku 2 then turns the same diagnosis on the believers themselves.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[63:1]`** "When the hypocrites come to you, they say, “We bear witness that you are Allah’s Messenger.” Allah knows that you are His Messenger, and Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are liars."
  * **Gloss:** The perfect crime of speech: the testimony is theologically true — and liturgically worthless, because Allah's counter-witness lands on the speakers. Truthful words, dishonest bearers: the surah's definition of nifaq in one verse.
* **`[63:2]`** "They treat their oaths as a cover, and so they repel others from Allah’s path. Evil is what they do."
  * **Gloss:** Oaths function as protective armor (junnah — Itani: "a cover"); the harm is missional ("repel others from Allah's path"), not merely self-deceptive.
* **`[63:3]`** "That is because they believed, and then disbelieved; so their hearts were sealed, and they cannot understand."
  * **Gloss:** The seal-mechanism, with its sequence stated: a prior belief (verbal, contractual), then unbelief (here-and-now), then closure — the "cannot understand" is consequence, not prior cause.
* **`[63:4]`** "When you see them, their appearance impresses you. And when they speak, you listen to what they say. They are like propped-up timber. They think every shout is aimed at them. They are the enemy, so beware of them. Allah condemns them; how deluded they are!"
  * **Gloss:** One ayah, five movements: bodies (impressive), speech (listened to), simile (propped-up timber — cut wood that only stands because it is leaned), paranoia (every cry against them), and the security verdict ("they are the enemy, so beware"). Task-flag resolution: both the "every cry" and "they are the enemy" clauses sit in this single composite 63:4 in this edition's numbering, not in 63:5–6.
* **`[63:5]`** "And when it is said to them, “Come, the Messenger of Allah will ask forgiveness for you,” they bend their heads, and you see them turning away arrogantly."
  * **Gloss:** The invitation to the Prophet's istighfar is received with neck-bending arrogance — the same body-language as 63:4's impressive appearance, now turned away.
* **`[63:6]`** "It is the same for them, whether you ask forgiveness for them, or do not ask forgiveness for them; Allah will not forgive them. Allah does not guide the sinful people."
  * **Gloss:** The forgiveness-clause closes; the verdict formula "does not guide the sinful people" is shared with `[61:5]` — exactly two occurrences in the corpus (verified couplet; cf. 62:5's variant "wrongdoing people").
* **`[63:7]`** "It is they who say: “Do not spend anything on those who side with Allah’s Messenger, unless they have dispersed.” To Allah belong the treasures of the heavens and the earth, but the hypocrites do not understand."
  * **Gloss:** The budget war: starve the Messenger's partisans until the camp breaks — an economic strategy answered by theology (the treasures are not in their vaults).
* **`[63:8]`** "They say, “If we return to the City, the more powerful therein will evict the weak.” But power belongs to Allah, and His Messenger, and the believers; but the hypocrites do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The political calculation — a return to the City with a power ranking — is inverted: might (ʿizza) belongs to the believer-triangle, and the class's defining flaw ends the ruku: "do not know" (against 63:3's "cannot understand" and 63:7's "do not understand" — the three degrees of the closure).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`munafiqun` (the hypocrites):** title-word, 63:1.
  * **Rendering:** "the hypocrites" throughout the surah.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:8]` "who say, “We believe in Allah and in the Last Day,” but they are not believers" (the opening-surah twin of 63:1's pattern — verified); `[4:142]` the prayer-laziness signature (cited in k-nifaq); `[3:167]` "And that He may know the hypocrites" — the battle-refuser — all verified in corpus.
* **`jiinnah` (cover):** `[63:2]` "They treat their oaths as a cover".
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:92]` — "Nor use your oaths as means of deception among you" — the oath-as-shield/deception nexus, different rendering of a cognate usage (verified; *Source: observable translator choice*).
* **`khatama` (sealed):** `[63:3]` "their hearts were sealed".
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:7]` "Allah has set a seal on their hearts and on their hearing" (the archetypal statement for 2:6–7's disbelievers); `[4:155]` and `[16:108]` "Allah has sealed their hearts..." — verified; the sequence believe→disbelieve→sealed is specific to 63:3 in this corpus.
* **ʿizza (might/power):** `[63:8]` "the more powerful... will evict the weak... But power belongs to Allah".
  * **Rendering:** drift within one verse: "more powerful" (aʿazz) → "power" (ʿizza) — observable; the divine-ownership reassignment answers the humans' internal ranking claim.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan, agreed in the type-lists (see index).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** Classical reports place 63:7–8 at the expedition of Banu al-Mustaliq (5 AH), with the leader of the hypocrites' "the more powerful will evict the weaker" boast upon return to Medina — and 63:5–6 with the same figure refusing the Prophet's offered forgiveness; "the City" (al-Madinah) is thus read as Medina itself. *Source: classical maghazi/tafsir reports (Ibn Kathir/Tabari family on 63:5–8) — agent labeling; verify.* The corpus states no occasion.
