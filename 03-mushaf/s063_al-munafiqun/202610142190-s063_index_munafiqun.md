* **Title etymology:** al-Munafiqun = "the hypocrites" (n-f-q root — the burrower, the one who exits the hole it hides in; *Source: standard lexicon note — verify against a print dictionary*). The title is a diagnosis, and the surah keeps the patient in the third person until the last three verses operate on the believers themselves.
* **Reading hint:** the knowledge-ladder inside ruku 1 is observable in the renderings: 63:3 "they cannot understand" → 63:7 "the hypocrites do not understand" → 63:8 "the hypocrites do not know" — three descending closures before the surah hands the mirror to the community (63:9).
* **Central theme:** Testimony as forgery and the economics of attention. Ruku 1 stages the hypocrites' front — "We bear witness that you are Allah's Messenger," answered by a double divine witness (He is, and they are liars, 63:1) — then strips the exterior: oaths as cover (63:2), believe-then-disbelieve and the sealed hearts (63:3), impressive bodies that are "propped-up timber," panic and enmity (63:4), refused forgiveness (63:5–6), a budget war against the Prophet's partisans (63:7), and the expulsion boast answered: "power belongs to Allah, and His Messenger, and the believers" (63:8). Ruku 2 pivots to the addressed community — possessions, children, the deathbed petition, and the un-delayed term (63:9–11).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Counter-witness (63:1):** human testimony about the Messenger validated by Allah — and the witnesses themselves indicted; the surah's method is to place every human speech-act under divine counter-speech.
* **Mechanism (63:2–3):** oaths as cover; belief then unbelief; the seal — and its consequence, "they cannot understand."
* **Aesthetics of the hollow (63:4):** bodies that impress, speech that is listened to — then two similes (timber; panic at every shout) in one verse.
* **Refusal (63:5–6):** the invitation to the Messenger's forgiveness is met with bent-necked turning-away; the verdict: unforgivable, unguided — the "sinful people" formula shared with 61:5 (verified couplet).
* **The budget war (63:7–8):** withhold spending so the Prophet's partisans disperse — answered by the treasures of heavens and earth; the Medina power-calculus — answered by whom power truly belongs to.
* **The turn inward (63:9–11):** the believers are the ones addressed about distraction; the surah ends where as-Saff ended its offer — with spending, and with Allah "Informed of what you do."
* **Verse-compression note (numbering, this Kufi-count edition):** 63:4 packs five sentences (appearance, speech, timber simile, panic, enemy-verdict) into one numbered ayah, and 63:1–3 each carry their own single charge — retrieval should key on sentence fragments, not ayah numbers, for this surah. *Source: observable corpus line structure.*
* **Mirror structure:** ruku 1 speaks about *them* (8 verses of third person), ruku 2 about *you* (3 verses of "O you who believe") — the surah's whole argument is that the border between those two pronouns runs through every household's ledger (possessions, children, timing of giving).
