# The Trumpet, the two verdicts, and the covenant on the stand: mouths sealed, limbs speaking (36:51–67)
**UID:** 202610141915
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 36:51–67 (Ruku 4 of 5 in Ya-Sin)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[36:51]` – `[36:67]` (17 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The blast 36:49 promised arrives: the Trumpet, a rush from the tombs, the sleepers' protest, and the narrator's verdict-line ("This is what the Most Gracious had promised") — then the Day splits into two performative utterances, "Peace—a saying" for the gardens and "step aside today, you criminals" for the fire, adjudicated by the Adamic covenant ("Did I not covenant with you, O Children of Adam") and executed by sealed mouths whose own hands and feet testify everything.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 5; the courtroom ruku — the promise-taunt of 48 answered at 52, the running martyr's "listen to me" (25) answered by limbs that cannot be silenced (65), and 36:4's oath-path finally defined at 61.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[36:51]`** "The Trumpet will be blown, then behold, they will rush from the tombs to their Lord."
  * **Gloss:** The schedule arrives without notice; "rush from the tombs" verbatim-twin `[70:43]` "emerge from the tombs in a rush, as though they were hurrying towards a target" (verified); the two-sounding structure of `[39:68]` (sounded… sounded another time… rise up, looking on) underwrites the sequence.
* **`[36:52]`** "They will say, “Woe to us! Who resurrected us from our resting-place?” This is what the Most Gracious had promised, and the messengers have spoken the truth.”"
  * **Gloss:** The protesters' question answered mid-verse by a voice the corpus leaves unassigned (transmitted reports say the angels or the believers — Source: consensus of classical tafsir — verify); the answer validates promise-and-messenger — 36:48's taunt "When will this promise be?" cashed; typographically the extra closing mark here balances the quote opened at `[36:20]`/`[36:22]` (whole-file totals verified 17 “ / 17 ”); "Woe to us!" belongs to the Day's cry-family with 36:30 "Alas for the servants" and `[6:31]` "Alas for us" (verified).
* **`[36:53]`** "It will be but a single scream; and behold, they will all be brought before Us."
  * **Gloss:** The task's "brought forth" is this: "brought before Us" — total, one-sound summoning; intra-surah triple: Cry (29) → blast (49) → scream (53), one sound three verdicts (note case drift "Cry"/"scream" — book typography).
* **`[36:54]`** "On that Day, no soul will be wronged in the least, and you will be recompensed only for what you used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The justice axiom: zero injustice, exact payback; verbatim-logic twin `[45:28]` "Today you are being repaid for what you used to do" (verified); the record of 36:12 becomes the receipt.
* **`[36:55]`** "The inhabitants of Paradise, on that Day, will be happily busy."
  * **Gloss:** Occupation, not boredom: fulfillment keeps them occupied with delight — the Day's positive half begins as absorbed joy.
* **`[36:56]`** "They and their spouses, in shades, reclining on couches."
  * **Gloss:** Spouses, shade, couches — the triple close is `[52:20]` "Relaxing on luxurious furnishings; and We will couple them with gorgeous spouses" (verified); cf. `[2:25]` pure spouses.
* **`[36:57]`** "They will have therein fruits. They will have whatever they call for."
  * **Gloss:** Demand becomes supply — the unearned abundance of 36:35 (grains "although their hands did not make it") consummated.
* **`[36:58]`** "Peace—a saying from a Most Merciful Lord."
  * **Gloss:** A kalima (saying) of salam spoken by the Rahman himself — greeting-family `[10:10]`, `[56:26]` "Peace, peace", `[14:23]` (all verified); the Word that was a sentence against the sealed (7, 70) is here a salutation: the kalima-bracket of the surah completes.
* **`[36:59]`** "But step aside today, you criminals."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-address, one verse later — separation as the negative sacrament; the criminals-line family `[77:46]` "you are indeed criminals" (verified); two utterances (58/59) divide the Day with no tribunal dialogue needed.
* **`[36:60]`** "Did I not covenant with you, O Children of Adam, that you shall not serve the devil? That he is your sworn enemy?"
  * **Gloss:** The covenant put on the stand — the Adamic interrogation-scene (verb-form twin `[20:115]` "We covenanted with Adam before, but he forgot"; testimony-scene cognate `[7:172]` "Am I not your Lord?"; oath-idiom `[5:7]`); "sworn enemy" exact idiom at `[7:22]`, `[12:5]` (verified), cf. `[18:50]`.
* **`[36:61]`** "And that you shall serve Me? This is a straight path."
  * **Gloss:** The covenant's second table — serve Me alone — then its definition: "This is a straight path," the surah answering its own oath-verse 36:4 (bracket closed); tawhid as the path's shape, not a maze of intermediaries (cf. 36:23's no-partners reductio).
* **`[36:62]`** "He has misled a great multitude of you. Did you not understand?"
  * **Gloss:** The devil's casualty-count stated, then the cognitive taunt ("Did you not understand?" — chain with 31 consider / 68 understand / 77 consider); the waylaying is `[7:16]` Iblis's own vow "I will waylay them on Your straight path" (verified) — the path is booby-trapped by its enemy's own admission.
* **`[36:63]`** "This is Hellfire, which you were promised."
  * **Gloss:** The other promised thing — demonstrative "This is" points; "which you were promised" rhymes 52's "had promised" (double fulfillment: promise as peace at 58, promise as fire here).
* **`[36:64]`** "Roast in it today, because you persistently disbelieved."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict executed: "persistently" names disbelief as practiced habit, not single act; the command-form echoes the covenant interrogation of 60–62.
* **`[36:65]`** "On this Day, We will seal their mouths, and their hands will speak to Us, and their feet will testify to everything they had done."
  * **Gloss:** The sealed mouths of 36:9's enshrouded people finally get their verdict — testimony redistributed to the body; dossier: `[24:24]` tongues/hands/feet testify, `[41:20]` hearing/sight/skins, `[41:21]` "Allah, Who made all things speak, made us speak" (all verified) — and against 36:50 (no will made) every limb files the will by proxy.
* **`[36:66]`** "If We will, We can blind their eyes as they rush towards the path—but how will they see?"
  * **Gloss:** The first standing option — blinding mid-rush; "how will they see?" answers 36:9's "cannot see" with irony: the path is rushed toward and cannot be seen; sovereignty held in reserve, "if We will."
* **`[36:67]`** "And if We will, We can cripple them in their place; so they can neither move forward, nor go back."
  * **Gloss:** The second standing option — paralysis in situ; "neither move forward, nor go back" re-runs the double barrier front/behind of 36:9 and the no-return of 36:50; "if We will" twice frames both options as pedagogy, not necessity.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`covenant`**: 36:60's verb-form interrogation.
  * **Rendering:** "Did I not covenant with you, O Children of Adam" — verb, first person, on the Day's stand.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:115]` "We covenanted with Adam before, but he forgot"; `[7:172]` — testimony scene; `[5:7]` "His covenant which He covenanted with you… Allah knows what the hearts contain" — all verified; the mithaq note's center-of-gravity anchor.
* **`the Trumpet`**: 36:51's opening event.
  * **Rendering:** "The Trumpet will be blown" — sur rendered Trumpet, capitalization stable here.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[39:68]` sounded twice; `[50:20]` "This is the Promised Day"; `[74:8]`, `[27:87]`; lowercase "the trumpet" at `[6:73]` — intra-book case drift (observable).
* **`sworn enemy`**: 36:60's devil-definition.
  * **Rendering:** "That he is your sworn enemy?" — aduww as sworn-enemy idiom.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:22]` "Satan is a sworn enemy to you"; `[12:5]` "Satan is man's sworn enemy"; `[18:50]` "when they are an enemy to you" — verified set.
