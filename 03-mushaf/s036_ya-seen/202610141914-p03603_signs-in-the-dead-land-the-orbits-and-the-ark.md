# Signs: the dead land revived, the pairs, the day stripped from the night, each luminary afloat — and the laden Ark (36:33–50)
**UID:** 202610141914
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 36:33–50 (Ruku 3 of 5 in Ya-Sin)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[36:33]` – `[36:50]` (18 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A stacked exhibit-list — dead earth brought back to life, unearned gardens, pairs without end, night flayed of day, sun and moon forbidden to collide, the Ark and its successors — is laid before the same sealed town that answers every sign with turning-away, fee-denial, and "when will this promise be?", so the ruku ends with the blast arriving mid-feud, too late for wills or goodbyes.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 5; the cosmological proof-block between the parable's verdict (32) and the Day's courtroom (51+); its 36:36 doxology opens the "Glory be to Him" bracket closed by 36:83.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[36:33]`** "And there is a sign for them in the dead land: We give it life, and produce from it grains from which they eat."
  * **Gloss:** Exhibit A, verbatim the "dead earth revived" dossier the task asked after; the explicit resurrection mapping is `[30:19]` "He revives the land after it had died. Likewise you will be resurrected" (verified twin; cf. `[7:57]` "Thus We bring out the dead—perhaps you will reflect").
* **`[36:34]`** "And We place in it gardens of palm-trees and vines, and cause springs to gush out of it."
  * **Gloss:** The revival over-delivers: grain, then palms, vines, springs — abundance beyond subsistence.
* **`[36:35]`** "That they may eat from its fruits, although their hands did not make it. Will they not be appreciative?"
  * **Gloss:** The unearned-harvest clause is the shukr audit's proof-text (gratitude owed where labor is absent); the question-form recurs at 36:73 — intra-surah pair.
* **`[36:36]`** "Glory be to Him who created all the pairs; of what the earth produces, and of their own selves, and of what they do not know."
  * **Gloss:** Doxology on polarity as craft — crops, humans, and the unknown-unknowns; correction on the ledger: no "that they may be reminded" here — that purpose clause belongs to `[51:49]` "We created all things in pairs, so that you may reflect and ponder" (verified); "Glory be to Him" brackets with 36:83.
* **`[36:37]`** "Another sign for them is the night: We strip the day out of it—and they are in darkness."
  * **Gloss:** Exhibit B: day extracted from night like a sheath pulled off (the task's "night covers them" is not this corpus's line); darkness as staged pedagogy, not mere atmosphere.
* **`[36:38]`** "And the sun runs towards its destination. Such is the design of the Almighty, the All- Knowing."
  * **Gloss:** The sun on the move toward mustaqarr — the destination shared with `[50:43]` "to Us is the destination" and `[22:48]`; "the All- Knowing" prints with an internal space in this edition [sic — class-1-style remnant, quoted as-is; 36:81 has "All-Knowing" clean].
* **`[36:39]`** "And the moon: We have disposed it in phases, until it returns like the old twig."
  * **Gloss:** The moon given stages (manazil) and a sickle finale — "the old twig" is a corpus-unique image (rg-verified); dated time rendered as thinning light.
* **`[36:40]`** "The sun is not to overtake the moon, nor is the night to outpace the day. Each floats in an orbit."
  * **Gloss:** The race with rules: no overtaking, no collision — the task's "each swimming in an orbit" reflects the classical sabaha gloss; the corpus word is "floats," and the verbatim cognate is `[21:33]` "each floating in an orbit" (verified); cf. `[55:5]` "move according to plan".
* **`[36:41]`** "Another sign for them is that We carried their offspring in the laden Ark."
  * **Gloss:** Exhibit C: salvation-history in timber — the Ark twin is `[54:13]` "a craft of planks and nails" (verified); "their offspring" (dhurriyyat) loads the vessel with posterity, i.e., with the resurrection-debate's own subject.
* **`[36:42]`** "And We created for them the like of it, in which they ride."
  * **Gloss:** The Ark generalizes into all riding-vessels: salvation-tech as ongoing mercy, familiar to the surah's seaborne-audience idiom (Source: agent inference — verify).
* **`[36:43]`** "If We will, We can drown them—with no screaming to be heard from them, nor will they be saved."
  * **Gloss:** The sea's dark memory: drowning without a cry — providence withheld is one sign away from the blast of 49; sovereignty held in reserve as pedagogy, "if We will."
* **`[36:44]`** "Except by a mercy from Us, and enjoyment for a while."
  * **Gloss:** Rescue only by mercy — "enjoyment for a while" prices the reprieve; the exception clause keeps the audience in suspense between sign and blast.
* **`[36:45]`** "Yet when it is said to them, “Beware of what lies before you, and what lies behind you, that you may receive mercy.”"
  * **Gloss:** The address no one completes acting on (a period, not a response — the sentence hangs, mirroring their suspension); "before you and behind you" recycles 36:9's double barrier: what was walled is now warned.
* **`[36:46]`** "Yet never came to them a sign of their Lord’s signs, but they turned away from it."
  * **Gloss:** The exhibit-list meets its audience-statistic; the turning-away-after-reminder pattern is verbatim-cognate `[32:22]` "when reminded of his Lord’s revelations, turns away" (verified).
* **`[36:47]`** "And when it is said to them, “Spend of what Allah has provided for you,” those who disbelieve say to those who believe, “Shall we feed someone whom Allah could feed, if He so willed? You must be deeply misguided.”"
  * **Gloss:** The infaq test-case: fatalism ("Allah could feed") as refusal of the command-twin `[2:254]` "Spend from what We have given you" (verified); the economy-theology of the infraq note in one town-meeting; note the reversal back onto the spenders: "you must be deeply misguided."
* **`[36:48]`** "And they say, “When will this promise be, if you are truthful?”"
  * **Gloss:** The time-stamp taunt; family drift verified: `[10:48]`/`[67:25]` "When will this promise be fulfilled…", `[21:38]` "When will this promise come true…" — Ya-Sin has the barest form.
* **`[36:49]`** "All they can expect is a single blast, which will seize them while they feud."
  * **Gloss:** The answer is a schedule with no advance notice: seized mid-argument — feud-vocabulary cognates `[22:19]`, `[50:28]`, `[38:64]` (verified); the Cry-family (29) pre-stages the capture.
* **`[36:50]`** "They will not be able to make a will, nor will they return to their families."
  * **Gloss:** Both exits closed — testament and homecoming; the counter-image of the running man who got the last word (25) and of 36:22's "to Whom you will be returned."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`pairs` (azwaj)**: 36:36's triptych — earth-produce, selves, unknowns.
  * **Rendering:** "who created all the pairs; of what the earth produces, and of their own selves, and of what they do not know."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[51:49]` "We created all things in pairs, so that you may reflect and ponder" (the purpose clause the task grafted onto 36:36); `[22:5]` earth sprouting "all kinds of lovely pairs" — pairs as sign on both sides of the audit.
* **`sign (ayah)`**: the ruku's organizing marker — "a sign for them" (33), "Another sign for them" (37, 41).
  * **Rendering:** three dossiers, one noun; the ayāt chain answers 36:6's "unaware" with escalating visibility.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[30:24]` "In this are signs for people who understand" (the Ar-Rum twin of the rain-and-dead-land motif, verified); 36:46 tallies their response rate to "a sign of their Lord's signs".

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical lists); the astronomy verses (37–40) are phenomenological witness-language in the audience's idiom — classical readers gloss "floats/runs" with sabaha ("swims"), and later readers litigate heliocentrism; neither gloss nor litigation is the corpus's claim. *Source: classical tafsir language (sabaha) + agent inference — verify before repeating.*
