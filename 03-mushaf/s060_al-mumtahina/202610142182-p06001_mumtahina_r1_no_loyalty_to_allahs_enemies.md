# No loyalty to Allah's enemies — and Abraham's example of disavowal (60:1–6)
**UID:** 202610142182
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 60:1–6 (Ruku 1 of 2 in al-Mumtahina)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[60:1]` – `[60:6]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens as a loyalty audit: secret affection for "My enemies and your enemies" is exposed as straying from the right way, then the criterion is made creedal — the model is Abraham and his people saying "We are quit of you... until you believe in Allah alone," with one quarantined exception (his father).
* **Placement:** Opening ruku of al-Mumtahina; the surah's thesis half. Ruku 2 (`[60:7]`–`[60:13]`) answers its hard line with hope (`60:7`) and casuistry (`60:10–12`), so this ruku must be read as one movement of a pair, not the surah's last word.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[60:1]`** "O you who believe! Do not take My enemies and your enemies for supporters, offering them affection, when they have disbelieved in what has come to you of the Truth. They have expelled the Messenger, and you, because you believed in Allah, your Lord. If you have mobilized to strive for My cause, seeking My approval, how can you secretly love them? I know what you conceal and what you reveal. Whoever among you does that has strayed from the right way."
  * **Gloss:** The charge is about covert intimacy ("secretly love") with people whose disbelief and expulsion of the believers is stated as fact; the watchword is divine omniscience, and the verdict idiom is "strayed from the right way."
* **`[60:2]`** "Whenever they encounter you, they treat you as enemies, and they stretch their hands and tongues against you with malice. They wish that you would disbelieve."
  * **Gloss:** Evidence for why the intimacy is delusional: the other side's hostility is unconditional and its wish is your apostasy.
* **`[60:3]`** "Neither your relatives nor your children will benefit you on the Day of Resurrection. He will separate between you. Allah is Observant of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The kinship channel is cut: the bonds that smuggle loyalty (relatives, children) are worthless at the separation of the Resurrection.
* **`[60:4]`** "You have had an excellent example in Abraham and those with him; when they said to their people, “We are quit of you, and what you worship apart from Allah. We denounce you. Enmity and hatred has surfaced between us and you, forever, until you believe in Allah alone.” Except for the words of Abraham to his father, “I will ask forgiveness for you, though I have no power from Allah to do anything for you.” “Our Lord, in You we trust, and to You we repent, and to You is the ultimate resort."
  * **Gloss:** The exemplar arrives: total disavowal of idolatry and its people, bounded by "until you believe in Allah alone" — loyalty tracks tawhid, not ethnicity. Abraham's father-exception is explicitly fenced ("Except for the words..."), and the quoted prayer pivots to trust-repentance-return. (Note: the corpus reproduces the book's unbalanced quotation marks here — class-2 typography, not an extraction artifact; see the fleeting note linked below.)
* **`[60:5]`** "Our Lord, do not make us a target for those who disbelieve, and forgive us, our Lord. You are indeed the Mighty and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The exemplar's prayer ends in dependence, not domination: "do not make us a target" (fitna) — disavowal without arrogance.
* **`[60:6]`** "There is an excellent example in them for you—for anyone who seeks Allah and the Last Day. But whoever turns away—Allah is the Self-Sufficient, the Most Praised."
  * **Gloss:** The model is universalized with a criterion ("anyone who seeks Allah and the Last Day") and the closing disclaimer: turning away costs Allah nothing.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`awliya'`** (supporters/friends): the loyalty register of `[60:1]`, "for supporters"; drifts to "friends"/"befriend" at `[60:9]`, `[60:13]`.
  * **Rendering:** "supporters" (60:1) — Itani keeps the same term-family for the prohibition across the surah.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:28]` "take disbelievers for friends instead of believers", `[5:51]` "do not take the Jews and the Christians as allies", `[9:23]` "do not ally yourselves with your parents and your siblings" — the dossier of loyalty-versus-tawhid, three Medinan addresses in one vocabulary.
* **`wadda` (affection):** "offering them affection" `[60:1]` — human affection smuggled to the enemy; answered by Allah planting it at `[60:7]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[58:22]` — "loving those who oppose Allah and His Messenger" is declared impossible for the believer, the exact mirror of 60:1's prohibition.
* **`uswa hasana` (excellent example):** `[60:4]`, `[60:6]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:21]` — "You have an excellent example in the Messenger of Allah; for anyone who seeks Allah and the Last Day" — the identical formula (including the seek-Clause) transferred from Abraham's band to the Prophet; observable in the corpus, a deliberate pairing.
* **`bara'a` (disavowal):** "We are quit of you... We denounce you" `[60:4]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[9:114]` — "Abraham asked forgiveness for his father only because of a promise... when it became clear to him that he was an enemy of Allah, he disowned him" — the later fulfillment of the exception fenced at 60:4 (Source: classical reports on 9:114 — verify).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[3:28]`, `[5:51]`, `[9:23]` — same prohibition, different audiences; 60:1 supplies the *reason* (they expelled you "because you believed").
* **Parallel:** `[58:22]` — the affection clause; its "even if they were their parents" matches 60:3's severing of kin-loyalty.
* **Parallel:** `[33:21]` — the uswa-formula twin (see Key Terms); Abraham-in-60 prefigures the Prophet-in-33 exemplar.
* **Within surah:** `[60:7]` — "perhaps Allah will plant affection" is the divine answer to the banned human affection of 60:1; `[60:13]` restates the opening prohibition as the closing one.
* **Formula:** `[5:12]` — "has strayed from the right way" (the same *sabil* verdict inside a covenant frame, linking 60:1's verdict to covenant breach).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical consensus; see the surah index). Treaty-era address: "offending" emigrant believers are suspected of a leaked letter to their kin in Quraysh.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** The transmitted occasion for 60:1 is Hatib ibn Abi Balta'ah's warning-letter to Quraysh ahead of the Prophet's march (Hudaybiyyah/post-Hudaybiyyah horizon): a Badr veteran "mobilized to strive" yet secretly loving what Allah's Messenger fled. 60:4–6's Abrahamic setting (Babylon) and the father-exception: *Source: classical maghazi/tafsir reports (Tabari and Ibn Kathir on 60:1, 9:114) — agent labeling; verify.*
