# Reconciliation hope, equitable limits, and the examined women (60:7–13)
**UID:** 202610142183
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 60:7–13 (Ruku 2 of 2 in al-Mumtahina)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[60:7]` – `[60:13]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The loyalty line of ruku 1 is simultaneously softened and systematized: enmity is not final (60:7), the legal criterion is hostile conduct rather than belief-claim (60:8–9), and the treaty's hardest casuistry — tested emigrant women, compensation in both directions, the women's pledge — is enacted as "the rule of Allah" (60:10–12), before the opening prohibition returns as the surah's last word (60:13).
* **Placement:** Closing ruku; it converts ruku 1's emotional prohibition into working law, and its `60:7` verse is the hinge that stops the surah from ending in permanent hatred.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[60:7]`** "Perhaps Allah will plant affection between you and those of them you consider enemies. Allah is Capable. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** "Perhaps" (ʿasā) puts hope on Allah's ledger, not the believers' negotiation: the very enmity surfaced at 60:4 is reversible by divine planting — and note the verbs: Allah plants *affection*, the human activity 60:1 forbade smuggling.
* **`[60:8]`** "As for those who have not fought against you for your religion, nor expelled you from your homes, Allah does not prohibit you from dealing with them kindly and equitably. Allah loves the equitable."
  * **Gloss:** The criterion is made negative and behavioral: no fight-over-religion, no expulsion → kindness (birr) and equity (qist) are permitted, indeed loved by Allah.
* **`[60:9]`** "But Allah prohibits you from befriending those who fought against you over your religion, and expelled you from your homes, and aided in your expulsion. Whoever takes them for friends—these are the wrongdoers."
  * **Gloss:** Mirror of 60:8, with the "aided in your expulsion" clause widening liability; the verdict-word shifts from "strayed" (60:1) to "wrongdoers."
* **`[60:10]`** "O you who believe! When believing women come to you emigrating, test them. Allah is Aware of their faith. And if you find them to be faithful, do not send them back to the unbelievers. They are not lawful for them, nor are they lawful for them. But give them what they have spent. You are not at fault if you marry them, provided you give them their compensation. And do not hold on to ties with unbelieving women, but demand what you have spent, and let them demand what they have spent. This is the rule of Allah; He rules among you. Allah is Knowing and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's name-verse: emigrant wives of unbelievers are tested (faith, not status), not returned — intermarriage across the treaty line is dissolved ("not lawful... nor lawful"), and the marital treasury is netted by mutual claims of "what they have spent." "The rule of Allah; He rules among you" sacralizes the adjudication.
* **`[60:11]`** "If any of your wives desert you to the unbelievers, and you decide to penalize them, give those whose wives have gone away the equivalent of what they had spent. And fear Allah, in whom you are believers."
  * **Gloss:** The reverse flow (believers' wives defecting to the other side) is handled by compensation, not retaliation — the balance-sheet ethic both ways.
* **`[60:12]`** "O prophet! If believing women come to you, pledging allegiance to you, on condition that they will not associate anything with Allah, nor steal, nor commit adultery, nor kill their children, nor commit perjury as to parenthood, nor disobey you in anything righteous, accept their allegiance and ask Allah's forgiveness for them. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The women's bay'ah, coded in six articles — shirk first, then property, bodies, children, speech, and authority — sealed with the Prophet's instruction to seek forgiveness for them, not to audit them further.
* **`[60:13]`** "O you who believe! Do not befriend people with whom Allah has become angry, and have despaired of the Hereafter, as the faithless have despaired of the occupants of the graves."
  * **Gloss:** The bookend: befriend-logic now runs through Allah's anger, and the double despair ("as the faithless... of the occupants of the graves") closes the surah on the un-befriendable.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`mawadda` (affection):** `[60:7]` "plant affection" — Allah as the sower.
  * **Rendering:** same noun-family as 60:1's banned "affection"; the ruku's arc is one word moving from human smuggling (60:1) to divine planting (60:7). *Source: observable translator consistency in corpus renderings.*
* **`birr` / `qist` (kindly, equitably):** `[60:8]` "dealing with them kindly and equitably... Allah loves the equitable."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:8]` — "let not the hatred of a certain people prevent you from acting justly. Adhere to justice, for that is nearer to piety" — the same equity-under-hostility ethic, verified in corpus; "Allah loves the equitable" answers 60:9's love-banned friendship.
* **`imtahanna` (test):** `[60:10]` "test them" — the surah's eponym (al-Mumtahina, "she who is tested").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:124]` Abraham "tested by his Lord" (family of b-l-w/ibtala in Itani's "tested" renderings) — a trial of veracity, not a background check; *Source: translator's verb choice, observable in corpus.*
* **`mahr` as "what they have spent":** `[60:10]`–`[60:11]` renders the dowry/compensation money as expenditure claims, while `[5:5]` uses "dowries" for the same institution.
  * **Rendering:** drift is visible across the corpus ("their compensation" also at 60:10); the treaty arithmetic (60:10–11) treats mahr as a transferable debt between camps. *Source: observable rendering comparison — verify Arabic-side terminology.*

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (classical consensus). The ruku reads as treaty jurisprudence of the Hudaybiyyah period — the clause "do not send them back" presupposes a return-agreement whose gender-symmetry the surah then re-legislates.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 60:10 is classically tied to the clause returning Meccan refugees and the case of Umm Kulthum bint Uqba, whose emigration triggered the article's renegotiation. 60:12 reports describe women pledging on the listed conditions. *Source: classical tafsir/maghazi reports (Ibn Kathir, Tabari on 60:10–12) — agent labeling; verify.*
* **Judgment flag (for orchestrator):** 60:7 was task-flagged as possibly a guidance-theme verse; it is not — no hidayah/sirat vocabulary is present. It is reconciliation hope inside the loyalty ethic, so it is not linked to the guidance note. *Source: agent inference from the corpus line.*
