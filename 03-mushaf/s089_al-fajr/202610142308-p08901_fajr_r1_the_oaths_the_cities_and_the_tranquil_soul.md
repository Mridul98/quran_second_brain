# Dawn-Oaths, the Destroyed Cities, and the Tranquil Soul (89:1–30)
**UID:** 202610142308
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 89:1–30 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Fajr)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[89:1]` – `[89:30]` (30 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Sworn on the four time-objects, the surah prosecutes prosperity-as-proof: three cities that "committed excesses" (Aad's Erum, Thamud's valley, Pharaoh's Stakes) show "your Lord is on the lookout," while man misreads stretched and straitened provision as honor and insult — until the social audit (orphan, poor, inheritance, wealth-love) and the leveling Hour make remembrance too late; and the surah closes by handing the only un-indicted voice its address: "O tranquil soul… Enter My Paradise."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1; the batch's hinge. 86's guarded soul, 87's two-life trade, and 88's two faces all resolve here into one soul with one command; 90 then gives the road this soul took, and 91 the alternative ("Failing is he who corrupts it").

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[89:1]`–`[89:4]`** "By the daybreak. / And ten nights. / And the even and the odd. / And the night as it recedes."
  * **Gloss:** Four oaths on time-figures: dawn breaks the batch's oath-pattern (sky/star in 86; here day/night). "Ten nights" and "the even and the odd" carry no corpus referent (see index §5b; classical = Dhul-Hijja days — transmitted gloss — verify); "the night as it recedes" is the identical phrase of `[81:17]` (rg-verified; cf. `[93:2]` "the night as it settles").
* **`[89:5]`** "Is there in this an oath for a rational person?"
  * **Gloss:** The oaths interrogate the reader back: evidence that needs no evidence — the corpus phrase "a rational person" occurs only here (rg-verified).
* **`[89:6]`–`[89:8]`** "Have you not seen how your Lord dealt with Aad? / Erum of the pillars. / The like of which was never created in the land."
  * **Gloss:** The archive opens with the unmatched builders; the seen-"how your Lord dealt" formula is the surah's historian's eye; the wind-end of Aad is detailed at `[69:6–7]` and `[46:24]` ("a wind in which is grievous suffering") (rg-verified).
* **`[89:9]`** "And Thamood—those who carved the rocks in the valley."
  * **Gloss:** The mountain-carvers, twins of `[7:74]` "carve out dwellings in the mountains," `[26:149]` "you skillfully carve houses in the mountains?", `[15:82]` "carved homes in the mountains, feeling secure" (rg-verified) — security-craft is the crime-scene irony.
* **`[89:10]`–`[89:12]`** "And Pharaoh of the Stakes. / Those who committed excesses in the lands. / And spread much corruption therein."
  * **Gloss:** "Pharaoh of the Stakes" — the exact title-twin of `[38:12]` (rg-verified); the three are then generalized: excess + corruption (fasād-family) is the common charge.
* **`[89:13]`** "So your Lord poured down upon them a scourge of punishment."
  * **Gloss:** Pouring + scourge (sāṭ): the scourge-word otherwise falls from heaven at `[29:34]` (rg-verified).
* **`[89:14]`** "Your Lord is on the lookout."
  * **Gloss:** The watch-keystone of the whole passage; the rendering-echo pairs it with Saleh's Thamud-line warning `[11:93]` "So look out; I am on the lookout with you" (rg-verified; Arabic marṣād/raqīb differ — agent inference — verify).
* **`[89:15]`–`[89:16]`** "As for man, whenever his Lord tests him, and honors him, and prospers him, he says, 'My Lord has honored me.' / But whenever He tests him, and restricts his livelihood for him, he says, 'My Lord has insulted me.'"
  * **Gloss:** The provision-test doublet: the same verb "tests" (b-l-w) both directions; expands/restricts livelihood is the fixed formula of `[13:26]`, `[17:30]`, `[29:62]`, `[30:37]`, `[34:36]` (rg-verified); 13:26's next clause — delight in the worldly life — names the disease 87:16 already diagnosed.
* **`[89:17]`–`[89:20]`** "Not at all. But you do not honor the orphan. / And you do not urge the feeding of the poor. / And you devour inheritance with all greed. / And you love wealth with immense love."
  * **Gloss:** "Not at all" (kallā — corpus twice here) cancels the self-reading; the audit is social, not creedal: orphan-honoring (cf. `[2:83]`, `[93:9]`), feeding-the-poor urging (cf. `[76:8]`), inheritance-devouring, wealth-love (cf. `[102:1]` "Abundance distracts you") (rg-verified pairs) — the plural "you" indicts the community that 90:13–16 will answer.
* **`[89:21]`–`[89:22]`** "No—when the earth is leveled, pounded, and crushed. / And your Lord comes, with the angels, row after row."
  * **Gloss:** The second "No—" opens the Hour: the leveling-verb joins `[84:3]` "the earth is leveled out," `[4:42]`, and sibling `[91:14]` "leveled it" (rg-verified); "row after row" is corpus-single here, answered by `[78:38]` "the Spirit and the angels stand in row" (rg-verified); the coming is quoted verbatim — interpretation transmitted-disputed (index §5b).
* **`[89:23]`–`[89:26]`** "And on that Day, Hell is brought forward. On that Day, man will remember, but how will remembrance avail him? / He will say, 'If only I had forwarded for my life.' / On that Day, none will punish as He punishes. / And none will shackle as He shackles."
  * **Gloss:** The too-late dhikr — remembrance without prior condition (contrast 87:9–10, 88:21); "forwarded for my life" is the forwarding-clause twin of `[59:18]` "let every soul consider what it has forwarded for the morrow" and `[2:110]` (rg-verified); the double rhetorical superlative (none punishes/shackles as He does) is unique in its form (rg-verified — the "none will" construction itself recurs at `[41:35]` and `[6:70]` with different content).
* **`[89:27]`–`[89:30]`** "But as for you, O tranquil soul. / Return to your Lord, pleased and accepted. / Enter among My servants. / Enter My Paradise."
  * **Gloss:** The batch's only loving address: the nafs al-mutma'inna — "tranquil" is corpus-single (rg-verified); the sakīna family ("tranquility… into the hearts of the believers" `[48:4]`; the Ark's "tranquility" `[2:248]`) supplies the nearness-quiet; "Return to your Lord" is the road 84:6 already swore ("you are laboring towards your Lord, and you will meet Him" — rg-verified); "pleased and accepted" (rāḍiya mardiyya) — the double favor, agent of pleasing and object of acceptance. The classically transmitted reading — the believer's soul at death — is *Source: transmitted reports (e.g., apparatus on 89:27–30) — verify*.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`al-Fajr` (daybreak)**: the oath-name.
  * **Rendering:** "By the daybreak" (89:1); header "THE DAWN".
  * **Elsewhere:** "the white streak of dawn" `[2:187]`; "the Quran at dawn… is witnessed" `[17:78]`; "Peace it is; until the rise of dawn" `[97:5]`; "breaks the dawn" `[6:96]` (rg-verified) — dawn as the corpus's standing sign of returning order.
* **`on the lookout` (bil-marṣād)**: divine ambush-post.
  * **Rendering:** "Your Lord is on the lookout" (89:14).
  * **Elsewhere:** the rendering-pair `[11:93]` (rg-verified); concept-kin `[86:9]` (secrets disclosed), `[90:7]` "Does he think that no one sees him?" (sibling), `[50:16]` (nearer than the jugular).
* **`tranquil soul` (nafs muṭma'inna)**: settled, assured self.
  * **Rendering:** "O tranquil soul" (89:27) — corpus-unique (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** the tranquility-word sakīna: `[2:248]`, `[9:40]`, `[30:21]`, `[48:4]` (rg-verified) — the soul 89:27 addresses is the soul in whom that had descended.
* **`kallā` (No—/Not at all)**: the veto-word.
  * **Rendering:** "Not at all" (89:17); "No—" (89:21) — two refusals bracketing the indictment.
  * **Elsewhere:** this edition's "Not at all" form is a 83–89 cluster only — `[83:7]`, `[83:14]`, `[83:15]`, `[89:17]`; "No—" stands alone at 89:21; "Nay!" at `[74:32]` (all rg-verified) — the veto-word's Meccan denial register.
