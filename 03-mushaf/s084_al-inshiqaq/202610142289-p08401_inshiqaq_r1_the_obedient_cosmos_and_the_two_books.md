# The Obedient Cosmos and the Book Handed Right or Behind-Back (84:1–25)
**UID:** 202610142289
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 84:1–25 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Inshiqaq)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[84:1]` – `[84:25]` (25 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The only disobedient thing in this surah is man: the ruptured sky and leveled earth each twice "hearken to their Lord, as they must," while the laboring human — who "thought he would never return" — will meet Him and take his book in the right hand or behind his back; the twilight oath seals it: "You will mount stage by stage," and the ending exception clause carves out the believers' "undiminished reward."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1 — the batch's third reckoning-surah. 82 diagnosed delusion, 83 named the archives, 84 hands the files over and stages the scene of receipt; its "O man!" is the corpus's only twin of 82:6 (rg-verified).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[84:1]`–`[84:2]`** "When the sky is ruptured. / And hearkens to its Lord, as it must."
  * **Gloss:** The rupture word here (inshiqaq — "is ruptured") differs from 82:1's "breaks apart" (infitar); and uniquely to this surah, the torn sky is not a victim but an obeying agent ("hearkens"), compelled by its constitution.
* **`[84:3]`–`[84:5]`** "And when the earth is leveled out. / And casts out what is in it, and becomes empty. / And hearkens to its Lord, as it must."
  * **Gloss:** The earth's three beats — flatten, disgorge, empty — with the identical obedience refrain repeated verbatim (84:2/84:5, the pair is corpus-unique — rg-verified); disgorging twin: `[99:2]` "the earth brings out its loads", "it will tell its tales" because "your Lord will have inspired it" (99:4–5).
* **`[84:6]`** "O man! You are laboring towards your Lord, and you will meet Him."
  * **Gloss:** The second of the corpus's exactly two "O man!" addresses (with 82:6 — rg-verified). Life is redefined as toil with a destination: the meeting is as certain as the fatigue.
* **`[84:7]`–`[84:9]`** "As for him who is given his book in his right hand. / He will have an easy settlement. / And will return to his family delighted."
  * **Gloss:** Right-hand receiving → account "easy" — and the scene ends domestically: delighted return to "his family," the mirror of the wicked man's worldly family-joy in 84:13.
* **`[84:10]`–`[84:12]`** "But as for him who is given his book behind his back. / He will call for death. / And will enter the Blaze."
  * **Gloss:** The corpus's ONLY "behind his back" handing (rg-verified): not even the left hand (that is `[69:25]`'s version) but a shameful behind-the-back receipt; the verdict-speech is two lines — a death-wish and the Blaze.
* **`[84:13]`–`[84:15]`** "He used to be happy among his family. / He thought he would never return. / In fact, his Lord was watching him."
  * **Gloss:** The retro-diagnosis: present euphoria, future-denial ("never return"), answered in five words — "his Lord was watching him"; the English "watching" chains to 82:10's "watchers" (84:15's Arabic is *basira*-type awareness, not *raqib* — *Source: agent inference on standard lexemes — verify*).
* **`[84:16]`–`[84:18]`** "I swear by the twilight. / And by the night, and what it covers. / And by the moon, as it grows full."
  * **Gloss:** A three-term oath on the day's dying: dusk, covering night, full moon — light measured out in its loss; "the night and what it covers" echoes the covering motif of 83:15's "screened."
* **`[84:19]`** "You will mount stage by stage."
  * **Gloss:** The oath's resolution — and note the task hint's "among the great tidings/gnarled warnings" corresponds to NO line in s084 (rg-verified; the surah's only "inform them" clause is the warning at 84:24): life is ascent through fixed stages — whether you like the direction or not.
* **`[84:20]`–`[84:21]`** "What is the matter with them that they do not believe? / And when the Quran is read to them, they do not bow down?"
  * **Gloss:** The inquest: unbelief is absurd because staged mounting is underway; the second charge is posture — bowing when the Quran is read; the model believers do exactly that: `[19:58]` "they would fall down, prostrating and weeping", `[17:107]` "they fall to their chins, prostrating" (rg-verified).
* **`[84:22]`–`[84:23]`** "In fact, those who disbelieve are in denial. / But Allah knows what they hide inside."
  * **Gloss:** Denial named as active kufr; omniscience re-entered as the answer to concealment — the watchers' function (82:12) asserted of Allah directly.
* **`[84:24]`–`[84:25]`** "So inform them of a painful punishment. / Except those who believe and do good deeds; they will have an undiminished reward."
  * **Gloss:** The tiding-line (the surah's only "inform them") and the exception clause: "Except those who believe and do…" — verbatim-frame twin of `[95:6]`, where Itani renders the reward idiom "without end"; here "undiminished" (both rg-verified; see index §5b lexeme note).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`inshiqaq` (rupture)**: splitting asunder.
  * **Rendering:** "When the sky is ruptured" (84:1) — vs 82:1 "breaks apart" (infitar); two rupture-nouns for one event across adjacent surahs.
  * **Elsewhere:** the Day-teardown oath-chain family: `[82:1–4]`, `[99:1–2]`, `[81:1–6]` (rolling seas, star-scattering) — pattern: cosmos unmade, then humans audited.
* **`hearkens / as it must`**: cosmic obedience (tawhid written into physics).
  * **Rendering:** identical twice (84:2, 84:5) — the surah's spine-refrain, corpus-unique as a pair (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[99:5]` "For your Lord will have inspired it" — the earth obeys by wahy; contrast man, addressed but not compelled (84:6–15).
* **`watching (84:15)`**: "In fact, his Lord was watching him."
  * **Rendering:** "was watching" — past-progressive surveillance answering the "thought he would never return."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[82:10]` "watchers", `[82:12]` "They know everything you do", `[50:18]` "a watcher by him, ready" — the English chain links the batch's surveillance dossier (Arabic lexemes differ — *Source: agent inference — verify*).
* **`undiminished reward (84:25)`**: the exception clause's payload.
  * **Rendering:** "an undiminished reward"; 95:6 renders the same idiom-class "a reward without end"; 11:108 uses "without end" for a different Arabic word (madzdud — *agent inference — verify*).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[95:6]` is the structural twin down to the "Except those who believe and do…" opening.
