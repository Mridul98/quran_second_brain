# Five anonymous snatchers and the seizure of Pharaoh (79:1–26)
**UID:** 202610142266
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 79:1–26 (Ruku 1 of 2 in an-Nazi’at)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[79:1]`–`[79:26]` (26 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Five unnamed agent-classes swear the Resumption into plausibility: the Day is one Quake followed by one Successor, a "single nudge" that leaves people awake (79:1–14) — and the surah's exhibit is Pharaoh, who was called, offered cleansing, shown the Greatest Miracle, and answered by claiming to be "your Lord, the most high," for which he was seized (79:15–26), "a lesson for whoever fears."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 2. Its oath-form is shared with the immediately preceding surah (77:1–5) and with 51:1–4, making a three-surah chain of "By those who…" oaths; its Moses file is the shortest of the corpus's tellings and pointedly omits the magician contest that Ta-Ha and ash-Shu'ara narrate (verified: no magicians, no prostration, no sea in 79:15–26).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[79:1]`** "By those who snatch violently." **`[79:2]`** "And those who remove gently." **`[79:3]`** "And those who glide smoothly."
  * **Gloss:** oath-classes 1–3: force, then gentleness, then unhindered motion — the sequence is a set of velocities, and the text names nothing else about the swearers.
* **`[79:4]`** "And those who race swiftly." **`[79:5]`** "And those who regulate events."
  * **Gloss:** classes 4–5 close the chain on speed and on governance ("regulate events"), so the oath ends where the surah's argument ends: arrangement implies an Arranger.
* **`[79:6]`** "On the Day when the Quake quakes." **`[79:7]`** "And is followed by the Successor."
  * **Gloss:** the oath's subject: the first convulsion and whatever takes its turn after it — two events, no interval described.
* **`[79:8]`** "Hearts on that Day will be pounding." **`[79:9]`** "Their sights downcast."
  * **Gloss:** the Day reported physiologically — inside the chest and at the eyes; no armies or scales yet (both renderings are corpus hapaxes, verified).
* **`[79:10]`** "They say, “Are we to be restored to the original condition?" **`[79:11]`** "When we have become hollow bones?”"
  * **Gloss:** the deniers' own words, in two lines; note the book's unbalanced quotation (opened at 79:10, closed only at 79:11) — quoted here as printed.
* **`[79:12]`** "They say, “This is a losing proposition.”"
  * **Gloss:** the third utterance: not a refusal but a verdict on the cost — resurrection cast as a bad deal.
* **`[79:13]`** "But it will be only a single nudge." **`[79:14]`** "And they will be awake."
  * **Gloss:** the rebuttal is compression: one shove, and consciousness returns. The verb's plainness (nudge/awake) is the argument — no process is described.
* **`[79:15]`** "Has the story of Moses reached you?"
  * **Gloss:** the exhibit opens with the formula the corpus shares verbatim with 20:9 — the same "has the story reached you?" hook.
* **`[79:16]`** "When His Lord called out to him in the sacred valley of Tuwa."
  * **Gloss:** location and mode: a valley named, a call direct — 20:12 gives the same "sacred valley of Tuwa" (verified).
* **`[79:17]`** "“Go to Pharaoh—he has transgressed.”"
  * **Gloss:** the mission's single diagnostic word ("transgressed", corpus-hapax in this form, verified).
* **`[79:18]`** "And say, “Do you care to be cleansed?" **`[79:19]`** "And I will guide you to your Lord, and you will turn reverent.”"
  * **Gloss:** the offer is stated as an invitation, not a demand: cleansing, then guidance, then reverence — the whole salvific sequence in two lines.
* **`[79:20]`** "He showed him the Greatest Miracle."
  * **Gloss:** one sign, singular and superlative (corpus-hapax, verified) — the teller does not list staff, hand, or flood.
* **`[79:21]`** "But he denied and defied." **`[79:22]`** "Then turned his back, and tried." **`[79:23]`** "And gathered and proclaimed."
  * **Gloss:** six verbs of refusal in three lines — denial, defiance, retreat, mobilization, assembly, announcement: the anatomy of a propaganda response to a sign.
* **`[79:24]`** "He said, “I am your Lord, the most high.”"
  * **Gloss:** the claim the surah exists to answer; the same self-elevation is put in Pharaoh's mouth at 28:38 ("I know of no god for you other than me", verified).
* **`[79:25]`** "So Allah seized him with an exemplary punishment, in the last and in the first."
  * **Gloss:** the verdict in one verb ("seized"), with a crux: "the last and the first" is left undefined by the text (see §6).
* **`[79:26]`** "In this is a lesson for whoever fears."
  * **Gloss:** the lesson-clause closes the file — and re-runs the oath's logic: the "whoever fears" of 79:26 is the "hearts pounding" of 79:8, made productive.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"those who snatch violently" (the oath-classes, an-Nazi’at)** — 79:1–5
  * **Rendering:** five participles — "snatch violently", "remove gently", "glide smoothly", "race swiftly", "regulate events"; the surah title is taken from class 1.
  * **Elsewhere:** the formal twin is the previous surah: `[77:1–5]` "By those unleashed in succession. Storming turbulently. Scattering far and wide. Separating decisively. Delivering a message." (verified); also `[51:1–4]` "By the spreaders spreading. And those carrying loads. And those moving gently. And those distributing as commanded." (verified) — three oath-runs of unnamed acting classes, none of which the text identifies.
* **"the Quake" / "the Successor"** — 79:6–7
  * **Rendering:** "the Quake quakes" — the event and its verb doubled in the English — and "the Successor"; both are hapaxes in this corpus (rg-verified single hits).
  * **Elsewhere:** the Day's other sound-names in the block: `[78:18]` "the Trumpet is blown", `[80:33]` "the Deafening Noise", `[79:34]` "the Great Cataclysm" — the surahs keep the event and change the noun each time.
* **"hollow bones"** — 79:11
  * **Rendering:** "When we have become hollow bones?" — the deniers' material objection.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[36:78]` "Who will revive the bones when they have decayed?" (verified); `[37:16]`/`[37:53]` "died and become dust and bones" (verified); `[75:3]` "Does man think that We will not reassemble his bones?" (verified) — the same objection family, with 79 as its most dismissive phrasing.
* **"a single nudge"** — 79:13
  * **Rendering:** "But it will be only a single nudge." / "And they will be awake." (79:14 also hapax).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:19]` "It will be a single nudge, and they will be staring." (verified — same rendering, same image); `[54:50]` "And Our command is but once, like the twinkling of an eye." (verified) — the corpus's two compression-idioms for resurrection's effortlessness.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (surah-level consensus). The addressee of 79:15 ("Has the story of Moses reached you?") is unnamed in the text; the report-form is the text's own, and nothing here dates the telling.
* **Identity of the five oath-classes:** the text gives only five verbs. Classical readers divide: angels who take souls at death (the "pulling" grammar), winds, stars, or created forces of governance. **Source: classical disagreement (the soul-taking reading is widespread in the tafsir tradition; verify against a printed source — Ibn Kathir / al-Tabari on 79:1–5)** — none of these names is in the corpus line.
* **"in the last and in the first" (79:25):** the text attaches the seizure to two unnamed referents. Classical readings take it as this world and the Next, or the earlier and later punishment, or the first warning and the final drowning. **Source: classical tafsir disagreement — verify.** Recorded as a crux.
* **Text-only note:** 79:14 "And they will be awake." is quoted exactly; the text does not say of what they are awake.
