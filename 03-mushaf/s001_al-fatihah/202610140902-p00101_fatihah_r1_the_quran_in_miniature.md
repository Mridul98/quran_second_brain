# The Quran in miniature: praise, surrender, and the petition for the path (1:1–7)
**UID:** 202610140902
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 1:1–7 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Fatihah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[1:1]` – `[1:7]` (7 ayahs — the basmala is ayah 1 in this edition's numbering; see Fleeting link below)
* **Thesis:** Seven lines stage the entire worshipper–God encounter: God is praised by His mercy-names and lordship, confessed as Master of the Judgment Day, worshipped and asked for help exclusively — and the sole petition is guidance to a road that already has travelers on it, the question the rest of the book answers.
* **Placement:** Global ruku 1; the Mushaf's opening unit and the recited pivot of every unit of the prayer (Source: consensus of classical fiqh/tafsir). Its praise→surrender→petition arc sets the frame; al-Baqarah 1–7 is the first answer (`[2:5]` "These are upon guidance from their Lord").

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[1:1]`** "In the name of Allah, the Gracious, the Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** Everything begins under God's name; the two mercy-names front the book. Numbered here as ayah 1 — a Hanafi/Kufi-consistent typographic decision in this edition (Source: observable corpus fact + the madhhab notes in the linked Fleeting note, still to verify against primary tafsir).
* **`[1:2]`** "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds."
  * **Gloss:** All praise/thanks belongs to Allah alone; *rabb al-'alamin* states nurture-and-ownership of every realm. The same doxology closes Paradise's liturgy (`[10:10]`) and the eschatological verdict (`[39:75]`) — the book opens its praise where it ends it.
* **`[1:3]`** "The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The mercy-names restated immediately after lordship: sovereignty is qualified by grace *before* judgment is named.
* **`[1:4]`** "Master of the Day of Judgment."
  * **Gloss:** The one Day the reader will face alone; the pivot from praise to accountability — and from third-person speech about God to direct address.
* **`[1:5]`** "It is You we worship, and upon You we call for help."
  * **Gloss:** The worshipper's answer: exclusive devotion and exclusive reliance — the practical heart of tawhid, and the hinge verse at the surah's exact center.
* **`[1:6]`** "Guide us to the straight path."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's sole petition: *hidayah* to a *sirat* — a road, not an abstraction. "upon You we call for help" occurs in no other corpus line; here it asks for the one help that matters.
* **`[1:7]`** "The path of those You have blessed, not of those against whom there is anger, nor of those who are misguided."
  * **Gloss:** The path is defined socially and historically — it has people on it: the blessed, plus two counterexamples (anger, straying). Guidance is a road already walked; the groups are left unnamed in the text.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ar-Rahman / ar-Rahim`** (the Gracious, the Merciful): rendered bare in `[1:1]`, elative ("Most…") in `[1:3]`.
  * **Rendering:** the unnumbered header "Basmala: In the name of Allah, the Gracious, the Merciful" precedes 112 of 114 surah files in the corpus; only s001 (whose basmala is the numbered `[1:1]`) and s009 (the transmitted exception) lack the header line (Source: observable corpus fact — verified via `rg -l Basmala`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[27:30]` quotes it inside Solomon's letter — the one numbered mid-corpus occurrence ("In the Name of Allah…" with capital "Name"). Classical distinction general-mercy/particular-mercy: Source: consensus of classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir on 1:3).
* **`sirat`** (path; "straight path" with *mustaqim*): the petition's object in `[1:6]`.
  * **Rendering:** "the straight path"; the phrase occurs in 32 corpus ayahs including this one — the most frequent completion of the verb "guides," so 1:6 asks in the book's own idiom (Source: observable corpus fact).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:153]` "This is My path, straight, so follow it. And do not follow the other paths…" — the programmatic answer; `[3:101]` cleaving to Allah = being "guided to a straight path"; `[2:142]`/`[2:213]` "Allah guides whom He wills to a straight path" — the road is God's to give.
* **`'ibadah / isti'ana`** (worship / call for help): the paired exclusivity of `[1:5]`.
  * **Rendering:** fronted "It is You we…" marks the constraint — none but He.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:21]` "O people! Worship your Lord who created you…" — the same verb opens al-Baqarah's call; `[19:36]` "Allah is my Lord and your Lord, so worship Him. That is a straight path." — worship and path fused exactly as in 1:5–6.
* **`yawm al-din`** (rendered "the Day of Judgment"): reckoning, recompense, and the debt of worship bundled in *din*.
  * **Rendering:** *malik* here is "Master"; the same root is rendered "The King of mankind" in `[114:2]` and "Owner of Sovereignty" in `[3:26]` — translator's register choice (Source: observable corpus fact).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:4]` "certain of the Hereafter"; `[51:12]` "When is the Day of Judgment?"; `[74:46]` the sinner's admission "we used to deny the Day of Judgment" — accountability vocabulary the Fatihah plants first.
* **`al-maghdub / al-dallin`** (those against whom there is anger / those who are misguided): the two counterexamples of `[1:7]`, unnamed in the verse itself.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:81]` "lest My wrath descends upon you. He upon whom My wrath descends has fallen"; `[2:90]` "they incurred wrath upon wrath"; `[5:60]` those "with whom He became angry" who are "further away from the right way" — the same anger/straying pairing recurs around covenant breach (Source: classical tafsir identifies the 1:7 groups with covenant-breakers — consensus attribution; the verse names no one; agent linkage — verify).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Answer-pair:** `[2:1–7]` — al-Baqarah describes the guided (`[2:2]` "a guide for the righteous," `[2:5]` "upon guidance from their Lord") and the sealed (`[2:6–7]`), answering Fatihah's two-path sorting verse-by-verse (Source: structural observation on Itani's renderings; consistent with classical unity-of-Quran analysis).
* **Parallel:** `[6:153]` — "My path, straight" vs "the other paths" defines what `[1:6]` asks for.
* **Parallel:** `[16:9]` — "It is for Allah to point out the paths, but some of them are flawed. Had He willed, He could have guided you all." — sovereignty plus real divergence, the tension the petition lives inside.
* **Within-corpus:** `[27:30]` — Solomon's letter opens with the basmala, quoting 1:1's phrase inside a numbered verse.
* **Echo:** `[10:10]`, `[39:75]` — "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds" bookends the cosmos with 1:2's doxology.
