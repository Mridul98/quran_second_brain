## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Meccan — consensus. **Revelation order 5** per the classical ordering count (al-Suyuti, *al-Itqan*, list of first-surahs-revealed; same number in Ibn Kathir's surah-introduction) — labeled here as transmitted ordering, not Quranic claim; some transmitted opinions instead call it among the very first complete units revealed for the prayer (Source: classical consensus / named classical references).
* **Size:** 7 ayahs, 1 ruku (global ruku 1 of 556 — `ruku_map.json`). In this edition the basmala is *numbered* as `[1:1]` (Kufi 6236 scheme) — Source: observable corpus fact; see [basmala numbering](../../01-fleeting/202610132006-f-basmala_numbering_in_this_edition.md). Seven verses, yet the whole book's vocabulary is present: names of God, worship, petition, guidance, election, wrath, straying.
* **Central theme:** The worshipper is taught what to say: God is praised by His mercy-names and lordship, acknowledged as Master of the Judgment Day, worshipped and relied upon exclusively — then the single petition: *guide us to the path*. The rest of the Quran reads as the answer to that request, beginning immediately in al-Baqarah (`[2:5]` "These are upon guidance from their Lord").
* **Naming:** *al-Fatihah* and the header title THE OPENING are this edition's naming (observable corpus header); *Umm al-Kitab* and *sab' al-mathani* (the seven oft-repeated) come from transmitted reports (Source: reports collected by Ibn Kathir on 1:1–7 — not Quranic text).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Opening mercy (1–3):** God names Himself before He is addressed; grace-frame first — the basmala, then lordship restated with the same two mercy-names.
* **The accountability pivot (4):** "Master of the Day of Judgment" — the only verse facing the Day; praise turns toward reckoning.
* **The worshipper's answer (5):** second person begins; exclusive worship and exclusive help-seeking — the surah's hinge and the practical statement of tawhid.
* **The one petition (6–7):** guidance to a *road*, defined socially — "The path of those You have blessed," with two counterexamples (anger, straying). The book's plot opens as an answer waiting to be given.
* **Rhetorical turn (observable in text):** vv. 1–4 speak *about* God in third person; from v. 5 the speaker addresses God in second person — the move from praise to prayer happens inside the recitation (Source: observable corpus fact; classical rhetoric names this *iltifat* — Source: classical tafsir convention).
* **Symmetry (observable):** 4 praise verses → 1 pledge verse → 2 petition verses — the surah deliberately ends open, on a road left undescribed; the description begins in the next surah (Source: agent inference from verse count — verify against a structural tafsir).
