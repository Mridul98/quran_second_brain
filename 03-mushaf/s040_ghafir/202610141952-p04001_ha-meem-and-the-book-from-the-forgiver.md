# Ha, Meem: the Book descends from the Forgiver, and the Throne-bearers pray (40:1–9)
**UID:** 202610141952
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 40:1–9 (Ruku 1 of 9 in Ghafir)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[40:1]` – `[40:9]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The letters Ha, Meem hand the Book over to its sender — a Forgiver whose mercy-names outrank His severity — and after history's precedent of rejected messengers, the ruku ends with the angels carrying the Throne praying *for* the repentant believers: the surah's whole economy of forgiveness is announced before its drama begins.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 9; the creedal frame (names → shahadah → precedent → angelic intercession) under which the Pharaoh narrative (23–50) and the creation arguments (51–85) will run. Its "to Him is the ultimate return" (3) is answered inside the surah at `[40:43]`.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[40:1]`** "Ha, Meem."
  * **Gloss:** Muqatta'at letters, transliterated and untranslated, numbered as ayah 1 — head of the Ha-Meem family (surahs 40–46, all seven openings rg-verified). The edition's refusal to gloss them is itself a stance (see Fleeting link).
* **`[40:2]`** "The sending down of the Scripture is from Allah the Almighty, the Omniscient."
  * **Gloss:** The Book-claim the letters front: revelation has a sender and a route ("sending down"); two names — might and knowledge — qualify it. Ledger: the task's "your Lord is the Mighty, the Wise" is not this verse's wording; the Mighty-Wise pairing here is "Almighty, the Omniscient," and "the Almighty, the Most Wise" arrives on the angels' lips at 40:8.
* **`[40:3]`** "Forgiver of sins, Accepter of repentance, Severe in punishment, Bountiful in bounty. There is no god but He. To Him is the ultimate return."
  * **Gloss:** The name-string verified verbatim: four attributes in ordered sequence — forgive, accept repentance, severe, bountiful — capped by the shahadah and the return. Mercy-names come first; severity is third; the surah's title (Ghafir) is anchored here.
* **`[40:4]`** "None argues against Allah’s revelations except those who disbelieve. So do not be impressed by their activities in the land."
  * **Gloss:** The first of the surah's three argue-without-authority portraits (cf. `[40:35]`, `[40:56]`); argument against signs is diagnosed as unbelief, not scholarship — and their success on earth is no evidence.
* **`[40:5]`** "Before them the people of Noah rejected the truth, as did the confederates after them. Every community plotted against their messenger, to capture him. And they argued with falsehood, to defeat with it the truth. But I seized them. What a punishment it was!"
  * **Gloss:** History becomes precedent: Noah, the confederates (the term recurs at `[40:30]` and in `[38:13]` where "Aad, Thamood, and the people of Lot… were the confederates"), the universal pattern of plot + false argument + seizure. The ledger correction lives here: *this* is the surah's Noah verse — a prayer of Noah is nowhere in s040 (the distressed cry "I am overwhelmed, so help me" is `[54:10]`).
* **`[40:6]`** "Thus the sentence of your Lord became realized against those who disbelieve, that they are to be inmates of the Fire."
  * **Gloss:** The divine sentence "became realized" — the promise-fulfilment grammar that stands in for covenant language in this surah (no mithaq/pledge word-form occurs in s040, rg-verified).
* **`[40:7]`** "Those who carry the Throne, and those around it, glorify their Lord with praise, and believe in Him, and ask for forgiveness for those who believe: “Our Lord, You have encompassed everything in mercy and knowledge; so forgive those who repent and follow Your path, and protect them from the agony of the Blaze."
  * **Gloss:** The unseen assembly: Throne-bearers who glorify, believe, and *intercede by prayer* for believers — mercy-and-knowledge encompassing all (the encompassing mercy is likewise declared in the divine answer that follows Moses's plea, "My mercy encompasses all things," `[7:156]`); the condition set is compact: repent + follow the path. The quotation opens here and runs to the close of 40:9 (book typography, quote as-is).
* **`[40:8]`** "And admit them, Our Lord, into the Gardens of Eternity, which You have promised them, and the righteous among their parents, and their spouses, and their offspring. You are indeed the Almighty, the Most Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The petition widens to families — the promise ("which You have promised them") binds to the 40:3 name-string; "Gardens of Eternity" is one of only two corpus sites of the phrase (the other `[98:8]`).
* **`[40:9]`** "And shield them from the evil deeds. Whomever You shield from the evil deeds, on that Day, You have had mercy on him. That is the supreme achievement.”"
  * **Gloss:** The final petition turns protective, not merely curative; mercy is redefined as shielding from deeds themselves, and the ruku closes on "the supreme achievement" — the same verdict-phrase used of the believers' admission at `[64:9]` and of Allah's approval at `[9:72]`; the angels' prayer brackets the surah's opening Book-claim.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Ha, Meem`**: transliterated letter-names, non-semantic.
  * **Rendering:** "Ha, Meem." exactly, as ayah 1; the family signature of surahs 40–46 (rg-verified at 40:1, 41:1, 42:1, 43:1, 44:1, 45:1, 46:1).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[46:2]` — the same letters fronted with the identical Book-claim structure ("The sending down of the Scripture is from Allah, the Honorable, the Wise"), the strongest evidence the letters work as a family header.
* **`Ghaffār` (Forgiver of sins)**: from g-f-r, to cover/pardon.
  * **Rendering:** "Forgiver of sins" — surah-title anchor, corpus-unique phrase in this exact form.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[38:66]` "the Mighty, the Forgiver" and `[40:42]` "the Mighty Forgiver" (corpus-unique in that order) keep the name alive across the surah's frame and its drama; `[39:5]` "the Almighty, the Forgiver" pairs it with the might-name exactly as 40:2–3 does.
* **`those who carry the Throne`**: the angelic porters of the divine throne.
  * **Rendering:** "Those who carry the Throne, and those around it" (40:7); "Owner of the Throne" at `[40:15]` (corpus-unique).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[69:17]` — "eight will be carrying the Throne of your Lord above them that Day," the only other corpus use of the carrying image.
* **`the ultimate return`**: masir/return to Allah.
  * **Rendering:** "To Him is the ultimate return."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[40:43]` — the believer's speech echoes it verbatim-in-sense, "our turning back is to Allah"; the phrase makes ruku 1 and ruku 5 rhyme across the Pharaoh narrative.
