# "Follow me, and I will guide you": the second speech, the rescue, and the Fire at morning and evening (40:38–50)
**UID:** 202610141956
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 40:38–50 (Ruku 5 of 9 in Ghafir)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[40:38]` – `[40:50]` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The concealed believer's second speech completes what the first only warned: follow me to the path of rectitude, because this life is fleeting enjoyment and the Hereafter the Home of Permanence — until he commits his case to Allah and the text commits theirs: Allah protects him, besieges Pharaoh's clan, and the Fire's morning-and-evening exposure gives way to the quarrel of leaders and followers who can no longer even trade blame away.
* **Placement:** Ruku 5 of 9; the resolution of the narrative arc begun at 40:23 — the only rescue-by-name in the surah (40:45) landing between the tower's defeat (40:37) and the eschatological jury of ruku 6–8.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[40:38]`** "The one who had believed said, “O my people, follow me, and I will guide you to the path of rectitude.”"
  * **Gloss:** For the first time he asks for followship — the hidden man converting concealment into open invitation; "the path of rectitude" is the Quran's sobriety-name for the road (`[7:146]`: the proud "will not adopt it for a path").
* **`[40:39]`** "“O my people, the life of this world is nothing but fleeting enjoyment, but the Hereafter is the Home of Permanence."
  * **Gloss:** The two-homes contrast; "fleeting enjoyment" is the corpus's only occurrence (rg-verified) and "Home of Permanence" recurs exactly once more, in Fatir's saved-speakers' confession, `[35:35]` "He Who settled us in the Home of Permanence." The quotation opens here and runs unbroken to 40:44 (book typography, class 2).
* **`[40:40]`** "Whoever commits a sin will be repaid only with its like. But whoever works righteousness, whether male or female, and is a believer—these will enter Paradise, where they will be provided for without account."
  * **Gloss:** The exchange rule (repayal strictly symmetrical; grace asymmetrical): "without account" — the phrase's only other corpus site is the generous gift at `[38:39]`; sex plus faith dissolves the reward's gender clause ("whether male or female, and is a believer").
* **`[40:41]`** "O my people, how is it that I call you to salvation, and you call me to the Fire?"
  * **Gloss:** The rhetorical inversion of the whole ruku: two callers, opposite cargoes — du'a as the surah's real battlefield (its verdict lands at 40:50).
* **`[40:42]`** "You call me to reject Allah, and to associate with Him what I have no knowledge of, while I call you to the Mighty Forgiver."
  * **Gloss:** The content of both calls named: association "without knowledge" (the surah's standing epistemic crime, cf. 40:56's argument "without any authority") versus the surah's title-name, "the Mighty Forgiver" — corpus-unique in this order, and the ruku-1 name-string (40:3) recalled at the climax.
* **`[40:43]`** "Without a doubt, what you call me to has no say in this world, or in the Hereafter; and our turning back is to Allah; and the transgressors are the inmates of the Fire."
  * **Gloss:** The invoked have no "say" (no authority/portion) in either world; "our turning back is to Allah" quotes 40:3's "To Him is the ultimate return" verbatim-in-sense; the inmates clause pre-bills 40:43→46's sequence.
* **`[40:44]`** "You will remember what I am telling you, so I commit my case to Allah. Allah is Observant of the servants.”"
  * **Gloss:** Tawakkul formula — "I commit my case to Allah" is the corpus's only such clause; the closing line "Allah is Observant of the servants" is verbatim the tail of `[3:15]`. **Speaker ledger (task):** this is *not* the "dominion that Day is the Truth for Allah" verse the task attached to 40:44 — Ghafir's Day-sovereignty line is `[40:16]` ("To whom does the sovereignty belong today? To Allah, the One, the Irresistible"); corpus near-twins of the task's phrasing are `[25:26]` and `[18:44]`.
* **`[40:45]`** "So Allah protected him from the evils of their scheming, while a terrible torment besieged Pharaoh’s clan."
  * **Gloss:** The single verb that answers the whole narrative risk: protection for the concealed believer (his concealment ends in rescue, not exposure-and-death); the same siege-verb ("besieged") returns at `[40:83]` for the mockers' ridicule.
* **`[40:46]`** "The Fire. They will be exposed to it morning and evening. And on the Day the Hour takes place: “Admit the clan of Pharaoh to the most intense agony.”"
  * **Gloss:** Two-stage exposure: the twice-daily showing (classical readings take this as the barzakh portion, the Hour's admission as the permanent one — *Source: tafsīr reports, e.g. Ibn Kathir on 40:46 — verify in print*), and the name-cluster "intense agony" shared with the next surah, `[41:27]` ("We will make those who disbelieve taste an intense agony"), also at `[23:77]`, `[50:26]`; the morning/evening rhythm is elsewhere the rhythm of dhikr (`[33:42]`, `[48:9]`, `[76:25]`) — here inverted into punishment's clock.
* **`[40:47]`** "As they quarrel in the Fire, the weak will say to those who were arrogant, “We were followers of yours; will you then spare us a portion of the Fire?”"
  * **Gloss:** The blame-trade: followership pleaded as mitigating evidence; the same leader/follower re-litigation runs in Saba, `[34:33]` ("It was your scheming by night and day").
* **`[40:48]`** "Those who were arrogant will say, “We are all in it; Allah has judged between the servants.”"
  * **Gloss:** The arrogant concede jurisdiction — an unwitting echo of 40:20's "Allah judges with justice": even the damned cite the verdict.
* **`[40:49]`** "And those in the Fire will say to the keepers of Hell, “Call to your Lord to lessen our suffering for one day.”"
  * **Gloss:** A final delegation of prayer — ask *for* us, one day's reduction; the surah's du'a motif reaches its parody stage.
* **`[40:50]`** "They will say, “Did not your messengers come to you with clear signs?” They will say, “Yes.” They will say, “Then pray, but the prayers of the disbelievers will always be in vain.”"
  * **Gloss:** The keepers' cross-examination uses the surah's own evidence formula (40:22's messengers-with-clear-proofs), extracts the admission, and closes: "Then pray" — with the counter-verdict to 40:60's "I will respond to you": answered prayer is conditional on the prayer's owner.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`the path of rectitude`**: the sound road.
  * **Rendering:** 40:38 — the concealed believer's first direct guidance-claim.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:146]` — "if they see the path of rectitude, they will not adopt it for a path": the same name on the proud's refusal-list.
* **`fleeting enjoyment` / `the Home of Permanence`**: the two homes.
  * **Rendering:** 40:39's contrast; "fleeting enjoyment" is corpus-unique (rg-verified), "Home of Permanence" occurs only again at `[35:35]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:35]` — the saved speak of it in the past tense as settled fact, the response to the hidden believer's future-tense invitation.
* **`the Mighty Forgiver`**: ghafūr with the might-name.
  * **Rendering:** 40:42, the counter-object of the call; the corpus's only occurrence of this order (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[40:3]` (ruku 1's title-string) and `[38:66]` "the Mighty, the Forgiver" — the pairing recurs on the lips of the Fire's inhabitants' opposite register.
* **`I commit my case to Allah`**: tafwīḍ — the entrusting verdict.
  * **Rendering:** 40:44, with the witness-line "Allah is Observant of the servants."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:15]` — the same final clause verbatim ("Allah is Observant of the servants"), there closing the description of the righteous; the corpus has no other "commit my case" line (rg-verified).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical lists); the morning/evening exposure of 40:46 became the standard proof-text for the barzakh's preliminary recompense in classical eschatology. *Source: reports in classical tafsīr (Ibn Kathīr on 40:46 among them) — verify in print.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** none in the corpus; that the protected believer is the same man of 40:28 is the plain narrative reading (the narrator re-labels him "the one who had believed" at 38 and answers at 45). *Source: agent inference — verify.*
