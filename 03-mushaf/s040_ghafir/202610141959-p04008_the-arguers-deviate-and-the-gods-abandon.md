# The arguers deviate; the gods abandon them; yokes, chains, the gates of Hell (40:69–78)
**UID:** 202610141959
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 40:69–78 (Ruku 8 of 9 in Ghafir)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[40:69]` – `[40:78]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The verdict scene for the surah's recurring criminal — the one who disputes Allah's revelations without authority: dragged in yokes and chains through boiling water to consumption, cross-examined about his gods who have abandoned him ("we were praying to nothing before"), and sealed with the gates of Hell; then the address turns back to the Prophet with the second patience-formula and the constitution of messengerhood: nobody brings a miracle except by Allah's leave, and when the command issues, vanity's seekers lose on the spot.
* **Placement:** Ruku 8 of 9; the enforcement panel of the argument-trilogy (40:4 → 35 → 56 → here at 69–70), and the surah's final word on how revelation actually travels (78) — the kalam note's messenger-logistics.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[40:69]`** "Have you not observed those who dispute regarding Allah's revelations, how they have deviated?"
  * **Gloss:** The observation-formula (the surah's evidence-procedure, cf. 40:21's travel summons) aimed now at the arguers themselves: their deviation is the datum.
* **`[40:70]`** "Those who call the Book a lie, and what We sent Our messengers with—they will surely know."
  * **Gloss:** The crime restated as denial of the Book and of everything sent with the messengers — the surah's opening Book-claim (40:2) closing the loop; "they will surely know" is suspense-as-verdict.
* **`[40:71]`** "When the yokes are around their necks, and they will be dragged by the chains."
  * **Gloss:** The knowledge scene: yokes (aṭwāq) and chains — the restraint-lexicon of `[13:5]` ("they will have yokes around their necks"), `[76:4]` ("chains, and yokes, and a Searing Fire"), and Saba's `[34:33]` ("We will put yokes around the necks of those who disbelieved").
* **`[40:72]`** "Into the boiling water, then in the Fire they will be consumed."
  * **Gloss:** Two-stage treatment: hamim (boiling water) then incineration — the sequence also frames the drink of `[10:4]` ("a drink of boiling water, and agonizing torment").
* **`[40:73]`** "Then it will be said to them, “Where are those you used to deify?"
  * **Gloss:** The roll-call of the deified — a question into an empty room.
* **`[40:74]`** "Instead of Allah?” They will say, “They have abandoned us. In fact, we were praying to nothing before.” Thus Allah sends the disbelievers astray."
  * **Gloss:** The cult's clients confess they were calling upon nothing (the invocation had zero referent all along — 40:20's "cannot judge with anything" and 40:43's "no say" arrive at their conclusion); and the narrator adds the drift-verdict: Allah sends the disbelievers astray — misguidance as consequence, the surah's constant grammar (40:33–34, 37).
* **`[40:75]`** "That is because you used to rejoice on earth in other than the truth, and because you used to behave with vanity."
  * **Gloss:** The causal "because" flips to second person (the speech addresses them directly): joy built on non-truth plus baghy (vanity/insolence) — the same pairing that will convict 40:83 ("they rejoiced in the knowledge they had"), and near-twin the tone of `[9:81]` ("Those who stayed behind rejoiced").
* **`[40:76]`** "Enter the gates of Hell, to remain therein forever. What a terrible dwelling for the arrogant."
  * **Gloss:** The gate-order: verbatim family in the corpus — `[16:29]` "Enter the gates of Hell, to dwell therein forever. Miserable is the residence of the arrogant" and `[39:72]` (the previous surah's version); "the arrogant" is the surah's closing class (40:47–48, 56, 60, 76 — kibr's full run).
* **`[40:77]`** "So be patient. The promise of Allah is true. Whether We show you some of what We have promised them, or take you to Us, to Us they will be returned."
  * **Gloss:** The patience-formula's second station (verbatim opening = 40:55 and `[30:60]`), now with the either/or consolation: whether the Prophet sees part of the threat or is taken before it, the return is to Allah — timing is not the believer's problem.
* **`[40:78]`** "We sent messengers before you. Some of them We told you about, and some We did not tell you about. No messenger can bring a miracle except by leave of Allah. Then, when the command of Allah is issued, fair judgment will be passed, and there and then the seekers of vanity will lose."
  * **Gloss:** The messenger-catalogue with its closed shelf (near-verbatim twin `[4:164]`, whose next clause — "And Allah spoke to Moses directly" — is Ghafir's own kalam signature given by its sibling surah); miracle-production is by leave alone (against the demand-and-perish posture of 40:5, 50); and when the command issues, judgment is "fair" (the 40:20 "judges with justice" verdict-time) and the vanity-seekers lose "and there and then" — the exact phrase of the ruku-9 finale (40:85).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`yokes`**: aṭwāq — collars.
  * **Rendering:** "the yokes are around their necks, and they will be dragged by the chains" (40:71).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:5]`, `[76:4]`, `[34:33]` — every other corpus site of the yoke-image, all punishment-contexts; Saba's 34:33 adds the night-and-day scheming clause the Ghafir believer warned of (40:47's followership).
* **`gates of Hell`**: abwāb al-jaḥīm.
  * **Rendering:** "Enter the gates of Hell, to remain therein forever. What a terrible dwelling for the arrogant" (40:76).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:29]` ("Miserable is the residence of the arrogant" — same verse-shape), `[39:72]` (az-Zumar's identical order) — a fixed entrance-formula across the Meccan surahs.
* **`we were praying to nothing`**: the zero-referent confession.
  * **Rendering:** 40:74 — the invoked themselves are said to have "abandoned us," then dissolved into "nothing."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[40:20]` ("those whom they invoke besides Him cannot judge with anything") and `[40:43]` ("has no say in this world, or in the Hereafter") — the surah's own three-step demolition of the invoked, completed here.
* **`Some of them We told you about, and some We did not`**: the closed messenger-catalogue.
  * **Rendering:** 40:78.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:164]` near-verbatim ("Some messengers We have already told you about, while some messengers We have not told you about. And Allah spoke to Moses directly") — the twin keeps revelation's selectivity and its speech-claim in one breath.
