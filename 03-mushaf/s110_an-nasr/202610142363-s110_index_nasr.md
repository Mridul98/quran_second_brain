## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** the corpus states nothing (header = title/source/basmala — rg-verified). Classical type-lists split: many assign 110 as the only *Medinan* surah of this closing six, and the transmitted occasion-report (revealed at the Farewell Pilgrimage) sits in the Hijrah period — the "all-Meccan" framing of the closing unit is therefore not unanimous. *Source: classical type-lists and occasion-reports — verify; disagreement noted, not papered over.* Revelation order: **transmitted nuzul tables — verify**.
* **Size:** 3 ayahs, 1 ruku (ruku_map global 552 — verified). One of the shortest surahs; second door of the closing bracket 109–114.
* **Title:** an-Nasr — Itani's header "VICTORY" tracks `[110:1]` "Allah’s victory" (*nasr*; the same root is rendered "support" at `[61:13]` "support from Allah, and imminent victory" — observable register drift).
* **Central theme:** The end-of-mission sign: victory + conquest arrive, people flood into Allah's religion — and the commanded response to triumph is NOT triumphalism: "Then celebrate the praise of your Lord, and seek His forgiveness. He is the Accepter of Repentance." The whole arc is three verses: event (1–2) → response + divine attribute (3).
* **Reading hint:** watch the mood-shift — mass success, and the only imperative is praise-and-istighfar. Classical readers took this as the mission's farewell ("when My servant has completed My favor, his command is to seek forgiveness"); the reading is transmitted, not stated (see §5b).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **The sign-event (110:1–2):** "When there comes Allah’s victory, and conquest. / And you see the people entering Allah’s religion in multitudes." — two nouns (*nasr*, *fath*) and one spectacle; the conditional "when" frames the whole surah as a clock, not a celebration.
* **The counter-intuitive command (110:3a):** praise + istighfar at the moment of maximum success — no victory-lap vocabulary anywhere in the surah (observable: zero endurance-, conquest-, or triumph-verbs in the imperatives).
* **The ground clause (110:3b):** "He is the Accepter of Repentance." — the surah's last three words are a divine name of renewal; the attribute is the SECOND SENTENCE OF VERSE 3, not a separate ayah (hint-correction — the 3-verse "event → command → attribute" arc actually runs 2 verses event + 1 verse command-AND-attribute).
* **Unit position:** between 109 (the partition of ways) and 111 (the fate of the partitioned enemy) — the unit moves: severance → success → judgment on the named adversary.

## 5b. Batch-Return Verification Flags (this index)
* **Hint renderings corrected:** "the help" → "**Allah’s victory**" (110:1, header "VICTORY"); "people entering in crowds" → "entering Allah’s religion **in multitudes**" (110:2); "glorify, bless, and seek forgiveness" → ONE verb-pair: "**celebrate the praise of your Lord, and seek His forgiveness**" (110:3 — tasbih-with-praise fused, rg-verified near-twin `[20:130]`); "He is ever Accepting of repentance" → "**He is the Accepter of Repentance.**"
* **"Event → command → divine attribute" arc: 3 verses ≠ 3 beats** — the attribute sentence lives inside 110:3 (see §3); cited so no future note splits the surah into a phantom 2+1+1.
* **Farewell-surah reading = CLASSICAL REPORT, not text:** the identification of 110 as the sign of the Prophet's approaching death (Farewell-Pilgrimage occasion; the celebrated Umar–Ibn Abbas exchange) is transmitted in hadith/tafsir apparatus on 110 — the surah itself never mentions death, farewell, or a date. *Source: transmitted reports — verify primary before quoting; label classical, never "the verse says."*
* **"Multitudes" census (rg-verified):** the noun-occurrences `[6:128]`, `[7:179]` ("multitudes of jinn and humans") vs 110:2's adverbial "in multitudes"; near-renderings "in crowds" `[7:38]`, "in droves" `[78:18]` — 110:2's mass-entry has no verbatim twin.
* **Meccan-premise flag:** the batch's "six Meccan surahs" framing conflicts with majority classical lists for 110 (Medinan) — recorded honestly above; corpus-neutral.
* **Victory-wait thread (rg-verified):** "Allah’s victory" phrase-twin at `[2:214]` ("When is Allah’s victory? Indeed, Allah’s victory is near") and Badr-past-tense `[3:123]` — 110:1's "When there comes" is the arrival-form of a waiting-corpus, not a one-off headline.
* **Tawwab census:** "Accepter of Repentance" verbatim has exactly two sites, `[2:54]` + 110:3; `[40:3]` "Accepter of repentance" is the third concept-site (rg-verified) — see passage §3.
