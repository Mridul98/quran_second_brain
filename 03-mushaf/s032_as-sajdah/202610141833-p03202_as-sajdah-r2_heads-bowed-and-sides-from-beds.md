# Heads bowed and sides from their beds: the two audiences on display (32:12–22)
**UID:** 202610141833
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 32:12–22 (Ruku 2 of 3 in as-Sajdah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[32:12]` – `[32:22]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah cuts to the Day and shows two audiences in their permanent postures: the guilty with bowed heads begging "send us back" — refused, because guidance was willed otherwise and the declaration stands (12–14); and the prostrate, bed-shunning, spending believers whose recompense no soul even knows (15–17) — then the verdict: believer and sinner are not equal, and the lesser torment is administered here first, so that they may return (18–22).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 3; the Day-scene answers ruku 1's denial "shall we be in a new creation?" (32:10) with the scene itself, and clears space for ruku 3's history-lesson mode (Moses, leaders, ruins).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[32:12]`** "If only you could see the guilty, bowing their heads before their Lord: “Our Lord, we have seen and we have heard, so send us back, and we will act righteously; we are now convinced.”"
  * **Gloss:** The remorse-scene (task's "hang their heads" vs. corpus "bowing their heads" — logged); their confession "we have seen and we have heard" is belief arriving as hindsight; the plea to be returned has its near-twin at `[63:10]` ("if only You would delay me for a short while, so that I may be charitable").
* **`[32:13]`** "Had We willed, We could have given every soul its guidance, but the declaration from Me will come true: “I will fill Hell with jinn and humans, altogether.”"
  * **Gloss:** The guidance-prerogative clause beside 28:56 (cited in the guidance note); the declaration "I will fill Hell with jinn and humans, altogether" is verbatim the divine word at `[11:119]` — the two scenes of Iblis' follow the same script.
* **`[32:14]`** "So taste, because you forgot the meeting of this Day of yours; We have forgotten you; so taste the eternal torment for what you used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The reciprocal forgetting — they forgot the liqa' (32:10's word, rg-checked root-phrase "meeting with their Lord"), so God "forgot" them: the forgetting is judicial, not amnesiac — Source: agent inference on the rendering; classical readings concur.
* **`[32:15]`** "They believe in Our communications, those who, when reminded of them, fall down prostrate, and glorify their Lord with praise, and are not proud."
  * **Gloss:** The name-verse: sajdah as the bodily answer to remembrance — the angels were commanded to prostrate to the spirit-breathed man (`[15:29]`, `[38:72]`), while Satan "refused, was arrogant" (`[2:34]`); this verse prostrates and "are not proud" — the anti-Iblis portrait, ruku 1's clay-and-spirit dossier now kneeling.
* **`[32:16]`** "Their sides shun their beds, as they pray to their Lord, out of reverence and hope; and from Our provisions to them, they give."
  * **Gloss:** Night prayer between khawf and raja' (reverence and hope) plus spending from provision — the infraq formula of `[2:3]` ("give from what We have provided for them") and `[22:35]`; the twin sleep-portrait is `[51:17]` "They used to sleep a little at night."
* **`[32:17]`** "No soul knows what eye’s delight awaits them—a reward for what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The hidden recompense: "eye's delight" is the corpus's qurrat-a'yun phrase — the world's eye's-delight was Pharaoh's wife's hope for the infant Moses `[28:9]` and the garden of `[43:71]` ("whatever the souls desire and delights the eyes"); here even the knowing of it is withheld.
* **`[32:18]`** "Is someone who is faithful like someone who is a sinner? They are not equal."
  * **Gloss:** The equality-denial question; the surah's two audiences (12 vs 15) get their verdict-form: la-astawayn — "They are not equal."
* **`[32:19]`** "As for those who believe and do righteous deeds, for them are the Gardens of Shelter— hospitality for what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** Jannat al-ma'wa — "Gardens of Shelter," unique in this corpus (rg-checked); the ma'wa/shelter wordplay continues in 32:20's opposite "ma'wa."
* **`[32:20]`** "But as for those who transgressed, their shelter is the Fire. Every time they try to get out of it, they will be brought back into it, and it will be said to them, “Taste the suffering of the Fire which you used to deny.”"
  * **Gloss:** The counter-shelter: the same word awa gives refuge to the believers and ownership to the Fire ("their shelter is the Fire"); the exit-refused scene answers 32:12's return-refused plea — both requests to go back are denied, in opposite directions.
* **`[32:21]`** "We will make them taste the lesser torment, prior to the greater torment, so that they may return."
  * **Gloss:** The pedagogical here-and-now: the "lesser torment" (unique phrase in corpus — rg-checked) is classically read as worldly calamity/pre-Hijrah battles so that they may return — Source: transmitted classical reading (e.g., Ibn Kathir-type glosses on 32:21; also "the day of Badr" in some reports) — verify in print; the purpose-clause "so that they may return" is the same ruju'-root as 32:12's failed request "send us back": return is offered now as mercy-with-consequence, not later as exception.
* **`[32:22]`** "Who is more wrong than he, who, when reminded of his Lord’s revelations, turns away from them? We will certainly wreak vengeance upon the criminals."
  * **Gloss:** The turn-away criminal (zalam) — verbatim-shaped twin of `[31:7]` (turns away when Verses are recited) and of the destruction-formula family "Who is more wrong than..." (`[46:5]`, rg-checked); the ruku closes on vengeance over the criminals (mujrimun, the same class as 32:12's guilty).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sajdah`** (prostration): the surah's name-word.
  * **Rendering:** "fall down prostrate" (32:15); title PROSTRATION from the corpus header.
  * **Elsewhere:** the prostration command to the angels `[15:29]`, `[38:72]`; the refusal of Satan `[2:34]`; universal prostration `[22:18]`; the magicians' fall `[20:70]` — this ruku's verse is the believing species of a gesture spanning the whole book.
* **`qurrat a'yun`** (eye's delight): 32:17.
  * **Rendering:** "what eye's delight awaits them."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[28:9]` "An eye's delight for me and for you"; `[43:71]` "whatever the souls desire and delights the eyes"; `[25:74]` "grant us delight in our spouses" — this corpus's single family for the phrase; only 32:17 keeps it hidden ("No soul knows").
* **khawf and raja'** (reverence and hope): 32:16's twin motives.
  * **Rendering:** "out of reverence and hope."
  * **Elsewhere:** the pair recurs in devotional descriptions (cf. `[32:16]`-style worship in `[22:35]`: hearts tremble + endure + spend — rg-checked family); the night-prayer twin `[51:17]`.
* **al-adhab al-asghar** (the lesser torment): 32:21.
  * **Rendering:** "the lesser torment, prior to the greater torment."
  * **Elsewhere:** unique phrase (rg-checked); concept-family: "We give them a little comfort; then We compel..." `[31:24]` — the same this-world-before-that sequencing.
