# Saved from the fire; the exit; Lot's plea (29:23–30)
**UID:** 202610141804
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 29:23–30 (Ruku 3 of 7 in al-Ankabut)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[29:23]` – `[29:30]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The trial sharpens to a death-sentence — "Kill him, or burn him" — and Allah answers with salvation-from-fire; Abraham's sermon exposes patronage-affection as a loan that defaults on Resurrection Day into mutual disowning and cursing; Lot believes, declares an exit "to my Lord," confronts a crime no world has seen, and pleads for help against the people of corruption.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 7; the Abraham-block's second half — the fire follows the livelihood argument (29:16–21), and Lot's exit hands the surah to the Sodom scene of ruku 4 (29:31–35).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[29:23]`** "Those who disbelieved in Allah’s signs and His encounter—these have despaired of My mercy. For them is a painful torment."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict on denial-of-signs-and-encounter (the 29:5 "meeting with Allah" inverted); despair-of-mercy stated as the disbelievers' self-exile — the mirror of `[39:53]` "do not despair of Allah's mercy."
* **`[29:24]`** "But the only response from his people was their saying, “Kill him, or burn him.” But Allah saved him from the fire. Surely in that are signs for people who believe."
  * **Gloss:** The testing question (29:2) now carried by one man: a capital vote, an execution, and a rescue — the fire-refusal twin of `[21:68–69]` "Burn him... O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham."
* **`[29:25]`** "And he said, “You have chosen idols instead of Allah, out of affection for one another in the worldly life. But then, on the Day of Resurrection, you will disown one another, and curse one another. Your destiny is Hell, and you will have no saviors.”"
  * **Gloss:** The patronage economics in one verse: mutual affection is the currency of this world only; the Day converts it to disowning (twin `[2:166]`) and cursing; "no saviors" rings back 29:22.
* **`[29:26]`** "Then Lot believed in him, and said, “I am emigrating to my Lord. He is the Noble, the Wise.”"
  * **Gloss:** Lot's faith-act and the exit-declaration; the emigration formula is 37:99's Abraham-line ("I am going towards my Lord, and He will guide me") — this edition's syntax attaches the words to Lot, while classical reports read the speaker as Abraham (Source: agent inference — verify; see Context note). "The Noble, the Wise" closes on names that ground the move.
* **`[29:27]`** "And We granted him Isaac and Jacob, and conferred on his descendants the Prophethood and the Book, and gave him his reward in this life; and in the Hereafter he will be among the upright."
  * **Gloss:** The Abrahamic grant: progeny, prophecy-in-lineage, present-and-future reward — near-twin of `[57:26]` "We sent Noah and Abraham, and established in their line Prophethood and the Scripture"; the patience of 29:14 pays as lineage.
* **`[29:28]`** "And Lot, when he said to his people, “You are committing an obscenity not perpetrated before you by anyone in the whole world."
  * **Gloss:** Lot's sermon opens with the unprecedented-crime charge — verbatim-logic twin of `[7:80]` "Do you commit lewdness no people anywhere have ever committed before you?" (rendering drift: "obscenity"/"lewdness").
* **`[29:29]`** "You approach men, and cut off the way, and commit lewdness in your gatherings.” But the only response from his people was to say, “Bring upon us Allah’s punishment, if you are truthful.”"
  * **Gloss:** Three indictments — sexual violation, highway "cut off the way" (unique wording, rg-verified), and public lewdness; the counter-response is the provocation formula of `[7:77]`/`[11:32]` "bring upon us what you threaten us with."
* **`[29:30]`** "He said, “My Lord, help me against the people of corruption.”"
  * **Gloss:** The plea — not a threat, a prayer; the adverb-law: the corrupted people are handed to justice (29:34), while the preacher asks help.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`saved him from the fire`** `[29:24]`:
  * **Rendering:** "But Allah saved him from the fire."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[21:69]` "O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham" — the same salvation told from the fire's side; `[37:97]` "Build a pyre for him, and throw him into the furnace" — the verdict on arson.
* **`affection` (mawadda)** `[29:25]`:
  * **Rendering:** "out of affection for one another in the worldly life."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[60:1]` offering "affection" to God's enemies; `[42:23]` "affection among the near of kin" — the word is neutral; 29:25 indicts its wrong address.
* **`disown`** `[29:25]`:
  * **Rendering:** "you will disown one another, and curse one another."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:166]` "Those who were followed will then disown those who followed them"; `[30:13]` "they will disown their partners" — the Day's vocabulary for dissolved patronage.
* **`emigrating` (hajara)** `[29:26]`:
  * **Rendering:** "I am emigrating to my Lord."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[37:99]` Abraham's "I am going towards my Lord, and He will guide me"; `[16:110]` "those who emigrated after being persecuted, then struggled and persevered" — exit-under-pressure as the surah's sabr test in act.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the fire-rescue as public sign matches a Meccan argument-address ("Surely in that are signs for people who believe," 29:24).
* **Speaker question at 29:26:** this edition's English syntax makes Lot the speaker of "I am emigrating to my Lord"; classical reports read the pronoun-chain as Abraham (Lot believed in him, and he [Abraham] said) — the ambiguity is a translation-side fact, not a corpus contradiction (Source: agent inference — verify against a print tafsir before teaching either reading).
