# Recite, pray, and argue in the best manner (29:45–51)
**UID:** 202610141806
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 29:45–51 (Ruku 5 of 7 in al-Ankabut)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[29:45]` – `[29:51]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** After the parables, the community gets its charter: recite the Scripture and hold the prayer — the prayer as moral immune system — and argue with the People of the Scripture only in the best way, staking the shared ground that "our God and your God is One"; then the sufficiency claim: a Prophet who neither read nor wrote, delivering clear signs lodged in the hearts of the knowing, is miracle enough — "Miracles are only with Allah."
* **Placement:** Ruku 5 of 7; the pivot from the ruin-exhibits (ruku 4) to the surviving-community sections (ruku 6–7): worship-form, debate-etiquette, and revelation-credentials in seven verses.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[29:45]`** "Recite what is revealed to you of the Scripture, and perform the prayer. The prayer prevents indecencies and evils. And the remembrance of Allah is greater. And Allah knows what you do."
  * **Gloss:** Two commands and one physiology: recitation feeds, prayer disciplines — "prevents indecencies and evils" (the same pair-list as `[6:151]` "do not come near indecencies, whether outward or inward"); "remembrance" pairs with `[20:14]` "practice the prayer for My remembrance"; the clause "Allah knows what you do" closes the ruku's surveillance frame.
* **`[29:46]`** "And do not argue with the People of the Scripture except in the best manner possible, except those who do wrong among them. And say, “We believe in what was revealed to us, and in what was revealed to you; and our God and your God is One; and to Him we are submissive.”"
  * **Gloss:** The debate protocol, verified verbatim as the task asked: common-ground confession (both revelations believed in; one God; submission to Him) with one exception carved — the actively wrongdoers among them; near-twin formula of `[2:139]` "Do you argue with us about Allah, when He is our Lord and your Lord... we are sincere to Him?"
* **`[29:47]`** "Likewise, We revealed to you the Scripture. Those to whom We gave the Scripture believe in it, and some of these believe in it. None renounce Our communications except the disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** The Scripture divides neither by age nor by claim: prior recipients believe, and some of "these" believe too — the prior-recipients line is verbatim-logic of `[28:52]` "Those to whom We gave the Scripture before it believe in it."
* **`[29:48]`** "You did not read any scripture before this, nor did you write it down with your right hand; otherwise the falsifiers would have doubted."
  * **Gloss:** The unlettered credential: no prior reading, no right-hand writing — an argument designed to remove the fabrication option ("the falsifiers" is the same class condemned at `[45:27]` "the falsifiers will lose" and `[77:24]`); the right-hand idiom's other corpus use is the record of deeds (`[17:71]`).
* **`[29:49]`** "In fact, it is clear signs in the hearts of those given knowledge. No one renounce Our signs except the unjust."
  * **Gloss:** The miracle's residence changes: signs lodged "in the hearts of those given knowledge" — the knowing class of 29:43 reappears as the Scripture's true locus.
* **`[29:50]`** "And they said, “If only a miracle from his Lord was sent down to him.” Say, “Miracles are only with Allah, and I am only a clear warner.”"
  * **Gloss:** The demand-management answer — the sender-of-miracles clause is near-verbatim of `[6:109]` "Say, 'The miracles are only with Allah'"; the warner's office is bounded (cf. `[20:133]`, where "Were they not given enough miracles in the former scriptures?").
* **`[29:51]`** "Does it not suffice them that We revealed to you the Scripture, which is recited to them? In that is mercy and a reminder for people who believe."
  * **Gloss:** The sufficiency verdict: the recited Book is the settled miracle — mercy-and-reminder for the believing class, echoing the ruku's opening command (recite).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`People of the Scripture` (ahl al-kitab)** `[29:46]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the People of the Scripture" — this edition's fixed plural.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:64]` renders the same address "O People of the Book" (rendering drift between the two formulas); `[4:171]`, `[5:59]`, `[98:1]` — the debate-etiquette of 29:46 and the invitation-formula of 3:64 are the corpus's two courtesy-protocols.
* **`prayer` (salat)** `[29:45]`:
  * **Rendering:** "perform the prayer. The prayer prevents indecencies and evils."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:14]` "practice the prayer for My remembrance" — function stated as remembrance there, as prevention here; `[11:114]` joins the prayer-command directly to "The good deeds take away the bad deeds" — the same moral-mechanic claim (Source: observable pairing in Itani's renderings).
* **`our God and your God is One`** `[29:46]`:
  * **Rendering:** "our God and your God is One; and to Him we are submissive."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:163]` "Your God is one God"; `[41:6]` "it is inspired in me that your God is One God. So be upright towards Him"; `[22:34]` "Your God is One God, so to Him submit" — the oneness-and-submission pairing recurs exactly where creed meets community.
* **`suffice`** `[29:51]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Does it not suffice them that We revealed to you the Scripture."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[29:52]` "Allah suffices as witness" (next ruku); `[39:38]` "Allah suffices for me" — the sufficiency-family transferring confidence from demanded signs to the given Book.
