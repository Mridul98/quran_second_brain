## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Meccan per the classical type-lists; the corpus carries no classification data (header fields only — rg-verified). Revelation order ≈ 15 in the transmitted nuzul tables (list-dependent) — *transmitted tables — verify*.
* **Size:** 3 ayahs, 1 ruku (ruku_map global 550 — verified). Three lines — with 103 and 110 the shortest surah by ayah count in this edition (verifiable against ruku ranges); classically it is named the shortest surah outright (by word count) — *Source: transmitted report — verify*.
* **Title:** al-Kawthar — the corpus header "PLENTY" tracks `[108:1]` "We have given you plenty."; *kawthar* is an elative noun of excess (k-th-r), so "Plenty" is the translation of the title-word, while "the Abundance" (hint's form) is the conventional English name of the same gift — *Source: translator's term choice observable in corpus; conventional name per classical usage*.
* **Central theme:** A consolation in three moves: the gift already given (v1), the worship-and-sacrifice it elicits (v2), and the verdict that flips the enemies' hope (v3). The surah's politics are zero-length: a nameless hater ("He who hates you") is told he, not the addressed prophet, is the loser. Read inside its bracket: 107 showed worship that withholds; 108 shows worship that *gives* — sacrifice as gratitude's two hands.
* **Reading hint:** the divine voice is fronted — "WE have given you" (plural of majesty, v1) while v2 says "your Lord" and v3 names the hater — grammatically, the giver-outnumber-the-hater at the line level (*Source: structural observation on corpus renderings*).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **v1 — the gift:** "We have given you plenty." — perfective: the giving is done before anything is asked; "plenty" is left semantically open (classical range: abundant good generally, and a named river/pool in Paradise specifically — *Source: classical reports incl. the Kawthar-pond hadith — verify; nothing in the text specifies*).
* **v2 — the response:** "So pray to your Lord and sacrifice." — two bodily rites (prayer; slaughter/offering), both re-addressed: "YOUR Lord," the one who gave. Sacrifice is gratitude made cost-bearing.
* **v3 — the reversal:** "He who hates you is the loser." — the only line spoken about a third party; the hater's verdict mirrors his wish (that the prophet be nameless/lineless) back onto him — the loser-word ("the loser") ties the surah to 103's "The human being is in loss" and the corpus's loser-refrain (all rg-verified).
* **Bracket position:** ruku 550 of 556; closes the 104–108 cluster on grace-after-audit: woe (104) → demolition (105) → favor (106) → indictment (107) → gift-and-reversal (108). Classical placement pairs it with the consolation surahs 93/94 (the "your Lord did not abandon you" family — see passage §4).
* **Person-grammar note (observable):** 108 is the cluster's only pure second-person-singular surah — "you / your Lord / you" three times in three lines; 104 indicted a third-person type, 105 a third-person crowd, 106 speaks "them", 107 mixes "him/them" — 108's intimacy is its structure. (*Source: structural observation on corpus renderings — verify.*)

## 5b. Batch-Return Verification Flags (this index)
* **Hint renderings corrected (all rg-verified against `corpus/s108_al-kawthar.md`):** "We gave you the Abundance" → "**We have given you plenty.**"; "your name-hater is the cut-off one" → "**He who hates you is the loser.**" — *abtar* rendered "the loser", not "cut off" (the cut-off/without-issue force of abtar is classical lexical content — verify before preaching the lineage-rhetoric as translation); "hates you" is the corpus's only such line (shani' — the hater/enemy of the addressed — unique, rg-verified). "pray to your Lord and sacrifice" — VERIFIED exact.
* **Sacrifice-motif links 37:107 + 22:36 — judged honestly:** the hint's "ransom" link is real but typologically careful: `[37:107]` narrates the *substitution* at Moriah ("redeemed him with a great sacrifice") — Itani's word is "redeemed," not "ransomed" (corrected); 108:2's *nusuk* is the rite of slaughter-in-devotion, same theological field (offerings to God alone) but not the Isaac-event. `[22:36]` is the closest liturgical twin (slaughter, naming God, eating, feeding, "that you may be thankful") and `[22:37]` supplies its inner meaning (piety reaches Him, not meat/blood) — these link with profit; do not claim 108:2 alludes to 37:107 in the text — *Source: motif-family judgment (agent inference — verify against tafsir of 108:2)*.
* **The hater unnamed — CONFIRMED:** no name, no tribe, no date in 108 (rg-verified). Classical asbab reports attach the moment to the death of the prophet's infant sons and a Qurayshite's gloat that his line is cut off (abtar) — no male heir (transmitted in the Ibn Sa'd/Tabari/Ibn Kathir families) — *Source: classical transmitted reports — verify; the text's generality ("He who hates you") is the point.*
* **Consolation-cluster verification:** `[93:3]` "Your Lord did not abandon you, nor did He forget.", `[93:5]` "And your Lord will give you, and you will be satisfied.", `[94:4]` "And raised for you your reputation?" — all rg-verified; the transmitted pairing of 93/94/108 as one revelation-of-consolation arc is *Source: classical reports — verify*, but the textual resonance (gift-to-come vs gift-given; reputation-raised vs hater-lost) is observable in English.
* **Loser-cluster verified:** "the loser" (108:3) is the sole singular; the plural refrain runs the corpus (2:27, 3:85, 18:103 "the greatest losers in their works", 39:15, 63:9 …) and 103:2 "The human being is in loss." frames it — 108:3 hands the label to one named function (the hater) inside the universal loss-diagnosis.
* **Aspect note (observable):** 108:1 is perfective ("We **have given**") while 108:2 opens with the consecutive imperative ("So pray…") — the gift's completedness grounds the rite's presentness; and 93:5's imperfective future ("your Lord **will give** you, and you will be satisfied") is the to-come twin of 108:1's already (all lines rg-verified; the consolation-reading is *agent inference — verify*).
