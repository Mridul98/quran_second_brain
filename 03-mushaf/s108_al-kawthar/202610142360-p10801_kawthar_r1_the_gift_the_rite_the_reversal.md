# The Gift, the Rite, the Reversal: Plenty Given, Hater Cut Short (108:1–3)
**UID:** 202610142360
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 108:1–3 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Kawthar)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[108:1]` – `[108:3]` (3 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The Quran's briefest argument runs gift → rite → verdict: God's giving is stated as already-done ("We have given you plenty"), the only fitting answer is worship with a cost ("So pray to your Lord and sacrifice"), and the last word de-cuts the one whose hope was that the prophet's line — and name — would die: "He who hates you is the loser." Consolation is delivered as accounting: the hater's arithmetic was wrong about who is truncated.
* **Placement:** Ruku 550 of 556; the close of the 104–108 cluster. Against 107's pray-ers who withhold (v4–7), 108 commands the prayer *and* the slaughter — worship that spends; against 104's man who counted wealth into immortality, 108's prophet receives abundance from the plural divine "We" with no ledger attached.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[108:1]`** "We have given you plenty."
  * **Gloss:** The majestic plural opens a three-line surah — giving is the loudest word; "plenty" (kawthar, elative of excess) names no referent: good, progeny, victory, the river of Paradise all crowd the word without being named (*Source: classical range of 108:1 — the Kawthar-as-pool specification comes from transmitted hadith reports — verify; nothing in the text specifies*). The tense is perfective: before the prayer-commanded, the gift is already done — grace is the premise, not the reward.
* **`[108:2]`** "So pray to your Lord and sacrifice."
  * **Gloss:** Two rites, both bodily (bowing; slaughter), both re-addressed to "your Lord" — the Giver of v1 is the sole recipient. The sacrifice (nusuk) makes gratitude cost-bearing: what a tribe spent on idols, the commanded spend on the One. 6:162's "My prayer and my worship… devoted to Allah, the Lord of the Worlds" is this pair's formula-cousin (Itani renders the same *nusuki* there "my worship", here "sacrifice" — observable drift).
* **`[108:3]`** "He who hates you is the loser."
  * **Gloss:** The reversal in six words. The hater (shani' — the one whose hate is active enmity; classical) expected truncation for the prophet (no male heir, no name after); the surah re-assigns the loss: *he* is the loser — the one "cut off" (the hint's "cut-off one" is the classical lexical force of abtar; Itani's chosen English is "the loser," aligning 108:3 with 103:2's species-diagnosis and the corpus's loser-refrain — *Source: translator's term choice, observable*).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`kawthar` (plenty)**: elative noun of overflow — the gift's name.
  * **Rendering:** "plenty" (108:1); header "PLENTY."
  * **Elsewhere:** the k-th-r root's adverbial cousin at `[72:16]` "Had they kept true to the Path, We would have given them plenty water to drink" (rg-verified — "plenty" in Itani's two sites: here and 72:16, both as divine giving). The pool-of-Kawthar eschatology is hadith-carried, not corpus-carried (*verify*).
* **`ṣalāh + nusuk` (pray… and sacrifice)**: the two-handed response.
  * **Rendering:** "pray to your Lord and sacrifice" (108:2); nusuk appears as "sacrifice" only here in Itani's English — the sibling site 6:162 reads "my worship" (rg-verified drift).
  * **Elsewhere:** the sacrifice dossier, all rg-verified: `[37:107]` "And We redeemed him with a great sacrifice." (the ransom at the binding — Itani says "redeemed," not "ransomed"); `[22:34]` "We have appointed a rite for every nation, that they may commemorate Allah's name over the livestock He has provided them"; `[22:36]` "So pronounce Allah's name upon them as they line up. Then, when they have fallen on their sides, eat of them and feed the contented and the beggar… that you may be thankful"; `[22:37]` "Neither their flesh, nor their blood, ever reaches Allah. What reaches Him is the righteousness from you."; `[2:196]`/`[5:97]` the pilgrim-offerings law-edge. 108:2 is the devotional kernel; 22:36–37 is its social-and-inner commentary — honest link, not a same-ritual claim (index §5b judgment).
* **`shani'` (the hater)**: active, forward enmity — the surah's only antagonist.
  * **Rendering:** "He who hates you" — the corpus's only "hates you" (rg-verified); love-verbs nearby run the other way: `[100:8]` "fierce in his love of wealth", `[89:20]` "you love wealth with immense love" — the cluster 104–108 thus pits mis-ordered love against a nameless hate, answered by a mis-counted man and a loser-hater.
  * **Elsewhere:** the kin-term appears nowhere else in Itani's English; the prophetic-hatred dossier is carried by 60 ("you have had an excellent example in Abraham…" `[60:4]`, the disavowal side) — no *shani'* vocabulary there, so 108:3's hater stands alone (verdict: unique lexical site, agent inference on the kin-set — verify).
* **`abtar` (the loser)**: truncated, cut-off, lineless — reassigned.
  * **Rendering:** "the loser" (108:3) — singular-is-the-loser is this verse alone in the corpus (rg-verified against the plural loser-refrain).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[103:2]` "The human being is in loss." — the species verdict; `[18:103]` "Shall We inform you of the greatest losers in their works?"; `[39:15]` "The losers are those who lose their souls and their people on the Day of Resurrection." — the loser-refrain gives 108:3 its courtroom: the hater is a case under the general verdict.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan per classical type-lists; no classification line in the corpus (rg-verified). Order ≈ 15 — *transmitted tables — verify*. The address is in the prophet's first person ("your Lord", "hates you") — a private consolation made public: the book preserves as scripture what was said to one man at his lowest report-window (*Source: structural observation; the "lowest report-window" framing draws on the asbab below*).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** transmitted reports attach 108 to the death of the prophet's infant sons, with a Qurayshite (named in some versions) gloating that the prophet is abtar — cut off, with no male heir to carry his name (Ibn Sa'd/Tabari/Ibn Kathir families). *Source: classical transmitted reports — verify in primary; the text itself names no hater, no death, no son.*
