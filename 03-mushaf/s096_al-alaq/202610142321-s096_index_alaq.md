# Surah 96: CLOT (al-’Alaq)
**UID:** 202610142321
**Type:** Index / Surah Map
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Meccan — agreed in the classical type-lists; **transmitted nuzul tables — verify** for the rank-order. The famous report making 96:1–5 the *first* revealed passage (the cave, the command "Read") is a transmitted asbab narrative, not a claim of the corpus (see passage §6) — and transmitted tables disagree about whether 74:1–7 or 96:1–5 preceded, which is why the order flag stays open.
* **Size:** 19 ayahs, 1 ruku (ruku_map global 538 — verified). The second-longest of the six batch-surahs (after 92); a five-verse opening movement followed by a fourteen-verse development, welded at "But to your Lord is the return" (96:8).
* **Title:** al-ʿAlaq — Itani's header "CLOT (al-’Alaq)" tracks `[96:2]` "Created man from a clot"; the word's only other corpus appearance is `[75:38]` "Then he became a clot" (rg-verified).
* **Central theme:** The revelation-moment and its counter-scene. First: the command to read *in the Name of your Lord who created* — creation-from-a-clot paired with teaching-by-the-pen, "Taught man what he never knew" (96:1–5). Then: man's self-forgetting (oversteps "when he considers himself exempt," 96:6–7), the return (96:8), and a second scene: the man who forbids a servant while he prays — asked whether he is even "upon guidance" (96:9–12), shown as the one who "disbelieved and turned away" under the eye of the God who sees (96:13–14), threatened with the forelock and the Guards, summoned to "kneel down, and come near" (96:15–19). The surah is the book's own birth-scene staged as confrontation: teaching against gatekeeping, prostration against power.
* **Reading hint:** watch the two pairs of verbs — *created/taught* (96:1–5) versus *prevents/forbids* (96:9–10): God's opening of knowledge against man's closing of prayer; the surah's last word is the liturgical answer, "kneel down, and come near."

## 3. Structural Arc
* **The command and its grounds (96:1–5):** "Read" ×2 bracketing creation-from-clot and pen-taught knowledge — five verses that read as the revelation-event naming itself; the *qalam* joins the corpus' pen-family: `[68:1]` "By the pen, and by what they inscribe" (rg-verified).
* **Anthropological pivot (96:6–8):** "man oversteps all bounds… when he considers himself exempt" — the clot-made-known forgets he is not self-sufficient; "But to your Lord is the return" welds the movements (kin goal-grammar: 94:8, `[53:42]` "the finality" — rg-verified).
* **The counter-scene (96:9–14):** "Have you seen him who prevents? A servant when he prays?" — the interrogatives (guidance? righteousness?) are irony; "Do you see how he disbelieved and turned away" is the same formula as `[92:16]` "He who denies and turns away" (rg-verified); "Does he not know that Allah sees?" — the "Does he not know" formula is corpus-unique to 96:14 (rg-verified), sitting inside the large "Allah is Seeing of what they do" cluster (2:96, 2:110, 2:233… rg-verified).
* **Escalation and verdict (96:15–18):** "We will drag him by the forelock" — the forelock joins 55:41 "taken by the forelocks and the feet" and 11:56 "He holds it by the forelock" (rg-verified); "Let him call on his gang. We will call the Guards" — the gang concept rhymes `[54:45]` "The multitude will be defeated" (rg-verified); the Guards ("the Guards" capitalized is corpus-unique — rg-verified; cf. `[74:30–31]` "Nineteen… wardens of the Fire", `[72:8]` "stern guards").
* **Close (96:19):** "No, do not obey him; but kneel down, and come near." — the prostration-answer to the prayer-ban; cf. `[53:62]` "So bow down to Allah, and worship!" (rg-verified) — recitation-prostration practice at this verse is transmitted (*verify*), not corpus-stated.
* **Title from humility:** the surah is named for its lowest word — the clot of 96:2 is the birth-scene's cheapest material, and the book keeps that word as the title of its own arrival (structural observation).
* **Question-ladder (96:9–14):** five interrogation strokes — "Have you seen… Do you think… Or advocates… Do you see… Does he not know…" — the densest question-cluster in the batch; "Have you seen" (96:9) and "Have you considered" (107:1) render one idiom with drifted English (rg-verified).

## 5b. Batch-Return Verification Flags (this index)
* **Hint renderings corrected:** "READ in the name of your Lord" → "Read: In the Name of your Lord who created." (96:1 — corpus prints "Name" capital; "In the Name of" occurs only at 96:1 and Solomon's letter `[27:30]` "In the Name of Allah, the Gracious, the Merciful" — rg-verified); "taught what he knew not" → "Taught man what he never knew." (96:5); "call his council" → "Let him call on his **gang**." (96:17 — *nadi* → gang, Itani's choice); "liar/sinner forelock" → "A **deceitful, sinful** forelock." (96:16); "prostrate and draw near" → "but **kneel down**, and **come near**." (96:19 — sajda rendered "kneel down" here, while 53:62 renders *sajid* "bow down": same act, drifted vocabulary, rg-verified); "seize the forelock" → "We will **drag** him by the forelock" (96:15).
* **"The tyrant who forbids" located:** the corpus says "him who prevents… a servant when he prays" (96:9–10) — the tyrant label is *hint gloss*; the classical identification of this man with Abu Jahl is transmitted asbab (see passage §6), never named in the text.
* **"See / knows God sees":** 96:9 "Have you seen…" and 96:14 "Does he not know that Allah sees?" — both verified against corpus; 96:14's formula is unique, the seeing-attribute pervasive (2:96/110/233 etc.).
