## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Medinan per classical consensus; the task's "order 54?" is ✗ — in the Ibn-Abbas-chain enumeration this brain's indices track (al-Baqarah 87, al-Anfal 88, al-Ahzab 90 — cf. the s008/s033 indices), Muhammad (47) sits at revelation order **95**, the 9th of the Medinan block (cross-checked externally: Tanzil/Wikipedia Medinan tables; Kassis records 95/96 variants). *Source: classical lists — verify in print; not derivable from the corpus.* Transmitted reports add: except v. 13, revealed en route during the Hijrah; battle-horizon reports tie the material to Badr/Uhud (2–3 AH). *Source: classical asbab tables — verify.*
* **Size:** 38 ayahs, 4 ruku (`data/ruku_map.json`: 47:1–11, 12–19, 20–28, 29–38 = 11+8+9+10 ✓; corpus file ends at `[47:38]` ✓). Named "Muhammad" at the sole in-text mention of the name, `[47:2]` "what was sent down to Muhammad" — one of only four places the Prophet is named by name in the corpus (`[3:144]`, `[33:40]`, `[47:2]`, `[48:29]`, rg-verified); "al-Qital" (the sword-surah) is a transmitted alternative title. *Source: classical naming reports — verify.*
* **Central theme:** The battle-and-hypocrisy surah. Its Leitwort is deed-nullification (habita): "nullifies/waste… deeds" strikes 8 times across the surah (rg-verified at 47:1, 4, 8, 9, 28, 32, 33, 35) — deeds come to nothing through disbelief, hatred of revelation, alliance with the enemies of revelation, or wavering when a decisive chapter names fighting. The surah sorts three populations — the rewarded believers (1–7, 17), the overt opponents with a law of war (4, 10–13), and the internal fifth column whose eyes roll "like someone fainting at death" (16, 20–30) — then closes on the spending audit (36–38): the same nullification grammar turned from the battlefield to the wallet.

* **Task Ledger (proposed claims checked against the corpus):**

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Sorting and the sword (1–11):** the two parties declared (1–3); the combat-captivity law mid-surah (4); the martyr's triple grant — guidance, improved state, named Paradise (5–6); the support contract "If you support Allah, He will support you" (7); perdition and journey-through-the-earth precedent (8–11).
* **Two banquets (12–19):** cattle-appetite vs the four-river Garden parable (12–15, the inventory corpus-unique); the sealed listeners vs the increased-guided (16–17); the Hour's tokens and the kalima command (18–19).
* **The fifth column (20–28):** the fainting gaze at the decisive chapter (20–21); mischief and severed ties (22–23); "locks upon their hearts" (24); the secret alliance "We will obey you in certain matters" (25–26); angels beating faces and backs at death (27–28).
* **Exposure and endurance (29–38):** malice exposed, recognized by tone (29–30); the test decree (31); the nullified opposers (32); obey and do not let deeds go to waste (33); no forgiveness for the unrepenting dead (34); do not waver or call for peace from strength (35); play, possessions, the Rich and the needy, replacement (36–38).
