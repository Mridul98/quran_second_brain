# Cattle-appetites and four rivers: evidence versus desire (47:12–19)
**UID:** 202610142103
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 47:12–19 (Ruku 2 of 4 in Muhammad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[47:12]` – `[47:19]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Two banquets define two kinds of life: the disbelievers eat as cattle eat and enjoy (12), while the righteous are promised a Garden whose inventory is named river by river (15) — and the question that decides between them is whether one stands on evidence from his Lord or on a evil deed dressed up as good (14), a question that the surah immediately gives a live specimen of: the listeners whose hearts are sealed (16).
* **Placement:** The surah's center of gravity for eschatology; the parable pivot (15) sits exactly between the evidence-question (14) and the sealed-listener portrait (16), converting theology into a census of hearts.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[47:12]`** "Allah will admit those who believe and do good deeds into gardens beneath which rivers flow. As for those who disbelieve, they enjoy themselves, and eat as cattle eat, and the Fire will be their dwelling."
  * **Gloss:** The bestiary verdict — enjoyment reduced to feeding; "eat as cattle eat" is corpus-unique in this rendering (rg "as cattle").
* **`[47:13]`** "How many a town was more powerful than your town which evicted you? We destroyed them, and there was no helper for them."
  * **Gloss:** Mecca speaks inside a Medinan text: "your town which evicted you" — the only direct autobiographical trace the surah gives of the expulsion; "no helper" closes every worldly refuge.
* **`[47:14]`** "Is he who stands upon evidence from his Lord, like someone whose evil deed is made to appear good to him? And they follow their own desires?"
  * **Gloss:** Task claim ✓. Comparative question (istifham inkari) — the bayyina-man versus the self-deceived zealous one. *Adjudication:* the text names no sect; its neighbors (16 sealed listeners, 16–14 shared "follow their own desires") make it nifaq-ADJACENT by placement, but the verse itself is a general epistemology of self-deception. *Source: agent inference from context — classical exegetes split between "hypocrites" and "general"; verify in print.*
* **`[47:15]`** "The likeness of the Garden promised to the righteous: in it are rivers of pure water, and rivers of milk forever fresh, and rivers of wine delightful to the drinkers, and rivers of strained honey. And therein they will have of every fruit, and forgiveness from their Lord. Like one abiding in the Fire forever, and are given to drink boiling water, that cuts-up their bowels?"
  * **Gloss:** Task claim ✓ — the four-river inventory (water/milk/wine/honey) is CORPUS-UNIQUE here: rg "rivers of milk" hits only s047. The opening formula "The likeness of the Garden promised to the righteous" is shared with `[13:35]`, but 13:35 lists only "rivers flowing beneath it"; the species-by-species water-parade is 47's alone. The anti-Garden: boiling water that cuts the bowels (cf. `[6:70]` "a drink of scalding water").
* **`[47:16]`** "Among them are those who listen to you, but when they leave your presence, they say to those given knowledge, “What did he say just now?” Those are they whose hearts Allah has sealed, and they follow their own desires."
  * **Gloss:** The zero-retention audience: no memory of the recitation among the ignorant, but the *people of knowledge* are asked as a cover; "sealed" + "follow their own desires" ties them back to 14 — one disease, two diagnoses.
* **`[47:17]`** "As for those who are guided, He increases them in guidance, and He has granted them their righteousness."
  * **Gloss:** The guidance-momentum verse — hudan compounds for those already on it (the k-guidance note's core grammar, mirrored at 9:124 for faith).
* **`[47:18]`** "Are they just waiting until the Hour comes to them suddenly? Its tokens have already come. But how will they be reminded when it has come to them?"
  * **Gloss:** The suddenness motif — near-twin `[43:66]` "Are they only waiting for the Hour to come upon them suddenly, while they are unaware?"; "tokens have already come" converts waiting into negligence.
* **`[47:19]`** "Know that there is no god but Allah, and ask forgiveness for your sin, and for the believing men and believing women. Allah knows your movements, and your restingplace."
  * **Gloss:** The kalima command — the shahada phrase as imperative to the Messenger himself; a brief for the whole community's forgiveness, sealed with total divine surveillance of "movements and restingplace."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **bayyina (evidence):** 47:14 "stands upon evidence from his Lord."
  * **Rendering:** "evidence"; the same epistemic claim in Moses's mouth at `[7:105]` "clear evidence from your Lord."
* **ahwa' (desires):** "follow their own desires" at 47:14 AND 47:16 — the ruku's internal stitch.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[18:28]` (heedless heart follows desires), `[45:23]` (desire taken as god) — the desire-couple is the corpus's standard portrait of the self-deceived.
* **khatm (sealed):** 47:16 "whose hearts Allah has sealed."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:88]` "Our hearts are sealed" (their claim, denied), `[4:155]`, `[63:3]`, `[16:108]`, `[9:87]` — the seal is a consequence pronounced on a prior choice (the k-nifaq note's decay line).
* **sirat/rivers — the Garden inventory:** four named rivers, each with a qualifier (pure, fresh, delightful, strained).
  * **Rendering:** Itani's parade of adjectives; the honey is "strained" (mustulla).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:35]` shares the parable-opening but not the inventory; the generic "gardens beneath which rivers flow" is ubiquitous (47:12 in this very ruku).
