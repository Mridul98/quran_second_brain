# Exposed by tone of speech: testing, obedience, and the spending audit (47:29–38)
**UID:** 202610142105
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 47:29–38 (Ruku 4 of 4 in Muhammad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[47:29]` – `[47:38]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The closing ruku turns exposure into policy: Allah will test the community until the strivers and the steadfast are *known* (31), the sick-hearted will be unmasked by their own tone of speech (29–30), the obedient are told not to let deeds go to waste (33), the wavering are told not to call for peace from strength (35) — and the surah ends by moving the habita audit from the battlefield to the treasury: spend, for Allah is the Rich and you are the needy (36–38).
* **Placement:** The surah's closing movement. The task called 47:35 the "closer" — correction: 47:35 is the battlefield-resolution; the actual pericope-closer is the infaq trilogy 47:36–38 (38 being the surah's last ayah, corpus-verified).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[47:29]`** "Do those in whose hearts is sickness think that Allah will not expose their malice?"
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's theme verb appears — expose (the Arabic verbs at 29 and 37 differ; Itani unifies them as expose — classical lexicography — Source: verify in print); the "malice" here ↔ "They say with their tongues what is not in their hearts" in the sibling surah `[48:11]` — both texts stage the heart–mouth gap and its exposure.
* **`[47:30]`** "Had We willed, We could have shown them to you, and you would have recognized them by their marks. Yet you will recognize them by their tone of speech. And Allah knows your actions."
  * **Gloss:** The epistemic compromise: no facial stigma delivered, but recognition "by their tone of speech" (corpus-unique "tone", rg); marks-by-which-the-guilty-are-recognized occurs at `[55:41]`.
* **`[47:31]`** "We will certainly test you, until We know those among you who strive, and those who are steadfast, and We will test your reactions."
  * **Gloss:** The test decree — "until We know" (classical readings flag anthropomorphic weight here; *Source: agent note — treat as transmitted interpretive topic, verify in print*); pairs with `[3:142]` "before He has distinguished those among you who strive, and... the steadfast" and `[3:154]` "tests what is in your minds."
* **`[47:32]`** "Those who disbelieve, and hinder from the path of Allah, and oppose the Messenger after guidance has become clear to them—they will not hurt Allah in the least, but He will nullify their deeds."
  * **Gloss:** The habita Leitwort reinstalled at the openers of the closing band — hindrance (sadd) restates `[47:1]`'s repulsion; "will not hurt Allah in the least" — the refusal damages no one but the refuser.
* **`[47:33]`** "O you who believe! Obey Allah, and obey the Messenger, and do not let your deeds go to waste."
  * **Gloss:** The obedience-command — the imperative twin of 47:21's missed "Obedience and upright speech"; formula twins `[5:92]`, `[64:12]` (`[3:32]` near-twin). Task's "Say: obedience — a known matter" belongs to 47:21, not here.
* **`[47:34]`** "Those who disbelieve, and hinder from Allah’s path, and then die as disbelievers—Allah will not forgive them."
  * **Gloss:** The unforgivable bracket: disbelief + hindrance + death unrepenting — the only explicit "will not forgive" in the surah; note the qualifier "then die as disbelievers" (repentance remains open until death). *Source: agent inference from the clause — verify classical statements.*
* **`[47:35]`** "So do not waver and call for peace while you have the upper hand. Allah is with you, and He will not waste your efforts."
  * **Gloss:** Task closer-claim ✓ with corrections: "upper hand" (Itani) where the task quoted "uppermost" (that word is `[3:139]`'s and `[20:68]`'s rendering of the same family); "call for peace" faces `[8:61]` "if they incline towards peace, then incline towards it" — this surah restrains peace-offers from strength, the Anfal verse receives peace-offers from the enemy: a classical fiqh pairing. *Source: classical readings — verify in print.* The verse is not the surah's end (see §1).
* **`[47:36]`** "The life of this world is nothing but play and pastime. But if you have faith and lead a righteous life, He will grant you your rewards, and He will not ask you for your possessions."
  * **Gloss:** The world-definition (la'ib + lu'ub formula; twin rendering "diversion and play" `[29:64]`, cf. `[6:70]`, `[57:20]`) — then the gentle clause: He will not ask for it all.
* **`[47:37]`** "Were He to ask you for it, and press you, you would become tightfisted, and He would expose your unwillingness."
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual honesty-audit: the exposure verb of 47:29 returns — ask and the miser revealed; the "spend test" mirrors the "fight test" of 47:20.
* **`[47:38]`** "Here you are, being called to spend in the cause of Allah. Among you are those who withhold; but whoever withholds is withholding against his own soul. Allah is the Rich, while you are the needy. And if you turn away, He will replace you with another people, and they will not be like you."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's last word: the infaq summons, the self-harm of withholding, "Allah is the Rich, while you are the needy" (cf. `[35:15]`, `[6:133]`), and the replacement threat (verbal twins `[9:39]`, `[11:57]`) — the community can be swapped for a people "who will not be like you."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **expose-motif (kashf-family):** 47:29 "expose their malice"; 47:37 "expose your unwillingness."
  * **Elsewhere:** the nifaq dossier's disclosure grammar — `[63:1]` counter-witness; the ruku's own "recognized... by their tone" (30).
* **ibtila' (test):** 47:31 "We will certainly test you."
  * **Rendering:** "test" for balwa/bala'; paired verb set: "strive... steadfast" (mujahid/sabir).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:142]`, `[3:154]` — the Uhud test-gramma; k-sabr note's stamina pair.
* **sadd (hinder):** 47:32, 47:34 "hinder from the path"; 47:1 "repel from the path."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[48:25]` "barred you from the Sacred Mosque" — the sibling surah's concrete instance of the same word-family; `[5:2]` "people who barred you."
* **fuqara' (the needy):** 47:38 "Allah is the Rich, while you are the needy."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:15]` "it is you who are the poor, in need of Allah; while Allah is the Rich"; `[6:133]` "the Rich Beyond Need."
