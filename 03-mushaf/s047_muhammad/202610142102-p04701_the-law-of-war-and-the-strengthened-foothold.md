# Two parties, one law of war: the nullified and the strengthened foothold (47:1–11)
**UID:** 202610142102
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 47:1–11 (Ruku 1 of 4 in Muhammad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[47:1]` – `[47:11]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens by sorting humanity into two economies of deed — works nullified (1–3, 8–9) and sins remitted (2) — then drops the only combat-captivity law in this stretch (4), cashes it out for the fallen (5–6), and seals the contract: support Allah and your foothold is strengthened (7); without a Master, nothing stands (10–11).
* **Placement:** The surah's establishing ruku; the habita (nullification) Leitwort appears four times here (1, 4, 8, 9) and governs the whole book.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[47:1]`** "Those who disbelieve and repel from the path of Allah—He nullifies their works."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict that titles the surah's argument; the task's "misguides their deeds" is another edition's wording — here it is *nullifies their works*, habita, not dalala.
* **`[47:2]`** "While those who believe, and work righteousness, and believe in what was sent down to Muhammad—and it is the truth from their Lord—He remits their sins, and relieves their concerns."
  * **Gloss:** The mirror-party double grant; the name "Muhammad" occurs here — one of only four in-text mentions in the corpus (3:144, 33:40, 47:2, 48:29 — rg-verified).
* **`[47:3]`** "That is because those who disbelieve follow falsehoods, while those who believe follow the truth from their Lord. Allah thus cites for the people their examples."
  * **Gloss:** The causal "that is because" (bi-dhalika) formula begins — the same hinge recurs at 9, 11, 26, 28: the surah's favorite explanatory joint (rg "That is because": exactly these five sites plus 47:3 itself).
* **`[47:4]`** "When you encounter those who disbelieve, strike at their necks. Then, when you have routed them, bind them firmly. Then, either release them by grace, or by ransom, until war lays down its burdens. Had Allah willed, He could have defeated them Himself, but He thus tests some of you by means of others. As for those who are killed in the way of Allah, He will not let their deeds go to waste."
  * **Gloss:** The engagement-and-captivity clause: strike, bind, then release by grace or ransom. **Neutral text-claims:** the verse commands melee and prescribes two disposal options for prisoners; "until war lays down its burdens" sets a terminus read by classical jurists as either deferred eschatology or an existing rule of engagement — *Source: classical fiqh readings (e.g., Ibn Kathir/Tabari on 47:4) — verify in print.* **Corrections to the task's claims:** the words "kill them" and "cast terror" do not occur here (the latter is `[8:12]`); there is no "if they turn away — Allah is Forgiving, Merciful" clause in 47:4 (nearest analogue `[9:5]` "if they repent... Allah is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful").
* **`[47:5]`** "He will guide them, and will improve their state of mind."
  * **Gloss:** For the slain (4): guidance and islah of condition — the nullification inverted.
* **`[47:6]`** "And will admit them into Paradise, which He has identified for them."
  * **Gloss:** Task claim ✓ — "identified for them" (a'rafahu): a Paradise with their name on it, particularized, not generic.
* **`[47:7]`** "O you who believe! If you support Allah, He will support you, and will strengthen your foothold."
  * **Gloss:** The reciprocal support contract; "strengthen your foothold" is the Badr diction verbatim (`[8:11]`; prayers at `[2:250]`, `[3:147]`).
* **`[47:8]`** "But as for those who disbelieve, for them is perdition, and He will waste their deeds."
  * **Gloss:** The party of 1 gets its second verdict; "perdition" (khusr) is a recurring quranic wallet-word (e.g., `[19:59]`, `[35:39]`, `[71:28]` — rg).
* **`[47:9]`** "That is because they hated what Allah revealed, so He nullified their deeds."
  * **Gloss:** The cause named: hatred (kariha) of revelation — the inward correlate of the outward "repel" (1).
* **`[47:10]`** "Have they not journeyed through the earth and seen the consequences for those before them? Allah poured destruction upon them, and for the unbelievers is something comparable."
  * **Gloss:** The travel-and-ruins evidence formula (exact twin `[40:21]`); "poured destruction" — corpus-unique rendering here (rg "poured destruction": only s047).
* **`[47:11]`** "That is because Allah is the Master of those who believe, while the disbelievers have no master."
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's keystone: wali as belonging-and-protection; the nullified are not merely punished — they are unowned.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **habita (nullify):** 8 "nullif.../waste...deeds" hits across s047 (rg: 1, 4, 8, 9, 28, 32, 33, 35).
  * **Rendering:** "nullifies their works" (1), "waste their deeds" (8), "not let their deeds go to waste" (4).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:19]` "Allah has nullified their works" — same verb against the same fainting-eyed portrait (see ruku 3).
* **qital band (strike / bind / release / ransom):** 47:4's law sequence.
  * **Rendering:** "strike at their necks... bind them firmly... release... by grace, or by ransom."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[8:12]` "strike above the necks" + "cast terror" (the phrase the task mislocated to 47:4); `[47:35]` closes the surah at the same war-peace seam.
* **th-b-t ("to make firm" — foothold):** 47:7 "strengthen your foothold" — root th-b-t, "to make firm."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[8:11]` (Badr, verbatim), `[2:250]`, `[3:147]` — the phrase tracks the community's hardest encounters.
* **wali (Master):** 47:11 "Allah is the Master of those who believe."
  * **Rendering:** "Master" for wilayah; "no master" closes the sentence in the same word.
