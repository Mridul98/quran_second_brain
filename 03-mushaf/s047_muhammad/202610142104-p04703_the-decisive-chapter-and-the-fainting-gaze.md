# The decisive chapter: fainting gazes, locks, and faces beaten at death (47:20–28)
**UID:** 202610142104
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 47:20–28 (Ruku 3 of 4 in Muhammad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[47:20]` – `[47:28]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** When the believers ask for a decisive chapter and fighting is named in it, the sick-hearted freeze — eyes rolling like a man fainting at death (20–21); the ruku then prosecutes the disease in three frames: the corrupt rulers who would sever family ties (22–23), the locked hearts that will not ponder the Quran (24), and the apostates who outsourced their obedience to the enemies of revelation (25–28), ending with angels beating faces and backs at the moment of death.
* **Placement:** The nifaq core of the surah — the corpus's clinical middle: from 2:8–20's portrait to 63's named surah, 47:20–30 is the stage where hypocrisy is caught at the specific shock of the qital command.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[47:20]`** "Those who believe say, “If only a chapter is sent down.” Yet when a decisive chapter is sent down, and fighting is mentioned in it, you see those in whose hearts is sickness looking at you with the look of someone fainting at death. So woe to them!"
  * **Gloss:** The petition answered in the worst way: "If only a chapter is sent down" — corpus-unique longing formula (rg "If only a chapter"); the decisive (muhkam) chapter with qital named in it produces syncope in the sick hearts — the fainting-at-death stare recurs verbatim-in-phrase at `[33:19]`.
* **`[47:21]`** "Obedience and upright speech. Then, when the matter is settled, being true to Allah would have been better for them."
  * **Gloss:** Task claim "Say: obedience — a known matter" — locational correction: it is **47:21**, not 47:33, and this edition renders it "Obedience and upright speech": what they should have said was simple compliance and a fair word; "other translations carry 'a known matter' — not this corpus" (rg). Once the matter was settled they were untrue.
* **`[47:22]`** "If you turn away, you are likely to make mischief on earth, and sever your family ties."
  * **Gloss:** The alternative to obedience: fasad + qat' al-arham — corruption and severed kinship as a matched pair (cf. `[2:27]` "sever what Allah has commanded to be joined, and commit evil on earth. These are the losers").
* **`[47:23]`** "Those are they whom Allah has cursed. He made them deaf, and blinded their sight."
  * **Gloss:** The curse-sensory chain: la'na → samam → ama — 2:18's "Deaf, dumb, blind. They will not return" re-dramatized (k-nifaq's decay line cites this verse).
* **`[47:24]`** "Will they not ponder the Quran? Or are there locks upon their hearts?"
  * **Gloss:** The kalam-question: "ponder the Quran" pairs exactly with `[4:82]` — the only other occurrence of the phrase in the corpus; "locks" (aqfal) is corpus-unique here (rg "locks upon their hearts": only s047).
* **`[47:25]`** "Those who reverted after the guidance became clear to them—Satan has enticed them, and has given them latitude."
  * **Gloss:** Inhiroq after bayana — apostasy by enticement; "given them latitude" (amlay lahum) — corpus-unique rendering here.
* **`[47:26]`** "That is because they said to those who hated what Allah has revealed, “We will obey you in certain matters.” But Allah knows their secret thoughts."
  * **Gloss:** The inside trade in one line: covert partial obedience to the haters of revelation (echoing 47:9's "hated"); Allah exposes what they conceal — "secret thoughts" (id'amir).
* **`[47:27]`** "How about when the angels take them at death, beating their faces and their backs?"
  * **Gloss:** Task claim "the faces scorched?" — correction: no scorching language in s047 (rg "scorch": 0 hits); the death scene is *beating* (darb) of faces and backs — front-and-rear blows for front-and-rear denial.
* **`[47:28]`** "That is because they pursued what displeases Allah, and they disliked His approval, so He nullified their works."
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's habita seal (4th in-surah): pursuit of the displeasing + dislike of the ridwan — nullified.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **marad (sickness):** 47:20 "in whose hearts is sickness."
  * **Rendering:** consistent "sickness" for marad across the corpus (verified hits: `[2:10]`, `[5:52]`, `[8:49]`, `[9:125]`, `[33:12]`, `[33:60]`, `[47:20]`, `[47:29]`, `[74:31]`, plus the question form `[24:50]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** 2:10 is the dictionary entry — "Allah has increased their sickness"; here the sickness finally shows on the face.
* **muhkam (decisive):** 47:20 "a decisive chapter."
  * **Rendering:** "decisive" (root hakama); the adjective appears only here for a surah in the corpus (rg "decisive chapter": s047 only; cf. "decisive speech" `[38:20]`, "decisive explanation" `[7:145]`).
* **tafakkur/ponder:** 47:24 "Will they not ponder the Quran?"
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:82]` — the only twin; both occurrences ask pondering as the test the sealed cannot pass.
* **darb (beat):** 47:27 "beating their faces and their backs."
  * **Rendering:** same root as 47:4's combat "strike"; the blows return at death — *Source: agent inference — the wordplay is observable in Arabic, visible here via the shared gloss "strike/beat."*
