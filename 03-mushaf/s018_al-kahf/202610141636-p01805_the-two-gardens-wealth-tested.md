# The two gardens — wealth tested (18:32–44)
**UID:** 202610141636
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 18:32–44 (Ruku 5 of 12 in al-Kahf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[18:32]` – `[18:44]` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The wealth-test runs as a self-labeled parable of two men: one given double gardens — vines, palms, crops, a river, full harvests (32–34) — who says the three denials in sequence ("this will never perish / the Hour is not coming / I will find something better" 35–36); the friend answers with the dust-to-man creed (37–38) and the etiquette the surah just legislated, "As Allah wills; there is no power except through Allah" (39); the warning lands (40–41), ruin closes in (42), no faction helps (43), and sovereignty is named (44).
* **Placement:** Ruku 5; the second of the four tests. It applies 18:7–8 (ornament, then barren waste) to one man's ledger and proves 18:23–24 by putting its phrase in a believer's mouth at 18:39.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[18:32]`** "And cite for them the parable of two men. To one of them We gave two gardens of vine, and We surrounded them with palm-trees, and We placed between them crops."
  * **Gloss:** "Parable" is the text's own word — a model, not a report; the gift is layered agriculture: vines, palmetto walls, interlaid grain.
* **`[18:33]`** "Both gardens produced their harvest in full, and suffered no loss. And We made a river flow through them."
  * **Gloss:** The ideal yield — double full harvests, zero loss, irrigation free; this is the "ornament" of 18:7 in close-up.
* **`[18:34]`** "And thus he had abundant fruits. He said to his friend, as he conversed with him, “I am wealthier than you, and greater in manpower.”"
  * **Gloss:** The test turns in one sentence: the conversation becomes a comparison — wealth and clan as the two metrics of a man.
* **`[18:35]`** "And he entered his garden, wronging himself. He said, “I do not think this will ever perish.”"
  * **Gloss:** Self-wronging while admiring (the gaze that forgets the Giver); denial #1: permanence claimed for the perishable.
* **`[18:36]`** "“And I do not think the Hour is coming. And even if I am returned to my Lord, I will find something better than this in return.”"
  * **Gloss:** Denial #2 (no Hour) and #3 — presuming even God's court as a better deal; the same mind that says "I will do that tomorrow" without "if Allah wills."
* **`[18:37]`** "His friend said to him, as he conversed with him, “Are you being ungrateful to Him who created you from dust, then from a sperm-drop, then evolved you into man?"
  * **Gloss:** The counter-parable voice: the charge is kufr — ungratitude to a three-stage Creator (dust → drop → shaped human), the corpus's standard biography of a person (35:11, 22:5).
* **`[18:38]`** "But as for me, He is Allah, my Lord, and I never associate with my Lord anyone.”"
  * **Gloss:** The personal confession in two moves: Lord = Allah alone; worship = no partners — the surah's tawhid line inside a garden conversation.
* **`[18:39]`** "When you entered your garden, why did you not say, “As Allah wills; there is no power except through Allah”? Although you see me inferior to you in wealth and children."
  * **Gloss:** The etiquette of 18:24 quoted back into the parable — the exception-phrase plus the admission that sight of lesser wealth is no argument; the surah's two speech-commandments now enacted.
* **`[18:40]`** "Perhaps my Lord will give me something better than your garden, and release upon it thunderbolts from the sky, so it becomes barren waste."
  * **Gloss:** The believer's modest wager, which 18:8 has already guaranteed: "barren waste" — the same rendering as the surah's opening threat.
* **`[18:41]`** "Or its water will sink into the ground, and you will be unable to draw it.”"
  * **Gloss:** The second, quieter failure mode: irrigation lost — total ruin does not need fire; a sunk water table is enough.
* **`[18:42]`** "And ruin closed in on his crops, and so he began wringing his hands over what he had invested in it, as it lays fallen upon its trellises. And he was saying, “I wish I never associated anyone with my Lord.”"
  * **Gloss:** Ruin arrives unbidden; the hand-wringing over sunk investment; and the regret that arrives at the wrong address — "I wish I never associated" — creed seen only through loss.
* **`[18:43]`** "He had no faction to help him besides Allah, and he was helpless."
  * **Gloss:** The "greater manpower" of 18:34 evaporates; factions cannot save; helplessness is the test's verdict.
* **`[18:44]`** "That is because authority belongs to Allah, the True. He is Best in rewarding, and Best in requiting."
  * **Gloss:** The moral of the parable stated from outside it: wilayah/authority is Allah's alone — reward and requital both issued from that office.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`wronging himself`** `[18:35]`:
  * **Rendering:** entering the garden "wronging himself" — self-harm through the possession-gaze.
  * **Elsewhere:** this exact English phrase is unique in the corpus (`rg "wronging himself" corpus/` → 18:35 only); classically the construction (zalama nafsahu) is the Quran's standard idiom for self-injury in sin — *Source: agent note on the Arabic idiom behind varied renderings; verify against print concordance.*
* **`sperm-drop` (nutfah)** `[18:37]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:11]` "created you from dust, then from a small drop"; `[22:5]` the full embryo ladder — Itani renders the same stage-word "small drop" there, "sperm-drop" here (observable term drift).
* **`As Allah wills; there is no power except through Allah`** `[18:39]`:
  * **Rendering:** the full exception-phrase, unique in this corpus at this line (`rg "no power except" corpus/` → only 18:39).
  * **Note:** the half-phrase "If Allah wills" governs 18:24 and 18:69; the paired sentence is the classical al-hawqla *Source: transmitted phrase-form — verify.*

## 6. Context Notes
