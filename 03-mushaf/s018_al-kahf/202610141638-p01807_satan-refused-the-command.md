# Satan was of the jinn and defied the command (18:50–53)
**UID:** 202610141638
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 18:50–53 (Ruku 7 of 12 in al-Kahf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[18:50]` – `[18:53]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The archetype of failed test appears as a flashback: the angels commanded to bow to Adam, and Satan — "of the jinn" — defying his Lord; the surah then turns the memory into an indictment of the present ("Will you take him and his offspring as lords instead of Me, when they are an enemy to you?"); and the end of that choice is staged — invoked "partners" who never answer, a barrier placed between, and sinners who see the Fire and know they tumble into it.
* **Placement:** Ruku 7; between the wealth-test and the knowledge-test sits the refusal-test: the first shirk was a bow withheld. Its indictment-word "lords" (18:50) answers the closing ruku's "masters instead of Me" (18:102).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[18:50]`** "We said to the angels, “Bow down to Adam.” So they bowed down, except for Satan. He was of the jinn, and he defied the command of his Lord. Will you take him and his offspring as lords instead of Me, when they are an enemy to you? Evil is the exchange for the wrongdoers."
  * **Gloss:** The bowing scene in four beats — command, compliance, exception, explanation ("of the jinn... defied"); then the verse pivots to the auditors: patronage of Satan's clan is the exchange (badala) that "is evil." Note: only here does the bowing verse say "He was of the jinn" in these words (`rg "was of the jinn" corpus/` → 18:50 only), and the refusal here is framed as defiance of command rather than pride (contrast 2:34 "refused, was arrogant"; 38:74 "too proud").
* **`[18:51]`** "I did not call them to witness the creation of the heavens and the earth, nor their own creation; and I do not take the misleaders for assistants."
  * **Gloss:** Divine disqualification of the false patrons: they witnessed nothing — not cosmogony, not even their own creation — and God takes no "misleaders" as helpers; the partnership claimed below is disowned above.
* **`[18:52]`** "On the Day when He will say, “Call on My partners whom you have claimed.” They will call on them, but they will not answer them. And We will place between them a barrier."
  * **Gloss:** The loyalty-test on stage: the claimants invoke, the claimed are mute, and a barrier (a recurring corpus motif — cf. 25:53's placed barrier between the seas) separates worshipper from worshiped for good.
* **`[18:53]`** "And the sinners will see the Fire, and will realize that they will tumble into it. They will find no deliverance from it."
  * **Gloss:** Certainty arrives too late — sight of the Fire yields the one true calculation the surah prizes (contrast the sleepers' honest "Your Lord knows best"); no deliverance remains.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`of the jinn`** `[18:50]`:
  * **Rendering:** "He was of the jinn, and he defied the command of his Lord" — Satan's class stated, then his crime (defiance, asa) named; among the corpus's bowing scenes (2:34, 7:11, 20:116, 38:74) this is the only one with the jinn-class line in this wording.
  * **Elsewhere:** the jinn's element is stated at `[55:15]` "And created the jinn from a fusion of fire" (cf. `[15:27]` "And the jinn We created before, from piercing fire"); 18:50's detail lets readers ask why a jinn was under the angelic command — classically debated. *Source: transmitted exegetical question (Ibn-Kathir-type discussion) — agent labeling, verify.*
* **`take ... as lords instead of Me`** `[18:50]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** internal answer at `[18:102]` "Do those who disbelieve think that they can take My servants for masters instead of Me?" — the surah brackets itself with the same illicit patronage; also `[20:117]`/`[43:62]` "enemy to you" for Satan's standing.
* **`barrier`** `[18:52]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:53]` "He placed between them a barrier, and an impassable boundary" — the barrier that keeps the two seas honest now keeps claimants from their claims; in the Zul-Qarnain ruku below, a built barrier (18:96) saves people from chaos instead.
