# Never say "tomorrow" — three hundred years, humble company, two homes (18:23–31)
**UID:** 202610141635
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 18:23–31 (Ruku 4 of 12 in al-Kahf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[18:23]` – `[18:31]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Two speech-commandments bracket the story's clock: never plan tomorrow without "If Allah wills" (23–24), and the sleep lasted three hundred years and nine — "Allah knows best how long they stayed" (25–26); then the recitation command and the company command: sit with those who call morning and evening, not with the glitter (27–28); and the ruku splits humanity open — molten-brass drink for the unjust, Gardens of Eden with green silk for the workers of righteousness (29–31).
* **Placement:** Ruku 4; the surah mid-cave. The inserted etiquette (23–24) will be quoted inside the next parable (18:39) — the book teaching its own manners to itself.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[18:23]`** "And never say about anything, “I will do that tomorrow.”"
  * **Gloss:** A hard stop on presumption, addressed mid-narrative — human speech must not foreclose the unseen.
* **`[18:24]`** "Without saying, “If Allah wills.” And remember your Lord if you forget, and say, “Perhaps my Lord will guide me to nearer than this in integrity.”"
  * **Gloss:** The exception-phrase installed; forgetting is recoverable by dhikr; and even failure is redirected toward "integrity" — classically the same rashad-word of 18:10's prayer (rendered "guidance" there; agent labeling, verify).
* **`[18:25]`** "And they stayed in their cave for three hundred years, adding nine."
  * **Gloss:** Bare chronology — the number stated then immediately relativized by 18:26; classical discussion reads the nine as solar-to-lunar adjustment. *Source: transmitted exegetical note — agent labeling, verify in print.*
* **`[18:26]`** "Say, “Allah knows best how long they stayed.” His is the mystery of the heavens and the earth. By Him you see and hear. They have no guardian apart from Him, and He shares His Sovereignty with no one."
  * **Gloss:** Epistemic curb, then sovereignty: God owns the unseen, is the ground of seeing and hearing, takes no partner in rule — a compact tawhid line.
* **`[18:27]`** "And recite what was revealed to you from the Book of your Lord. There is no changing His words, and you will find no refuge except in Him."
  * **Gloss:** Return to the 18:1 Book-claim: recite, for His words are unchangeable (the clause recurs at 6:34/6:115 "no change to Allah's words") and refuge is only in their Speaker.
* **`[18:28]`** "And content yourself with those who pray to their Lord morning and evening, desiring His Presence. And do not turn your eyes away from them, desiring the glitter of this world. And do not obey him whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance— so he follows his own desires—and his priorities are confused."
  * **Gloss:** The company command: bind yourself to the humble regulars (twin of 6:52's "do not drive away those who call upon their Lord, morning and evening"); the glitter is a banned gaze-object; the heedless-hearted desire-follower is the anti-companion.
* **`[18:29]`** "And say, “The truth is from your Lord. Whoever wills—let him believe. And whoever wills—let him disbelieve”. We have prepared for the unjust a Fire, whose curtains will hem them in. And when they cry for relief, they will be relieved with water like molten brass, which scalds the faces. What a miserable drink, and what a terrible place."
  * **Gloss:** The free-will declaration (paired with 2:256's "no compulsion in religion") followed by what rejection prepares: hemming curtains and a drink of molten brass (the phrase recurs at 70:8 for the sky itself).
* **`[18:30]`** "As for those who believe and lead a righteous life—We will not waste the reward of those who work righteousness."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-declaration in one line: no reward lost — a formula the corpus repeats (7:170 "We will not waste the reward of the reformers"; 3:171 "Allah will not waste the reward of the faithful"; 3:195 "I will not waste the work of any worker").
* **`[18:31]`** "These will have the Gardens of Eden, beneath which rivers flow. Reclining on comfortable furnishings, they will be adorned with bracelets of gold, and will wear green garments of silk and brocade. What a wonderful reward, and what an excellent restingplace."
  * **Gloss:** Eden inventory — rivers, couches, gold bracelets, green silk and brocade — an ornament that will not be revoked, answering 18:2's "excellent reward" and bracketing the garden parable about to be told.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`If Allah wills`** `[18:24]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[18:69]` — Moses' companion pledges "You will find me, Allah willing, patient" (the etiquette enacted by the learner three ruku later); `[37:102]` near-verbatim: "you will find me, Allah willing, one of the steadfast" (Ishmael's answer).
* **`integrity` (rashad)** `[18:24]`:
  * **Rendering:** "nearer than this in integrity" — guidance asked as straightness of affair. *Source: classically the same word (rashad) as 18:10's "bless our affair with guidance," rendered differently here — agent labeling, verify against print concordance.*
  * **Elsewhere:** `[21:51]` "We gave Abraham his integrity formerly" — the word's exemplar is Abraham.
* **`morning and evening`** `[18:28]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:52]` (the twin verse of the humble regulars), `[76:25]`, `[33:42]`, `[48:9]`, `[24:36]` — the phrase marks the prayer-people everywhere in the corpus; heedlessness itself: `[16:108]` "Allah has sealed their hearts... they are the heedless"; `[30:7]` "heedless of the Hereafter."
