# You would think them awake — the sleepers found out (18:18–22)
**UID:** 202610141634
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 18:18–22 (Ruku 3 of 12 in al-Kahf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[18:18]` – `[18:22]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The sealed sleep becomes spectacle and proof: sleepers who look awake and terrify an onlooker (18), awakened to a dialogue in which they misjudge centuries as "a day, or part of a day" (19), still fearing stoning and forced reconversion (20), deliberately discovered so that "the promise of Allah is true, and of the Hour there is no doubt" (21) — and at once disputed over, in head-count guesses and a monument-build (22).
* **Placement:** Ruku 3; the narrative's evidentiary payoff — the resurrection motif stated by the text itself — and its first warning about what communities do with signs: argue about them.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[18:18]`** "You would think them awake, although they were asleep. And We turned them over to the right, and to the left, with their dog stretching its paws across the threshold. Had you looked at them, you would have turned away from them in flight, and been filled with fear of them."
  * **Gloss:** The phenomenology of protected sleep: turning, stillness, dread — and the startlingly unpolished detail of the dog at the threshold. Wonder here is uncanny, not cute.
* **`[18:19]`** "Even so, We awakened them, so that they may ask one another. A speaker among them said, “How long have you stayed?” They said, “We have stayed a day, or part of a day.” They said, “Your Lord knows best how long you have stayed.” “Send one of you to the city, with this money of yours, and let him see which food is most suitable, and let him bring you some provision thereof. And let him be gentle, and let no one become aware of you.”"
  * **Gloss:** Duration misjudged from inside the sleep — the phrase is a verbatim twin of the century-sleeper at 2:259 ("I have tarried for a day, or part of a day"); the epistemic reflex "Your Lord knows best" governs; then quiet logistics: pure food, gentleness, no exposure.
* **`[18:20]`** "“If they discover you, they will stone you, or force you back into their religion; then you will never be saved.”"
  * **Gloss:** The stakes of discovery named: death by stoning or coerced reconversion — forced religion is condemned from the inside of a rescue plan.
* **`[18:21]`** "So it was, that We caused them to be discovered, that they would know that the promise of Allah is true, and that of the Hour there is no doubt. As they were disputing their case among themselves, they said, “Build over them a building.” Their Lord knows best about them. Those who prevailed over their case said, “We will set up over them a place of worship.”"
  * **Gloss:** Divine plot-twist: the hidden is revealed on purpose — the sleepers become evidence of resurrection; and within one verse the community splits: memorial building vs. place of worship, with "Their Lord knows best about them" as the narrator's curb on both.
* **`[18:22]`** "They will say, “Three, and their fourth being their dog.” And they will say, “Five, and their sixth being their dog,” guessing at the unknown. And they will say, “Seven, and their eighth being their dog.” Say, “My Lord knows best their number.” None knows them except a few. So do not argue concerning them except with an obvious argument, and do not consult any of them about them."
  * **Gloss:** The head-count quarrel (3+dog, 5+dog, 7+dog) is labeled "guessing at the unknown"; the command: epistemic restraint — argue only with obvious proof, and do not seek counsel from the guessers. The surah interrupts its own story to legislate how not to read stories.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`a day, or part of a day`** `[18:19]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:259]` — the man caused to die for a hundred years answers the same phrase before correction; the corpus's sleep-resurrection motif runs through both. `[36:52]` on the Day: "Who resurrected us from our resting-place?" — death imaged as a nap.
* **`guessing at the unknown`** `[18:22]`:
  * **Rendering:** the head-counters' method dismissed in one phrase.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[45:24]` "But they have no knowledge of that; they are only guessing" — the same rebuke aimed at those who deny resurrection; guessing fails at both ends of the Hour.
* **`place of worship` (masjid)** `[18:21]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[72:18]` "The places of worship are for Allah. So do not call, besides Allah, upon anyone else" — the monument quarrel's terms, sharpened by the jinn-surah's exclusivity clause.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:259]` — the sleeper-who-woke motif, verbatim duration language; the cave story is al-Kahf's version of Baqarah's resurrection-by-time sign.
* **Parallel:** `[45:32]` — "The promise of Allah is true, and of the Hour there is no doubt": near-verbatim of 18:21's purpose clause; Jathiyah quotes the very phrase the cave was built to prove.

* **Parallel:** `[2:256]` — "force you back into their religion" (18:20) is coercion's mirror; cf. also 18:29's "Whoever wills—let him believe."

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus) — resurrection-proof and intra-community dispute fit a Meccan audience debating the Hour; the monument controversy is told of the sleepers' later admirers, not the Prophet's community. *Source: agent observation of the text's own dispute-language — verify.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the number of the sleepers is classically contested (the "few who know"); the text itself withholds it. *Source: transmitted exegetical disagreement (numbers 3/5/7 and the sleepers' names appear in lore, not in the corpus) — agent labeling, verify in print.*
