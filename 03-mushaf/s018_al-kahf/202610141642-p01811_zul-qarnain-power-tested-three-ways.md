# Zul-Qarnain — power tested three ways (18:83–101)
**UID:** 202610141642
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 18:83–101 (Ruku 11 of 12 in al-Kahf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[18:83]` – `[18:101]` (19 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The fourth test is power, given as an answer to a question ("And they ask you about Zul-Qarnain," 83): a king established on earth and given "all kinds of means" (84) travels three courses — west, where the sun sets in a murky spring and he is handed a people's verdict with the choice of penalty or kindness (85–88), east, to a people with no shelter from it (89–91), and between two barriers, where a speech-tarined people pay for a dam of iron and fire that he credits as "a mercy from my Lord" (92–98); and the ruku snaps the camera to the Day — nations surging, Trumpet blown, the screen-eyed disbelievers presented to Hell (99–101).
* **Placement:** Ruku 11; the power-test and the surah's third named geography. Where the cave-sleeper surrendered control, the garden-man hoarded it, and Moses' road required patience, here control itself is handed to a human and audited on the spot.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[18:83]`** "And they ask you about Zul-Qarnain. Say, “I will tell you something about him.”"
  * **Gloss:** The surah's ask-formula (cf. 2:189/217/219/222; 17:85 "And they ask you about the Spirit. Say...") — revelation answers on demand but with reserve: "something about him," not his whole dossier; the name is in-text here (unlike Moses' nameless companion).
* **`[18:84]`** "We established him on earth, and gave him all kinds of means."
  * **Gloss:** Power defined as establishment (tamkin) plus means (asbab — every resource-cause); the grant is the test's premise.
* **`[18:85]`** "He pursued a certain course." — **Gloss:** The course-refrain begins (a path of means, 84's asbab in motion).
* **`[18:86]`** "Until, when he reached the setting of the sun, he found it setting in a murky spring, and found a people in its vicinity. We said, “O Zul-Qarnain, you may either inflict a penalty, or else treat them kindly.”"
  * **Gloss:** The western edge, seen as from his standpoint — sun "setting in a murky spring" (the corpus's only use of the phrase); and sovereignty is delegated with an open clause: punish or be kind — the choice is itself the test.
* **`[18:87]`** "He said, “As for him who does wrong, we will penalize him, then he will be returned to his Lord, and He will punish him with an unheard-of torment."
  * **Gloss:** The king's ruling keeps jurisdictional honesty: the here-and-now penalty is his; the ultimate torment is reserved — he does not claim God's share.
* **`[18:88]`** "“But as for him who believes and acts righteously, he will have the finest reward, and We will speak to him of Our command with ease.”"
  * **Gloss:** The kindness-branch: the believer gets the finest reward and easy speech of command — the yusri/ease word of 18:16 returns as a policy of power.
* **`[18:89]`** "Then he pursued a course." — **Gloss:** Refrain two.
* **`[18:90]`** "Until, when he reached the rising of the sun, he found it rising on a people for whom We had provided no shelter from it."
  * **Gloss:** The eastern edge — a naked people, no screen from the sun; the report is ethnographic and unresolved, and the text will not allegorize it.
* **`[18:91]`** "And so it was. We had full knowledge of what he had."
  * **Gloss:** Divine audit of the whole western-eastern circuit — power watched while it works.
* **`[18:92]`** "Then he pursued a course." — **Gloss:** Refrain three, toward the middle.
* **`[18:93]`** "Until, when he reached the point separating the two barriers, he found beside them a people who could barely understand what is said."
  * **Gloss:** Between the two barriers (saddayn) — a people barely able to take speech; power meets the edge of communication itself.
* **`[18:94]`** "They said, “O Zul-Qarnain, the Gog and Magog are spreading chaos in the land. Can we pay you, to build between us and them a wall?”"
  * **Gloss:** The plea: corruption (fasad) from Gog and Magog (the pair appears only here and at 21:96, where they will be "let loose... swarm down from every mound"); they offer payment — toll-economics enters the story.
* **`[18:95]`** "He said, “What my Lord has empowered me with is better. But assist me with strength, and I will build between you and them a dam.”"
  * **Gloss:** The tested king declines the money: his empowertainment is already better; what he takes instead is labor-share ("assist me with strength") — a dam co-built, not a wall bought.
* **`[18:96]`** "“Bring me blocks of iron.” So that, when he had leveled up between the two cliffs, he said, “Blow.” And having turned it into a fire, he said, “Bring me tar to pour over it.”"
  * **Gloss:** The engineering scene — iron blocks, bellows, fire, poured tar (the corpus's only tar-pour); force, heat, and chemistry in four imperative lines.
* **`[18:97]`** "So they were unable to climb it, and they could not penetrate it."
  * **Gloss:** The dam's double negation: neither over nor through — containment, not annihilation; chaos is held, not erased.
* **`[18:98]`** "He said, “This is a mercy from my Lord. But when the promise of my Lord comes true, He will turn it into rubble, and the promise of my Lord is always true.”"
  * **Gloss:** The power-audit passes on one sentence: the achievement is read as mercy (as the sleepers' cave was, 18:16), and its future demolition is conceded — the same "rubble/ barren-waste" grammar of 18:8; only God's promise is permanent.
* **`[18:99]`** "On that Day, We will leave them surging upon one another. And the Trumpet will be blown, and We will gather them together."
  * **Gloss:** The dam's "when" becomes the Day: Gog and Magog surging (cf. 21:96), Trumpet blown (verbatim twin 36:51 "The Trumpet will be blown"), the total gathering — containment ends on schedule.
* **`[18:100]`** "On that Day, We will present the disbelievers to Hell, all displayed."
  * **Gloss:** The presentation scene — public, displayed, no cover left.
* **`[18:101]`** "Those whose eyes were screened to My message, and were unable to hear."
  * **Gloss:** The cause is the counter-seal: eyes screened, hearing unable — the dark twin of the sleepers whom God sealed for protection (18:11); the same miracle-reversal completes the ruku.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Zul-Qarnain`** `[18:83]`:
  * **Rendering:** the corpus spells him "Zul-Qarnain" (classical English: Dhul-Qarnayn, "he of the two horns"); named in-text — the answer to the ask — while his historic identity (Alexander? a Himyarite king? a Cyrus-type) stays exegetically disputed. *Source: classical identification debate (reported in Ibn-Kathir-type commentaries) — agent labeling, verify in print.*
* **`two barriers / dam (sadd)`** `[18:93–95]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:53]` "an impassable boundary" and `[18:52]`'s placed barrier — barrier as divine separator; here it becomes human engineering licensed as mercy, the only "wall" the surah praises.
* **`Gog and Magog`** `[18:94]`:
  * **Elsewhere:** `[21:96]` "Until, when Gog and Magog are let loose, and they swarm down from every mound" — the pair occurs only twice in the corpus; al-Anbiya narrates the dam's opening, al-Kahf its building.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus); the Zul-Qarnain episode is transmitted as one of the answers demanded of the Prophet by his Meccan interrogators. *Source: classical occasion-reports — agent labeling, verify.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** geography of the "murky spring," the unsheltered east-people, and the iron gorge is debated; the text reports standpoints, not coordinates. *Source: silence of the text — observable; identifications are lore.*
