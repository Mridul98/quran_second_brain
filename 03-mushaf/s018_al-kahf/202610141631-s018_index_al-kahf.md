## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Meccan — consensus of the classical surah classifications. *Source: transmitted classification (al-Itqan-type surah introductions; no strong dissent recorded) — verify in print.* Revelation order **69** in the Ibn-Abbas-derived enumeration. *Source: transmitted nuzul-order tables (Tanzil-type lists; the s016 index logged an-Nahl as **70** under the same convention — Kahf and Nahl sit together in the late-Meccan block) — verify.*
* **Size:** 110 ayahs, 12 ruku (per `data/ruku_map.json`: 12+5+5+9+13+5+4+6+11+12+19+9 = 110). Mushaf position 18, between al-Isra (17) and Maryam (19).
* **Name:** al-Kahf, "The Cave" (corpus header prints "THE CAVE"). The word "cave" occurs in five numbered ayahs only — `[18:9]`, 11, 16, 17 (twice), 25. The sleepers' story supplies the surah's title-name, but its two famous figures split: Zul-Qarnain is named in-text (`[18:83]`); Moses' companion is nameless — "a servant of Ours" (`[18:65]`); the name Khidr does not occur anywhere in the corpus (`rg -l "Khidr" corpus/` → zero hits).
* **Central theme:** The surah of tests. It states its own axiom — "We made what is upon the earth an ornament for it, to test them as to which of them is best in conduct" (`[18:7]`) — and then runs four test-narratives: faith under persecution (the cave, 9–26), wealth (the two gardens, 32–44), knowledge (Moses at the junction, 60–82), power (Zul-Qarnain, 83–98). It is bracketed by two Book-claims: the distortion-free Book sent to His servant (1–2) and the Book's words that the oceans could not write out (109). *Source: the "four tests" quartet is a homiletic organization — agent inference from `[18:7]` plus the narrative set; verify against classical thematic divisions.*
* **Task framing verified against the corpus (honest notes):** `[18:1]` reads "Praise be to Allah, who revealed the Book to His servant, and allowed in it no distortion" — the phrase "held upright" is not this edition's rendering (that belongs to translations of qayyiman; Itani puts "Valuable—" at 18:2). "Warn of severe punishment... deliver good news" is confirmed at `[18:2]`. The sleepers do "wake years later": sealed (`[18:11]`), awakened (`[18:12]`), misjudging the time as "a day, or part of a day" (`[18:19]`). The Moses episode is indeed `[18:60]`–`[18:82]` = ruku 9–10, at "the junction of the two rivers" (this edition renders *bahran* as "rivers" here; cf. "the two seas" at `[25:53]`, `[55:19]`). **Correction:** "My mercy encompasses all things" is NOT in al-Kahf — it is `[7:156]`; the surah's mercy-anchor near its pivot is `[18:58]` "Your Lord is the Forgiver, Possessor of Mercy" (with `[18:16]`, `[18:65]`, `[18:82]`, `[18:98]`). `[18:109]` confirmed verbatim: "If the ocean were ink for the words of my Lord, the ocean would run out..."

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Book and test (1–8):** praise of the revealed Book and its warn/glad-tidings office (1–5); the grieving messenger halted (6); the test axiom and the coming "barren waste" (7–8) — ornament given to be revoked.
* **The cave (9–31):** the wondrous sign told in truth (9–22): stand, flight, sealed ears, waking, discovery, monument dispute; the speech-etiquette insert (23–24) brackets the chronology coda (25–26); recite, keep humble company, choose your home (27–31).
* **Wealth and reckoning (32–49):** the two-garden parable (32–44); the debris parable and the lasting deeds (45–46); mountains moved, ranks presented, the enumerated book (47–49).
* **Refusal and respite (50–59):** Satan's archetype-defiance (50–51); partners who never answer (52–53); the Book's method vs. human argument (54–57); "the Forgiver, Possessor of Mercy" sets an appointed time (58–59).
* **Moses' road (60–82):** the journey to the junction (60–64); the apprentice covenant (65–70); three unendurable acts and their mercy-interpretation (71–82) — knowledge tested beyond reason.
* **The king and the close (83–110):** Zul-Qarnain's three courses and the dam (83–98); the surging Day (99–101); the losers, the Gardens, the inexhaustible words, the closing tawhid (102–110) — the surah seals with the same Book-claim it opened with.
