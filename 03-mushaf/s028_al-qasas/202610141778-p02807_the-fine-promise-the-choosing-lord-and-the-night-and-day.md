# The fine promise, the choosing Lord, and the night and day (28:61–75)
**UID:** 202610141778
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 28:61–75 (Ruku 7 of 8 in al-Qasas)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[28:61]` – `[28:75]` (15 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The weighing ruku: the fine-promise holder is not equal to the glitter-enjoyer dragged to arraignment; the Day's interrogation runs its script twice (62/74) around a creed-center — silent partners, disowning leaders, a Lord who creates and chooses, hidden and revealed equally known — and the sermon's final proof is meteorological: imagine the night or the day frozen forever, and the inventory terminates, as always, "that you may give thanks."
* **Placement:** Ruku 7 of 8; between the guidance-prerogative spine (28:56) and the Qarun case-study (28:76): doctrine stated as cosmology, framed as courtroom.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[28:61]`** "Can someone to whom We have made a fine promise—which he will attain—be equal to someone to whom We have given enjoyments in this world, but who will be, on Resurrection Day, among the arraigned?"
  * **Gloss:** The comparison opens with unequal currencies: "someone to whom We have made a fine promise—which he will attain" versus a world-enjoyer who on Resurrection Day stands "among the arraigned" — the court-word of `[36:32]` ("every single one of them, will be arraigned before Us").
* **`[28:62]`** "On the Day when He will call to them, and say, “Where are My associates whom you used to claim?”"
  * **Gloss:** Interrogation one: "Where are My associates whom you used to claim?" — the claim-graded verb; the same question-formula at `[41:47]` ("Where are My associates?") and `[16:27]` ("Where are My associates for whose sake you used to dispute?").
* **`[28:63]`** "Those against whom the sentence is justified will say, “Our Lord, these are they whom we misled. We misled them, as we were misled. We beg Your forgiveness; it was not us they used to worship.”"
  * **Gloss:** The prosecuted eat their own defense: "We misled them, as we were misled. We beg Your forgiveness; it was not us they used to worship" — leaders demoted to co-defendants (the disavowal-scene twin `[2:166]`); "the sentence is justified" verbatim-twins `[46:18]`.
* **`[28:64]`** "And it will be said, “Call on your partners.” And they will call on them, but they will not respond to them. And they will see the suffering. If only they were guided."
  * **Gloss:** The laboratory of idolatry: "Call on your partners" — silence; `[46:5]`'s definition ("those who will not answer him until the Day of Resurrection") performed on stage; "If only they were guided" — the wish-word's own prerogative, one verse's distance from 28:56.
* **`[28:65]`** "On the Day when He will call to them, and say, “What did you answer the Messengers?”"
  * **Gloss:** Interrogation two, from the other side of the messenger: "What did you answer the Messengers?" — the question `[5:109]` asks the messengers themselves ("What response were you given?") now asked of the populations.
* **`[28:66]`** "They will be blinded by the facts on that Day, and they will not question each other."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict of confusion: "They will be blinded by the facts on that Day, and they will not question each other" — the crowd that disowned (63) can no longer even interrogate; "blinded by the facts" is unique in the corpus.
* **`[28:67]`** "But he who repents, and believes, and does righteous deeds, may well be among the winners."
  * **Gloss:** The open door left in the courtroom: "But he who repents, and believes, and does righteous deeds, may well be among the winners" — winner-language of `[59:20]` and `[24:52]`; "may well" keeps the grammar hopeful, not certifying (Source: observable term choice in Itani).
* **`[28:68]`** "Your Lord creates whatever He wills, and He chooses. The choice is not theirs. Glory be to Allah, and exalted be He above the associations they make."
  * **Gloss:** The creed-keystone: "Your Lord creates whatever He wills, and He chooses. The choice is not theirs" — prerogative against every appointment-claim; the choosing-family `[2:105]` ("Allah chooses for His mercy whomever He wills"), `[22:75]` (messengers chosen), `[42:13]` (chosen to Himself); the transcendence-tune closes on the associates of 62.
* **`[28:69]`** "And your Lord knows what their hearts conceal, and what they reveal."
  * **Gloss:** Interior-omniscience: "And your Lord knows what their hearts conceal, and what they reveal" — verbatim identical to `[27:74]`, the previous surah's creed-passage: choice and knowledge (68–69) together rule the courtroom.
* **`[28:70]`** "And He is Allah. There is no god but He. To Him belongs all praise in this life, and in the next. And His is the decision, and to Him you will be returned."
  * **Gloss:** The doxology: "To Him belongs all praise in this life, and in the next. And His is the decision, and to Him you will be returned" — the decision-claim of `[82:19]` ("the decision on that Day is Allah's") and `[6:57]` ("the decision belongs solely to Allah"); the return-clause rings toward 28:88.
* **`[28:71]`** "Say, “Have you considered? Had Allah made the night perpetual over you until the Day of Resurrection, which god other than Allah will bring you illumination? Do you not hear?”"
  * **Gloss:** The night thought-experiment: "Had Allah made the night perpetual over you until the Day of Resurrection, which god other than Allah will bring you illumination?" — the illumination-word back to 28:43's Scripture-light; the test-audience: "Do you not hear?"
* **`[28:72]`** "Say, “Have you considered? Had Allah made the day perpetual over you until the Day of Resurrection, which god other than Allah will bring you night to rest in? Do you not see?”"
  * **Gloss:** The day mirrored: perpetual daylight with no rest — "which god other than Allah will bring you night to rest in?"; `[10:67]` "It is He who made the night for your rest, and the daylight for visibility" states the same design-argument indicatively.
* **`[28:73]`** "It is out of His mercy that He made for you the night and the day, that you may rest in it, and seek some of His bounty; and that you may give thanks."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict of design: "It is out of His mercy that He made for you the night and the day" — mercy behind meteorology, three purposes: rest, "seek some of His bounty" (the trade-verb of `[2:198]` "seeking bounty from your Lord"), and the terminal clause "that you may give thanks" — the gratitude-circuit the surah has been auditing since 28:58.
* **`[28:74]`** "On the Day when He will call to them, and say, “Where are My associates whom you used to claim?”"
  * **Gloss:** 28:62 replayed verbatim — the frame's second stroke: the interrogation is structural, not narrative; between its two panels the creed (68–73) was installed, and it is now re-tested by the same question.
* **`[28:75]`** "And We will draw out from every community a witness, and say, “Produce your evidence.” Then they will realize that the truth is Allah’s, and those they used to invent have forsaken them."
  * **Gloss:** The closing court: witnesses drawn "from every community" — `[16:89]` "We raise in every community a witness against them, from among them"; "Produce your evidence" verbatim from `[27:64]`; and the collapse: "the truth is Allah's, and those they used to invent have forsaken them" — `[46:28]` "In fact, they abandoned them."

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`associates`** `[28:62]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Where are My associates whom you used to claim?" — identical at 28:74; a two-stroke frame around the creed-center.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[41:47]`, `[16:27]` carry the same Day-question; 28:68's closing "exalted be He above the associations they make" completes the cluster (rg-verified: unique to s028).
* **`witness`** `[28:75]`:
  * **Rendering:** "And We will draw out from every community a witness, and say, 'Produce your evidence.'"
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:89]` near-verbatim twin; `[27:64]` "Produce your evidence, if you are truthful"; s028's own chain — unwitnessed revelation (28:44), witnessing God (28:28), drafted witnesses (28:75).
* **`chooses`** `[28:68]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Your Lord creates whatever He wills, and He chooses. The choice is not theirs."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:105]`, `[22:75]`, `[42:13]` — the choosing-verb belongs to God in election contexts; 28:68 generalizes the 28:56 formula ("whom He wills") into ontology.
* **`that you may give thanks`** `[28:73]`:
  * **Rendering:** "that you may rest in it, and seek some of His bounty; and that you may give thanks."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:152]` "And thank Me, and do not be ungrateful"; `[2:198]` "seeking bounty from your Lord" — the inventory→thanks circuit of the shukr note, run here on night and day instead of crops.
