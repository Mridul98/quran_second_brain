# The fire at the Mount and the tower of Hamaan (28:29–42)
**UID:** 202610141775
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 28:29–42 (Ruku 4 of 8 in al-Qasas)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[28:29]` – `[28:42]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The commission ruku: the contract term is completed, the fugitive's family is warmed by a fire that speaks first, Moses pleads his fear and Aaron's eloquence, and God attaches his brother to his arm — then the two face Pharaoh, who answers the bush with fired bricks and a tower to the sky, and the sea answers him; the drowned are counter-promoted to "leaders calling to the Fire."
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 8; closes the continuous Moses biography (1–42) and contains both scenes flagged in the task brief — the fire commission (29–35) and Pharaoh's tower, which sits at `[28:38]`, inside this ruku (not in a 36–43 block).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[28:29]`** "When Moses had completed the term, and departed with his family, he noticed a fire by the side of the Mount. He said to his family, “Stay here, I have glimpsed a fire. Perhaps I can bring you some information from there, or an ember from the fire, that you may warm yourselves.”"
  * **Gloss:** The term (28:27's eight/ten years) is completed; "a fire by the side of the Mount" is the scene, and the task-phrase "perhaps I can bring from it" is verified here in Itani's exact clause: "Perhaps I can bring you some information from there, or an ember from the fire" — practical hesitancy from a man with a wife and cargo, at an unfamiliar mountain.
* **`[28:30]`** "When he reached it, he was called from the right side of the valley, at the Blessed Spot, from the bush: “O Moses, it is I, Allah, the Lord of the Worlds."
  * **Gloss:** The fire speaks before he can offer anything: called "from the right side of the valley, at the Blessed Spot, from the bush"; the self-introduction is maximal — "it is I, Allah, the Lord of the Worlds" (the parallel account `[27:9]` says "Allah, the Almighty, the Wise"; `[20:12]` says "I—I am your Lord").
* **`[28:31]`** "Throw down your staff.” And when he saw it wiggling, as if it were possessed, he turned his back to flee, and did not look back. “O Moses, come forward, and do not fear, you are perfectly safe."
  * **Gloss:** The staff wriggles "as if it were possessed" and flight is reflexive (`[27:10]` renders the same moment "as though it were a demon"); the command-triple — come forward, do not fear, "you are perfectly safe" — reverses 28:18–21's "fearful and vigilant" posture (Source: structural observation on the corpus).
* **`[28:32]`** "Put your hand inside your pocket, and it will come out white, without blemish. And press your arm to your side, against fear. These are two proofs from your Lord, to Pharaoh and his dignitaries. They are truly sinful people.”"
  * **Gloss:** Two proofs issued against a regime: the white hand "without blemish" (verbatim twin `[27:12]` "Put your hand inside your pocket"; cf. `[20:22]` "press your hand to your side; it will come out white, without a blemish") and the arm pressed "against fear" — signs for a mission, "to Pharaoh and his dignitaries."
* **`[28:33]`** "He said, “My Lord, I have killed one of them, and I fear they will kill me."
  * **Gloss:** Moses pleads his record — the killing of 28:15 makes him a wanted man: "I have killed one of them, and I fear they will kill me"; twin-shaped clause `[26:14]` "so I fear they will kill me."
* **`[28:34]`** "And my brother Aaron, he is more eloquent than me, so send him with me, to help me, and to confirm my words, for I fear they will reject me.”"
  * **Gloss:** Aaron is requested for his eloquence — "he is more eloquent than me" (the only occurrence of "eloquent" in this corpus, rg-verified); the prophet names a weakness, and God does not rebuke it but supplies a partner.
* **`[28:35]`** "He said, “We will strengthen your arm with your brother, and We will give you authority, so they will not touch you. By virtue of Our signs, you and those who follow you will be the triumphant.”"
  * **Gloss:** The divine answer grants the request plus authority: "We will strengthen your arm with your brother" — echoed from Moses' own petition `[20:31]` "Strengthen me with him"; "you and those who follow you will be the triumphant," the victory-word `[23:111]` gives the persevering.
* **`[28:36]`** "But when Moses came to them with Our signs, clear and manifest, they said, “This is nothing but fabricated magic, and We never heard of this from our ancestors of old.”"
  * **Gloss:** Pharaoh's court reads evidence as theater: "fabricated magic" (phrase unique to this verse in the corpus) plus precedent-dismissal "We never heard of this from our ancestors of old" — the council-script verbatim-twin of `[23:24]` "We never heard of this from our forefathers of old."
* **`[28:37]`** "Moses said, “My Lord is well aware of him who brings guidance from Him, and him who will have the sequel of the abode. The wrongdoers will not succeed.”"
  * **Gloss:** Moses refuses the archaeology argument and files both parties under God's knowledge: him "who brings guidance from Him" versus him "who will have the sequel of the abode" — the sequel-word (uqba) doing its eschatological work as at `[13:35]` and `[6:135]`.
* **`[28:38]`** "Pharaoh said, “O nobles, I know of no god for you other than me. So fire-up the bricks for me O Hamaan, and build me a tower, that I may ascend to the God of Moses, though I think he is a liar.”"
  * **Gloss:** Task-verified, the tower located here: Pharaoh's counter-confession "I know of no god for you other than me" (79:24's "I am your Lord, the most high" in register) is followed by kiln-bricks and "build me a tower, that I may ascend to the God of Moses, though I think he is a liar" — twin `[40:36–37]`: "build me a tower... so that I may glance at the God of Moses; though I think he is lying."
* **`[28:39]`** "He and his troops acted arrogantly in the land, with no justification. They thought they would not be returned to Us."
  * **Gloss:** Diagnosis before verdict: arrogance "with no justification," grounded in denied return — "They thought they would not be returned to Us": the surah's return-theology (28:70, 28:88) stated in the negative; "acted arrogantly in the land" verbatim-twins `[29:39]`.
* **`[28:40]`** "So We seized him, and his troops, and We threw them into the sea. Observe, therefore, what was the end of the oppressors."
  * **Gloss:** The fear of 28:6 ("the very thing they feared") shown in full: "So We seized him, and his troops, and We threw them into the sea" — near-verbatim twin `[51:40]` "So We seized him and his troops, and threw them into the sea"; the imperative "Observe" is the surah's eyewitness-rhetoric.
* **`[28:41]`** "And We made them leaders calling to the Fire. And on Resurrection Day, they will not be saved."
  * **Gloss:** Counter-promotion: the same "leaders" 28:5 promised to the oppressed now labels the drowned — "leaders calling to the Fire"; the identical office, the opposite loadstar; "on Resurrection Day, they will not be saved."
* **`[28:42]`** "And We pursued them in this world with a curse. And on Resurrection Day, they will be among the despised."
  * **Gloss:** The curse runs twice: "in this world" pursued, and on Resurrection Day "among the despised" — the word belongs to the outcast (cf. `[7:18]` "Get out of it, despised", `[68:49]` "fully despised", `[12:32]` "one of the despised").

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`glimpsed a fire`** `[28:29]`:
  * **Rendering:** "I have glimpsed a fire. Perhaps I can bring you some information from there, or an ember from the fire" — the task's "perhaps I can bring from it" is precisely this Itani clause.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[27:7]` verbatim twin "When Moses said to his family, 'I have glimpsed a fire'" — same scene-word in the next surah's version.
* **`the bush / Blessed Spot`** `[28:30]`:
  * **Rendering:** "he was called from the right side of the valley, at the Blessed Spot, from the bush" — "bush" and "Blessed Spot" occur only here in the corpus (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:12]` locates the same scene as "the sacred valley of Tuwa"; `[27:8]` blesses the occupant instead — "Blessed is He who is within the fire" (one scene, three locative labels; Source: structural observation on the English corpus).
* **`two proofs`** `[28:32]`:
  * **Rendering:** "These are two proofs from your Lord, to Pharaoh and his dignitaries."
  * **Elsewhere:** the count appears only here; `[27:12]` carries the same hand-miracle verbatim and adds "among nine miracles to Pharaoh and his people."
* **`the triumphant`** `[28:35]`:
  * **Rendering:** "you and those who follow you will be the triumphant" — victory attached to the follower-set, not only the prophet.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[23:111]` "They are the ones who are the triumphant" — same English label for the patient of the Garden; the internal foil is "leaders" at 28:41, which `[28:5]` first gave to the oppressed.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the brick-firing and tower are classically read against real Egyptian building-corvée — the corpus supplies only the command (Source: agent inference — verify).
* **Corrections to the task brief, verified against this corpus:** (1) the clause "My Hearing and Seeing is with you" does **not** occur in s028; the corpus's assurance verse is `[20:46]` "Do not fear, I am with you, I hear and I see," and this ruku's assurance is 28:31 "you are perfectly safe." (2) Pharaoh's tower is at `[28:38]` — located, inside ruku 4 ✓.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** Hamaan as Pharaoh's minister-of-works is a transmitted identification; the corpus pairs him with Pharaoh at 28:6 and 28:8 and receives the brick-command here without a title (Source: classical reports — verify).
