# Median: the shade prayer and the eight-year contract (28:22–28)
**UID:** 202610141774
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 28:22–28 (Ruku 3 of 8 in al-Qasas)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[28:22]` – `[28:28]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The fugitive's prayer at a foreign well — "I am in dire need of whatever good you might send down" — is answered inside one day by work, protection, and a marriage contract drafted with a witness clause: the surah's most human ruku, where providence runs through hiring practices and father-in-law negotiations.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 8; closes the "flight" movement (14–28) and completes the biography's hidden years before the fire at the Mount (29); the same Median sojourn is recalled by Moses himself at `[20:40]` ("you stayed years among the people of Median").

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[28:22]`** "As he headed towards Median, he said, “Perhaps my Lord will guide me to the right way.”"
  * **Gloss:** The weakest possible claim to guidance from the most wanted man in Egypt — "perhaps" — and the surah's first post-flight dua; the itinerary is surrendered to the Guide (the phrase "the right way" is a corpus-wide target, see §3).
* **`[28:23]`** "And when he arrived at the waters of Median, he found there a crowd of people drawing water, and he noticed two women waiting on the side. He said, “What is the matter with you?” They said, “We cannot draw water until the shepherds depart, and our father is a very old man.”"
  * **Gloss:** Arrival as observation: he asks before he acts, and the answer is structural (crowd, elder father, two women held aside) — the injustice is small, daily, and unremarkable enough to need a passerby.
* **`[28:24]`** "So he drew water for them, and then withdrew to the shade, and said, “My Lord, I am in dire need of whatever good you might send down to me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's center: one unrequested favor, then deliberate withdrawal (he does not wait for payment), then the most open-ended petition in the corpus — "whatever good" (fa-za anzalta), a prayer that asks without specifying the shape of the answer.
* **`[28:25]`** "Then, one of the two women approached him, walking bashfully. She said, “My father is calling you, to reward you for drawing water for us.” And when he came to him, and told him the story, he said, “Do not fear, you have escaped from the wrongdoing people.”"
  * **Gloss:** The shade prayer is answered before sunset; the old man's first act is reassurance using 28:21's exact phrase ("wrongdoing people") — the stranger's vocabulary shows the prayer worked even before the story was told.
* **`[28:26]`** "One of the two women said, “Father, hire him; the best employee for you is the strong and trustworthy.”"
  * **Gloss:** The labor recommendation — two qualifications, strength (quwwa) and trust (amina): the woman who was pushed aside at the well now staffs the household's hiring decision; the word-roots are messenger-roots (see §3).
* **`[28:27]`** "He said, “I want to marry you to one of these two daughters of mine, provided you work for me for eight years. But if you complete ten, that is up to you. I do not intend to impose any hardship on you. You will find me, Allah willing, one of the righteous.”"
  * **Gloss:** The contract: marriage priced in labor, eight years as the obligation and ten as the voluntary extra ("that is up to you" — the more-able term offered without imposing it); the father seals it with "Allah willing" and a claim to his own righteousness.
* **`[28:28]`** "He said, “Let this be an agreement between you and me. Whichever of the two terms I fulfill, there shall be no reprisal against me; and Allah is witness over what we say.”"
  * **Gloss:** Moses accepts, fixes the anti-reprisal clause, and escalates the contract's witness beyond both parties — "Allah is witness over what we say": a marriage-license drafted like a covenant document.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`the right way`** `[28:22]`:
  * **Rendering:** "guide me to the right way" — the phrase-classical readers identify as *sawa' al-sabil*, the level middle road. *Source: agent inference on an English-only corpus — verify.*
  * **Elsewhere:** `[15:41]` "This is a right way with Me"; `[2:256]`, `[5:12]`, `[5:60]`, `[60:1]` carry the same English label "the right way" — note that the English corpus cannot confirm these are the same Arabic word (some likely render *sirat*, the guidance note's road-word); treat drift as open until print-checked.
* **`strength and trust`** `[28:26]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the best employee for you is the strong and trustworthy" — two qualifications, no lineage required.
  * **Elsewhere:** "trustworthy" is this edition's messenger-credential — `[26:178]` "I am to you a trustworthy messenger", `[7:68]` "a trustworthy adviser to you" — and the same English word carries `[2:256]`/`[31:22]` "the most trustworthy handle"; a shepherd's hiring note is written in prophetic vocabulary (word-family claims are English-level: rg "trustworthy" — verify against Arabic print).
* **`Allah is witness`** `[28:28]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Allah is witness over what we say."
  * **Elsewhere:** the witness-formula recurs across contract and testimony scenes (e.g., `[46:8]` "He is sufficient witness between me and you") — agreements written under observation behave differently than agreements written under law alone (Source: agent inference — verify).
* **`whatever good you send down`** `[28:24]`:
  * **Rendering:** "I am in dire need of whatever good you might send down to me" — the request leaves the shape of the answer blank.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:105]` "that any good should be sent down to you from your Lord" — the same English collocation for revealed/sent-down good; `[2:213]` "He sent down with them the Scripture" shows the "send down" verb doing revelation duty in this edition — so the shade-prayer hovers between bread and wahy (Source: observable term choice; agent gloss — verify).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[20:40]` — Ta-Ha's retrospective on this exact ruku: "And you stayed years among the people of Median. Then you came back, as ordained, O Moses" — the eight/ten years read there as tested years (also covers 28:7–13's sister line).
* **Within surah:** `[28:21]`'s "deliver me from the wrongdoing people" answered verbatim at `[28:25]` "you have escaped from the wrongdoing people" — the petition and its answer share the corpus's exact words.
* **Parallel:** `[7:142]` — the forty-nights schedule (thirty completed with ten) is the surah-neighbor's other Mosaic term-count; the ten-over-eight optional extra at 28:27 shares its shape, though the events differ (Source: structural observation; agent inference — verify).
* **Parallel:** `[46:8]` — "He is sufficient witness between me and you": the witness-over-agreement formula 28:28 invokes.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the contract casuistry (27–28) reads as the Quran teaching commerce and marriage terms through story, pre-legislation (Source: agent inference — verify).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the old man is classically identified as the priest of Median (Jethro/Shu'ayb in transmitted identifications); the corpus names him only as "their father... a very old man" — do not merge with the Shu'ayb narratives of 7/11/26 without checking (Source: classical reports — verify).
