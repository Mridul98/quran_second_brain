# The blow, the forgiveness, and the fearful flight (28:14–21)
**UID:** 202610141773
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 28:14–21 (Ruku 2 of 8 in al-Qasas)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[28:14]` – `[28:21]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The promised child grows into a young man who sins in haste, repents instantly, and is forgiven — then learns the cost of justice-work in a police state: one killing makes him the most wanted man in Egypt, and his exit is "fearful and vigilant," a fugitive's first hijrah.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 8; the bridge ruku between the restored family (1–13) and the Median exile (22–28); the surah's clearest single portrait of *a forgiven savior-of-a-moment* — and of Moses as a man with a temper God still uses.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[28:14]`** "And when he reached his maturity, and became established, We gave him wisdom and knowledge. Thus do We reward the virtuous."
  * **Gloss:** The divine resume: maturity + strength → *hikmah* and *'ilm*; the general rule "Thus do We reward the virtuous" (verbatim twin `[37:131]`) universalizes Moses' story mid-narrative — God's habit with the ihsan-people.
* **`[28:15]`** "Once he entered the city, unnoticed by its people. He found in it two men fighting—one of his own sect, and one from his enemies. The one of his sect solicited his assistance against the one from his enemies; so Moses punched him, and put an end to him. He said, “This is of Satan's doing; he is an enemy that openly misleads.”"
  * **Gloss:** One verse of combat and its verdict: the Israelite's own cry for help, the single punch, the death — and Moses' instant diagnosis ("of Satan's doing"). "Put an end to him" (fa-qadasa) is the corpus's euphemism — the blow is lethal without being relabeled murder by the text.
* **`[28:16]`** "He said, “My Lord, I have wronged myself, so forgive me.” So He forgave him. He is the Forgiver, the Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The shortest crime-to-pardon arc in the corpus: a wrong against *himself* (not against God's right), an ask, an immediate "So He forgave him" — and the name-pair *al-Ghafur al-Rahim* recorded as permanent character (`[42:5]`, `[46:8]`, `[10:107]`).
* **`[28:17]`** "He said, “My Lord, in as much as you have favored me, I will never be a supporter of the criminals.”"
  * **Gloss:** Gratitude converted into policy: the favor of forgiveness produces a vow of non-alignment with mujrimun — an oath the surah will echo at its close (`[28:86]` "do not be a supporter of the disbelievers").
* **`[28:18]`** "The next morning, he went about in the city, fearful and vigilant, when the man who had sought his assistance the day before was shouting out to him. Moses said to him, “You are clearly a troublemaker.”"
  * **Gloss:** The same city, the next sunrise, the same caller — and Moses' reading of him flips from victim to "manifest troublemaker" (ghawi): the sequel of the punch is a test of whether the impulse repeats.
* **`[28:19]`** "As he was about to strike the one who was their enemy, he said, “O Moses, do you intend to kill me, as you killed someone yesterday? You only want to be a bully in the land, and do not want to be a peacemaker.”"
  * **Gloss:** The would-be victim's accusation — the only voice in the ruku that charges Moses directly; "bully... not a peacemaker" (mala'un vs muslih) frames the day after the sin as a public-relations trial of an inner one.
* **`[28:20]`** "And a man came from the farthest part of the city running. He said, “O Moses, the authorities are considering killing you, so leave; I am giving you good advice.”"
  * **Gloss:** A named-anonymous ally (the classical tradition supplies identities; Source: transmitted reports — verify): the warn-and-flee motif, with the flight framed as good counsel — the adviser-word (nasiha) appears elsewhere for messengers (`[7:68]` "a trustworthy adviser to you") — here a stranger gives Moses what the regime's justice never will.
* **`[28:21]`** "So he left, fearful and vigilant. He said, “My Lord, deliver me from the wrongdoing people.”"
  * **Gloss:** "Fearful and vigilant" repeats verbatim from 28:18 — the posture that began the day becomes the posture of exile; and the two-word petition "deliver me from the wrongdoing people" is the Quran's stock escape-prayer (cf. `[66:11]`, `[7:47]`, `[23:28]`).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`hikmah + 'ilm` (wisdom and knowledge)** `[28:14]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We gave him wisdom and knowledge."
  * **Elsewhere:** the pair distributes widely (e.g., `[31:12]` Luqman "endowed with wisdom"); the corpus here binds both to maturity — competence as answered prayer (`[28:7]`'s messenger-promise arrives one stage at a time).
* **`ghafur rahim` (the Forgiver, the Merciful)** `[28:16]`:
  * **Rendering:** "He is the Forgiver, the Merciful."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[42:5]` "Allah is indeed the Forgiver, the Merciful"; `[46:8]`, `[10:107]` — the name-pair attached to finished acts of pardon, so 28:16 can stand as its proof-case.
* **`ista` (to take as support/ally)** — "I will never be a supporter of the criminals" `[28:17]`:
  * **Rendering:** "supporter" for the noun of one who makes himself a helper of the mujrimun.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[28:86]` "do not be a supporter of the disbelievers" (same construction inside this surah); the same verb roots the sanctuary-politics of `[5:51]` "do not take the Jews and the Christians as allies" — supporter-hood is a theology, not a feeling.
* **`fearful and vigilant`** `[28:18]`, `[28:21]`:
  * **Rendering:** fixed phrase "fearful and vigilant," repeated exactly twice in this ruku — the pairing occurs nowhere else in the corpus.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:56]` "we are a vigilant multitude" renders a different Arabic word (a separate root, rg-verifiable only by the shared English); the corpus fingerprint is s028's own doublet — the refugee's watchfulness, not an army's.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[26:19]` — Pharaoh, decades later, holds the killing up to Moses' face: "And you committed that deed you committed, and you were ungrateful" — the same unnamed "deed," recalled by the other side, in the Shu'araa retelling.
* **Parallel:** `[7:23]` — Adam's and Moses' repentances share grammar: "we have done wrong to ourselves" / "I have wronged myself" — the Quran's standard first-person sin-formula (near-verbatim; singular/plural shift is the speakers' number).
* **Within surah:** `[28:14]`'s "Thus do We reward the virtuous" = `[37:131]` verbatim; `[28:17]`'s non-supporter vow answered at `[28:86]`; the flight of 28:21 lands in `[28:22]` "As he headed towards Median."
* **Parallel:** `[40:24]` — "To Pharaoh, Hamaan, and Quaroon. But they said, 'A lying sorcerer'": the regime named at 28:6/8/15 will answer the *commissioned* Moses in exactly these words (28:36).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the ruku's hijrah-shaped exit reads naturally to an audience about to emigrate — the fugitive's "fearful and vigilant" as prototype (Source: agent inference — verify).
