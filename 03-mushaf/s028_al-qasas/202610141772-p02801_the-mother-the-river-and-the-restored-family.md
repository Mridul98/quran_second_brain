# The mother, the river, and the restored family (28:1–13)
**UID:** 202610141772
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 28:1–13 (Ruku 1 of 8 in al-Qasas)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[28:1]` – `[28:13]` (13 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's title-word does its work here: God narrates the Moses–Pharaoh history "in truth" as evidence for believers, and the first act of that truth is a promise dismantling a genocide — the slaughtered son is picked up by the slaughterer's house, nursed by his own mother, and returned to her lap, "that she may be comforted, and not grieve, and know that Allah's promise is true."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 8; opens the eight-ruku continuous Moses biography (1–42) and plants the promise-thread the surah will pull at 28:61 and 28:85.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[28:1]`** "Ta, Seen, Meem."
  * **Gloss:** Untranslated letter-cluster (see Fleeting note); the same "Ta, Seen, Meem" opens Ash-Shu'ara `[26:1]`, and `[27:1]` shortens it — this edition leaves the letters unrendered throughout.
* **`[28:2]`** "These are the Verses of the Clear Book."
  * **Gloss:** The Quran's self-designation as *mubin* — making things clear — immediately before a story that will be told with maximum clarity; verbatim twin of `[12:1]`.
* **`[28:3]`** "We narrate to you from the history of Moses and Pharaoh—in truth—for people who believe."
  * **Gloss:** The qasas formula (also `[7:101]`, `[11:120]`, `[12:3]`, `[20:99]`) with a truth-claim and an address-list: this narration is for believers — a criterion the ruku will exercise on Pharaoh's household.
* **`[28:4]`** "Pharaoh exalted himself in the land, and divided its people into factions. He persecuted a group of them, slaughtering their sons, while sparing their daughters. He was truly a corrupter."
  * **Gloss:** The villain thesis: exaltation + divide-and-slaughter; the infanticide is stated verbatim, the crime the whole ruku reverses. "Exalted himself" (ista-kbara) sets the surah's counter-value that 28:83 finally names.
* **`[28:5]`** "But We desired to favor those who were oppressed in the land, and to make them leaders, and to make them the inheritors."
  * **Gloss:** The divine counter-will in three stages — favor, leadership (a'imma), inheritance (warithun): oppressed → imams → heirs; the grammar of reversal is God's "desire" (aradna), not human strategy.
* **`[28:6]`** "And to establish them in the land; and to show Pharaoh, Hamaan, and their troops, the very thing they feared."
  * **Gloss:** Establishment in the same "land" Pharaoh exalted himself in; the triad Pharaoh–Hamaan–troops is named as the target of a fear coming true from the side it was feared (the risen slave-child).
* **`[28:7]`** "We inspired the mother of Moses: “Nurse him; then, when you fear for him, cast him into the river, and do not fear, nor grieve; We will return him to you, and make him one of the messengers.”"
  * **Gloss:** Wahy — the same verb as prophetic inspiration (`[16:123]`, `[42:7]`; twin of `[20:38]`) — delivered to an unnamed woman: a command (cast), a comfort (do not fear, nor grieve), and a double promise (return + messengerhood), both kept inside this ruku.
* **`[28:8]`** "Pharaoh’s household picked him up, to be an opponent and a sorrow for them. Pharaoh, Hamaan, and their troops were sinners."
  * **Gloss:** The river delivers the child to the palace itself; divine narration states the outcome ("opponent and sorrow") before the characters foresee anything — irony as dramatic engine.
* **`[28:9]`** "Pharaoh’s wife said, “An eye's delight for me and for you. Do not kill him; perhaps he will be useful to us, or we may adopt him as a son.” But they did not foresee."
  * **Gloss:** A woman of the palace stays the executioner's hand — the slaughter machine jammed from within; "But they did not foresee" closes the verse on the difference between her hope and God's certainty. This same wife is named and prayed-for at `[66:11]` (Source: corpus cross-reference; her title "Asiya" comes from transmitted reports — verify).
* **`[28:10]`** "The heart of Moses’ mother became vacant. She was about to disclose him, had We not steadied her heart, that she may remain a believer."
  * **Gloss:** The promise and the pain run in the same breast: her heart "became vacant" (empty/void), nearly betraying the secret ("disclose him"); the same steadying (rabata) that answers 28:5's plan keeps her faith intact — feeling is not failure.
* **`[28:11]`** "She said to his sister, “Trail him.” So she watched him from afar, and they were unaware."
  * **Gloss:** Surveillance as motherhood: the sister posted at a distance — the setup for the ruku's pivot line.
* **`[28:12]`** "We forbade him breastfeeding at first. So she said, “Shall I tell you about a family that can raise him for you, and will look after him?”"
  * **Gloss:** God refusals arrange God returns: the baby rejects every wet-nurse, so the little girl can propose the exact right "family" — the palace hires his own mother. Note: this edition says "a family that can raise him," not the "nurse" phrasing of other translations (twin `[20:40]` "Shall I tell you about someone who will take care of him?").
* **`[28:13]`** "Thus We returned him to his mother, that she may be comforted, and not grieve, and know that Allah’s promise is true. But most of them do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The promise of 28:7 is paid in full — return, comfort, no grief, knowledge; "But most of them do not know" recurs verbatim-pattern at 28:57 and frames the whole surah's epistemic split.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`al-Kitab al-Mubin` (the Clear Book)** `[28:2]`:
  * **Rendering:** "the Clear Book" — Itani's fixed rendering of *mubin* here.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:1]` verbatim twin "These are the Verses of the Clear Book"; `[11:6]` and `[27:75]` use "Clear Book" for the heavenly register — the same clarity claimed both above and in the recited book.
* **`qasas` (history/stories)** `[28:3]`:
  * **Rendering:** "from the history of Moses and Pharaoh—in truth."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:101]` "We narrate to you some of their tales"; `[12:3]` "the most accurate history"; `[11:120]` "Everything We narrate to you... is to strengthen your heart therewith" — narration as truth-claim and heart-strengthening, not entertainment.
* **`wahy` (inspiration)** `[28:7]`:
  * **Rendering:** "We inspired the mother of Moses."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:38]` "When We inspired your mother with the inspiration" (verbatim twin); `[16:123]` "Then We inspired you"; `[42:7]` "Thus We inspired you with an Arabic Quran" — the verb ranges from infant-mother to Prophet, one channel.
* **slaughtering the sons** `[28:4]`:
  * **Rendering:** "slaughtering their sons, while sparing their daughters."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[14:6]` "slaughtering your sons while sparing your daughters" (near-verbatim); `[2:49]` and `[7:141]` render the same event "killing your sons and sparing your women" — the Israelite memory-recitations of this surah's opening crime.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[20:38–40]` — Ta-Ha's first-person retelling of the same ruku: inspiration to the mother, the river, the sister's proposal, "So We returned you to your mother, that she may be comforted" — the closest full twin in the corpus.
* **Parallel:** `[66:11]` — "the wife of Pharaoh... save me from Pharaoh and his works": the intercessor of 28:9 later counted among the believers' exemplars.
* **Parallel:** `[40:25]` — "Kill the sons of those who have believed with him": Pharaoh's slaughter reflex re-run against Moses' mission — the crime in 28:4 is a pattern, not a one-off.
* **Within surah:** `[28:5–6]`'s "leaders... inheritors" is answered by `[28:41]` "We made them leaders calling to the Fire" — the same noun (a'imma) on opposite sides of the verdict.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan — a period and a city where the public readout of "the history of Moses and Pharaoh" carried the surah's claim that the same God overthrows exalters (Source: classical lists — verify).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the river is classically read as the Nile, and Hamaan as Pharaoh's minister; the corpus itself supplies no names beyond "Pharaoh, Hamaan" (Source: agent inference from classical tafsir genre — verify).
