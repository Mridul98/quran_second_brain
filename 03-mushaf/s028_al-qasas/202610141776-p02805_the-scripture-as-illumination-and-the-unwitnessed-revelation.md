# The Scripture as illumination and the unwitnessed revelation (28:43–50)
**UID:** 202610141776
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 28:43–50 (Ruku 5 of 8 in al-Qasas)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[28:43]` – `[28:50]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The camera turns from the story to its reciter: the Scripture that came to Moses reaches a man who was at none of its scenes — not on the Western Side, not by the Mount, not in Median — so un-eyewitnessedness becomes the evidence; and when the demand for a Mosaic rerun arrives, the surah issues the comparative challenge (bring a book better-guided than both) and the diagnosis (refusal is appetite, not deficit).
* **Placement:** Ruku 5 of 8; the hinge between the Moses biography (1–42) and the community-weighing (51–75) — it converts the narrated history into an epistemic claim about this Book, in the surah's own remember/not-know vocabulary.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[28:43]`** "We gave Moses the Scripture after We had annihilated the previous generations; as an illumination for mankind, and guidance, and mercy, so that they may remember."
  * **Gloss:** The Torah's givenness stated as mercy-timing: given "after We had annihilated the previous generations; as an illumination for mankind, and guidance, and mercy, so that they may remember" — the chain "illumination for mankind, and guidance, and mercy" near-verbatim-twins `[45:20]`, where the same words describe the Quran; `[21:48]` gave Moses and Aaron "the Criterion, and illumination."
* **`[28:44]`** "You were not on the Western Side when We decreed the command to Moses, nor were you among the witnesses."
  * **Gloss:** Direct address to the Prophet, mid-biography: "You were not on the Western Side when We decreed the command to Moses, nor were you among the witnesses" — "Western Side" occurs only here in the corpus; the scene referred to is the commission just narrated (28:29–35).
* **`[28:45]`** "But We established many generations, and time took its toll on them. Nor were you among the people of Median, reciting Our revelations to them. But We kept sending messengers."
  * **Gloss:** The argument widens across time — "We established many generations, and time took its toll on them" (phrase unique here); "Nor were you among the people of Median" — the very town of ruku 3 (28:22–28) — "reciting Our revelations to them. But We kept sending messengers": secondhand chains are the ordinary way knowledge travels.
* **`[28:46]`** "Nor were you by the side of the Mount when We proclaimed. Rather, it was a mercy from your Lord, that you may warn people who received no warner before you, so that they may take heed."
  * **Gloss:** Third absence: "Nor were you by the side of the Mount when We proclaimed" — the site of 28:29–30 again; and the positive: "it was a mercy from your Lord, that you may warn people who received no warner before you" — the verbatim clause-twin `[32:3]` "to warn a people who received no warner."
* **`[28:47]`** "Otherwise, if a calamity befell them as a result of what their hands have perpetrated, they would say, “Our Lord, if only You had sent us a messenger, we would have followed Your revelations, and been among the believers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The excuse-defense explained: if calamity came "as a result of what their hands have perpetrated" (formula shared with `[30:36]` and `[42:48]`) they would protest "Our Lord, if only You had sent us a messenger" — near-verbatim twin `[20:134]`, where the same protest stands in for an un-sent messenger.
* **`[28:48]`** "But when the truth came to them from Us, they said, “If only he was given the like of what was given to Moses.” Did they not disbelieve in what was given to Moses in the past? They said, “Two works of magic backing one another.” And they said, “We are disbelieving in both.”"
  * **Gloss:** When truth arrives, they ask for a rerun: "If only he was given the like of what was given to Moses" — answered by their own history ("Did they not disbelieve in what was given to Moses in the past?") and by their own verdict, "Two works of magic backing one another" (phrase unique here). Source: classical tafsir pairs the "two" differently (Torah and Quran; Moses and Muhammad) — verify.
* **`[28:49]`** "Say, “Then bring a scripture from Allah, more conductive to guidance than both, and I will follow it, if you are truthful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The challenge is scaled to the demand: "bring a scripture from Allah, more conductive to guidance than both, and I will follow it, if you are truthful" — the comparative of the standing challenge-form ("produce a chapter like these" `[2:23]`; "produce a single chapter like it" `[10:38]`); the Prophet binds his own acceptance to it.
* **`[28:50]`** "But if they fail to respond to you, know that they follow their fancies. And who is more lost than him who follows his fancy without guidance from Allah? Allah does not guide the unjust people."
  * **Gloss:** Realism about hearts: refusal is diagnosed as appetite — "they follow their fancies... who is more lost than him who follows his fancy without guidance from Allah?" (the noun-cluster is unique here; cf. `[45:23]` "him who has taken his desire for his god") — and the closed formula "Allah does not guide the unjust people" verbatim at `[3:86]`, `[9:19]`, `[9:109]`, `[46:10]`.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`illumination`** `[28:43]`:
  * **Rendering:** "as an illumination for mankind, and guidance, and mercy, so that they may remember."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[45:20]` near-verbatim "This is an illumination for mankind, and guidance, and mercy for people who believe with certainty"; `[21:48]` "the Criterion, and illumination"; within s028 the word returns as the light 28:71 asks which god "will bring you."
* **`warner`** `[28:46–47]`:
  * **Rendering:** "that you may warn people who received no warner before you, so that they may take heed."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[32:3]` verbatim clause "to warn a people who received no warner"; `[34:34]` and `[43:23]` "We sent no warner"; `[5:19]` answers the no-preacher complaint.
* **`fancies`** `[28:50]`:
  * **Rendering:** "they follow their fancies. And who is more lost than him who follows his fancy without guidance from Allah?"
  * **Elsewhere:** unique noun-cluster in this corpus; siblings `[45:23]` "taken his desire for his god" and `[6:116]` "They follow nothing but assumptions."
* **`witnesses`** `[28:44]`:
  * **Rendering:** "nor were you among the witnesses."
  * **Elsewhere:** the petition of `[3:53]`/`[5:83]` "count us among the witnesses" asks to be where 28:44 says the reciter was not; `[28:75]` will draft the Day's witnesses — the surah's chain runs unwitnessed → witnessing God (28:28) → drafted witnesses.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the second-person passages (44–46) are read as answering the charge that the Muhammadan Moses-narrative is secondhand reportage — the ruku inverts the charge: the un-eyewitnessed book is the proof (Source: classical tafsir — verify).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** "Two works of magic backing one another" — referents disputed already in the classical literature (Moses and Muhammad; the Torah and the Quran) (Source: classical tafsir — verify).
