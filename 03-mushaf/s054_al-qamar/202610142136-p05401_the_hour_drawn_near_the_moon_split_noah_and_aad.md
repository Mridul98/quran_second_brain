# The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split: Noah's eyes, Aad's wind, and the refrains installed (54:1–22)
**UID:** 202610142136
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 54:1–22 (Ruku 1 of 3 in al-Qamar)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[54:1]` – `[54:22]` (22 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens with a sign refused ("whenever they see a miracle… 'Continuous magic'," 2) and then demonstrates the counter-move: two destructions are staged — Noah's flood inside an ark "Sailing before Our eyes" (9–15) and Aad's "screaming wind" (18–21) — each sealed by the court-question "So how were My punishment and My warnings?" and reopened as the invitation "We made the Quran easy…" (17, 22), so that the surah's own hearing is put on trial beside the drowned and the blown-away.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 3; installs both refrains that structure the whole surah, and its 6–8 Caller-Day material pre-runs the Hour that returns at 46.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[54:1]`** "The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split."
  * **Gloss:** Two facts in one breath: proximity (the Hour) and a cracked sky (the moon) — either as sign demanded-or as eschatological physics; the corpus leaves the join unexplained, which is the point. Twin: `[21:1]` "Mankind's reckoning has drawn near, but they turn away heedlessly."
* **`[54:2]`–`[54:3]`** "Yet whenever they see a miracle, they turn away, and say, 'Continuous magic.' / They lied, and followed their opinions, but everything has its time."
  * **Gloss:** The reflex diagnosed in present tense ("whenever"): every miracle re-labelled *sihr* that never lapses; opinion-following named as the epistemic vice; "everything has its time" bounds the mockery — the deadline-note the whole ruku will cash out. (Task's "every matter is settled" is this clause in another translation, or `[54:49]`.)
* **`[54:4]`–`[54:5]`** "And there came to them news containing a deterrent. / Profound wisdom—but warnings are of no avail."
  * **Gloss:** "News" (anba', the destruction reports coming at 9+) is *ḥikmah bālighah* — but the verse states its own possible failure: warnings that avail nothing where they land. The surah's chilling self-limitation, placed before the sermon.
* **`[54:6]`–`[54:7]`–`[54:8]`** "So turn away from them. On the Day when the Caller calls to something terrible. / Their eyes humiliated, they will emerge from the graves, as if they were swarming locusts. / Scrambling towards the Caller, the disbelievers will say, 'This is a difficult Day.'"
  * **Gloss:** The command to stop arguing, and a cut-scene: the Caller, the graves, locust-swarm imagery, eyes down — eschatology as crowd-behavior; "difficult Day" (yaumun 'asir) answers the easy invitation that arrives at 17.
* **`[54:9]`–`[54:10]`** "Before them the people of Noah disbelieved. They rejected Our servant, and said, 'Crazy,' and he was rebuked. / So he appealed to his Lord, 'I am overwhelmed, so help me.'"
  * **Gloss:** Case 1 opens on the same two crimes as verse 3 (rejection + name-calling — here "Crazy"; cf. the Quraysh's "bewitched" language at `[17:47]`); Noah's prayer is the most desperate sentence in the prophets' roll-call — "I am overwhelmed."
* **`[54:11]`–`[54:12]`** "So We opened the floodgates of heaven with water pouring down. / And We made the earth burst with springs, and the waters met for a purpose already destined."
  * **Gloss:** Deliver from above and below simultaneously; the meeting of waters is framed as appointment — "a purpose already destined" — the decree-grammar that returns at 49–50. Twin scene: `[11:40]` (the volcano at "Our command came").
* **`[54:13]`–`[54:14]`** "And We carried him on a craft of planks and nails. / Sailing before Our eyes; a reward for him who was rejected."
  * **Gloss:** The ark in crude materiality ("planks and nails") — and the strangest audit: the voyage is "a reward for him who was rejected," i.e., the ship is payment to the one his people threw out (cf. `[11:37]` "build the Ark, under Our eyes").
* **`[54:15]`** "And We left it as a sign. Is there anyone who would take heed?"
  * **Gloss:** The first refrain-echo: the ark left as a surviving *sign* — and the open question, same closing-form as 17/22/32/40/51 minus the "Quran" clause (*the shared Arabic question-phrase behind them is recitation-tradition knowledge — verify in print*).
* **`[54:16]`–`[54:17]`** "So how were My punishment and My warnings? / We made the Quran easy to learn. Is there anyone who would learn?"
  * **Gloss:** Court-question #1 (rhetorical: the answer is the story just told) + ease-refrain #1. Itani runs with the four refrains' verbs differently — learn (17), remember (22), understand (32), memorize (40) — four English registers; *Source: translator's choice, observable in corpus; the single repeated Arabic formula behind them is recitation-tradition knowledge — verify in print.*
* **`[54:18]`–`[54:19]`–`[54:20]`** "Aad denied the truth. So how were My punishment and My warnings? / We unleashed upon them a screaming wind, on a day of unrelenting misery. / Plucking the people away, as though they were trunks of uprooted palm-trees."
  * **Gloss:** Case 2 in three lines: denial stated *and* refrained inside one verse (18 carries both the docket and the question — unique among the five); the wind "screaming" (same rendering as `[41:16]` "a screaming wind"); bodies felled like palm-trunks — the image twin of `[69:7]` "stumps of hollow palm-trees."
* **`[54:21]`–`[54:22]`** "So how were My punishment and My warnings? / We made the Quran easy to remember. Is there anyone who would remember?"
  * **Gloss:** Refrain pair #2 closes Aad; "remember" is the refrain's own native verb — the Book is easy *to remember*, which is how a surah this incantatory proves the claim.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sa'ah` (the Hour):** 1, 6-Day, 46.
  * **Rendering:** "The Hour has drawn near" (1); "their appointed time" (46, ruku 3).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[21:1]` "has drawn near… they turn away heedlessly" — the same proximity-motif; `[53:57]` "The inevitable is imminent" — the sibling surah's version.
* **`sihr` (magic):** 2 "Continuous magic" (siḥr mustamirr — magic with no intermission).
  * **Elsewhere:** the charge-cluster: `[20:63]` "These two are magicians," `[52:15]` "Is this magic, or do you not see?" — the accusation that inverts into the surah's theme (what they call magic is the *easy* Quran).
* **`āyah / sign / miracle`:** same root twice in eight verses, two renderings: "a miracle" (2), "a sign" (15).
  * **Elsewhere:** translator's double register observable in-corpus; the miracle-demand they answer (20:133 "Why does he not bring us a miracle…?") is the exact reflex this ruku narrates.
* **`nudhur` (warnings):** 5, 16, 18, 21; the series claim of `[53:56]` ("just like the first warnings") here becomes plural and personal — "My warnings."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[53:56]`; the ease-refrain's object ("remember") is the same duty the warnings ask.
