# Triumph, the silenced courtroom, and the Day of Reality (78:31–40)
**UID:** 202610142263
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 78:31–40 (Ruku 2 of 2 in an-Naba’)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[78:31]`–`[78:40]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku inverts ruku 1's sentence: the same "fitting" word that priced Hell's drink now prices the Garden's gift (78:26 vs 78:36); the Garden is then framed as a courtroom where the Spirit and the angels stand in row and no one speaks without permission (78:37–38), and the surah closes by handing the reader a door and a warning — "So whoever wills, let him take a way back to his Lord" (78:39), "the Day when a person will observe what his hands have produced" (78:40).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 2 and the surah's terminus. Ruku 1 argued the Event from favors; this unit argues it from outcomes, then from sovereignty, then from the reader's own will — the shortest of the two ruku, but the one that names the Day "the Day of Reality."

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[78:31]`** "But for the righteous there is triumph."
  * **Gloss:** the counter-scene opens on the outcome-word the neighbouring surahs qualify ("the clear triumph" 45:30, "the supreme triumph" 37:60 — see §3); by 78:36 the address has shifted to "your Lord."
* **`[78:32]`** "Gardens and vineyards." **`[78:33]`** "And splendid spouses, well matched." **`[78:34]`** "And delicious drinks."
  * **Gloss:** four line-items answering the revoked favors of 78:14–16 and the deprivations of 78:24–25: water→vineyards, pairs→matched spouses, the day's livelihood→drinks. The nearest twin is 52:20, whose English also presents spouses as a granted line-item ("We will couple them with gorgeous spouses").
* **`[78:35]`** "They will hear therein neither gossip, nor lies."
  * **Gloss:** the negative definition of the Garden's atmosphere — speech purified, where 78:29's counted record was speech of indictment; cf. 52:23's cup "wherein is neither harm, nor sin."
* **`[78:36]`** "A reward from your Lord, a fitting gift."
  * **Gloss:** the hinge: the same English adjective as 78:26's "A fitting requital" now prices the gift — one measure, opposite direction (lexical caveat in §3); the source is named "your Lord," the surah's first direct divine-address to the righteous.
* **`[78:37]`** "Lord of the heavens and the earth, and everything between them—The Most Merciful—none can argue with Him."
  * **Gloss:** the sentence's jurisdiction is stated before the scene: total lordship, mercy named inside the sovereignty, and the closing clause removes litigation — "none can argue with Him."
* **`[78:38]`** "On the Day when the Spirit and the angels stand in row. They will not speak, unless it be one permitted by the Most Merciful, and he will say what is right."
  * **Gloss:** the courtroom's choreography: standing ranks, and speech only on permission — the permitted speaker "will say what is right"; cf. 20:109 and 53:26, where intercession requires the same permission.
* **`[78:39]`** "That is the Day of Reality. So whoever wills, let him take a way back to his Lord."
  * **Gloss:** the Day is renamed "of Reality" (al-ḥaqq), and the whole dispute of 78:1–3 is resolved into an address to the will: a "way back" — the surah's final imperative, and the standard will-cluster formula (73:19, 76:29, 25:57).
* **`[78:40]`** "We have warned you of a near punishment—the Day when a person will observe what his hands have produced, and the faithless will say, “O, I wish I were dust.”"
  * **Gloss:** the warner's own claim ("We have warned you"), the punishment qualified as "near," and the last human line in the surah is the denier's regret: to be dust — the very state whose reversal they denied (50:3, 37:16, 27:67).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`fawz` (triumph)** — 78:31 "But for the righteous there is triumph."
  * **Rendering:** bare "triumph" here; the corpus elsewhere qualifies it: "the clear triumph" `[45:30]`, "the supreme triumph" `[37:60]`, "the great triumph" `[85:11]`, `[57:12]` (all verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** the word's pairing with gardens in `[45:30]` and `[57:12]` is the same collocation as 78:31–32.
* **"fitting" (the edition's rendering-pair)** — 78:26 "A fitting requital." / 78:36 "a fitting gift."
  * **Rendering:** one English word, twice, straddling the two ruku — "fitting requital" for Hell, "fitting gift" for the Garden. rg-verified: "fitting" occurs only in these two lines plus `[19:92]` ("It is not fitting for the Most Merciful to have a son"), an unrelated sense. Because the corpus is translation-only, lexical identity of the two Arabic nouns cannot be verified from it — **Source: agent inference on Itani's word-choice — verify**.
  * **Elsewhere:** whichever the Arabic, the effect of the shared English adjective is that reward and punishment are both stated as *adequate* — the shukr note's audit framing, run in both directions.
* **`ar-Rūḥ` (the Spirit)** — 78:38 "On the Day when the Spirit and the angels stand in row."
  * **Rendering:** "the Spirit" paired with "the angels" (also `[97:4]` "In it descend the angels and the Spirit, by the leave of their Lord, with every command" — verified; 42:52 uses "spirit" for revealed inspiration, a different referent).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:192–193]` "the Honest Spirit" as revelation-bearer (k-kalam's line) — the same term doing two jobs: descending with the Book, standing in the Day's ranks.
* **`ma'āb` (return / destination)** — 78:39 "let him take a way back to his Lord."
  * **Rendering:** "a way back"; the same English slot at 78:22 renders Hell as "a destination" for the oppressors — one word, two directions (Source: agent inference on Itani's word-choice in 78:22/78:39 — verify, the corpus is translation-only).
  * **Elsewhere:** the idiom is the corpus's standard closer: "let him take a path to his Lord" `[73:19]`, `[76:29]`; "may take a path to his Lord" `[25:57]` (verified); harder twin at `[18:29]` "Whoever wills—let him believe. And whoever wills—let him disbelieve".

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (surah-level consensus). The unit contains no legal material; its horizon is the reckoning.
* **The "near punishment":** the text says only that the warning is of "a near punishment" (78:40). Classical readers split on whether nearness means the Hour's certainty, the Day of Badr, or each person's death. **Source: classical tafsir disagreement (verify against Ibn Kathir / al-Tabari on 78:40)** — recorded here as an open crux, not resolved.
* **Identity of "the Spirit" (78:38):** the corpus line pairs it with "the angels," which marks it as distinct from them. **Source: classical reports identify it as Jibril or as a created being apart; the identification is not in the text — verify.**
