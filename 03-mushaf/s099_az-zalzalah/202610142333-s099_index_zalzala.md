## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** DISPUTED like 98 — transmitted lists mostly assign az-Zalzalah to the Medinan period, while a transmitted minority reads it Meccan; nothing in the 8 verses decides it internally (no addressee-community is named). Labeled as found. *Source: transmitted type-lists — verify.* Revelation order: **transmitted nuzul tables — verify** (corpus asserts no nuzul; unnumbered Basmala heads the surah).
* **Size:** 8 ayahs, 1 ruku (ruku_map global 541 — verified). No divine name "Allah" in any numbered verse: the entire governance of the event runs on the anaphoric "your Lord" (99:5) — the single Lord-mention steers earth, humanity, and the ledger alike (rg-verified count).
* **Title:** az-Zalzala — corpus header "THE QUAKE" tracks 99:1 "shaken with its quake." English "quake" in this edition also carries the destroyed-nations reports `[7:78]`/`[7:91]` and the "Day when the Quake quakes" `[79:6]` (rg-verified family); whether all three sites share one Arabic word (zalzala/rajfa/ṣayḥa) is invisible in an English corpus — *verify against Arabic before asserting a word-family.*
* **Central theme:** The earth becomes a person: shaken, parturient, questioned ("What is the matter with it?"), and finally SPEAKING — "it will tell its tales," empowered only because "your Lord will have inspired it" (99:4–5). People then emerge in droves to be shown their works (99:6), and the surah closes the smallest ledger ever drawn: the atom's weight of good and of evil, seen (99:7–8).
* **Reading hint:** watch who has speech in this surah: only the earth gets a verb of telling; humanity gets a verb of bewildered question (99:3) and then a verb of being shown (99:6) — the planet testifies while the witnesses are exhibited.

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Convulsion and expulsion (99:1–2):** "is shaken with its quake… brings out its loads" — the ground moves and empties itself; both verbs passive-events under "When."
* **The human question (99:3):** "And man says, 'What is the matter with it?'" — the only human line in the surah, and it is an exclamation of ignorance.
* **The earth's answer and its cause (99:4–5):** "On that Day, it will tell its tales. For your Lord will have inspired it" — speech attributed to geology, theology the efficient cause (ilhām/waḥy verb — see passage §3).
* **The human scene (99:6):** second "On that Day": people "emerge in droves, to be shown their works" — the frame pivots from speaking earth to exhibited humanity.
* **The atomic doublet (99:7–8):** two mirror-verses — good and evil at atom-scale, both "will see it" — the surah's whole judgment-grammar in twelve words each (Source: structural observation on corpus renderings).

## 5b. Batch-Return Verification Flags (this index)
* **Hint renderings corrected:** "what is wrong with it" → "**What is the matter with it?**" (99:3); "it tells its news" → "it will tell its **tales**" (99:4); "your Lord reveals to it" → "For your Lord will have **inspired** it" (99:5); "people scattered in groups" → "the people will **emerge in droves**" (99:6).
* **Atom-weight doublet VERIFIED:** "Whoever has done an atom's weight of good will see it. / And whoever has done an atom's weight of evil will see it." (99:7–8, exact).
* **"in droves" exact-phrase twin:** `[78:18]` "you will come in droves" — the pair 99:6/78:18 is corpus-exclusive (rg-verified).
* **Unique coinages verified:** "brings out its loads" (99:2) and "tell its tales" (99:4) occur nowhere else in the corpus (rg-verified) — the earth's twin verbs, emptying and speaking, are az-Zalzala's own diction in this English.
* **"On that Day" frame ×2:** 99:4 and 99:6 — the twin framing that splits the surah between earth-speech and human-exhibition (rg-verified within file).
* **Header artifact note:** unnumbered "Basmala:" line above 99:1 as in every corpus file; ayah keys unaffected.
* **Quake-family caution:** the English "quake" cluster (99:1, 7:78, 7:91, 79:6, plus 23:60 "hearts quake") is a RENDERING family, not a verified Arabic-word family — flagged for Arabic check before any "same root" claim.
