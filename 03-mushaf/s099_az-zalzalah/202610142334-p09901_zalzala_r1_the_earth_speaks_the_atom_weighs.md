# The earth tells its tales because her Lord inspired it; humanity sees its atoms (99:1–8)
**UID:** 202610142334
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 99:1–8 (Ruku 1 of 1 in az-Zalzalah)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[99:1]` – `[99:8]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** A single Day is narrated twice over — first from the earth's side (shaken, emptying its loads, and given speech to tell its tales by inspiration from her Lord), then from humanity's side (emerging in droves to be shown their works) — and the unit closes on the micro-ledger: every atom's weight of good and of evil will be SEEN, which makes the planet's autobiography and the person's ledger one document.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1. The batch's first pure eschatology-surah: 98 named the Evidence; 99 shows the cosmos compelled to testify; 100 will take the witness-stand inward to the human heart.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[99:1]`** "When the earth is shaken with its quake."
  * **Gloss:** The temporal "When" opens without an addressee; "its quake" (internal cognate accusative — zalzalatu-hā) makes the earth's convulsion its OWN event, not a borrowed word.
* **`[99:2]`** "And the earth brings out its loads."
  * **Gloss:** Second verb, same subject: parturition — the ground expels "its loads" (*athqāl*, heavy things); what the loads are (the dead? buried secrets?) is left open in the English (see §3).
* **`[99:3]`** "And man says, “What is the matter with it?”"
  * **Gloss:** The only human speech-act in the surah: a bewildered question addressed TO the earth — the formula "What is the matter" is otherwise aimed at people (e.g., `[57:8]`, `[84:20]`, `[71:13]` — rg-verified); here the ground is the strange one.
* **`[99:4]`** "On that Day, it will tell its tales."
  * **Gloss:** First "On that Day" frame: the earth becomes NARRATOR — "its tales" (akhbāruhā, its news/reports) — creation's autobiography delivered on the Day.
* **`[99:5]`** "For your Lord will have inspired it."
  * **Gloss:** The causal clause (fa-inna) grounds the impossible speech: divine INSPIRATION (awḥā) — the same verb used of the bee, Moses' mother, and the human self; "your Lord" is the surah's only Lord-mention (rg-count: 1).
* **`[99:6]`** "On that Day, the people will emerge in droves, to be shown their works."
  * **Gloss:** Second "On that Day": the camera flips to humans leaving the earth that just spoke — "in droves" (awālīj, scattered bands), purpose-clause: "to be shown their works" — the exhibits are their own deeds.
* **`[99:7]`** "Whoever has done an atom's weight of good will see it."
  * **Gloss:** The ledger's lower bound: *dharrat* — the dust-mote/atom unit — good side; "will see it": seeing = being shown = having it present, in one verb.
* **`[99:8]`** "And whoever has done an atom's weight of evil will see it."
  * **Gloss:** Mirror verse, evil side; the doublet's perfect symmetry (same size, same verb, opposite sign) is the surah's closing equalizer — no act under threshold.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`zalzala` ("its quake")**: the shaking named from itself.
  * **Rendering:** "is shaken with its quake" — cognate form kept literal.
  * **Elsewhere (English family, rg-verified):** `[79:6]` "On the Day when the Quake quakes"; `[7:78]`/`[7:91]` past nations seized by "the quake"; `[23:60]` hearts that "quake." Arabic identities (zalzala/rajfa/ṣayḥa) not visible in translation — *verify before root-claims.*
* **`athqāl` ("its loads")**: the earth's heavy contents.
  * **Rendering:** "brings out its loads" (99:2) — corpus-unique phrase (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere (concept-twins):** `[22:7]` "Allah will resurrect those in the graves"; `[36:51]` "they will rush from the tombs to their Lord" — the loads as the buried dead is the standard reading — *Source: consensus-type tafsir — verify.*
* **`awḥā` ("will have inspired it")**: non-prophetic divine address.
  * **Rendering:** "For your Lord will have inspired it" (99:5).
  * **Elsewhere (rg-verified family):** `[91:8]` the self "inspired… with its wickedness and its righteousness"; `[16:68]` the bee; `[28:7]` Moses' mother; `[42:51]` (k-kalam's modes-of-speech anchor). k-kalam does not yet cite 99:5 — the earth's inspired speech is an append candidate this passage carries.
* **`awālīj` ("in droves")**: emergent scattered groups.
  * **Rendering:** "emerge in droves" (99:6) — exact phrase twin ONLY `[78:18]` "you will come in droves" (rg-verified two-site pair).
* **`mithqāl dharrat` ("an atom's weight")**: the smallest ledger unit.
  * **Rendering:** "an atom's weight of good/evil" (99:7–8).
  * **Elsewhere (rg-verified):** `[4:40]` "Allah does not commit an atom's weight of injustice"; `[34:3]` nothing "an atom's weight… escapes His knowledge"; `[34:22]` partners possess no atom's weight. The sibling micro-unit is the mustard-seed: `[21:47]` (brought up on the scales) and `[31:16]` (Allah will bring it to light) — two weight-images for one doctrine, 21:47 bridging directly to 101's scales.
