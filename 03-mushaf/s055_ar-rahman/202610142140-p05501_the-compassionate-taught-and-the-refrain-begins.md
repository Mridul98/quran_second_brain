# The Compassionate taught — and the refrain begins (55:1–25)
**UID:** 202610142140
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 55:1–25 (Ruku 1 of 3 in ar-Rahman)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[55:1]` – `[55:25]` (25 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens with God's name standing as a whole verse — "The Compassionate." — and at once defines mercy as *teaching*: the Quran, the human, and clear expression; then the cosmos is exhibited in order (reckoning, prostration, the imposed balance) and the earth stocked with favors, until at `[55:13]` the refrain detonates and alternates with every remaining line: six times in this ruku, 31 times in the surah.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 3; the litany's engine starts here. The teaching-frame (1–4) is the surah's kalam anchor; the balance (7–9) joins cosmology to commerce; the address is already dual (jinn and humans implied — the "two" grammar: 17 "two Easts," 19 "two seas"; explicit by `[55:33]`).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[55:1]`** "The Compassionate." — **Gloss:** A bare Name as ayah 1 — the only surah opening on a divine name as its own verse (Source: structural observation on this corpus; "the only surah that opens with a name of Allah" per transmitted catalogs — verify in print). Itani renders ar-Rahman here "The Compassionate" though the basmala line renders the same word "the Gracious" — drift ledger in §3.
* **`[55:2]`** "Has taught the Quran." — **Gloss:** The Name's first predicate is *teaching His speech* — mercy = address (kalam).
* **`[55:3]`** "He created man." — **Gloss:** The second object of mercy: the human itself.
* **`[55:4]`** "And taught him clear expression." — **Gloss:** *Bayān* — articulation, the capacity to mean things aloud; man's creation completes only when he can answer the refrain. The task's "eloquent speech" is other translations' phrasing; this edition says "clear expression," corpus-unique (rg).
* **`[55:5]`** "The sun and the moon move according to plan." — **Gloss:** *ḥusbān* — reckoning/account; astronomy as arithmetic obedience. Twin: `[36:40]` "The sun is not to overtake the moon… Each floats in an orbit."
* **`[55:6]`** "And the stars and the trees prostrate themselves." — **Gloss:** *najm* and *shajar* in sujud — vegetation and sky-lights both bowing (the task's "herbs" reflects classical glosses of *najm* as plants without flowers; the translator chose "stars" — Source: classical lexicon vs translator's choice, verify in print). Near-verbatim witness: `[22:18]` "…the sun, and the moon, and the stars, and the mountains, and the trees… prostrates."
* **`[55:7]`** "And the sky, He raised; and He set up the balance." — **Gloss:** Lifting and leveling in one line: the *mīzān* is imposed right beside the raised heaven — cosmic justice and commercial justice are one grammar.
* **`[55:8]`** "So do not transgress in the balance." — **Gloss:** The first *command* of the surah is about weights — *baghy* (transgression) in measurement.
* **`[55:9]`** "But maintain the weights with justice, and do not violate the balance." — **Gloss:** Justice (*qisṭ*) in weighing, double-guarded; the measure-sermons of Shuaib echo here: `[7:85]`, `[11:85]`, `[17:35]` "weigh with accurate scales. That is fair…"
* **`[55:10]`** "And the earth; He set up for the creatures." — **Gloss:** Lowered for the *anām* — everything that crawls and lives; the stage for the favor-inventory.
* **`[55:11]`** "In it are fruits, and palms in clusters." — **Gloss:** Garden-fruit and date-palm; the inventory begins.
* **`[55:12]`** "And grains in the blades, and fragrant plants." — **Gloss:** *rayḥān* — the grain-sheath and the sweet-smelling; provision and pleasure in the same row. (The same word returns at `[56:89]` rendered "flowers" — drift ledger.)
* **`[55:13]`** "So which of your Lord’s marvels will you deny?" — **Gloss:** THE REFRAIN, 1st of 31 (rg-tallied). The *alā'* — bounties/marvels — interrogated; "deny" (*tukadhdhibūn*, call-lie) makes every denial of favor a lie told at the Giver: the k-shukr circuit (niʿma→shukr→kufr) as liturgy.
* **`[55:14]`** "He created man from hard clay, like bricks." — **Gloss:** The clay-materials of the species; *ījūn* "like bricks" — the task's "sounding clay" is other translations (cf. `[15:26]` "clay, from molded mud").
* **`[55:15]`** "And created the jinn from a fusion of fire." — **Gloss:** The other accountable species; *mārij* "a fusion of fire" (the task's "smokeless flame" is a classical gloss carried by other translations; "fusion" is corpus-unique here; kin: `[15:27]` "the jinn We created before, from piercing fire," and Iblis's own claim `[38:76]`).
* **`[55:16]`** "So which of your Lord’s marvels will you deny?" — **Gloss:** Refrain 2/31 — now welded to a *dual* audience: the two creations just named are the "you both" of the Arabic.
* **`[55:17]`** "Lord of the two Easts and Lord of the two Wests." — **Gloss:** Not one sunrise but two (east and west of winter/summer solstice — Source: classical astronomy-gloss, verify): sovereignty over the seasonal extremes. Plural twins: `[37:5]` "Lord of the Easts," `[70:40]` "I swear by the Lord of the Easts and the Wests"; the idiom of vast distance at `[43:38]`.
* **`[55:18]`** "So which of your Lord’s marvels will you deny?" — **Gloss:** Refrain 3/31.
* **`[55:19]`** "He merged the two seas, converging together." — **Gloss:** The adjoining waters in one press — near-verbatim twin `[25:53]` "he merged the two seas; this one fresh and sweet, and that one salty and bitter."
* **`[55:20]`** "Between them is a barrier, which they do not overrun." — **Gloss:** *barzakh* — the restraint inside contact; barrier-sites `[23:100]` (until resurrection), `[18:94]` (the great dam), and the partition at `[27:61]` which ends in the *counter-refrain*: "Is there another god with Allah?"
* **`[55:21]`** "So which of your Lord’s marvels will you deny?" — **Gloss:** Refrain 4/31.
* **`[55:22]`** "From them emerge pearls and coral." — **Gloss:** From the pressed pair, value: *lulū'* and *marjān*; "coral" is s055's only corpus site (rg) — and its word returns at `[55:58]` to describe the Garden's maidens: the sea's jewelry previews paradise's people. Pearls elsewhere: `[56:23]`, `[37:49]`, `[52:24]`, `[76:19]`.
* **`[55:23]`** "So which of your Lord’s marvels will you deny?" — **Gloss:** Refrain 5/31.
* **`[55:24]`** "His are the ships, raised above the sea like landmarks." — **Gloss:** Ships as standing signposts (*'ilām*); the same comparison at `[16:16]` for mountains-and-guidance, while `[42:32]` renders the ships' simile "like flags" — drift ledger. Sea-service ends in thanks everywhere: `[45:12]`, `[31:31]`, `[35:12]`.
* **`[55:25]`** "So which of your Lord’s marvels will you deny?" — **Gloss:** Refrain 6/31 closes the ruku — six strikes: total-favors, man, the seascape pair, the sea's wealth, the sea's transit. The litany is now the surah's heartbeat and will not stop until `[55:77]`.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ar-Rahman`**: the Name-as-title.
  * **Rendering:** "The Compassionate" (55:1, header "THE COMPASSIONATE") vs "the Gracious" in every basmala line — one Arabic word, two English faces in one edition.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:163]` "the Benevolent, the Compassionate"; Name-cluster sites s009, s052, s057, s059, s067 (rg).
* **`mīzān` (balance)**: 7–9 three times in three lines.
  * **Rendering:** "balance"; "weights with justice."
  * **Elsewhere:** the measure-sermons `[7:85]`, `[11:85]`, `[17:35]`; idiom-shift at `[5:77]` "the balanced way" — the word-form "balance" appears in the corpus only here and there (rg).
* **`alā' → "marvels"`**: the refrain's bounty-word.
  * **Rendering:** always "marvels" (31×); other editions render the same word "favors" — which is this translator's choice for *niʿma* at `[16:18]`: "if you tried to enumerate the favors of Allah, you will not be able to count them."
  * **Elsewhere:** the "which of your Lord's marvels" formula is s055-exclusive except `[53:55]` (once) — the litany's only twin site, at an-Najm's end.
* **`tukadhdhibūn (will you deny)`**: the refrain's verb — *deny/call false*.
  * **Rendering:** "will you deny" — kufr as lying about a known favor (k-shukr's definition: "They recognize Allah's blessing, but then deny it," `[16:83]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** the same charge frames the counter-litany "Is there another god with Allah?" ×5 in `[27:60]`–`[27:64]` (rg-verified) — ar-Rahman interrogates favor, an-Naml interrogates partnership: one method, two angles.
