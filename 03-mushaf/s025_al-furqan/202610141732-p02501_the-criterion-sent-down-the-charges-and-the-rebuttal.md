# The Criterion sent down — the Sender's credentials against the four charges (25:1–9)
**UID:** 202610141732
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 25:1–9 (Ruku 1 of 6 in al-Furqan)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[25:1]` – `[25:9]` (9 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens with a doxology that certifies the book ("sent down the Criterion"), certifies its Sender (no son, no partner, creator of measure), demolishes the competitors (gods that "create nothing, but are themselves created"), and then lines up the prosecution's case against the Messenger — fabricated lie, ancient tales, borrowed help, a spellbound man — answering the whole docket with one sentence: "It was revealed by He who knows the Secret in the heavens and the earth."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 6; the trial-scene of the whole surah is set here, and the charges recur and get answered downstream (the marketplace taunt of 25:7 answered near-verbatim at 25:20; the "at once?" demand of 25:32 answered by "in stages"). The first of the surah's three "Blessed is He" doxologies (1, 10, 61).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[25:1]`** Blessed is He who sent down the Criterion upon His servant, to be a warning to humanity.
  * **Gloss:** The book names itself *al-Furqan* — "the Criterion" — and its office is *warning to humanity*; "His servant" is the prophet's own title here (cf. the servant language of 17:1, 18:1 — echo, not yet checked in those folders).
* **`[25:2]`** He to whom belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth, who took to Himself no son, who never had a partner in His kingship; who created everything and determined its measure.
  * **Gloss:** Four creed-clauses in one breath: ownership, no son, no partner, creation-with-measure — the counter-credentials to every charge.
* **`[25:3]`** And yet, instead of Him, they produce for themselves gods that create nothing, but are themselves created; that have no power to harm or benefit themselves; and no power over life, death, or resurrection.
  * **Gloss:** The rival gods fail three tests: uncreative, self-impotent, dead over life and resurrection — a criterion applied to divinity itself.
* **`[25:4]`** Those who disbelieve say, "This is nothing but a lie that he made up, and others have helped him at it." They have committed an injustice and a perjury.
  * **Gloss:** Charge one: fabrication plus conspiracy; the corpus's verdict on the speakers in the same line — "an injustice and a perjury."
* **`[25:5]`** And they say, "Tales of the ancients; he wrote them down; they are dictated to him morning and evening."
  * **Gloss:** Charge two: ancient tales, transcribed and relayed on a schedule — the Book made derivative and mechanical.
* **`[25:6]`** Say, "It was revealed by He who knows the Secret in the heavens and the earth. He is always Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The rebuttal is a provenance claim: *kunzīya* ("the Secret") — the source is the knower of secrets; and the line ends not in threat but "Forgiving and Merciful."
* **`[25:7]`** And they say, "What sort of messenger is this, who eats food, and walks in the marketplaces? If only an angel was sent down with him, to be alongside him a warner."
  * **Gloss:** Charge three: the scandal of ordinariness — appetite and the bazaar; the demand: an angelic second.
* **`[25:8]`** Or, "If only a treasure was dropped on him." Or, "If only he had a garden from which he eats." The evildoers also say, "You are following but a man under spell."
  * **Gloss:** The caricature of prophetic economics — treasure or garden instead of wages — then charge four: "a man under spell" (*shāṭir* — Itani's unique rendering).
* **`[25:9]`** Look how they invent examples for you. They have gone astray, and cannot find a way.
  * **Gloss:** The gavel: comparison-logic gone wrong; "cannot find a way" seeds the way-motif the surah will run on (27, 34, 42, 44).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`al-Furqan`** (the Criterion): the discerning given book.
  * **Rendering:** "the Criterion" at 25:1; the same root elsewhere is "the Scripture and the Criterion" ([2:53]), Ramadan's credential "…and the Criterion" ([2:185]), "He sent down the Criterion" ([3:4]), "We gave Moses and Aaron the Criterion" ([21:48]).
  * **Elsewhere:** [8:41] drifts — the same idiom prints "the Day of Distinction" there (observable in corpus).
* **`tabāraka`** ("Blessed is He"): a transcendence-blessing verb reserved for God.
  * **Rendering:** "Blessed is He" — here, at [25:10], and at [25:61]; the surah holds 3 of the corpus's 5 occurrences (others: [27:8], [67:1] — verified by rg).
* **`asāṭīr al-awwalīn`** ("Tales of the ancients"): the stock slander of Meccan opponents.
  * **Rendering:** "Tales of the ancients" at 25:5; the same idiom renders "Legends of the ancients" at [16:24], [27:68], [23:83], [83:13], "tales of the ancients" at [46:17] (term-choice drift observable in this book).
* **`shāṭir`** ("under spell"): "a man under spell" — 25:8.
  * **Rendering:** unique in the corpus: the word "spell" appears nowhere else (verified) — other editions' "possessed/bewitched" family is not Itani's choice here.
