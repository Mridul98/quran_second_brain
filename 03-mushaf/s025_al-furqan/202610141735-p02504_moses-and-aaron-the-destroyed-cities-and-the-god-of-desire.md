# Moses and Aaron, the destroyed cities, and him who chose his desire as his god (25:35–44)
**UID:** 202610141735
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 25:35–44 (Ruku 4 of 6 in al-Furqan)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[25:35]` – `[25:44]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The criterion turns historical: the pattern "Scripture given → signs rejected → total destruction" runs from Moses and Aaron through Noah, Aad, Thamood, the inhabitants of Arras, and the city "drenched by the terrible rain" — and the surah's present mockers live on that same caravan-road of evidence ("Did they not see it?"); the unit's real verdict then drops from dead nations to a live anatomy of idolatry: the man "who chose his desire as his god" is not merely misinformed but booted below the level of cattle.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 6; the history-act of the trial-surah. Correction on the task brief: **Pharaoh is never named in this surah** (rg-verified, zero hits in s025) — the duo is Moses and Aaron, and "the people who rejected Our signs" (36) are classically identified as Pharaoh's people. *Source: classical tafsir identification — agent note, verify.*

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[25:35]`** We gave Moses the Scripture, and appointed his brother Aaron as his assistant.
  * **Gloss:** The pattern opens with gift, not plague: Scripture plus an "assistant" — the exact word Ta-Ha's petition asked: [20:29] "And appoint an assistant for me, from my family. [20:30] Aaron, my brother."
* **`[25:36]`** We said, "Go to the people who rejected Our signs," and We destroyed them completely.
  * **Gloss:** Commission and endpoint in one breath — "destroyed them completely"; the rejectors unnamed in the text (classically: Pharaoh's people — Source: tafsir identification, agent note, verify).
* **`[25:37]`** And the people of Noah: when they rejected the messengers, We drowned them, and made them a lesson for mankind. We have prepared for the wrongdoers a painful retribution.
  * **Gloss:** The template generalizes ("rejected the messengers," plural); purpose clause: "a lesson for mankind" — history as *ʿibra*, then the prepared retribution brackets past and future.
* **`[25:38]`** And Aad, and Thamood, and the inhabitants of Arras, and many generations in between.
  * **Gloss:** The roster — two famous names, one singular ("Arras," a corpus-unique term, verified), and "many generations in between" that no one memorialized; the unlisted ones are not excused.
* **`[25:39]`** To each We presented the parables; and each We devastated utterly.
  * **Gloss:** No destruction without prior warning-parables — the trial's due-process clause.
* **`[25:40]`** And they came upon the city that was drenched by the terrible rain. Did they not see it? But they do not expect resurrection.
  * **Gloss:** The overthrown city, named only by its weather; "Did they not see it?" makes the audience eyewitnesses — the root of their failure is not sightlessness at the end ("they do not expect resurrection"), echoing 25:21's "do not expect to meet Us."
* **`[25:41]`** And when they see you, they take you for nothing but mockery: "Is this the one Allah sent as a messenger?"
  * **Gloss:** The live mockers appear: the same question as 25:7's taunt — the trial continues in the present tense.
* **`[25:42]`** "He nearly led us away from our gods, had we not patiently adhered to them." But they will know, when they witness the torment, who is further away from the way.
  * **Gloss:** Their victory-lap quoted ironically ("patiently adhered"); the ballot: at torment-time they "will know who is further away from the way" — the comparative of 25:34 returned.
* **`[25:43]`** Have you seen him who chose his desire as his god? Would you be an agent for him?
  * **Gloss:** The surah's most quoted diagnosis: divinized preference itself; "Would you be an agent for him?" — responsibility for another's worship-engine is transferred back to its owner.
* **`[25:44]`** Or do you assume that most of them hear or understand? They are just like cattle, but even more errant in their way.
  * **Gloss:** The descent: cattle follow instinct and find pasture; desire-followers "are just like cattle, but even more errant in their way" — the way-motif bottoms out here.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`wazīr` ("assistant")**: 25:35 — Aaron installed.
  * **Rendering:** "appointed his brother Aaron as his assistant"; the same word is Moses's own petition word at [20:29] ("appoint an assistant for me") — Ta-Ha asks, al-Furqan reports the grant (observable across the two corpus files).
* **`Arras` ("the inhabitants of Arras")**: 25:38 — a destroyed people named nowhere else in the corpus (verified: unique).
  * **Elsewhere:** classical identification with "al-Rass" of tafsir literature is outside the corpus — *Source: agent inference — verify.*
* **`maṭar` ("the terrible rain")**: 25:40 — the city "drenched by the terrible rain" (city unnamed in the text).
  * **Rendering/Elsewhere:** the rain-of-punishment family: [26:173] "We rained down on them a rain. Dreadful is the rain of those forewarned," [27:58] "We rained upon them a rain. Miserable was the rain…," [15:74] "rained down upon them stones of baked clay." Classically the city is Sodom's (Lot's people) — *Source: classical tafsir identification — agent note, verify.*
* **`hawā` ("desire as god")**: 25:43 "who chose his desire as his god."
  * **Elsewhere:** [45:23] "him who has taken his desire for his god" — near-twin opening ("Have you seen/considered"), with 45 adding the sealing imagery; 25:44's cattle-comparison twin is [7:179] "These are like cattle. In fact, they are further astray."
