# No good news when they see the angels — the regret, the abandoned Quran, and the staged Book (25:21–34)
**UID:** 202610141734
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 25:21–34 (Ruku 3 of 6 in al-Furqan)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[25:21]` – `[25:34]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The people who "do not expect to meet Us" demand angels or a visible Lord — and the surah grants their wish at the worst possible moment: the Day they see the angels descending in streams brings *no good news*, works dissolve to scattered dust, and the friends who led one another astray become regrets; the unit turns from the Day to *this* Book's method — not dropped "at once" but "revealed it in stages," heart-strengthening, truth-answering — and closes on the Messenger's complaint: "my people have abandoned this Quran."
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 6; the high point of the surah's courtroom drama — the angels of 25:7 and 25:21 arrive at last, but as witnesses of a difficult Day (25–26); the staged-revelation answer (32) closes the opening argument with ruku 1's fabrication charges.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[25:21]`** Those who do not expect to meet Us say, "If only the angels were sent down to us, or we could see our Lord." They have grown arrogant within themselves, and have become excessively defiant.
  * **Gloss:** The demand for unverifiable credentials; the diagnosis is inward arrogance — "expect to meet Us" (the *liqā'* verb) frames faith as expectation of an encounter.
* **`[25:22]`** On the Day when they see the angels— there will be no good news for sinners on that Day; and they will say, "A protective refuge."
  * **Gloss:** The wish granted as terror: angels seen, but "no good news"; the cry "A protective refuge" — the phrase is unique in the corpus (verified).
* **`[25:23]`** We will proceed to the works they did, and will turn them into scattered dust.
  * **Gloss:** The audit of works ends in zero: deeds atomized — the same "scattered dust" phrase as [56:6] where mountains become dust.
* **`[25:24]`** The companions of Paradise on that Day will be better lodged, and more fairly accommodated.
  * **Gloss:** The counter-position: lodging-comparative — "better lodged, more fairly accommodated."
* **`[25:25]`** The Day when the sky is cleft with clouds, and the angels are sent down in streams.
  * **Gloss:** Sky split, angels "sent down in streams" (*tanazzulan* — unique rendering here, verified); descent at last — the wrong kind of arrival for the demanders.
* **`[25:26]`** On that Day, true sovereignty will belong to the Merciful, and it will be a difficult Day for the disbelievers.
  * **Gloss:** Kingship exclusive on the Day — cf. [40:16] "To whom does the sovereignty today?" and [20:114] "the True King"; "the Merciful" again carries the name the pagans disputed (60).
* **`[25:27]`** On that Day, the wrongdoer will bite his hands, and say, "If only I had followed the way with the Messenger.
  * **Gloss:** Regret in body-language — "bite his hands" (the rage-bite; cf. [3:119] "bite their fingers in rage"); the way-motif returns, 25:9's lost "way" found too late. [sic: quote opens here, closes only at 29.]
* **`[25:28]`** Oh, woe to me; I wish I never took so-andso for a friend.
  * **Gloss:** The friend named "so-andso" — the corpus prints the compound solidified (book typography [sic]); anonymity makes every bad companion an defendant.
* **`[25:29]`** He led me away from the Message after it had come to me; for Satan has always been a betrayer of man."
  * **Gloss:** The chain of misdirection ends at Satan, "a betrayer of man"; the Message (*dhikr*) arrived — and was deflected.
* **`[25:30]`** And the Messenger will say, "My Lord, my people have abandoned this Quran."
  * **Gloss:** The prophet's counter-complaint: the people's verdict-book abandoned; note the corpus's irony — elsewhere men cry "They have abandoned us" about their gods ([7:37], [40:74]); here a Messenger is abandoned *by* men *with* a Book.
* **`[25:31]`** Likewise, to every prophet We assign enemies from among the wicked. But your Lord suffices as a Guide and Savior.
  * **Gloss:** Consolation-by-law: enmity is the prophetic norm — the near-twin formula is [6:112] "Likewise, We have assigned for every prophet an enemy"; the cure: "your Lord suffices."
* **`[25:32]`** Those who disbelieve say, "Why was the Quran not revealed to him at once?" Thus in order to strengthen your heart thereby, and We revealed it in stages.
  * **Gloss:** The final charge answered on the Book's own terms: not a single drop but *tafṭīl* — "in stages" (the identical phrase at [17:106]) — pedagogy named: "to strengthen your heart thereby" (cf. [11:120]).
* **`[25:33]`** Whatever argument they come to you with, We provide you with the truth, and a better exposition.
  * **Gloss:** The dialectical promise: every *mathal* met with "the truth and a better exposition" — the surah itself performing it.
* **`[25:34]`** Those who are herded into Hell on their faces—those are in a worse position, and further astray from the way.
  * **Gloss:** Face-dragging imagery (cf. [17:97] "gather them…on their faces," [54:48] "dragged upon their faces"); "worse position, further astray" — the lodging-comparative of 24 inverted.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ḥisāb/ʿamal` → "We will proceed to the works"**: the reckoning of deeds, 25:23.
  * **Rendering:** "turn them into scattered dust" — exact phrase elsewhere only [56:6]; the two uses gloss each other (works lose mass as mountains do).
* **`tanazzul` ("in streams")**: staged descent, 25:25.
  * **Elsewhere:** unique here as angels-descent; the stage-motif returns at [25:32] "We revealed it in stages" — identical phrase at [17:106], and "created you in stages" at [71:14] (observable shared English across related Arabic).
* **`mahjūr` ("abandoned")**: 25:30 "my people have abandoned this Quran."
  * **Elsewhere:** the same English verb family marks idols deserting worshippers at [7:37], [40:74], [46:28] — Itani's rendering makes 25:30 the mirror case (agent observation on the translation).
* **`kafā` ("suffices")**: "your Lord suffices as a Guide and Savior" — 25:31.
  * **Elsewhere:** the same divine-sufficiency formula runs [39:38] "Allah suffices for me. On Him the reliant rely," [4:6] "suffices as a Reckoner," [4:132] "suffices as Manager," [48:28] "suffices as Witness" — and returns in this surah at [25:58] (the two s025 instances bracket the second half of the surah).
