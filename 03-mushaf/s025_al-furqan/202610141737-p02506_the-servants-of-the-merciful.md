# Blessed is He — the servants of the Merciful, the ten-mark portrait, and the verdict of prayer (25:61–77)
**UID:** 202610141737
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 25:61–77 (Ruku 6 of 6 in al-Furqan)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[25:61]` – `[25:77]` (17 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The third "Blessed is He" skies the astronomy evidence (constellations, lamp, illuminating moon) and dedicates night-and-day alternation to "whoever desires to reflect, or desires to show gratitude" — then the courtroom surah delivers its counter-exhibit: not the charges but the characters, the "servants of the Merciful" in a walking portrait (humility, "Peace," night prayer, middle-way spending, the three-prohibition triad, no false witness, heedful remembrance, the family petition), crowned by the Chamber "for their patience" and closed by the verdict line: God has no use for you without your prayers — and the inevitable is coming.
* **Placement:** Final ruku of 6; answers 25:60's name-war by *showing* the Merciful's servants, redeems the surah's way-motif (9, 27, 34, 42, 44) by finally putting people on the road, and its "gratitude" (62) answers 25:50's "thanklessness."

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[25:61]`** Blessed is He who placed constellations in the sky, and placed in it a lamp, and an illuminating moon.
  * **Gloss:** Doxology #3 (after 1, 10); the sky as evidence rack: zodiac-lights, the sun-a-lamp, the lit moon — astronomy vocabulary in three terms, all creation predicates of *tabāraka*.
* **`[25:62]`** And it is He who made the night and the day alternate—for whoever desires to reflect, or desires to show gratitude.
  * **Gloss:** The alternation has a purpose clause addressed to *desire* — reflection or gratitude, either one is enough; the two faculties the whole surah has been demanding (cf. "that they may reflect," 25:50).
* **`[25:63]`** The servants of the Merciful are those who walk the earth in humility, and when the ignorant address them, they say, “Peace.”
  * **Gloss:** The portrait opens mid-stride: gait and speech — low walking, and the one-word defense "Peace" when jostled by the ignorant (*jāhilūn*); the epithet "servants of the Merciful" answers 25:60's "And what is the Merciful?"
* **`[25:64]`** And those who pass the night prostrating themselves to their Lord and standing up.
  * **Gloss:** Mark two: the night hours spent *sujjadan wa qiyāman* — prostration and standing, the prayer posture named twice in one line; the surah's only explicit prayer-description.
* **`[25:65]`** And those who say, ‘‘Our Lord, avert from us the suffering of Hell, for its suffering is continuous.
  * **Gloss:** Mark three, in quoted speech — the only fear statement in the list; [sic: the corpus opens this quote with the backtick pair ‘‘ and closes only at 66 — extraction artifact].
* **`[25:66]`** It is indeed a miserable residence and destination.”
  * **Gloss:** Hell as real-estate verdict — "residence and destination"; the exact phrase returns inverted at 25:76 as paradise's praise, bracketing the whole portrait.
* **`[25:67]`** And those who, when they spend, are neither wasteful nor stingy, but choose a middle course between that.
  * **Gloss:** Mark four — the economy verse: spending as the virtue between two named extremes ("wasteful" — *isrāf* — and "stingy" — *iqtiṛāṛ*); a middle course, not a low way.
* **`[25:68]`** And those who do not implore besides Allah any other god, and do not kill the soul which Allah has made sacred—except in the pursuit of justice—and do not commit adultery. Whoever does that will face penalties.
  * **Gloss:** Mark five, triple-loaded: shirk, unlawful killing, adultery — the triad of the 6:151 charge-sheet; the penalty clause (*yalqawna ghayyan* — "face penalties") hangs over the three.
* **`[25:69]`** The punishment will be doubled for him on the Day of Resurrection, and he will dwell therein in humiliation forever.
  * **Gloss:** The aggravation clause: doubling, permanence, humiliation — the triple consequence the text itself attaches to the unrepentant agent of the triad.
* **`[25:70]`** Except for those who repent, and believe, and do good deeds. These—Allah will replace their bad deeds with good deeds. Allah is ever Forgiving and Merciful.
  * **Gloss:** The great exception — the portrait is not the sinless but the repentant; "replace their bad deeds with good deeds" is unique in the corpus as deed-exchange (other "replace" verses swap skins or peoples).
* **`[25:71]`** Whoever repents and acts righteously— has inclined towards Allah with repentance.
  * **Gloss:** Repentance re-rendered as *anāba* — a leaning-toward, the same direction-verb as [8:61]'s "if they incline towards peace"; the penitent's stance is direction, not event.
* **`[25:72]`** And those who do not bear false witness; and when they come across indecencies, they pass by with dignity.
  * **Gloss:** Marks six and seven in one line: the tongue (no *zuhūr* — false testimony) and the feet (walk past seen indecency with dignity — neither endorsing nor peeping).
* **`[25:73]`** And those who, when reminded of the revelations of their Lord, do not fall before them deaf and blind.
  * **Gloss:** Mark eight: hearing-and-seeing when addressed — the surah's sensory audit (44 "do you assume that most of them hear or understand?") now answered in the positive; "deaf and blind" is corpus-unique here (verified).
* **`[25:74]`** And those who say, “Our Lord, grant us delight in our spouses and our children, and make us a good example for the righteous.”
  * **Gloss:** Mark nine, a petition for the ordinary: *qurrat a'yun* — "delight" in spouse and children (the same phrase-family as 28:9's "An eye's delight for me"), plus the last ask: leadership of the God-fearing.
* **`[25:75]`** Those will be awarded the Chamber for their patience, and will be greeted therein with greetings and peace.
  * **Gloss:** The award "the Chamber" (*al-ghurf* — twin [34:37] "reside in the Chambers") is paid for *sabr*; the peace they spoke to the ignorant (63) returns to them as heavenly greeting — cf. [10:10], [33:44], [56:26].
* **`[25:76]`** Abiding therein forever—it is an excellent residence and destination.
  * **Gloss:** Permanence seals it; 25:66's "miserable residence and destination" inverted verbatim — the bracket closes, both homes are real-estate verdicts.
* **`[25:77]`** Say, “What are you to my Lord without your prayers? You have denied the truth, and the inevitable will happen.”
  * **Gloss:** The surah's last word is *du'ā'* — supplication as the whole point of the portrait; without it the human vote counts nothing ("What are you to my Lord without your prayers?"); and the denial verdict with the closing formula: "the inevitable will happen" (twin of 20:129).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ʿibād al-Raḥmān` ("the servants of the Merciful")**: 25:63 — the portrait's title.
  * **Rendering:** exact phrase unique in the corpus (verified: only 25:63); "the Merciful" as name recurs at [25:26], and in this unit 25:60 twice and 25:59 "The Most Merciful."
  * **Elsewhere:** the name-war of 25:60 makes 25:63 the answer — the pagans rejected the name; the surah answers with the name's *servants*.
* **`burūj` ("constellations")**: 25:61 — the astronomy vocabulary the task brief flagged.
  * **Rendering:** "constellations" here; "We placed constellations in the sky" at [15:16] (same verb+object), and the oath "By the sky with the constellations" at [85:1] (surah al-Buruj). The brief's "Great Bear" is another English version's rendering of *burūj* — not this corpus. *Source: comparison with non-corpus translations — agent note, verify.*
  * **Elsewhere (lamp/moon):** [71:16] "He made the sun a lamp," [78:13] "a blazing lamp"; drift at [10:5] "made the sun radiant, and the moon a light."
* **`duʿāʾ` ("prayers")**: 25:77 — supplication, the surah's final datum.
  * **Rendering:** "your prayers" = *du'ā'*, not *ṣalāh*; the same English word renders *ṣalāh* at [2:238] "Guard your prayers" — one English word, two Arabic registers (observable in corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** [40:60] "Pray to Me, and I will respond to you" — the twin promise; [35:14] "If you pray to them, they cannot hear your prayer" — the twin warning about wrong addressees.
* **`qiwām` ("a middle course")**: 25:67 — the spending ethic.
  * **Rendering:** "neither wasteful nor stingy, but choose a middle course between that" = *qiwāman* (poise); the adjacent-but-different *wasat* root prints "a moderate community" at [2:143]; restraint twin [17:29] "do not keep your hand tied to your neck, nor spread it out fully"; the *isrāf* family prints "excessive" at [7:31] (drift).
* **The portrait's mark-count**: eight "and those who" clusters (63–74); the traditional enumeration reaches "ten marks" by counting 25:68's three prohibitions separately. *Source: classical enumeration convention — agent note, verify.*

## 6. Context Notes
