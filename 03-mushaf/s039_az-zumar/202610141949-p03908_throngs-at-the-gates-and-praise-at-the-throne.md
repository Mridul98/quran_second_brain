# Throngs at the gates; praise at the Throne (39:71–75)
**UID:** 202610141949
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 39:71–75 (Ruku 8 of 8 in az-Zumar)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[39:71]` – `[39:75]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's name arrives last: the disbelievers are driven to Hell "in throngs" and the God-fearers led to Paradise "in throngs" — two processions, two sets of gates, two gatekeeper speeches (the warner-interrogation and its confession on one side; "Peace be upon you, you have been good" on the other) — and the book closes above the parade: angels hovering around the Throne, judgment passed equitably, and the final word spoken by no one in particular and everyone at once: "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds," the Fatihah's second verse verbatim, the surah's own hamd-refrain (39:29) come home.
* **Placement:** Ruku 8 of 8; the terminal pageant. The task's "Forgiving, the Merciful" close is not this surah's — az-Zumar ends on praise, not pardon (pardon was the 39:53 station; the Throne is the exit).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[39:71]`** "Those who disbelieved will be driven to Hell in throngs. Until, when they have reached it, and its gates are opened, its keepers will say to them, “Did not messengers from among you come to you, reciting to you the revelations of your Lord, and warning you of the meeting of this Day of yours?” They will say, “Yes, but the verdict of punishment is justified against the disbelievers.”"
  * **Gloss:** Drive ("driven," saq — the herded verb) vs. the next verse's lead; the gatekeepers' question is the whole surah's case-file in one clause — reciting + warning were delivered by their own; the confession is juridical, not penitential: "Yes, but the verdict... is justified."
* **`[39:72]`** "It will be said, “Enter the gates of Hell, to abide therein eternally.” How wretched is the destination of the arrogant."
  * **Gloss:** The sentence passes in the same speech-register as the greeting of 73 — same verb-forms, opposite doors; "the arrogant" (mutakabbirin) collects 39:59/60's crime-list for the final label; the "Enter the gates of Hell... destination of the arrogant" formula is near-verbatim at 16:29 and 40:76.
* **`[39:73]`** "And those who feared their Lord will be led to Paradise in throngs. Until, when they have reached it, and its gates are opened, its keepers will say to them, “Peace be upon you, you have been good, so enter it, to abide therein eternally.”"
  * **Gloss:** "Led" (yusaq — the gentle verb in the same root, a deliberate rhyme against 71's driving); the greeting pre-dates the entry: salam first ("you have been good" — tab'tum: you were pleasant/pleasantly dealt with), entry after; the sabr-reward formula of `[13:24]` ("Peace be upon you, because you endured patiently") restated without its cause-name.
* **`[39:74]`** "And they will say, “Praise be to Allah, who has fulfilled His promise to us, and made us inherit the land, enjoying Paradise as we please.” How excellent is the reward of the workers."
  * **Gloss:** The answered promise: 39:20's "Allah never breaks a promise" cashed; inheritance-language ("made us inherit the land") — the same verb as the dispossessed towns' heirs (7:100) but here the land is Paradise; "as we please" closes the 39:34 wish-reward loop; "the workers" (amilun) ties back to 39:35 "what they used to do" and 39:39's "work according to your ability."
* **`[39:75]`** "And you will see the angels hovering around the Throne, glorifying their Lord with praise. And it will be judged between them equitably, and it will be said, “Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds.”"
  * **Gloss:** The camera tilts up mid-verdict: the Throne's periphery already praising (twin scene at `[40:7]` — "Those who carry the Throne, and those around it, glorify their Lord with praise"); the judgment equity formula (69) reprised one last time; then the surah's closing sentence, unattributed to any speaker — "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds," word-for-word the Fatihah's `[1:2]` and Ghafir's last words (`[40:65]`) in this edition's renderings.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`in throngs` (zumar)** `[39:71, 73]`:
  * **Rendering:** "driven to Hell in throngs" / "led to Paradise in throngs" — the surah's name-word appears exactly at these two rows (observable in corpus: the only "throngs" renderings anywhere in it); the naming is therefore anchored where the two human groups are finalized.
  * **Elsewhere:** the muster-kin: `[78:18]` "you will come in droves" (the Trumpet-day, echoing 39:68), `[99:6]` "the people will emerge in droves"; `[37:173]` "Our troops will be the victors" and `[27:17]` Solomon's troops "all held in strict order" — the army-register the task's "ARMIES" picked up belongs to these, not to 39:71/73.
* **`keepers` (khazana)** `[39:71, 73]`:
  * **Rendering:** "its keepers will say" at both gates — the same officer-corps, opposite scripts.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[67:8]` "Every time a batch is thrown into it, its keepers will ask them, 'Has no warner come to you?'" — the interrogation-twin verse (with 67:9's "we heard them" confession answering 39:71's "Yes, but..."); `[40:49]` the inmates' request to the keepers — the same officials as intercession-refusers.
* **`Peace be upon you`** `[39:73]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Peace be upon you, you have been good, so enter it."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:24]` "Peace be upon you, because you endured patiently. How excellent is the Final Home" — the sabr-conditioned twin; `[16:32]` "Peace be upon you; enter Paradise, for what you used to do" — the works-conditioned twin; `[6:54]` the greeting commanded to the community on earth before it is ever spoken at the gate.
* **`Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds`** `[39:74–75]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Praise be to Allah, who has fulfilled His promise to us" (74, the saved speaking) → "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds" (75, the universe spoken over).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[1:2]` verbatim identity of 39:75's close; `[40:65]` Ghafir's closing "Praise be to Allah, the Lord of the Worlds" — the diptych ends in lockstep; internally 39:29 "Praise be to Allah, but most of them do not know" is the mid-surah hamd the close answers.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical lists) — the two-throngs finale is the classical ascription source of the surah's name (az-Zumar = the groups), observable in the corpus itself (the word-sense occurs only at 71/73). *Source: naming convention + corpus observation; classical lists — verify.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** none transmitted; the unattributed final "Praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds" is classically glossed as the words of the people of Paradise and/or of all creation — the corpus states no speaker, which this ruku preserves. *Source: agent inference on the grammar — verify.*
