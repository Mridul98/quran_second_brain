# Is Allah not enough for His servant? The truth-bringer, the liar, and guidance privatized (39:32–41)
**UID:** 202610141945
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 39:32–41 (Ruku 4 of 8 in az-Zumar)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[39:32]` – `[39:41]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku sorts humanity by its handling of the truth — the liar-about-God who denies it on arrival vs. the one who brings the truth and confirms it, rewarded past arithmetic — then pivots to the emotional core the sorting exposes: "Is Allah not enough for His servant?" The creator-reductio makes the associators convict their own intercessors (they would say, "Allah" — then can those nullities lift harm or hold mercy?), the mutual "work according to your ability" challenge is issued, and the Book's legal status is finalized: guidance and loss are self-addressed, and the Prophet is not their overseer.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 8; the surah's sufficiency hinge. It closes the courtroom opened at 39:29–31 and opens the death-and-intercession audit (ruku 5).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[39:32]`** "Who is more evil than he who lies about Allah, and denies the truth when it has come to him? Is there not in Hell room for the ungrateful?"
  * **Gloss:** The superlative-evil formula: fabrication + arrival-rejection as one crime; "room in Hell for the ungrateful" — kufr rendered by its ingratitude-face, the shukr-note vocabulary inside the tawhid argument.
* **`[39:33]`** "But he who promotes the truth, and testifies to it—these are the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-figure: the truth-bringer and the truth-confirmer (siddiq grammar — singular in 32, plural in 33: the archetype and his school); their title: "the righteous."
* **`[39:34]`** "They will have whatever they please with their Lord. Such is the reward for the virtuous."
  * **Gloss:** Reward as wish-fulfillment, "with their Lord" — presence, not just parcel; "the virtuous" (muhsinin) pays back 39:10's "those who do good."
* **`[39:35]`** "Allah will acquit them of the worst of their deeds, and will reward them according to the best of what they used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The double favor: the worst expunged, the best priced — the surah's best-of/worst-of ledger (echoing 39:18's "follow the best of it"): grace skews every account.
* **`[39:36]`** "Is Allah not enough for His servant? And they frighten you with those besides Him. Whomever Allah sends astray, for him there is no guide."
  * **Gloss:** The rhetorical keystone: hasb — sufficiency as question; the fear-mongering of the besides-Him set against it; then the hard clause (misguidance without rescue) reprising 39:23 verbatim in shape.
* **`[39:37]`** "And whomever Allah guides, for him there is no misleader. Is Allah not Powerful and Vengeful?"
  * **Gloss:** The symmetry clause — guidance needs no security detail either; the names close the pair: Powerful, Vengeful (intiqam) — ruku 1's "Almighty, the Forgiver" (39:5) with its justice-face shown.
* **`[39:38]`** "And if you asked them, “Who created the heavens and the earth?” they would say, “Allah.” Say, “Have you seen those you pray to instead of Allah? If Allah willed any harm for me, can they lift His harm? And if He willed a blessing for me, can they hold back His mercy?” Say, “Allah suffices for me. On Him the reliant rely.”"
  * **Gloss:** The creator-reductio with a verbatim concession built in ("they would say, 'Allah'"), then the harm/blessing stress-test of the prayed-to — both fail; the Prophet's own answer is the hasb confession: "Allah suffices for me. On Him the reliant rely."
* **`[39:39]`** "Say: “O my people, work according to your ability; and so will I. Then you will know."
  * **Gloss:** The truce of deeds: each side works its own line; knowledge comes on schedule, not by argument.
* **`[39:40]`** "Who will receive a humiliating punishment, and on whom will fall a lasting torment.”"
  * **Gloss:** The schedule's content: humiliation (khizy) and permanence (adhab muqim) as the two discriminating outcomes.
* **`[39:41]`** "We sent down upon you the Book for mankind in truth. He who follows guidance does so for the good of his soul. And he who strays in error does so to its detriment. You are not their overseer."
  * **Gloss:** The ruku's verdict-note: Book universal ("for mankind"), guidance/loss self-addressed (the 17:15 formula), and the Prophet released from custodial responsibility — "You are not their overseer" (wakil: guardian-of-accounts).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`suffices / enough` (hasb)** `[39:36, 38]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Is Allah not enough for His servant?"; "Allah suffices for me. On Him the reliant rely."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:67]` "On Him I rely, and on Him let the reliant rely" — Jacob's exact phrase, the corpus twin of 39:38's close; `[4:132]` "Allah suffices as Manager"; `[25:31/58]` "your Lord suffices as a Guide and Savior... He suffices as the All-Informed" — the sufficiency-family this ruku makes a question.
* **`lies about Allah`** `[39:32]`:
  * **Rendering:** "Who is more evil than he who lies about Allah, and denies the truth when it has come to him?"
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:94]` "Whoever forges lies about Allah after that—these are the unjust"; `[29:68]` "calls the truth a lie when it has come to him? Is there not in Hell a dwelling for the blasphemers?" — 39:32 is 29:68's near-twin; `[7:37]` the same superlative; internal payoff at 39:60 (the liars' blackened faces).
* **`denies the truth when it has come`** `[39:32]`:
  * **Rendering:** arrival-rejection as the co-crime of fabrication.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[50:5]` "they denied the truth when it has come to them, so they are in a confused state"; `[6:5]` "They denied the truth when it has come to them; but soon will reach them the news of what they used to ridicule" — the idiom is a fixed pericope across surahs.
* **`overseer` (wakil)** `[39:41]`:
  * **Rendering:** "You are not their overseer."
  * **Elsewhere:** the wakil-family is broad; this ruku's own 39:38 ("Allah suffices") is the theological underside — if He suffices, no human is another's guarantor; cf. `[4:132]` "Allah suffices as Manager" (same root rendered "Manager" there — observable term-choice drift).
