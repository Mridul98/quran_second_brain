# The best of narrations: opened and hardened hearts, the parable of two masters, and the dying declaration (39:22–31)
**UID:** 202610141944
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 39:22–31 (Ruku 3 of 8 in az-Zumar)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[39:22]` – `[39:31]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku anatomizes what the Book does to whom it does it: opened hearts walk in light, hardened hearts are lost in error, and the "best of narrations" runs a full body-reflex — skins shiver, then skins and hearts soften; then the Quran's self-report (every ideal cited, defectless Arabic) and the surah's own master-parable (a man shared by squabbling partners vs. a man belonging to one) make the tawhid argument a household economics question — before "You will die, and they will die" drops the mortality hammer and schedules the Day's quarrel.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 8; the Book-and-heart centerpiece. Ruku 2 ended on water becoming debris; this ruku ends on people becoming corpses and then litigants — the same reminder-genre, aimed at the reader's own end.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[39:22]`** "What about someone whose heart Allah has opened to Islam, so that he follows a light from His Lord? Woe to those whose hearts are hardened against the mention of Allah. Those are in manifest error."
  * **Gloss:** Third and fourth links of the a-faman chain (begun 39:19): the opened heart (sharh al-sadr) walks with a light; the case-hardened heart is defined by its reaction to the *dhikr* — "the mention of Allah" — not by its creed-formula.
* **`[39:23]`** "Allah has sent down the best of narrations: a Scripture consistent and paired. The skins of those who reverence their Lord shiver from it, then their skins and their hearts soften up to the remembrance of Allah. Such is Allah’s guidance; He guides with it whomever He wills. But whomever Allah leaves astray, for him there is no guide."
  * **Gloss:** The Book's genre-defect: it is *narration* (qasas) rated "best," "consistent and paired" (mathani) — one body-response sequence, dread-then-ease; then the guidance clause with its double edge (whomever He wills / no guide for the left astray).
* **`[39:24]`** "What about someone who covers his face against the terrible misery of the Day of Resurrection? To the evildoers it will be said, “Taste what you used to earn.”"
  * **Gloss:** Fifth a-faman: the face-shielding panic of that Day. Task correction — the "faces of those who disbelieve" (blackened) is `[39:60]`; 39:24 is the man shielding his own face from misery. The "Taste what you used to earn" formula recurs 39:35's accounting and the gate-scenes.
* **`[39:25]`** "Those before them also denied the truth, so the penalty came upon them from where they did not perceive."
  * **Gloss:** The precedent-move: earlier deniers, penalty from an unperceived vector — the surah's security-theme: the danger with no address.
* **`[39:26]`** "Allah made them taste disgrace in the present life, but the punishment of the Hereafter is worse, if they only knew."
  * **Gloss:** Double-taste: worldly disgrace now, worse hereafter — with the epistemic taunt "if they only knew."
* **`[39:27]`** "We have cited in this Quran for mankind every ideal, that they may take heed."
  * **Gloss:** The Quran's self-description as total case-file: every *mathal* (rendered "ideal" here — see Key Terms) for all humanity, purpose-clause: heed-taking.
* **`[39:28]`** "An Arabic Quran, without any defect, so they may become righteous."
  * **Gloss:** Language and flawlessness clauses: Arabic — clear; "without any defect" (awja-free) — no crooked seam; purpose: taqwa.
* **`[39:29]`** "Allah cites the example of a man shared by partners at odds, and a man belonging exclusively to one man. Are they equal in status? Praise be to Allah, but most of them do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The parable the surah itself is arguing: co-owned, quarreling vs. singly-owned; the answer is felt before it is spoken; "Praise be to Allah, but most of them do not know" installs the hamd-refrain the ruku 8 close will complete.
* **`[39:30]`** "You will die, and they will die."
  * **Gloss:** Six words of pure fact — the Prophet's-death clause that the community refused at Uhud, here made universal ("and they will die"): mortality cancels every protective claim in 39:3/25.
* **`[39:31]`** "Then, on the Day of Resurrection, you will be quarrelling before your Lord."
  * **Gloss:** The "then" is the resurrection; the verdict is not silence but litigation — every party argues (the leaders, the followed, the selves), the surah's courtroom announced.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`opened to Islam` (sharh sadr)** `[39:22]`:
  * **Rendering:** "whose heart Allah has opened to Islam, so that he follows a light from His Lord."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:125]` "Whomever Allah desires to guide, He spreads open his heart to Islam; and... He makes his heart narrow, constricted" — the twin verse, with a rendering split between 22 ("opened") and 125 ("spreads open").
* **`consistent and paired` (mathani)** `[39:23]`:
  * **Rendering:** "a Scripture consistent and paired" — the only book-descriptive use of "paired" in this corpus.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[81:7]` "When the souls are paired" is the corpus's only other "paired" (the resurrection's pairing) — the descriptor is unique to az-Zumar; classically read as repetition-with-agreement (Source: classical tafsir genre — verify).
* **`every ideal` (mathal)** `[39:27–29]`:
  * **Rendering:** drift in one breath — 39:27 "every ideal," 39:29 "the example of a man...": the same root gets two English nouns two verses apart (Source: translator's term choice, observable in corpus).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:75]` "Allah cites the example of a bonded slave, who has no power over anything" — the owned-vs-owning parable twin; `[16:112]` the town that "turned unappreciative" — the precedent-parable genre 39:25–26 joins.
* **`quarrelling`** `[39:31]`:
  * **Rendering:** "you will be quarrelling before your Lord."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[34:32–33]` — Saba's Day-quarrel in full ("Was it us who turned you away from guidance...?" "It was your scheming by night and day") is the scripted performance of 39:31's future-tense announcement; `[2:197]` quarrelling banned at the Hajj it is licensed for the Day.
