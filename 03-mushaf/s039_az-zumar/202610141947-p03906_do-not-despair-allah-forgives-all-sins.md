# Do not despair: Allah forgives all sins — turn before the retribution (39:53–63)
**UID:** 202610141947
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 39:53–63 (Ruku 6 of 8 in az-Zumar)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[39:53]` – `[39:63]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's mercy-climax: the transgressors-against-themselves are addressed as "O My servants" — the same possessive the fire-sermon used (39:16) — and forbidden to despair, because "Allah forgives all sins"; then the deadline machinery starts: turn and submit *before*, follow the best of what is revealed *before*, and the three "if only" regret-cries (neglect, misguidance-blame, another chance) are answered mid-surah by the divine rebuttal "My Verses did come to you"; the ruku closes on blackened faces vs. the harm-free place of salvation, and on the Creator who is in Charge of all things and holds the reins.
* **Placement:** Ruku 6 of 8; the pivot the whole surah has been building toward — hope issued with an expiry date. 39:53 is the most quoted verse in the surah and the classical candidate for the Quran's most hopeful passage; judgment on the task's link-question: this ruku feeds the **guidance** note (the turn, the "Had Allah guided me" excuse-cries) and the **tawhid** note (forgiveness centralized to Him alone — no saint holds the pardon) as strongly as it feeds any mercy-theme.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[39:53]`** "Say, “O My servants who have transgressed against themselves: do not despair of Allah’s mercy, for Allah forgives all sins. He is indeed the Forgiver, the Clement.”"
  * **Gloss:** The verse: address ("My servants" — the status survives the excess), command (do not despair), ground (He forgives all sins — total, no category carve-out), seal (Forgiver, Clement — the task's "the Merciful" is not this edition's word; ra'uf rendered "the Clement"). "Transgressed against themselves" is 39:27's self-harm idiom (12:53-family) upgraded to *israf*: squandering.
* **`[39:54]`** "And turn to your Lord, and submit to Him, before the retribution comes upon you. Then you will not be helped."
  * **Gloss:** The mercy's terms: anuba (the return-toward) + taslim, with a hard front edge — "before... Then you will not be helped." The plea survives only while the clock runs.
* **`[39:55]`** "And follow the best of what was revealed to you from your Lord, before the punishment comes upon you suddenly, while you are unaware."
  * **Gloss:** The third verb: itba' ahsan al-ma — follow the best of what is revealed: 39:18's listening-rule re-issued as emergency instruction; "suddenly, while you are unaware" — the surprise-vector of 39:25 restated as deadline-condition.
* **`[39:56]`** "So that a soul may not say, “How sorry I am, for having neglected my duty to Allah, and for having been of the scoffers.”"
  * **Gloss:** Regret-cry one: negligence (tahta al-zahr — "turned my back") + membership in the mocker-crew; the subjunctive purpose-clause means: this verse exists so that cry never has to be made.
* **`[39:57]`** "Or say, “Had Allah guided me; I would have been of the pious.”"
  * **Gloss:** Regret-cry two: the misguidance-blame — the if-only guidance had been sent first; the surah's guidance-doctrine (39:36–37, 41) is quoted back at God as complaint.
* **`[39:58]`** "Or say, when it sees the penalty, “If only I had another chance, I would be of the virtuous.”"
  * **Gloss:** Regret-cry three: the return-plea — this is az-Zumar's version of the "return us" scene; the task's quoted plea is `[6:27]` ("If only we could be sent back...") and `[32:12]` ("so send us back, and we will act righteously"), and 26:102 is its kin; note the delay: the wish only surfaces "when it sees the penalty."
* **`[39:59]`** "Yes indeed! My Verses did come to you, but you called them lies, turned arrogant, and were of the faithless."
  * **Gloss:** The rebuttal, mid-surah: the plea's condition ("another chance") is answered with the fact (Verses already came); the three crimes: takdhib, takabbur, kufr — exactly the sequence the plea's "if only I had believed" forgets. Saba's answer to its own plea is the same: "They are liars" (`[6:28]`).
* **`[39:60]`** "On the Day of Resurrection, you will see those who told lies about Allah with their faces blackened. Is there not a place in Hell for the arrogant?"
  * **Gloss:** The face-verse the task pointed at from memory: blackened faces here (cf. `[3:106]` "some faces will be whitened, and some faces will be blackened"), tied to the liar-about-Allah category of 39:32 — the ruku closes the 32–41 courtroom loop; the Hell-room question now targets "the arrogant."
* **`[39:61]`** "And Allah will save those who maintained righteousness to their place of salvation. No harm will touch them, nor will they grieve."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-team: the waqaya (guarded) saved to a *place of salvation* — the same root as the "horrid" punishment, routed; twin of the angels' greeting "Do not fear, and do not grieve" (`[41:30]`).
* **`[39:62]`** "Allah is the Creator of all things, and He is in Charge of all things."
  * **Gloss:** The compressed creed: khalq (total creation) + wikala (total charge) — the wakil-line of 39:41 ("You are not their overseer") read upward: one overseer, all things.
* **`[39:63]`** "To Him belong the reins of the heavens and the earth. But those who blaspheme against the revelations of Allah—it is they who are the losers."
  * **Gloss:** "The reins" (maqalid — keys/handles of sovereignty, the corpus's only use): the besides-Him hold nothing; the blasphemers-of-the-revelations are the losers — 39:15's definition applied, with the losers-word reappearing at 39:65.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`do not despair` (qanuta/ya's)** `[39:53]`:
  * **Rendering:** "do not despair of Allah's mercy" — the command forbids the verdict-category, not merely the mood.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:87]` "do not despair of Allah's comfort. None despairs of Allah's comfort except the disbelieving people"; `[15:56]` "who despairs of his Lord's mercy but the lost?"; `[29:23]` the mirror-verdict — "these have despaired of My mercy": the rejected despair as its own punishment; 39:53 is the invitation, 29:23 the warning-side.
* **`turn` (anaba)** `[39:54]`:
  * **Rendering:** "And turn to your Lord, and submit to Him."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[89:28]` "Return to your Lord, pleased and accepted" — the successful completion of the same turn; the inaba-family's Quranic arc runs command (az-Zumar) → destination (al-Fajr).
* **`another chance` (the return-plea)** `[39:58]`:
  * **Rendering:** "If only I had another chance, I would be of the virtuous."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:27]` "If only we could be sent back, and not reject the revelations of our Lord" + 6:28's rebuttal "They are liars"; `[32:12]` "so send us back, and we will act righteously"; `[26:102]` "If only we could have another chance, we would be among the faithful"; `[2:167]` the follow-lead "If only we can have another chance" and "Allah will show them their deeds, as regrets to them" — the regret-pericope is a fixed scene; az-Zumar owns its sharpest three-cry staging.
* **`the Forgiver, the Clement` (Ghafur, Ra'uf)** `[39:53]`:
  * **Rendering:** "He is indeed the Forgiver, the Clement" — ra'uf rendered "Clement" throughout this edition.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:263]` "Allah is Rich and Clement"; `[5:101]` "Allah is Forgiving and Clement"; `[16:7]` "Your Lord is Clement and Merciful" — the ra'uf-name is rare and clustered in forgiveness contexts; 39:5's "Almighty, the Forgiver" was the seed, 39:53 the harvest.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (classical lists) — the mercy-climax's placement directly after the intercession-monopoly ruku is the surah's architecture: with no guardians left to appeal to, the only appeal left is the direct pardon. *Source: agent inference from sequence — verify.*
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the tafsir genre transmits reports treating 39:53 as the most hope-laden verse of the Quran (classically among the "widest verses of mercy"); no specific occasion is attached in the corpus. *Source: classical tafsir motif — verify.*
