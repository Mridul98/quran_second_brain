# Five oaths and the two verdicts: the Fire interrogated, the Garden inventoried (52:1–28)
**UID:** 202610142123
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 52:1–28 (Ruku 1 of 2 in at-Tur)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[52:1]` – `[52:28]` (28 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Five oath-exhibits over six verses — the Mount, an inscribed Book in a published scroll, the frequented House, the elevated roof, the seething sea — swear one sentence: the punishment is coming, unavertible; the ruku then stages both verdicts as courtroom speech: the Fire's address to deniers ("This is the Fire which you used to deny... Is this magic, or do you not see?") and the Garden's full inventory, ending inside the saved ones' own gratitude-testimony.
* **Placement:** The surah's evidence-half; ruku 2 audits the people who made these verdicts implausible. Its oath-catalog (things revealed, things built, things burned) pre-scores the whole book: Book (2–3), House (4), roof/sky (5), sea (6) — creation and revelation side by side under oath.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[52:1]`** "By the Mount."
  * **Gloss:** The lead oath-exhibit: the Mount (Sinai by classical identification) named alone as the first witness — creation sworn before revelation (Book follows in v.2).
* **`[52:2]`** "And a Book inscribed."
  * **Gloss:** The second oath-exhibit: an inscribed Book — revealed scripture; some read the Quran itself (*Source: classical tafsir — verify*).
* **`[52:3]`** "In a published scroll."
  * **Gloss:** The scroll-image extends the Book-oath: sahifa manshur — "published scroll" is corpus-unique here (rg); task's "unrolled scrolls" ✓ in sense.
* **`[52:4]`** "And the frequented House."
  * **Gloss:** The third oath-exhibit: al-Bayt al-Ma'mur, the angel-visited House in the ascent-reports (*Source: classical tafsir/hadith geography — verify*); "frequented" is corpus-unique to this verse (rg).
* **`[52:5]`** "And the elevated roof."
  * **Gloss:** The fourth oath-exhibit: the sky as "elevated roof" (not "canopy" — the word canopy: 0 corpus hits); cf. `[50:6]` "the sky above them... constructed" — pattern only.
* **`[52:6]`** "And the seething sea."
  * **Gloss:** The fifth oath-exhibit: "seething sea" (not "swollen"); "seething" elsewhere in the corpus describes hell-water (`[44:46]` "the boiling of seething water", `[55:44]` "a seething bath" — rg), so the sea-oath already smells like the verdict.
* **`[52:7]`** "The punishment of your Lord is coming."
  * **Gloss:** The oath's sworn conclusion, first clause: future-certainty — the punishment named as inevitable.
* **`[52:8]`** "There is nothing to avert it."
  * **Gloss:** The oath's sworn conclusion, second clause: no-interceptor — the surah deletes every intermediary before deleting the disbelievers' intermediaries (cf. `[32:4]` "you have no master and no intercessor" — rg-pulled).
* **`[52:9]`** "On the Day when the heaven sways in agitation."
  * **Gloss:** The landscape-collapse, first element: heaven with a seismic sway (task's "swept away, an unimpeded dust" is not this edition's phrasing).
* **`[52:10]`** "And the mountains go into motion."
  * **Gloss:** Mountains mobilized as cosmic sign: verb-twin `[81:3]` "set in motion" (rg) — the fixed earth undone alongside the swaying heaven of v.9.
* **`[52:11]`** "Woe on that Day to the deniers."
  * **Gloss:** The woe-formula inaugurates the Fire-verdict: "on that Day" ties the condemnation to the landscape-collapse just named (9–10).
* **`[52:12]`** "Those who play with speculation."
  * **Gloss:** The deniers defined by their epistemic posture: khawd-family twin `[6:91]` "toying away in their speculation" (rg) — idle conjecture as the opposite of the oath-certainty above.
* **`[52:13]`** "The Day when they are shoved into the Fire of Hell forcefully."
  * **Gloss:** The verb is a hauling-shove: the Day is named twice (11, 13) around the accusation-line — frame-by-denial, close-by-fire.
* **`[52:14]`** ""This is the Fire which you used to deny."
  * **Gloss:** Direct speech from the Fire itself: the denial-echo formula ("which you used to deny") mirrors `[44:50]` "This is what you used to doubt" (rg) — the accused confronted by their own rejected warning.
* **`[52:15]`** "Is this magic, or do you not see?"
  * **Gloss:** Sihr repurposed as eschatological taunt: what they called the revelation in life (`[38:4]`, `[51:39]` — rg) is now the only lens left for their burning — sight-language fails at the moment of proof.
* **`[52:16]`** "Burn in it. Whether you are patient, or impatient, it is the same for you. You are only being repaid for what you used to do.”"
  * **Gloss:** The patience-market closes: here sabr has no yield (contrast the Garden's patience-payoffs across the corpus — k-sabr's ledger; this is its threat-side); the repayment formula "for what you used to do" — the corpus's retributive refrain: `[16:32]` (enter Paradise for what you used to do), `[27:90]`, `[37:39]`, `[45:28]`, `[66:7]` (all rg — same English clause, opposite signs).
* **`[52:17]`** "But the righteous will be amid gardens and bliss."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict-pivot: "amid gardens and bliss" — phrasing-twin `[51:15]`, `[15:45]` (rg) — the righteous placed inside the reward as the Fire's deniers were placed inside punishment.
* **`[52:18]`** "Enjoying what their Lord has given them, and their Lord has spared them the suffering of Hell."
  * **Gloss:** Two gifts in one breath: enjoyment of what is given, plus the subtraction of Hell's suffering — safety precedes luxury, defining the Garden first by what it removes.
* **`[52:19]`** "Eat and drink happily, for what you used to do."
  * **Gloss:** The imperative of enjoyment with a causal-clause: the labor credited (task's "your labor appreciated" is other editions' wording; Itani's engine is this exact "for what you used to do" formula — nearest systematic kin 16:32's angelic greeting, rg-verified).
* **`[52:20]`** "Relaxing on luxurious furnishings; and We will couple them with gorgeous spouses."
  * **Gloss:** Reclining on "luxurious furnishings" — verbatim-twin `[56:15]` "On luxurious furnishings", `[83:35]` same (rg); "couple them with gorgeous spouses" — task's "marry them to fair-eyed companions" is another edition: "gorgeous" is corpus-unique here (rg; "fair-eyed" gets 0 hits anywhere — verified).
* **`[52:21]`** "Those who believed, and their offspring followed them in faith—We will unite them with their offspring, and We will not deprive them of any of their works. Every person is hostage to what he has earned."
  * **Gloss:** The offspring-reunion (the task's "parents' fate scene" inverted — in this surah children join parents, not the reverse; "my Lord, they are my family" = 0 corpus hits; the parent-grief scenes live in Noah/Lot: `[11:45]`, `[26:169]` — rg): faith-continuity reunites the household; the hostage-clause is verbatim-twin of `[74:38]` "Every soul is hostage to what it has earned" (rg) — reunion is by grace, the account is personal; the verse holds both in one breath.
* **`[52:22]`** "And We will supply them with fruit, and meat; such as they desire."
  * **Gloss:** Menu of desire-satisfaction: fruit and meat supplied without limit — the Garden's provision answers the Fire's deprivation.
* **`[52:23]`** "They will exchange therein a cup; wherein is neither harm, nor sin."
  * **Gloss:** The wine-cup purified of its two earthly costs — "neither harm, nor sin" (afth... ithm); the cup-cleaners' twins: `[37:47]` "Never polluted, and never intoxicating", `[56:19]` "Causing them neither headache, nor intoxication" (rg).
* **`[52:24]`** "Serving them will be youths like hidden pearls."
  * **Gloss:** The pearl-boy imagery — "hidden," i.e. stored, untouched: the Garden's valuables are vault-kept — family-twins `[56:23]` "The likenesses of treasured pearls", `[37:49]` (the fruit "as if they were closely guarded pearls" — rg).
* **`[52:25]`** "And they will approach one another, inquiring."
  * **Gloss:** Society, not solitude: they come toward each other asking (ta'arud? — mutual approach-and-question: task's "facing each other" — this edition renders "approach one another, inquiring" — the dialogue the saved had in 26–28 is what they now tell one another).
* **`[52:26]`** "They will say, "Before this, we were fearful for our families."
  * **Gloss:** The saved ones' testimony begins: the honest memory of fear "for our families" — the very family-attachment of v.21 now read as piety's root: they feared *for* them, so they are *with* them.
* **`[52:27]`** "But Allah blessed us, and spared us the agony of the Fiery Winds."
  * **Gloss:** The testimony continues: divine blessing named as rescue; "the agony of the Fiery Winds" — the wind-name back: the same wind that made Aad "decayed ruins" (`[51:41]`, rg) is what they were spared; fear-for-family was the ticket out of family-reunion.
* **`[52:28]`** "Before this, we used to pray to Him. He is the Good, the Compassionate.”"
  * **Gloss:** The closing line of the entry-testimony: worship here, names there — "the Good" (ra'uf — Itani's divine-adjective rendering, near-unique) + "the Compassionate" (rahim); the Garden's last word about God is two mercy-names: k-shukr's circuit (recognized favor → acknowledged Giver) completed at the gate.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **Kitab manshur (inscribed book, published scroll):** 52:2–3.
  * **Rendering:** "a Book inscribed. In a published scroll" — task's "unrolled scrolls" ✓ in sense (sahifa manshur); the scroll-image is the corpus's only "published scroll" (rg).
  * **Elsewhere:** the Book-oath kin: `[50:1]` "By the Glorious Quran", `[50:4]` "a comprehensive book", `[68:1]` "By the Pen... you are, by the grace of your Lord, not insane" (68:1-2 oath-by-Kitab + insult-audit parallel — not quoted; pattern: [k-kalam](../../02-knowledge/202610132005-k-kalam_allah_revelation_as_speech.md)).
* **rahāhīn (hostage):** 52:21 — "Every person is hostage to what he has earned."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[74:38]` verbatim twin (rg) — the hostage-clause sits, in both surahs, beside the ledger-image (74:37 "it neither leaves nor overlooks" family) — a single corpus-law: every self is pledged to its own account (Source: observation on the two co-located lines).
* **sihr (magic):** 52:15 — "Is this magic, or do you not see?"
  * **Rendering:** the eschatological mirror of the life-accusations: `[38:4]` "a lying magician", `[51:39]` "a sorcerer or a madman" (rg) — what they called the warning is what the Fire turns out to be in their eyes; the word's reversal is the verse's whole rhetoric.
* **gratitude-vocabulary:** 52:19 "for what you used to do" + 26–28 "Allah blessed us... we used to pray to Him" — no "thank" word-form occurs in s052 (rg-verified): the thankfulness is enacted in testimony, not named — the shukr-adjacency the task asked about is structural (see k-shukr's "acknowledging the Giver" definition applied to 52:28).
