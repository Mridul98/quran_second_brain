# Plagues, promise, and first idols at the sea (7:130–141)
**UID:** 202610141436
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:130–141 (Ruku 16 of 24 in al-A'raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:130]` – `[7:141]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The full plague cycle runs on one mechanism — each lifting is bought with a promise that is then broken (7:134–135) — until the drowning fulfills "the fair promise" to the Israelites through their *endurance* (7:137); and the very next scene, before the sea's shore is dry of gratitude, asks Moses for a god like the pagans' statues (7:138), showing the liberated are heirs of the same disease.
* **Placement:** The climax of the Pharaoh confrontation (7:103–157) and the pivot of the whole Moses dossier: vengeance-and-inheritance closes the oppressor's story, but 7:138–141 opens the freed people's story — the calf (7:148) is next horizon. The surah's thesis — covenant-breakers recur — turns here from Egypt to Israel.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:130]`** "And We afflicted the people of Pharaoh with barren years, and with shortage of crops, that they may take heed."
  * **Gloss:** Pressure first (the 7:94 pattern): famine economics as a summons to heed.
* **`[7:131]`** "When something good came their way, they said, “This is ours.” And when something bad happened to them, they ascribed the evil omen to Moses and those with him. In fact, their omen is with Allah, but most of them do not know."
  * **Gloss:** The double pathology: prosperity breeds claim ("This is ours"), adversity breeds superstition — and the omen-projectors are told whose omen they actually are.
* **`[7:132]`** "And they said, “No matter what sign you bring us, to bewitch us with, we will not believe in you.”"
  * **Gloss:** A pre-emptive refusal of every future sign — the 7:101 seal-logic spoken in first person.
* **`[7:133]`** "So We let loose upon them the flood, and the locusts, and the lice, and the frogs, and blood—all explicit signs—but they were too arrogant. They were a sinful people."
  * **Gloss:** The five-plague list, each called an *explicit sign* — the counterpart to 7:132's refusal.
* **`[7:134]`** "Whenever a plague befell them, they would say, “O Moses, pray to your Lord for us, according to the covenant He made with you. If you lift the plague from us, we will believe in you, and let the Children of Israel go with you.”"
  * **Gloss:** Negotiated repentance, invoking Moses' covenant with God as the instrument — bargain piety.
* **`[7:135]`** "But when We lifted the plague from them, for a term they were to fulfill, they broke their promise."
  * **Gloss:** The cycle snaps: lifted "for a term they were to fulfill" — the respite had a deadline; the promise breaks.
* **`[7:136]`** "So We took vengeance on them, and drowned them in the sea—because they rejected Our signs, and paid no heed to them."
  * **Gloss:** The sea becomes the verdict; the causal clause restates the crime as sign-rejection plus heedlessness.
* **`[7:137]`** "And We made the oppressed people inherit the eastern and western parts of the land, which We had blessed. Thus the fair promise of your Lord to the Children of Israel was fulfilled, because of their endurance. And We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people had built, and what they had harvested."
  * **Gloss:** Inheritance for the *mustad'afun* (the oppressed), conditioned on *endurance*; Pharaoh's building-and-harvesting economy erased in the same verse.
* **`[7:138]`** "And We delivered the Children of Israel across the sea. And when they came upon a people who were devoted to some statues of theirs, they said, “O Moses, make for us a god, as they have gods.” He said, “You are truly an ignorant people.”"
  * **Gloss:** The miracle of liberation meets instant idol-appetite; Moses' rebuke is blunt: *ignorant people*.
* **`[7:139]`–`[7:140]`** "“What these people are concerned with is perdition, and their deeds are based on falsehoods.” / He said, “Shall I seek for you a god other than Allah, when He has favored you over all other people?”"
  * **Gloss:** The devoted-to-statues are bankrupt by definition; and the request insults the very favor just executed (the crossing).
* **`[7:141]`** "Remember how We saved you from Pharaoh’s people, who subjected you to the worst of sufferings—killing your sons and sparing your women. In that was a tremendous trial from your Lord."
  * **Gloss:** Memory-arrest: the Exodus is reframed as *ni'mah + bala'* — favor and trial in one event (verbatim twin of 2:49's closing).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`ta'ir` (omen):** "they ascribed the evil omen to Moses" `[7:131]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[27:47]` "We consider you an ill omen" / "Your omen is with Allah" — same debate, "ill" vs "evil" rendering drift; `[36:18]` "We see an evil omen in you" — the omen-accusation is the corpus's standard anti-messenger speech (three sites, all addressed to messengers).
* **The plague list:** "the flood, and the locusts, and the lice, and the frogs, and blood" `[7:133]` — the only enumerated five in the corpus (all other sites mention parts: `[20:78]` the sea, `[7:64]` Noah's drowning).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:48]` "seven difficult ones, which will consume what you have stored" — the Joseph-cycle famine twin of `[7:130]` "barren years... shortage of crops".
* **`wa'd` (promise):** "the fair promise of your Lord... was fulfilled, because of their endurance" `[7:137]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[40:55]` "The promise of Allah is true" — the divine-promise as patience-anchored, exactly 7:137's grammar; within surah, `[7:49]` "Are these the ones you swore Allah will not touch with mercy?" — the promise inverted at the Elevations scene.
* **`bala'` (trial):** "a tremendous trial from your Lord" `[7:141]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:49]` — verbatim identical phrase (same event, second telling); within surah `[7:155]` "This is but Your test" (ahead) and `[7:168]` "We tested them with fortunes and misfortunes" — trial is the dossier's recurring frame.

* **Parallel:** `[2:115]` "To Allah belong the East and the West" — the east-west land of 7:137 as Allah's property.

* **Within surah (foreshadow):** `[7:138]`'s "make for us a god" is answered by the calf of 7:148 (next ruku-group) — the request precedes the event in the surah's order.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus). The promise-then-break plague bargaining at 7:134–135 is the surah's case study for the covenant note's "renewed by grace" pattern applied to a pagan court.
* **Source:** reading "for a term they were to fulfill" (7:135) as a fixed respite (ajal) is classical (per the phrase's use in 7:185) — *agent inference — verify*.
