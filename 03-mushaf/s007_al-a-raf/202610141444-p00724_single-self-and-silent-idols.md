# A single self, silent partners, and the listening (7:189–206)
**UID:** 202610141444
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:189–206 (Ruku 24 of 24 in al-A'raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:189]` – `[7:206]` (18 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah closes by running tawhid through the whole human stack: from the single self and its mate, where even grateful parents set up partners in what God gave (7:189–190), through the interrogation of silent idols that create nothing and answer nobody (7:191–198), to the Prophet's own etiquette manual — tolerance, refuge from Satan, and listening when the Quran is recited (7:199–205) — ending where the book began its creation story: the angels at His presence, too humble ever to refuse the bow (7:206).
* **Placement:** The final ruku of al-A'raf answers its first movement: the Adam-prologue's bowing command (7:11) and Satan's refusal receive their resolution in the last verse's prostration (7:206); the Eden temptation (7:20–22) and the post-Eden shirk (7:190) rhyme; the recitation-ethic of 7:204 closes the surah that opened "A Scripture was revealed to you — let there be no anxiety" (7:2).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:189]`** "It is He who created you from a single person, and made from it its mate, that he may find comfort with her. Then, when he has covered her, she conceives a light load, and she carries it around. But when she has grown heavy, they pray to Allah their Lord, “if You give us a good child, we will be among the thankful.”"
  * **Gloss:** From ontology to intimacy to anxiety: the single-origin clause becomes the pregnancy vow — gratitude pledged in advance.
* **`[7:190]`** "But when He has given them a good child, they attribute partners to Him in what He has given them. Allah is exalted above what they associate."
  * **Gloss:** The vow breaks at the moment of answer — partners set up *inside the thank-you*; the surah's covenant-mechanism (pledge → bounty → breach) at its most personal.
* **`[7:191]`–`[7:192]`** "Do they idolize those who create nothing, and are themselves created? / And can neither help them, nor help their own selves?"
  * **Gloss:** The two-test argument: no creation, no aid — against the made things.
* **`[7:193]`** "And if you invite them to guidance, they will not follow you. It is the same for you, whether you invite them, or remain silent."
  * **Gloss:** The silence-equivalence: the invited-idols never answer, so the inviter gains nothing either way — a mock of the worshippers' own practice.
* **`[7:194]`** "Those you call upon besides Allah are servants like you. So call upon them, and let them answer you, if you are truthful."
  * **Gloss:** "Servants like you" — the partners are fellow creatures, not patrons; the challenge: make them answer.
* **`[7:195]`** "Do they have feet with which they walk? Or do they have hands with which they strike? Or do they have eyes with which they see? Or do they have ears with which they hear? Say, “Call upon your partners, then plot against me, and do not wait.”"
  * **Gloss:** The anatomy audit (feet/hands/eyes/ears) against 7:179's dead-hearted organs; then the open dare: plot now.
* **`[7:196]`** "“My Master is Allah, He Who sent down the Book, and He takes care of the righteous.”"
  * **Gloss:** The warner's counter-plot: tawakkul grounded in the sent-down Book — 7:2's revelation now cited as protection.
* **`[7:197]`–`[7:198]`** "Those you call upon besides Him cannot help you, nor can they help themselves. / And if you call them to guidance, they will not hear. And you see them looking at you, yet they do not see."
  * **Gloss:** The repeat-with-inversion: now they cannot hear — "you see them looking at you, yet they do not see": statue-eyes as the surah's final image of false patrons.
* **`[7:199]`** "Be tolerant, and command decency, and avoid the ignorant."
  * **Gloss:** Three-line community etiquette: take the easy path, enjoin what is fair and known, withdraw from the ignorant — the surah's whole ma'ruf-motif condensed (cf. 7:156–157, 7:164–165).
* **`[7:200]`–`[7:201]`** "And when a suggestion from Satan assails you, take refuge with Allah. He is Hearing and Knowing. / Those who are righteous—when an impulse from Satan strikes them, they remind themselves, and immediately see clearly."
  * **Gloss:** The refuge protocol for the Prophet and the self-repair protocol for the muttaqun — the 7:20 whisper-cycle gets its answer-song.
* **`[7:202]`** "But their brethren lead them relentlessly into error, and they never stop short."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-type: Satan's kin push error without pause — contrast with the remind-and-see of 7:201.
* **`[7:203]`** "If you do not produce a miracle for them, they say, “Why don’t you improvise one.” Say, “I only follow what is inspired to me from my Lord.” These are insights from your Lord, and guidance, and mercy, for a people who believe."
  * **Gloss:** The demand to manufacture signs (cf. Pharaoh's "present it" 7:106) answered by the pure passivity of wahy — and this Book rebilled as basirah/hudan/rahmah, the Tablets' own résumé (7:154, 7:52).
* **`[7:204]`** "When the Quran is recited, listen to it, and pay attention, so that you may experience mercy."
  * **Gloss:** The liturgical close-in-miniature: recitation → silence-attentiveness → mercy; the only other "pay attention" in the corpus is 27:62's rebuke.
* **`[7:205]`** "And remember your Lord within yourself, humbly and fearfully, and quietly, in the morning and the evening, and do not be of the neglectful."
  * **Gloss:** Private dhikr, low-voice, twice daily — the interior counterpart of 7:204's public listening; cf. 7:55's "humbly and privately" command.
* **`[7:206]`** "Those who are in the presence of your Lord are not too proud to worship Him. They recite His praises, and to Him they bow down."
  * **Gloss:** The angels: not-too-proud (against 7:13 Iblis's arrogance), praising, bowing — the surah ends in the prostration that was commanded at its Adam-prologue.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"a single person" (nafs wahida):** `[7:189]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:1]` "created you from a single soul, and created from it its mate" (verified); `[39:6]` "created you from one person, then made from it its mate" (verified); `[6:98]` "produced you from a single person" (verified) — one origin-formula across the book; note the soul/person rendering drift.
* **"partners":** "they attribute partners to Him" `[7:190]`, "Call upon your partners" `[7:195]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:66]` "Those who invoke other than Allah do not follow partners; they follow only assumptions" — the partners demoted to assumption (verified); k-tawhid's whole dossier.
* **"servants like you":** `[7:194]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:55]` "they serve what neither profits them nor harms them" — the profit/harm test (verified); the idol-serving verb of `[7:194]` ("call upon") is the same grammar 7:197–198 then exposes.
* **"morning and evening" dhikr:** `[7:205]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[18:28]` "those who pray to their Lord morning and evening" (verified); `[33:42]` "glorify Him morning and evening" (verified); `[40:55]` "proclaim the praise of your Lord evening and morning" (verified) — the bidaily remembrance is a corpus-wide liturgy; within surah `[7:5]` "by night, or while they were napping" — seizure comes to the un-remembering at these same hours.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus) — the closing etiquette verses (7:199–206) were classically prized as compendium-verses (Ibn Kathir's transmitted "comprehensive verse" report on 7:199 — *Source: classical report — verify wording*).
