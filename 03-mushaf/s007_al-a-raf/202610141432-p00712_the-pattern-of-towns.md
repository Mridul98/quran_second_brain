# The pattern of the towns: humility bought, prosperity spent, plan made (7:94–99)
**UID:** 202610141432
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:94–99 (Ruku 12 of 24 in al-A’raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:94]` – `[7:99]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The five town-narratives pause for their law: every prophet sent to any town was followed by affliction (misery and adversity, to bend them downward) and then exchange (prosperity in place of hardship) — until the good times wrote off the test as hereditary ("adversity and prosperity has touched our ancestors") and seizure came suddenly, unawares; the ruku then issues the two security questions — by night, by day — and one unanswerable: none feels safe from Allah's plan except losing people.
* **Placement:** The coda of the destruction-cycle (ruku 8–11) and the closing bracket of the surah's first movement: 7:4's "Our might came upon them by night, or while they were napping" is re-asked as 7:97–98's warning — the ruku turns narrative back into question for the Meccan audience. Ruku 13 opens the Moses/Pharaoh cycle (7:103ff, out of range).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:94]`** "We did not send any prophet to any town but We afflicted its people with misery and adversity, so that they may humble themselves."
  * **Gloss:** The universal of the cycle: prophecy implies pressure; the target of affliction is tadarru' — humility, not information.
* **`[7:95]`** "Then We substituted prosperity in place of hardship. Until they increased in number, and said, “Adversity and prosperity has touched our ancestors.” Then We seized them suddenly, while they were unaware."
  * **Gloss:** The exchange and the rationalization: the test gets inherited away ("routine for the bloodline"), population grows, and the seizure files no notice — 7:28's ancestor-excuse wearing work-clothes.
* **`[7:96]`** "Had the people of the towns believed and turned righteous, We would have opened for them the blessings of the heaven and the earth; but they rejected the truth, so We seized them by what they were doing."
  * **Gloss:** The counterfactual economy: faith + rectitude = open sky-and-soil blessings (the 7:57–58 rain-parable's policy stated); seizure follows the crime's own manufacture.
* **`[7:97]`** "Do the people of the towns feel secure that Our might will not come upon them by night, while they sleep?"
  * **Gloss:** Security-question #1, recycling 7:4's night-and-nap frame onto the present tense.
* **`[7:98]`** "Do the people of the towns feel secure that Our might will not come upon them by day, while they play?"
  * **Gloss:** Security-question #2: the day-shift of distraction ("while they play") — the heedlessness 7:51 indicted in jest-religion terms.
* **`[7:99]`** "Do they feel safe from Allah’s plan? None feel safe from Allah’s plan except the losing people."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict: makr — Allah's counter-plan — is the one security gap no town closes; "the losing people" (khāsirūn, 7:9 and 7:90's word) is the only class that sleeps well.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"misery and adversity" / "prosperity in place of hardship":** the trial pair.
  * **Rendering:** 7:94 "afflicted its people with misery and adversity"; 7:95 "We substituted prosperity in place of hardship" — the exact pair-phrases occur only here in this rendering (`rg`-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:42]` near-twin — "We afflicted them with suffering and hardship, that they may humble themselves"; `[21:35]` "We burden you with adversity and prosperity—a test"; `[23:76]` "gripped them with suffering, but they did not surrender...nor did they humble themselves" — the same failed curriculum.
* **"humble themselves" (tadarruʿ):** the wanted output.
  * **Rendering:** "so that they may humble themselves" (`[7:94]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[11:23]` the believers' standing trait "humble themselves before their Lord"; `[21:90]` Abraham's family "used to humble themselves to Us"; `[23:76]` its absence as indictment — the verb tracks the whole cycle.
* **"feel secure / feel safe":** the forbidden emotion.
  * **Rendering:** "Do the people of the towns feel secure..." (7:97–98); "Do they feel safe from Allah's plan?" (7:99).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[16:45]` "Do those who scheme evils feel secure that Allah will not cause the earth to cave in"; `[12:107]` "Do they feel secure that...the Hour will not come upon them suddenly, while they are unaware?" — verbatim partner of 7:95's sudden-unaware formula (also `[29:53]`, `[43:66]`).
* **"Allah's plan" (makr):** the counter-scheme.
  * **Rendering:** "None feel safe from Allah's plan" (`[7:99]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:54]` "they planned, and Allah planned; but Allah is the Best of planners" — makr returned at Allah as superlative; `[27:50]` "They planned a plan, and We planned a plan, but they did not notice"; `[7:183]` = `[68:45]` "And I will encourage them. My plan is firm" — the surah's own later re-quote of this verdict.
