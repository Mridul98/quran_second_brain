# The calf in his absence, the brothers' prayer (7:148–151)
**UID:** 202610141438
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:148–151 (Ruku 18 of 24 in al-A'raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:148]` – `[7:151]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The forty-night appointment is betrayed within the same absence: a lowing ornament-calf is worshipped, regret comes only after ("unless our Lord extends His mercy"), and Moses returns angry enough to throw the Tablets and drag his brother's head — until the two brothers' shared prayer, "forgive me and my brother," converts the scene from rupture to intercession.
* **Placement:** The immediate payoff of 7:138's request ("make for us a god, as they have gods") and the emotional center of the Moses dossier; the Tablets' arc (inscribed 7:145 → thrown here → re-taken 7:154) is the surah's revelation-as-object drama in four verses.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:148]`** "In his absence, the people of Moses adopted a calf made from their ornaments— a body which lowed. Did they not see that it could not speak to them, nor guide them in any way? They took it for worship. They were in the wrong."
  * **Gloss:** The idol is defined by its materials (melted ornaments), its only motion (a lowing sound), and its two disqualifications — cannot speak, cannot guide; the corpus's mid-dash spacing "ornaments— a body" reproduced verbatim.
* **`[7:149]`** "Then, when they regretted, and realized that they had erred, they said, “Unless our Lord extends His mercy to us, and forgives us, we will be among the losers.”"
  * **Gloss:** Repentance formula near-identical to Adam's at 7:23 — the surah deliberately rhymes the first sin with this one.
* **`[7:150]`** "And when Moses returned to his people, angry and disappointed, he said, “What an awful thing you did in my absence. Did you forsake the commandments of your Lord so hastily?” And he threw down the tablets; and he took hold of his brother’s head, dragging him towards himself. He said, “Son of my mother, the people have overpowered me, and were about to kill me; so do not allow the enemies to gloat over me, and do not count me among the unjust people.”"
  * **Gloss:** Anger and grief in one gesture-set: Tablets thrown, brother seized; Aaron's defense is a plea about blame — "do not count me among the unjust" — the regent overwhelmed (cf. his deputy-charge 7:142).
* **`[7:151]`** "He said, “My Lord, forgive me and my brother, and admit us into Your mercy; for you are the Most Merciful of the merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** Moses' anger collapses into a dual forgiveness prayer — the surah's cleanest repentance line, and the setup for 7:153's open mercy clause.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"lowed":** "a body which lowed" `[7:148]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:88]` "a mere body which lowed" — the verb occurs only in these two calf scenes (verified); Itani's choice makes the idol's one animistic trait its own indictment.
* **"calf" (`ijl`):** "adopted a calf made from their ornaments" `[7:148]`; "Those who idolized the calf" `[7:152]` (next ruku).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:51]` "you took to worshiping the calf after him" — the forty-nights→calf sequence verbatim-twin of this ruku's frame; `[2:93]` "their hearts became filled with the love of the calf" — Baqarah locates the sin in the heart, this ruku in the absence.
* **"could not speak... nor guide":** the double test `[7:148]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:88]` adds the taunt "the god of Moses, but he has forgotten"; within surah `[7:191]`–`[7:198]` (final ruku) — the same mute-idol argument reused against the Meccan partners — the surah bookends its Moses episode and its closing creed with one test (observable structure).
* **"Son of my mother":** Aaron's address `[7:150]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:94]` — same address, more physical detail: "do not seize me by my beard or my head"; the two dossier-versions cross-confirm the head-grab (verified pair).
* **"Most Merciful of the merciful":** `[7:151]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:64]`, `[12:92]`, `[21:83]` — three other sites, all in supplication contexts (Joseph's forgiveness prayer, Job's lament) — the phrase is the corpus's signature for desperate, hopeful petitions.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Within-corpus pair:** `[7:23]` — Adam's "Unless You forgive us, and have mercy on us" echoed almost verbatim at 7:149; the surah frames Israel's calf as humanity's first sin re-staged.
* **Parallel:** `[20:88]`–`[20:94]` — the Ta-Ha dossier: same calf, same brothers, fuller Samiri detail absent here (observable: al-A'raf never names the calf-maker).
