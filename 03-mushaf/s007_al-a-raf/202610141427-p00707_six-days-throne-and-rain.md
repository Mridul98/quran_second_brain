# Creation, command, and rain: sovereignty with manners (7:54–58)
**UID:** 202610141427
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:54–58 (Ruku 7 of 24 in al-A’raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:54]` – `[7:58]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** After the two homes and the Elevations, the surah lifts the camera to the cosmos: six days, the Throne, night chasing day, sun/moon/stars conscripted — "His is the creation, and His is the command" — and the only proper responses are etiquette in prayer (humbly, privately, no corruption, no excess) and reading the rain: dead land revived is the sermon that ends with "Thus We bring out the dead—perhaps you will reflect."
* **Placement:** The creedal keystone between the A‘rāf visions (ruku 5–6) and the prophet-cycle (ruku 8–11): every messenger that follows will preach inside this sovereignty map ("Lord of the Worlds," Noah's and Hud's phrase, is defined here at 7:54).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:54]`** "Your Lord is Allah; He who created the heavens and the earth in six days, then established Himself on the Throne. The night overtakes the day, as it pursues it persistently; and the sun, and the moon, and the stars are subservient by His command. His is the creation, and His is the command. Blessed is Allah, Lord of all beings."
  * **Gloss:** (The mid-sentence capital "He who" is book typography [sic].) The tawhid syllabus in five movements: identity, six-day creation, throne, astronomical conscription, and the two-lane claim — creation and command belong to Him alone.
* **`[7:55]`** "Call upon your Lord humbly and privately. He does not love the aggressors."
  * **Gloss:** Sovereignty breeds etiquette, not fear-mongering: low-voice worship; "aggressors" — in prayer as in war — are the opposite style.
* **`[7:56]`** "And do not corrupt on earth after its reformation, and pray to Him with fear and hope. Allah’s mercy is close to the doers of good."
  * **Gloss:** Social etiquette completes the personal one: the anti-corruption clause (the surah's recurring iṣlāḥ word, echoed for Saleh and Shuaib), then the two-engine prayer — rahba and raghba.
* **`[7:57]`** "It is He who sends the wind ahead of His mercy. Then, when they have gathered up heavy clouds, We drive them to a dead land, where We make water come down, and with it We bring out all kinds of fruits. Thus We bring out the dead—perhaps you will reflect."
  * **Gloss:** Weather is not weather: winds are mercy's outriders, clouds are drafted, dead land is the resurrection classroom.
* **`[7:58]`** "As for the good land, it yields its produce by the leave of its Lord. But as for the bad, it produces nothing but hardship and misery. Thus We explain the revelations in various ways for people who are thankful."
  * **Gloss:** The soil parable: identical rain, different ground — hearts as land; the Book's detailing (7:52) aims at gratitude.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"established Himself on the Throne" (istiwāʾ):**
  * **Rendering:** "then established Himself on the Throne" (`[7:54]`) — but "settled over the Throne" at `[10:3]` and `[57:4]`, "settled on the Throne" at `[25:59]` (observable drift); `[32:4]` matches 7:54 verbatim.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[11:7]` "—and His Throne was upon the waters—in order to test you" adds the test-frame; the how-reading is classically deferred — *Source: classical dispute on the attributes (tafwid vs ta'wil) — verify.*
* **"subservient" (taskhkhir):** conscripted cosmos.
  * **Rendering:** "the sun, and the moon, and the stars are subservient by His command" (`[7:54]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[22:65]` "Allah made everything on earth subservient to you" — the same favor-verb; the cosmic conscripts of 7:54 will answer to no one else, so none may be worshipped.
* **"corrupt...after its reformation" (fasād/ṣalāḥ):** the political clause.
  * **Rendering:** 7:56 "do not corrupt on earth after its reformation"; the twin idiom reappears for Shuaib as "do not corrupt the land once it has been set right" (`[7:85]`) and negatively for Saleh's people "roam the earth corruptingly" (`[7:74]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:11]` "they say, 'We are only reformers'" — the corruptionist's self-label, the exact foil of 7:56; `[26:152]` "Who spread turmoil on earth, and do not reform" — the same fasād/ṣalāḥ contrast.
* **"dead land" (mawt):** resurrection's proof-case.
  * **Rendering:** "We drive them to a dead land, where We make water come down" (`[7:57]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[35:9]` near-verbatim twin — winds→clouds→dead land→"Likewise is the Resurrection"; `[43:11]` "so We revive thereby a dead land. Thus you will be brought out"; `[25:49]`, `[36:33]` — the same lesson repeated.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Creed twin:** `[10:3]` — "Your Lord is Allah, who created the heavens and the earth in six days, then settled over the Throne, governing all things. There is no intercessor except after His permission" — Yunus opens with the same sentence as 7:54 and its intercession-clause answers 7:53's doomed wish (ruku 6).
* **Verbatim pair:** `[32:4]` — 7:54's exact phrase plus "Apart from Him, you have no master and no intercessor" — tawhid's two exclusions (no wali, no shafi) stated from the Throne.
* **Prayer etiquette:** `[7:205]` — "remember your Lord within yourself, humbly and fearfully, and quietly" — the surah's coda restates 7:55's adab (within-surah inclusio across 150 ayahs).
* **Within surah:** `[7:56]` ↔ `[7:74]`, `[7:85]` — the iṣlāḥ clause is the prophets' shared constitution; `[7:58]` "people who are thankful" ↔ `[7:10]` "rarely do you give thanks" — the opening accusation closes here as the parable's aim.
* **Aggressors:** `[2:190]` and `[5:87]` — "Allah does not love the aggressors" verbatim — 7:55's clause in its war-law and diet-law settings.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan; the six-days/Throne/cosmos formula is a recurring Meccan creed-paragraph (10:3, 11:7, 25:59, 32:4, 50:38, 57:4 — all observable in corpus).
* **Interpretive caution:** "His is the creation, and His is the command" is classically read as separating what belongs to Allah by making vs by legislating — *Source: agent observation on Itani's rendering of 7:54; classical usage (e.g. in tafsir of the 'amr/khalq distinction) — verify.*
