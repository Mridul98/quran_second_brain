# Two garments and one enemy: the dress code of revelation (7:26–31)
**UID:** 202610141423
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:26–31 (Ruku 3 of 24 in al-A’raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:26]` – `[7:31]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Immediately after the fall, Allah answers the leaves-of-the-Garden improvisation with a double provision — body-covering and the "clothing of piety, that is best" — then warns the children of Adam about the enemy who strips, refutes the "our parents did it / Allah commanded it" excuse, and re-issues the dress code as worship etiquette: adornment, prayer, moderation.
* **Placement:** The hinge between the Adam-drama (ruku 2) and the law-of-lifestyle passage (ruku 4, 7:32–33): the garment theology here becomes the surah's running metaphor for revelation itself (its ruku opening is the dress of 7:26).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:26]`** "O children of Adam! We have provided you with clothing to cover your bodies, and for luxury. But the clothing of piety—that is best. These are some of Allah’s revelations, so that they may take heed."
  * **Gloss:** The divine reply to 7:22's self-made coverings: textile grace (function + ornament) crowned by one better garment — taqwa — called "some of Allah's revelations."
* **`[7:27]`** "O Children of Adam! Do not let Satan seduce you, as he drove your parents out of the Garden, stripping them of their garments, to show them their nakedness. He sees you, him and his clan, from where you cannot see them. We have made the devils friends of those who do not believe."
  * **Gloss:** The trauma-memory turned warning: the same stripper is still operating, invisible-in-ambush; his "friends" (awliyā') are chosen, not just fallen to.
* **`[7:28]`** "And when they commit an indecency, they say, “We found our parents doing this, and Allah has commanded us to do it.” Say, “Allah does not command indecencies. Are you attributing to Allah what you do not know?”"
  * **Gloss:** The second sin after the first: laundering bad practice through ancestry and then through God — the attribution-crime the whole surah keeps naming (7:33, 7:37).
* **`[7:29]`** "Say, “My Lord commands justice, and to stand devoted at every place of worship. So call upon Him, and dedicate your faith to Him alone. Just as He originated you, so you will return.”"
  * **Gloss:** The counter-command: qist ("justice"), upright devotion, pure dedication — with return-to-Origin as its eschatological verb.
* **`[7:30]`** "Some He has guided, and some have deserved misguidance. They have adopted the devils for patrons rather than Allah, and they assume that they are guided."
  * **Gloss:** The split of the human field; the pathos clause — the deviled are "guided" in their own estimation (cf. 7:27's pattern and 2:14's "we were only ridiculing").
* **`[7:31]`** "O Children of Adam! Dress properly at every place of worship, and eat and drink, but do not be excessive. He does not love the excessive."
  * **Gloss:** The third "O Children of Adam" call closes the ruku where 7:26 opened it: adornment is worship, appetite is lawful, excess is the ban.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`libās` (clothing/garments):** the ruku's master metaphor.
  * **Rendering:** "clothing" (`[7:26]`), "garments" (`[7:27]`) — one Arabic-family word for body-cover, luxury, and the fallen parents' lost dress.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:142]` livestock "some for clothing"; `[16:81]` "garments to protect you from the heat, and garments to protect you from your violence" — the same double-provision theology of dress.
* **`libās al-taqwā` (the clothing of piety):** the best of the two garments.
  * **Rendering:** "the clothing of piety—that is best" (`[7:26]`) — unique phrase in this corpus rendering (verified by `rg "clothing of piety"`).
  * **Elsewhere:** taqwa is "the clothing" classically read either as good deeds or shame-consciousness (*Source: classical tafsir disagreement on 7:26 — verify*); pairs with the Sabr-and-taqwa note's "watchfulness that keeps the road visible."
* **`zīnah` (adornment/finery):** dressing as a command.
  * **Rendering:** "Dress properly" (`[7:31]`) and "Allah’s finery" (`[7:32]`) — same root across the ruku boundary, one word two renderings.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[24:60]` "provided they do not flaunt their finery"; `[67:5]` "We have adorned the lower heaven with lanterns"; the Garden's bracelets-and-silk pictures (`[18:31]`, `[35:33]`) are zīnah's final form.
* **`fāḥishah` (indecency):** the named wrong.
  * **Rendering:** "an indecency/indecencies" (`[7:28]`), but "immoralities" (`[7:33]`) and "lewdness" (`[7:80]`) — three English renderings of the same root-family inside this surah (observable corpus fact via rg).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:151]` "do not come near indecencies, whether outward or inward" ≈ 7:33's "open and secret"; `[29:45]` "The prayer prevents indecencies and evils" — the antidote to 7:28's excuse.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan; the "place of worship"/dress-and-appetite rulings address a cultic milieu in which circumambulation practice is classically reported to have included nudity and self-imposed abstentions — *Source: classical reports on pre-Islāmic tawāf practice (widely transmitted, e.g. in Bukhari-type prayer collections) — verify wording in a printed edition before teaching.*
* **Structural fact (observable):** three consecutive "O Children of Adam!" addresses (7:26, 7:27, 7:31) bracket this ruku as the surah's direct-address to the species.
