# Untranslated letters, a Book without anxiety, and the just scales (7:1–10)
**UID:** 202610141421
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:1–10 (Ruku 1 of 24 in al-A’raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:1]` – `[7:10]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens with letters that refuse translation, then hands the Messenger a Book that must not weigh on his heart — warn with it — and flashes the end first: towns destroyed by night, messengers questioned, scales weighed justly; finally it drops the reader back onto the gift he is already standing in: "We have established you firmly on earth...but rarely do you give thanks."
* **Placement:** The threshold of the longest Meccan narrative surah. 7:1–3 = the Book's address, 7:4–9 = a flash-forward to reckoning, 7:10 = the earthly setup that ruku 2's Adam scene (`[7:11]`ff) immediately cashes in ("We created you, then We shaped you").

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:1]`** "Alif, Lam, Meem, Saad."
  * **Gloss:** Four letter-names, numbered as an ayah, deliberately untranslated — the surah's signature (see Fleeting note below; the same device opens 2:1, 3:1; "Saad" alone opens 38:1).
* **`[7:2]`** — two clause-chunks:
  * "A Scripture was revealed to you, so let there be no anxiety in your heart because of it."
  * "You are to warn with it—and a reminder for the believers."
  * **Gloss:** The commission with its emotional medicine: the Book's crushing weight is met by stating its origin ("revealed" — it is not his invention); its function splits — universal warning, particular comfort.
* **`[7:3]`** "Follow what is revealed to you from your Lord, and do not follow other masters beside Him. Little you recollect."
  * **Gloss:** The audience's mirror-command: follow revelation, take no other *awliyā'* ("masters") — and the mournful tail "Little you recollect" diagnoses the whole surah.
* **`[7:4]`** "How many a town have We destroyed? Our might came upon them by night, or while they were napping."
  * **Gloss:** History as precedent — destruction arrives at the two moments of unconsciousness: sleep and the noon nap.
* **`[7:5]`** "When Our might came upon them, their only cry was, “We were indeed wrongdoers.”"
  * **Gloss:** Their final honest sentence — confession with no cash value at that point; the timing is re-asked as a warning question at `[7:97]` (ruku 12).
* **`[7:6]`** "We will question those to whom messengers were sent, and We will question the messengers."
  * **Gloss:** A two-sided interview: preached and preachers both held to account.
* **`[7:7]`** "We will narrate to them with knowledge, for We were never absent."
  * **Gloss:** The reckoning is grounded in total witness — narration with divine knowledge, not inference.
* **`[7:8]`** "The scales on that Day will be just. Those whose weights are heavy—it is they who are the successful."
  * **Gloss:** The Day's arithmetic: a just balance, and success as weight.
* **`[7:9]`** "But as for those whose weights are light—it is they who have lost their souls, because they used to mistreat Our revelations."
  * **Gloss:** The loss is self-inflicted ("lost their souls"), and its charge is negligence *of the revelations* — tying the scales back to 7:2's Book.
* **`[7:10]`** "We have established you firmly on earth, and made for you in it livelihood—but rarely do you give thanks."
  * **Gloss:** From scales to soil: the same audience already living in un-thanked provision — the surah's gratitude motif and the direct lead-in to Adam's story.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **Muqatta'at (disconnected letters):** transliterated letter-names as ayah 1.
  * **Rendering:** "Alif, Lam, Meem, Saad." — untranslated, by program.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:1]` "Alif, Lam, Meem." and `[3:1]` the same; `[38:1]` "Saad. By the renowned Quran." — letters followed by a Book-oath, the pattern the Fleeting note tracks; letter-openings cluster around Scripture-claims.
* **`kitāb` (Scripture) / "revealed":** the Book defined by its channel.
  * **Rendering:** "A Scripture was revealed to you" (`[7:2]`), "Follow what is revealed to you" (`[7:3]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[40:2]` and `[46:2]` "The sending down of the Scripture is from Allah" (letter-opening + Book-claim pair); `[29:51]` "We revealed to you the Scripture...in that is mercy and a reminder for people who believe."
* **`mīzān` (scales) / weights:** the Day's justice instrument.
  * **Rendering:** "The scales on that Day will be just" (`[7:8]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[23:102–103]` almost verbatim twin — "Those whose scales are heavy—those are the successful. But those whose scales are light—those are they who have lost their souls"; `[101:6]`, `[101:8]` the same heavy/light split; `[21:47]` "the scales of justice."
* **`dhikr` (reminder) / *nadhir* (warner):** two audiences, one Book.
  * **Rendering:** "a reminder for the believers" (`[7:2]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[73:19]` "This is a reminder. So whoever wills, let him take a path to his Lord."; `[38:49]`, `[88:21]`; the reminder-word returns for Noah and Hud (`[7:63]`, `[7:69]`) — one thread from ruku 1 through the prophets.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Structural twin:** `[2:1–2]` — letters, then verbatim “This is the Book in which there is no doubt, a guide for the righteous”: the opening move of Baqarah (letters → Book → guidance) replayed here as letters → Book → warn/reminder.
* **Parallel:** `[16:93]` — "And you will surely be questioned about what you used to do" — 7:6's two-sided questioning given its general law.
* **Within surah (forward frame):** `[7:97]` — "Our might will not come upon them by night, while they sleep?" re-opens 7:4's destroyed towns as the surah's closing warning (ruku 12).
* **Within surah (forward):** `[7:172]` — the primordial question "Am I not your Lord?" is the covenant behind 7:3's "do not follow other masters beside Him": lordship was testified before it could be traded away (pairs with the Covenant knowledge note).
* **Answer-echo:** `[1:6]` — the Fatihah's petition for the straight path is here answered by a revealed Book to "follow" (7:3); the path is on sale, with scales to weigh it (7:8).
