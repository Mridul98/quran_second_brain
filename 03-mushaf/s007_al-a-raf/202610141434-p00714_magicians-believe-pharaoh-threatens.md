# The magicians believe, Pharaoh threatens (7:109–126)
**UID:** 202610141434
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:109–126 (Ruku 14 of 24 in al-A'raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:109]` – `[7:126]` (18 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The duel of the staff is decided not by spectacle but by whose side the spectators defect to: Pharaoh's hired magicians, who came asking a wage, end up the first believers of the Exodus narrative — and the tyrant's only reply is torture, met by a prayer for patience.
* **Placement:** The dramatic peak of the Moses dossier's first movement (7:103–157); the reversal (7:120–122 "the magicians fell to their knees") answers the surah's prologue: where Satan refused to bow to Adam (7:11–13), men whom Pharaoh sent to defeat Moses bow to the Lord of Moses and Aaron.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:109]`–`[7:110]`** "The notables among Pharaoh's people said, “This is really a skilled magician.” / “He wants to evict you from your land, so what do you recommend?”"
  * **Gloss:** The elite re-label the sign as *craft* and frame Moses as a political evictor — the dossier's recurring slander.
* **`[7:111]`–`[7:112]`** "They said, “Put him off, and his brother, and send heralds to the cities.” / “And let them bring you every skillful magician.”"
  * **Gloss:** The counter-program: delay the prophet, marshal the professionals.
* **`[7:113]`–`[7:114]`** "The magicians came to Pharaoh, and said, “Surely there is a reward for us, if we are the victors.” / He said, “Yes, and you will be among my favorites.”"
  * **Gloss:** Belief-as-wage is stated openly on Pharaoh's payroll — reward and proximity-for-hire.
* **`[7:115]`–`[7:116]`** "They said, “O Moses! Either you throw, or we are the ones to throw.” / He said, “You throw!” And when they threw, they beguiled the eyes of the people, and intimidated them, and produced a mighty magic."
  * **Gloss:** The showmanship first: ropes and staffs "beguile the eyes" — magic as intimidation, not truth.
* **`[7:117]`** "And We inspired Moses: “Throw your staff.” And at once, it swallowed what they were faking."
  * **Gloss:** Revelation (wahy to Moses) answers spectacle; "what they were faking" — the corpus's own verdict word.
* **`[7:118]`** "So the truth came to pass, and what they were producing came to nothing."
  * **Gloss:** The duel's verdict sentence: truth established, production nullified.
* **`[7:119]`** "There they were defeated, and utterly reduced."
  * **Gloss:** Location and humiliation: "there" — on their own ground.
* **`[7:120]`–`[7:122]`** "And the magicians fell to their knees. / They said, “We have believed in the Lord of the Worlds. / “The Lord of Moses and Aaron.”"
  * **Gloss:** Prostration and confession — the surah's first human believers in this arc; Aaron enters the titulary because the magicians know the commission (cf. 20:29–36 in the Ta-Ha version).
* **`[7:123]`–`[7:124]`** "Pharaoh said, “Did you believe in Him before I have given you permission? This is surely a conspiracy... You will surely know.” / “I will cut off your hands and your feet on opposite sides; then I will crucify you all.”"
  * **Gloss:** Permission-theology exposed: Pharaoh claims the prerogative of belief; the punishment is the hirabah-style limb-crucifixion list.
* **`[7:125]`–`[7:126]`** "They said, “It is to our Lord that we will return.” / “You are taking vengeance on us only because we have believed in the signs of our Lord when they have come to us.” “Our Lord! Pour out patience upon us, and receive our souls in submission.”"
  * **Gloss:** The converts' three-layer reply: return to God, name the tyranny, ask for *sabr* to the end — the first "Our Lord" prayer after belief in the dossier.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sihr` (magic):** the rival "sign" system.
  * **Rendering:** "a skilled magician" `[7:109]`, "every skillful magician" `[7:112]`, "a mighty magic" `[7:116]`, "what they were faking" `[7:117]` — one root, four renderings in nine verses (observable drift).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:66]` "their ropes and sticks appeared to him, because of their magic, to be crawling swiftly" — the same duel; `[7:132]` "to bewitch us with" (next ruku, the charge persists).
* **`sujud` (prostration):** 7:120's fall to the knees.
  * **Rendering:** "fell to their knees."
  * **Elsewhere (within surah):** `[7:11]` "Bow down before Adam" and `[7:13]` "Get down from it!" — the magicians' descent-in-worship inverts Iblis's descent-in-pride; the surah frames the whole arc by this verb.
* **`rabb al-'alamin` → "Lord of the Worlds":** `[7:121]`, qualified at `[7:122]` "The Lord of Moses and Aaron."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[1:2]`, `[26:16]`, `[7:104]` (this ruku's opening) — the titulary brackets the confrontation.
* **`sabr` (patience):** `[7:126]` "Pour out patience upon us."
  * **Rendering:** the first *patience* in the dossier — petitioned, not yet commanded.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:153]` "Seek help through patience and prayers. Allah is with the steadfast" — the same pairing of sabr with divine help that Moses commands at 7:128; `[12:18]` Yusuf's father's patience line — patience under a brother's betrayal (cf. Aaron at 7:150).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus); the magicians' immediate conversion under threat is the surah's counter-parable to 7:40's "doors of Heaven will not be opened" arrogance class.
* **Narrative detail:** Ta-Ha (20:71) carries the same permission-line ("Did you believe in him before I have given you permission?") plus the palm-trunk crucifixion, and 20:72 gives the magicians' "We will not prefer you to the proofs that have come to us"; 7:123–126 is the condensed register of that exchange (observable across the two dossier versions).
