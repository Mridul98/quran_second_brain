# Seek help in Allah, and be patient (7:127–129)
**UID:** 202610141435
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:127–129 (Ruku 15 of 24 in al-A'raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:127]` – `[7:129]` (3 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The shortest ruku of the dossier is its hinge of stamina: Pharaoh renews the infanticide program, and Moses answers with a three-clause doctrine — help is sought from Allah alone, the earth is Allah's to inherit, and the future belongs to the righteous — while the people's complaint ("persecuted before you and after you") is met with the promise *and* the condition: "then He will see how you behave."
* **Placement:** A 3-verse bridge between the magicians' duel (7:109–126) and the plague sequence (7:130–137); the sabr-and-inheritance theology here is quoted back at the people across the rest of the surah's Moses material.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:127]`** "The chiefs of Pharaoh's people said, “Will you let Moses and his people cause trouble in the land, and forsake you and your gods?” He said, “We will kill their sons, and spare their women. We have absolute power over them.”"
  * **Gloss:** The charge is sedition and god-abandonment; Pharaoh's reply re-opens the demographic weapon (the 2:49/28:4 infanticide). Note the corpus's rare straight apostrophe in "Pharaoh's" here (book typography, verbatim).
* **`[7:128]`** "Moses said to his people, “Seek help in Allah, and be patient. The earth belongs to Allah. He gives it in inheritance to whomever He wills of His servants, and the future belongs to the righteous.”"
  * **Gloss:** Four sentences in one breath: isti'anah (help sought from Allah), sabr, divine ownership of land, and the eschatological rule that outcomes belong to the pious.
* **`[7:129]`** "They said, “We were persecuted before you came to us, and after you came to us.” He said, “Perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy, and make you successors in the land; then He will see how you behave.”"
  * **Gloss:** The honest complaint of a people crushed both pre- and post-prophet; the answer is "perhaps" — deliverance framed as succession *under trial*: God will watch what the heirs do with the land.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`isti'anah` (seeking help):** "Seek help in Allah" `[7:128]`.
  * **Rendering:** paired immediately with patience — help is not a substitute for endurance but its companion.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:153]` "Seek help through patience and prayers. Allah is with the steadfast" — the identical pairing, the k-sabr note's anchor; `[1:5]` "upon You we call for help" — the Fatihah's petition given its historical scene here.
* **`aqibah` (the future/end):** "the future belongs to the righteous" `[7:128]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[11:49]` "So be patient. The future belongs to the pious" — same idiom, "righteous" here vs "pious" there (observable rendering drift for the *taqwa* root); within surah, `[7:103]` "consider the end of the evildoers" is the inverse face of the same rule.
* **`khilafah/succession` (heirs of the land):** "make you successors in the land" `[7:129]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:14]` "Then We made you successors on earth after them, to see how you would behave" — near-verbatim twin of 7:129's closing clause; `[6:165]` "It is He who made you successors on the earth... in order to test you"; within surah `[7:100]` "those who inherit the land after its inhabitants" — the same warning now aimed at the heirs themselves.
* **"absolute power":** Pharaoh's self-description, "We have absolute power over them" `[7:127]`.
  * **Rendering:** the tyrant's claim of total mastery, set against the very next verse's "The earth belongs to Allah" `[7:128]` — the ruku stages two sovereignties in direct sequence (observable juxtaposition).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[28:4]` "Pharaoh exalted himself in the land... He was truly a corrupter" — the same self-claim class; `[7:136]` (next ruku) — "So We took vengeance on them, and drowned them": the surah's answer to 7:127's "absolute power."

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Parallel:** `[2:49]` — "killing your sons and sparing your women. Therein was a tremendous trial" — the memory-form of 7:127's threat; 7:141 re-narrates it inside this same ruku-adjacent span.
* **Parallel:** `[28:4]` — "He persecuted a group of them, slaughtering their sons" — the Qasas register of the same policy.
* **Parallel:** `[10:14]` and `[6:165]` — succession-after-destruction as standing test; 7:129's "He will see how you behave" is the clause the covenant note calls the book's central refrain.
* **Within surah:** `[7:100]` — the heirs warned generically now receive a named inheritance promise; `[7:137]` (next ruku) — "the oppressed people inherit the eastern and western parts," the 7:128 doctrine cashed out.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (consensus) — a stamina text for a persecuted community; *Source: agent inference — verify*: the 7:129 complaint reads as addressed to the Quraysh-era believers' own experience of pre- and post-revelation pressure.
* **Source discipline:** reading 7:128's "the future belongs to the righteous" as a general law of history (not only the Israelite case) is *agent inference — verify*; the verse itself is unconditional in wording (observable).
