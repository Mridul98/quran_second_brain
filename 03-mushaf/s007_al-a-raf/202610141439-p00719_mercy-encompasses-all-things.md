# Mercy encompasses all things (7:152–157)
**UID:** 202610141439
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 7:152–157 (Ruku 19 of 24 in al-A'raf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[7:152]` – `[7:157]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** After the calf, the surah opens the mercy ledger: wrath is real but bounded (7:152), repentance is unbounded (7:153), the seventy elders learn that the test belongs to God (7:155), and the divine self-disclosure "My mercy encompasses all things" (7:156) is then *specified* — written for the doers of righteousness, the givers of zakat, the believers — and the specification lands on the Unlettered Prophet, described in the Torah and the Gospel, who unloads their burdens and shackles (7:157).
* **Placement:** The resolution movement of the Moses dossier (7:142–157): from the thrown Tablets (7:150) to re-taken Tablets "in their transcript is guidance and mercy" (7:154), then the universalization — Moses' people's request for goodness becomes the surah's portrait of the final Messenger, the dossier's bridge to 7:158's open call to all people.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[7:152]`** "Those who idolized the calf have incurred wrath from their Lord, and humiliation in this life. We thus requite the innovators."
  * **Gloss:** The calf verdict: wrath plus this-worldly humiliation; "the innovators" (muhdin) names the sin as invention — same charge family as 7:28's fabricated commands.
* **`[7:153]`** "As for those who commit sins, and then repent afterwards and believe—your Lord, thereafter, is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The open clause: sin → repent → believe, and the name of God that answers is two-fold (Ghafur, Rahim).
* **`[7:154]`** "When the anger abated in Moses, he took up the tablets. In their transcript is guidance and mercy for those in awe of their Lord."
  * **Gloss:** Re-collection of the thrown Word; the Tablets' content re-billed as hudan + rahmah — the pair the surah gave the Quran at 7:52.
* **`[7:155]`** "And Moses chose from his people seventy men for Our appointment. When the tremor shook them, he said, “My Lord, had You willed, You could have destroyed them before, and me too. Will you destroy us for what the fools among us have done? This is but Your test—with it You misguide whomever You will, and guide whomever You will. You are our Protector, so forgive us, and have mercy on us. You are the Best of Forgivers.”"
  * **Gloss:** The seventy's appointment, a shake, and Moses' daring intercession: collective punishment questioned, guidance/misguidance conceded wholly to God, forgiveness pleaded for the group.
* **`[7:156]`** "“And inscribe for us goodness in this world, and in the Hereafter. We have turned to You.” He said, “My punishment—I inflict it upon whomever I will, but My mercy encompasses all things. I will specify it for those who act righteously and practice regular charity, and those who believe in Our signs.”"
  * **Gloss:** The central verse: mercy universal in scope, specific in assignment — the three marks (righteousness, zakat, faith in the signs) that flow into 7:157.
* **`[7:157]`** "Those who follow the Messenger, the Unlettered Prophet, whom they find mentioned in the Torah and the Gospel in their possession. He directs them to righteousness, and deters them from evil, and allows for them all good things, and prohibits for them wickedness, and unloads the burdens and the shackles that are upon them. Those who believe in him, and respect him, and support him, and follow the light that came down with him— these are the successful."
  * **Gloss:** The specification names its community: the followers of the ummic Prophet, carrying the full prophetic job description (ma'ruf/evil, tayyibat/wickedness, burden-shifting) — the surah's most explicit Muhammad-portrait.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"mercy encompasses all things":** `[7:156]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[40:7]` "You have encompassed everything in mercy and knowledge" — the throne-bearers' prayer, same encompassing grammar; within surah `[7:151]` "admit us into Your mercy" (previous ruku) and `[7:52]` the Book as "guidance and mercy" — mercy is this ruku's keyword chain.
* **"Unlettered Prophet" (ummi):** `[7:157]`, repeated at `[7:158]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[62:2]` "He sent among the unlettered a messenger from themselves" — same title paired with Torah-and-Scripture failure context at `[62:5]` (the donkey-carrying-books parable), which 7:157's "in their possession" quietly evokes.
* **"burdens and the shackles":** `[7:157]`.
  * **Rendering:** "unloads the burdens and the shackles that are upon them" — liberation language for the new law's lightness.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[36:8]` "We placed shackles around their necks, up to their chins" and `[73:12]` "With Us are shackles" — the same "shackles" image is otherwise punishment-only in the corpus (verified sites): what the Prophet unloads here is what the Hereafter forges there (observable inversion).
* **"the innovators":** "We thus requite the innovators" `[7:152]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** within surah `[7:37]` "he who invents lies about Allah" and `[7:71]` "names, which you and your ancestors invented" — the innovation charge family; the counter-image is 7:157's Prophet who *unloads* what was laid on them, and `[7:170]` (two ruku ahead) "the reward of the reformers" — innovators/reformers is this passage's hidden pair (observable).
