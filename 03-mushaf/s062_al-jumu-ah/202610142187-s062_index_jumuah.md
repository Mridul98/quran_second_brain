## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Medinan — an agreed case in the type-lists (no Meccan minority report is standardly transmitted for this surah). *Source: classical type-lists — verify.* Revelation order **110** in the enumeration this brain's indices use (same list: al-Mumtahina 91, al-Munafiqun 104, as-Saff 109; the task's guessed "113" seats at-Tawbah in that list, not al-Jumu'ah). *Source: classical revelation-order lists — verify against a printed 'ulum al-Quran table before teaching the number.* The corpus carries no nuzul statement; the unnumbered Basmala heads the surah. **Note:** the corpus file is `corpus/s062_al-jumu-ah.md` — the folder/slug keeps the corpus's hyphen-for-apostrophe form; this note file follows the assigned short title `jumuah`.
* **Size:** 11 ayahs, 2 ruku. Named for the one Quranic occurrence of "Congregation Day" (`[62:9]`).
* **Title etymology:** al-Jumu'ah = "the Congregation/Friday assembly" — the name-word appears in the corpus only in 62:9 (hapax, verified); the liturgical institution outgrew the surah that names it once.
* **Reading hint:** ruku 1 is about *carrying* (scripture carried like a donkey's load vs scripture recited-purifying-teaching); ruku 2 is about *timing* (hasten/disperse/scramble). One surah, two verbs for the same failure: right content, wrong hour.
* **Central theme:** The grace of commissioned teaching versus the curse of uncarried scripture. Allah sent, among the unlettered, a messenger from themselves who recites, purifies, and teaches the Scripture and wisdom (62:1–4) — the very triple Abraham prayed for (`[2:129]`) — while those entrusted with the Torah and failed to carry it are figured as a donkey carrying books (62:5), and the claim of exclusive election is met with the challenge to wish for death (62:6–8). Ruku 2 legislates the rhythm that keeps the community's praise honest: hasten when the call sounds, disperse afterward seeking bounty, and do not scramble toward entertainment at the word of the Messenger (62:9–11).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Cosmic opening (62:1):** the tasbih formula again — this time the names expand to "the Sovereign, the Holy" (al-Malik al-Quddus), the only name-expansion of its kind in the five openings (57:1/59:1 end "Almighty, the Wise"; 61:1 likewise; 64:1 instead appends the kingdom-and-power clause — all lines verified in corpus).
* **The gift described (62:2–4):** recitation, purification, instruction — grace (fadl) "which He grants to whomever He wills," including "others from them, who have not yet joined" (62:3): the mission exceeds its first audience.
* **The counter-example (62:5):** the entrusted who failed — the corpus's only donkey-of-books image; "Miserable is the example" echoes `[7:177]`.
* **The confrontation (62:6–8):** election claimed → election tested ("wish for death"); what their hands advanced already known; the fleeing death certain.
* **The formation (62:9–11):** the weekly rehearsal of right ordering — remembrance first, then lawful dispersal; the closing rebuke (62:11) of the scramble at merchandise/entertainment gives the surah its concrete occasion-ethics. The obligation-verse (62:9) and its work-permission (62:10) are one breath: piety is stated as timing, never as withdrawal.
* **Twin-scene inventory (verified):** 62:2↔`[3:164]` (mission triple) and ↔`[2:129]` (Abraham's prayer for it); 62:6↔`[2:94]` (wish-for-death challenge); 62:8↔`[4:78]` (death will overtake); 62:10↔`[73:20]` (seeking bounty); 62:5↔`[7:177]` (miserable-example formula). This surah is one of the densest echo-panels in the short-surah zone — useful for retrieval drills.
