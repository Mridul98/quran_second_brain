# Surah 90: THE LAND (al-Balad)

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Meccan — agreed in the classical type-lists; the self-flattered rich-man indictment and the itemized moral economics fit the Meccan address. *Source: classical type-lists — verify.* Revelation order: **transmitted nuzul tables — verify** (corpus states no nuzul; unnumbered Basmala heads the surah).
* **Size:** 20 ayahs, 1 ruku (ruku_map global 532 — verified). Fifth door of the 86–91 run; the shortest route in the batch from creed to bill: one "ascent" (90:11–12) unpacked into manumission, famine-feeding, and mutual counsel.
* **Title:** al-Balad — "THE LAND" tracks `[90:1]` "I swear by this land"; the balad-oath names no sanctity epithet in this translation (see §5b).
* **Central theme:** The over-estimated man. Sworn on the land, "And you are a resident of this land" (90:2), and on "a father and what he fathered" (90:3), the surah states man's condition — "We created man in distress" (90:4) — then cites his two delusions ("Does he think that no one has power over him?" / "no one sees him?", 90:5/7) against God's three gifts (two eyes, tongue and two lips, the shown two ways, 90:8–10). He did not brave the ascent; the ascent is then itemized: freeing a slave, feeding on a day of hunger an orphan near of kin or a destitute in the dust, then joining the community that "advise[s] one another to patience, and advise[s] one another to kindness" (90:17). The two columns close: people of happiness vs people of misery, "a padlocked Fire" (90:18–20).
* **Reading hint:** watch the verbs of sight — 90:5 "power," 90:7 "sees," 90:8 "two eyes," 90:10 "shown the two ways": the man who thinks he is unwatched is the same man who was given eyes and a road; the ascent is spending on what the eyes see (orphan, dust-bound poor), not hoarding what the boast claims ("so much money," 90:6).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Triple oath (90:1–3):** this land; you its resident; a father and what he fathered — place, prophet, progeny.
* **Condition + delusion (90:4–7):** created in distress; thinks unseen and unpowerable; boasts "I have used up so much money."
* **Gift counter-argument (90:8–10):** eyes, tongue, lips, shown two ways — sight and speech given to one who claims blindness of fate.
* **The ascent not braved (90:11–16):** "But he did not brave the ascent" + the tease "And what will explain to you what the ascent is?" answered by freeing / famine-feeding / kin-orphan / dust-bound destitute.
* **The community clause (90:17):** belief + mutual counsel (patience, kindness) — the ascent is corporate, not solo.
* **Final columns (90:18–20):** people of happiness; deniers of the revelations = people of misery; "Upon them is a padlocked Fire." (*Source: structural observation on corpus renderings.*)

## 5b. Batch-Return Verification Flags (this index)
* **Hint renderings corrected:** "this secure city" → "**I swear by this land**" (90:1) — the word "secure" is nowhere in this corpus s090 (rg-verified: corpus "secure" only at `[77:21]`, `[44:51]`, `[23:13]` in other senses); the Mecca/Haram identification of the land and the prophet-resident gloss are *transmitted — verify*. "parent and offspring" → "a father and what he fathered" (90:3, corpus-unique phrase). "man in hardship" → "We created man in **distress**" (90:4). "the steep road" → "the **ascent**" (90:11–12) — "steep" is the classical epithet (ʿaqabat al-akbar) but not the corpus word. "freeing a neck" → "**The freeing of a slave**" (90:13; raqaba is the Arabic's neck-word — translator's domestic rendering, observable). "orphan kin and needy neighbor" → "An orphan **near of kin**. / Or a destitute **in the dust**." (90:15–16) — "neighbor" is not in this rendering; the dust-bound idiom's corpus twin is `[16:59]` "bury it in the dust" (rg-verified). "who counsel mercy" → "advise one another to **patience**, and advise one another to **kindness**" (90:17 — marḥama rendered "kindness", not "mercy"; rg-verified drift). "people of the right hand vs the namesakes of Hell" → "**people of happiness**" / "**people of misery**" (90:18–19) — no right-hand idiom here (that is `[56:27]`/`[56:41]` "those on the Right/Left", rg-verified); the misery column closes "Upon them is a **padlocked** Fire" (90:20) — padlocked is corpus-unique, twin in sense at `[104:8]` "It closes in on them" (rg-verified).
