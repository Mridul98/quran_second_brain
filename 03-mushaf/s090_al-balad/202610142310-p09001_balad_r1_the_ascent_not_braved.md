# The Ascent Not Braved (90:1–20)
**UID:** 202610142310
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 90:1–20 (Ruku 1 of 1 in al-Balad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[90:1]` – `[90:20]` (20 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Against a man who thinks his spending invisible and his power unaccountable ("Does he think that no one has power over him?", 90:5) and whose claim is banked ("I have used up so much money", 90:6), the surah sets what God actually did for him — eyes, tongue, two lips, the shown two ways (90:8–10) — and what he refused: to brave the ascent, which turns out to be nothing mystical but money spent on the nearest hungry faces (a freed neck-equivalent, an orphan of kin, a destitute in the dust), inside a community that counsels patience and kindness (90:17); the two final columns, happiness and misery, end on a fire with the door welded shut (90:20).
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1; the batch's practical answer to 89's indictment — where 89:17–18 lists failures (orphan unhonored, feeding not urged), 90:13–16 lists the ascent that repairs exactly those, keeping the same vocabulary.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[90:1]`–`[90:3]`** "I swear by this land. / And you are a resident of this land. / And by a father and what he fathered."
  * **Gloss:** Triple oath: the land, the prophet's residence in it (the classical identification as Mecca's Haram is transmitted gloss — *Source: classical tafsir — verify*; no sanctity epithet appears in this rendering), and generational man — "a father and what he fathered" is corpus-unique (rg-verified), setting up the kin-obligation that 90:15 will cash in.
* **`[90:2]`** "And you are a resident of this land."
  * **Gloss:** The second oath-term personalizes the first: the sworn land is the prophet's own dwelling-place — residence as covenant, the addressee bound to the geography he inhabits.
* **`[90:4]`** "We created man in distress."
  * **Gloss:** *kabad* — hardship/struggle as the human default; kin to the creation-as-test formula `[76:2]` "We created man from a liquid mixture, **to test him**" and the adversary-line `[16:4]` "He created the human being from a drop of fluid, yet he becomes an open adversary" (rg-verified).
* **`[90:5]`–`[90:7]`** "Does he think that no one has power over him? / He says, 'I have used up so much money.' / Does he think that no one sees him?"
  * **Gloss:** Double delusion framed by two "Does he think" questions: unpunishable and unseen. The money-claim is the boast-cluster with `[68:14]` "Just because he has money and children" and Qarun `[28:78]` (k-shukr's citation — rg-verified); the seeing-God half pairs with the batch's surveillance frame `[86:9]–[86:10]` and `[89:14]` (rg-verified renderings).
* **`[90:6]`** "He says, 'I have used up so much money.'"
  * **Gloss:** The boast voiced: spending as self-proof — the money-claim answers the first "Does he think" with a ledger of expenditure, mistaking consumption for power.
* **`[90:8]`–`[90:10]`** "Did We not give him two eyes? / And a tongue, and two lips? / And We showed him the two ways?"
  * **Gloss:** The gift-counter-argument (the "Did We not" endowment formula): sight, speech, and the road — twin cluster `[76:2]–[76:3]` "We made him hearing and seeing" / "We guided him to the way, be he appreciative or unappreciative" (rg-verified); "the two ways" (*bayḍān*) is corpus-unique; concept-home is `[16:9]` "It is for Allah to point out the paths" (k-guidance's own anchor, rg-verified).
* **`[90:9]`** "And a tongue, and two lips?"
  * **Gloss:** Speech-apparatus enumerated: tongue plus paired lips — the instruments of articulation given as endowment, answering the man who uses them to boast (90:6) instead of to guide.
* **`[90:11]`–`[90:12]`** "But he did not brave the ascent. / And what will explain to you what the ascent is?"
  * **Gloss:** The refusal named by a rare verb ("brave" — *ʿabaqa*), then the tease-formula: 90:12's "what will explain to you" is the exact twin of `[74:27]` ("what will explain to you what Saqar is?"), variant of `[86:2]` (rg-verified) — the two "terrible-unknown" reveals in the corpus answer each other: Saqar is the descent refused, the ascent the climb.
* **`[90:13]`–`[90:16]`** "The freeing of a slave. / Or the feeding on a day of hunger. / An orphan near of kin. / Or a destitute in the dust."
  * **Gloss:** The ascent itemized: manumission and famine-feeding, with kin-proximity ("near of kin") and public abasement ("in the dust" — idiom-twin `[16:59]` "bury it in the dust?", rg-verified) as the costly edges. The legal dossier: `[2:177]` (righteousness includes giving money "to near relatives, and orphans, and the needy… and for the freeing of slaves"), `[5:89]` ("freeing a slave"), `[9:60]` ("for freeing slaves… the poor, and the destitute"), `[76:8]`–`[76:9]` (feeding "the poor, and the orphan, and the captive" asking no thanks) — all rg-verified; the sibling charge `[89:17]–[89:18]` (orphan unhonored, feeding not urged) is the negative print of this positive (batch pair).
* **`[90:14]`** "Or the feeding on a day of hunger."
  * **Gloss:** The second ascent-item: food given precisely when scarcity bites — "a day of hunger" specifies timing as cost, making the charity acute rather than routine.
* **`[90:15]`** "An orphan near of kin."
  * **Gloss:** The third ascent-item: kinship specified — the orphan is not abstract but proximate ("near of kin"), cashing in the father-oath of 90:3 as social obligation.
* **`[90:17]`** "Then he becomes of those who believe, and advise one another to patience, and advise one another to kindness."
  * **Gloss:** The ascent is corporate: joining the tawāṣū community — "advise one another" is corpus-unique here (rg-verified); same-root cousins in this edition: `[103:3]` "encourage truth, and recommend patience" and `[3:200]` "Be patient, and advocate patience" — three renderings, one verb; *marḥama* lands as "kindness" (not "mercy"; standard lexical mapping — agent inference, verify). k-sabr's quadrant "sabr as social contract" (its own line on 103:3) has no [90:17] yet — append candidate.
* **`[90:18]`–`[90:20]`** "These are the people of happiness. / But as for those who defy Our revelations—these are the people of misery. / Upon them is a padlocked Fire."
  * **Gloss:** The two columns — happiness/misery folk (the paired idiom is corpus-unique; concept-kin: `[11:105]–[11:108]` "Some will be miserable, and some will be happy", rg-verified; NOT the right/left idiom, which `[56:27]`/`[56:41]` render "those on the Right/Left" — rg-verified). "Padlocked" is corpus-unique; its sense-twin is `[104:8]` "It closes in on them" (same lock-verb family, rg-verified) — the ascent refused becomes the exit locked.
* **`[90:19]`** "But as for those who defy Our revelations—these are the people of misery."
  * **Gloss:** The negative column named by defiance of revelations (āyāt): misfortune defined not as bad luck but as active rejection — the mirror of the believers' counsel-community at 90:17.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`balad` (land/city)**: sworn place.
  * **Rendering:** "I swear by this land… a resident of this land" (90:1–2) — no "secure/city of safety" qualifier (see index §5b).
  * **Elsewhere:** on the translation surface "land" covers both arḍ and balad, so no English-only anchor can be certified; 21:71 "the land that We blessed for all people" is classically the balad-word (Source: standard lexical mapping — verify against Arabic).
* **`ascent` (ʿaqaba)**: the steep climb.
  * **Rendering:** "the ascent" (90:11–12), glossed by the items 90:13–16.
  * **Elsewhere:** corpus-unique (rg-verified); tease-formula kin `[74:27]` (Saqar) — rg-verified.
* **`advise one another` (tawāṣā)**: mutual counsel.
  * **Rendering:** twice in 90:17 (patience; kindness).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[103:3]` (encourage/recommend), `[3:200]` (advocate) — the edition's three-way drift for one root (rg-verified).
* **`padlocked` (muṣadda)**: fire with welded doors.
  * **Rendering:** "Upon them is a padlocked Fire" (90:20).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[104:8]` "It closes in on them" + `[104:9]` "In extended columns" (rg-verified) — the batch's neighbor 104 will repeat the lock.
