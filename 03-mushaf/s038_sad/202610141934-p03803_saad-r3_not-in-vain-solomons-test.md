# Creation not in vain; the blessed Book; Solomon tested (38:27–40)
**UID:** 202610141934
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 38:27–40 (Ruku 3 of 5 in Saad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[38:27]` – `[38:40]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ground under the vicegerent's bench is laid: creation is not created "in vain" (27), the pious and the shameless will not be equated (28), and the Book — the corpus's only "blessed Book" — is sent down precisely so that minds ponder (29); the exhibit is Solomon, David's "excellent servant," tested twice (the horses, the body on his throne), forgiven, and granted an unrepeatable kingdom whose wind, builders and divers are dispensed "without account" (30–40).
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 5; the surah's kalam hinge at its exact middle. The fitna-chain binds it to ruku 2: "We were testing him" (38:24, David) → "We tested Solomon" (38:34) → "We found him patient" (38:44, Job, ruku 4); the "nearness to Us… place of return" verdict-formula of 38:25 is re-issued at 38:40.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[38:27]`** "We did not create the heaven and the earth and everything between them in vain. That is the assumption of those who disbelieve—so woe to those who disbelieve because of the Fire."
  * **Gloss:** Anti-nihilism as the premise of judgment: purposeless creation is "the assumption" (zann) of the unbelievers — the answer to 38:16's impatience and the ground of 38:26's deviation-warning; near-twin `[44:38]` "We did not create the heavens and the earth and what is between them to play."
* **`[38:28]`** "Or are We to treat those who believe and do righteous deeds like those who make trouble on earth? Or are We to treat the pious like the shameless?"
  * **Gloss:** Two refused equations — God's non-indifference is the justice of the vicegerent's archetype; "the pious like the shameless" (muttaqin/fajjarin) is the surah's own census vocabulary for the two "places of return" (38:49, 55).
* **`[38:29]`** "A blessed Book that We sent down to you, that they may ponder its Verses, and for those with intelligence to take heed."
  * **Gloss:** The kalam anchor: the Book is mubarak ("blessed Book" is unique to this line in the corpus — rg-verified) and its purpose is cognitive — pondering, not recitation alone; cf. `[4:82]` "Do they not ponder the Quran?"
* **`[38:30]`** "And We granted David, Solomon, an excellent servant. He was penitent."
  * **Gloss:** The Davidic line continues: "an excellent servant" (ni'ma-l-'abd — the formula that returns at 38:44 of Job); here awwab renders "penitent" where David's instance rendered "obedient" (38:17) — translator drift flagged in Key Terms.
* **`[38:31]`** "When the beautiful horses were paraded before him in the evening."
  * **Gloss:** The first test opens mid-scene ("When…" without a main clause — the corpus leaves the frame standing): rang'd horses at dusk.
* **`[38:32]`** "He said, "I have preferred the love of niceties to the remembrance of my Lord—until it disappeared behind the veil."
  * **Gloss:** The confession-formula: the love of good things overtook dhikr "until it disappeared behind the veil" — the referent ("it" = the sun) is left un-named in the corpus (Source: classical reading — verify); the quote opens here and closes at `[38:33]`.
* **`[38:33]`** "Bring them back to me." And he began caressing their legs and necks."
  * **Gloss:** The remedy is ambiguous by design: "caressing" (mas-h) — classical reports split between wiping in honor and cutting in devotion (Source: classical tafsir, contested — verify).
* **`[38:34]`** "We tested Solomon, and placed a body on his throne; then he repented."
  * **Gloss:** The second test: a jasad — "a body" — deposited on his throne, left entirely unnamed in the corpus (classical reports fill it differently; Source: classical material — verify); "then he repented" — the awwab pattern in action.
* **`[38:35]`** "He said, "My Lord, forgive me, and grant me a kingdom never to be attained by anyone after me. You are the Giver.""
  * **Gloss:** The prayer that names God "the Giver" (al-Wahhab — the same name at 38:9, where no human owns the treasuries; cf. `[3:8]` "You are the Giver"); ask for the unrepeatable.
* **`[38:36]`** "So We placed the wind at his service, blowing gently by his command, wherever he directed."
  * **Gloss:** Answered: the wind made obedient (cf. `[21:81]` "the stormy wind, blowing at His command towards the land that We have blessed" — another "blessed" cross-pairing).
* **`[38:37]`** "And the demons—every builder and diver."
  * **Gloss:** And the shayatin: builders and divers — the same workforce as `[21:82]` (divers and "other, lesser tasks," kept restrained) and `[34:12–13]` (sprites, sanctuaries, basins — "O House of David, work with appreciation").
* **`[38:38]`** "And others fettered in chains."
  * **Gloss:** The refractory ones in irons — the kingdom's coercive residue inside a doxological reign.
* **`[38:39]`** "This is Our gift; so give generously, or withhold; without account."
  * **Gloss:** The gift-economy in one line: dispense or withhold, "without account" — the reckoning-free provision cluster (`[40:40]` "provided for without account"; `[39:10]` "without reckoning"; `[3:37]`, `[24:38]`); Solomon holds nothing as proprietor — the verse 38:9–10's ownership reductio, enacted in a king.
* **`[38:40]`** "For him is nearness to Us, and a beautiful resort."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict-formula of 38:25 re-issued — nearness, and a "beautiful resort"; forgiven kingship ends the ruku exactly where forgiven kingship began.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`in vain` (38:27)**: creation without purpose (batil).
  * **Rendering:** "We did not create the heaven and the earth and everything between them in vain."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:191]` "Our Lord, You did not create this in vain, glory to You" — the same denial prayed by the mindful; `[23:115]` "Did you think that We created you in vain"; `[44:38]` renders the cognate claim "to play."
* **`blessed Book` (38:29)**: mubārak — the corpus's only phrase "blessed Book" (rg-verified).
  * **Rendering:** "A blessed Book that We sent down to you, that they may ponder its Verses."
  * **Elsewhere:** the pondering-verb recurs at `[4:82]`, `[51:49]` "so that you may reflect and ponder," `[54:51]` "Is there anyone who would ponder?", `[16:12]` "signs for people who ponder."
* **`the Giver` (38:35; and 38:9)**: Wahhab — unexhausted generosity.
  * **Rendering:** "You are the Giver" (Solomon's prayer); "the Majestic, the Giver" (38:9, where the treasuries are not theirs).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:8]` "bestow on us mercy from Your presence; You are the Giver" — the identical invocation.
* **`without account` (38:39)**: hisab-free dispensation.
  * **Rendering:** "so give generously, or withhold; without account."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[40:40]` "they will be provided for without account"; `[39:10]` "paid their wages in full, without reckoning" (the sabr-note anchor, one surah later).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the kingdom-as-test material addresses the same claim to authority that 38:9–10 raised against the messengership.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the horses episode's missing referent ("it disappeared behind the veil"), the "body on his throne" identity, and the caressing/cutting of legs and necks are all left open by the corpus; the classical fills differ (Sun-setting; a devil or a sudden death-and-return; wiping or striking) — Source: classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir / at-Tabari on 38:31–34) — verify; none of these fills is textual here.
