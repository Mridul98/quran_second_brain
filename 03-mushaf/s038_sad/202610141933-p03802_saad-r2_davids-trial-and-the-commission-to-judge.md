# The writ demanded, the king judged: David's test and the vicegerent's commission (38:15–26)
**UID:** 202610141933
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 38:15–26 (Ruku 2 of 5 in Saad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[38:15]` – `[38:26]` (12 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Against the mockers' demand to have the writ served early (15–16), God commands patience (17) and enters the surah's centerpiece exhibit: David — worshipper of the mountains and birds, holder of "wisdom and decisive speech" (18–20) — who is judged by a case he adjudicates: two disputants, ninety-nine ewes and one (21–24), a parable that turns on its judge until he kneels; forgiveness, nearness, and the vicegerent-commission "judge between the people with justice, and do not follow desire" (25–26).
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 5; the hinge between the confederates' roll-call (ruku 1) and the cosmological ground of judgment (ruku 3, 38:27). The "Day of Account" named in the mockers' taunt (16) becomes the commission's enforcement clause (26) — the demand is answered not by hastening but by appointing judges.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[38:15]`** "These can expect only a single scream, from which there is no recovery."
  * **Gloss:** The confederates' future compressed to one cry — twin of `[36:53]` "It will be but a single scream; and behold, they will all be brought before Us."; no second wind, no "escape" (cf. 38:3).
* **`[38:16]`** "And they say, "Our Lord, hasten Your writ upon us, before the Day of Account.""
  * **Gloss:** The mockers' prayer: expedite the record before the yawm al-hisab — the Day is named three times across the surah (16, 26, 53), and the impatient demand is the foil for 38:17's patience command.
* **`[38:17]`** "Be patient in the face of what they say, and mention Our servant David, the resourceful. He was obedient."
  * **Gloss:** Two imperatives: sabr toward the taunt, and the exhibit — David, dhul-ayd "the resourceful" (same root as 38:45's "ability"), "He was obedient" rendering awwab (see Key Terms).
* **`[38:18]`** "We committed the mountains to glorify with him, in the evening and at daybreak."
  * **Gloss:** Creation liturgizes around him — worship before judgment; the twin scene at `[21:79]` "We subjected the mountains along with David to sing Our praises, and the birds as well."
* **`[38:19]`** "And the birds, gathered together. All obedient to him."
  * **Gloss:** The doxology extends to the birds, "gathered" — a kingdom whose liturgy is its obedience.
* **`[38:20]`** "And We strengthened his kingdom, and gave him wisdom and decisive speech."
  * **Gloss:** The judge's equipment: hikma and fasl al-khitab, "decisive speech" — the court of the next verses arrives with its instrument already described.
* **`[38:21]`** "Has the story of the two disputants reached you? When they scaled the sanctuary?"
  * **Gloss:** The narrative hook ("Has the story… reached you?" — the surah's own qasas-formula): two khasiman intrude over the mihrab — "sanctuary," not "garden" (task correction; same word as Mary's/Zechariah's "sanctuary" at `[3:37]`, `[3:39]`).
* **`[38:22]`** "When they entered upon David, and he was startled by them. They said, "Do not fear. Two disputants; one of us has wronged the other; so judge between us fairly, and do not be biased, and guide us to the straight way.""
  * **Gloss:** The plea forces the verdict-formula: fair judgment, no bias, guidance "to the straight way" — petition vocabulary that 38:26's commission will answer (David "was startled" — the corpus's own word).
* **`[38:23]`** "This brother of mine has ninety nine ewes, and I have one ewe, and he said, 'Entrust it to me,' and he pressured me with words."
  * **Gloss:** The case: ninety-nine and one — the strong man's injustice is economic and verbal ("he pressured me with words").
* **`[38:24]`** "He said, "He has done you wrong by asking your ewe in addition to his ewes. Many partners take advantage of one another, except those who believe and do good deeds, but these are so few." David realized that We were testing him, so he sought forgiveness from his Lord, and fell down to his knees, and repented."
  * **Gloss:** David rules for the weak, generalizes to all partnerships ("but these are so few"), and is then ruled: "David realized that We were testing him" — the parable-as-judgment turns on the judge; he kneels (the surah's one sajdah-ayah by the transmitted lists) and repents. The wife/Uriah detail is absent from the text (Source: classical identification, Ibn Kathir / at-Tabari on 38:21–25 — verify).
* **`[38:25]`** "So We forgave him that. And for him is nearness to Us, and a good place of return."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict on David: forgiveness, zulfa "nearness," "a good place of return" (maad) — the phrase reused for the devout at 38:49 and inverted at 38:55 ("a miserable return").
* **`[38:26]`** "O David, We made you a ruler in the land, so judge between the people with justice, and do not follow desire, lest it diverts you from Allah's path. Those who stray from Allah's path will have a painful punishment, for having ignored the Day of Account."
  * **Gloss:** The commission: khalifa rendered "ruler" (not "vicegerent" — see Key Terms); vicegerency defined as adjudication with justice under the warning against hawa, "desire"; the Day of Account (third naming) enforces it, with "a painful punishment" for the heedless.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`khalifa` (38:26)**: vicegerent/steward on earth.
  * **Rendering:** "We made you a ruler in the land" — Itani's only singular human-khalifa rendering outside `[2:30]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:30]` "I am placing a successor on earth" (the same lemma as "successor"); plural khalā'if consistently "successors" at `[6:165]`, `[10:14]`, `[24:55]`, `[27:62]` — humanity's generic stewardship; David's is a courtroom commission.
* **`disputants / sanctuary` (38:21–22)**: khasiman over the mihrab.
  * **Rendering:** "two disputants" — the noun "disputants" occurs only here in the corpus (rg-verified); "scaled the sanctuary" for the mihrab climb.
  * **Elsewhere:** mihrab is also "sanctuary" in `[3:37]`/`[3:39]` (Zechariah and Mary) and haram "a sanctuary" in `[2:125]` — one Arabic word for consecrated space, entered here as trespass.
* **`awwab` (38:17, 30, 44)**: the one who keeps returning (to God).
  * **Rendering:** drifts inside this surah: "He was obedient" of David (38:17) and Job (38:44), "He was penitent" of Solomon (38:30) — translator's term choice, observable in the corpus.
  * **Elsewhere:** "penitent" at `[11:75]` (Abraham), `[50:8]`, `[66:5]` — the constant rendering outside Saad; the classical meaning is "frequently returning/turning" (Source: consensus of classical tafsir — verify).
* **`writ` (38:16)**: kitab in the mockers' taunt-prayer.
  * **Rendering:** "hasten Your writ upon us" — a legal diction unique here; elsewhere kitāb of the Day is "book": `[69:19, 25]` "given his book…"; `[45:28]` "every community will be called to its Book."

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); a king-judge exhibit fits a Meccan argument about authority and accountability, addressed to the community of 38:4–8's wonderers through the patience command of 38:17.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the Uriah–Bathsheba identification of the parable is classical, not textual — the corpus supplies only the ewes, the testing, the kneeling and the repentance (Source: classical identification — verify; Israelite-material provenance is disputed even inside the tafsir tradition — agent note). The sajda at `[38:24]` is from the transmitted sajdah lists (Source: transmitted lists — verify).
