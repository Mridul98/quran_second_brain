# Job's cry, the patriarchs of the Home, and the two places of return (38:41–64)
**UID:** 202610141935
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 38:41–64 (Ruku 4 of 5 in Saad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[38:41]` – `[38:64]` (24 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The patience exhibits conclude: Job's du'a against affliction is answered from under his own foot (41–44), the patriarchs are chosen for "the remembrance of the Home" (45–48), and the surah's verdict vocabulary splits the world into two "places of return" — the devout's opened doors of Eden (49–54) and the transgressors' roast, "boiling and bitter cold," and mutual recrimination (55–64), where the mockers of this life go looking for the men they mocked.
* **Placement:** Ruku 4 of 5; the longest unit and the surah's reward-and-reckoning centerpiece, flanked by the nearness-formulas: 38:25/40 (forgiven kings) now answered by 38:49 "a good place of return" versus 38:55 "a miserable return".

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[38:41]`** "And mention Our servant Job, when he called out to his Lord, "Satan has afflicted me with hardship and pain.""
  * **Gloss:** The third servant-exhibit, du'a-form: the complaint names its source and its two currencies — exhaustion ("hardship," nuṣb) and "pain" ('adhāb); the twin cry at `[21:83]` adds "and you are the Most Merciful of the merciful."
* **`[38:42]`** "Stamp with your foot—here is cool water to wash with, and to drink."
  * **Gloss:** The answer is subterranean and immediate — the remedy struck up from beneath his own feet, bath and drink in one command.
* **`[38:43]`** "And We restored his family for him, and their like with them; as a mercy from Us, and a lesson for those who possess insight."
  * **Gloss:** Restoration doubled — family "and their like with them"; `[21:84]` narrates the same event as "a mercy from Us, and a reminder for the worshipers" — this edition's "lesson for those who possess insight."
* **`[38:44]`** ""Take with your hand a bundle, and strike with it, and do not break your oath." We found him patient. What an excellent servant! He was obedient."
  * **Gloss:** Oath-judiciary: a bundle of grass satisfies a binding vow — mercy legislated into the resolution; then the praise formulas: "We found him patient" (the sabr anchor of the ruku) and "What an excellent servant!" (ni'ma-l-'abd, as 38:30), closing "He was obedient" — again awwab rendered "obedient."
* **`[38:45]`** "And mention Our servants Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—endowed with ability and vision."
  * **Gloss:** The patriarchs: ulul-aydi wal-absar — hands-and-eyes people; the "ability" (ayd) root is the same as David's "the resourceful" at 38:17.
* **`[38:46]`–`[38:47]`** "We distinguished them with a distinct quality: the remembrance of the Home." / "To Us they are among the chosen, the outstanding."
  * **Gloss:** The distinguishing mark is dhikr — of "the Home" (al-dar: this edition's Hereafter-word, cf. `[2:94]` "the Final Home," `[29:64]` "the Home of the Hereafter is the Life"); election language brackets: "the chosen, the outstanding."
* **`[38:48]`** "And mention Ishmael, Elisha, and Ezekiel; all are among the outstanding."
  * **Gloss:** The roster extends — Ezekiel recurs at `[21:85]` ("each was one of the steadfast"), putting 38:48's "outstanding" in the sabr roster's company.
* **`[38:49]`** "This is a reminder. The devout will have a good place of return."
  * **Gloss:** The passage self-labels "a reminder" (dhikr — the same word as 38:1's "the renowned Quran" and 38:87's close), and re-issues 38:25's "a good place of return" for "the devout" (muttaqin).
* **`[38:50]`–`[38:51]`** "The Gardens of Eden, with their doors wide-open for them." / "Relaxing therein, and calling for abundant fruit and beverage."
  * **Gloss:** The ma'ad scene: 'Adn with open doors, reclining, abundance on request — hospitality answered where 38:59 will find it refused.
* **`[38:52]`** "With them will be attendants with modest gaze, of same age."
  * **Gloss:** The companions: this edition's "attendants with modest gaze, of same age" — cf. `[37:48]` "bashful women with lovely eyes," `[56:22]` "lovely companions."
* **`[38:53]`** "This is what you are promised for the Day of Account."
  * **Gloss:** The Day of Account named a third time (16, 26, 53) — the addressee flips: what the mockers demanded be hastened (16) is here announced as already promised.
* **`[38:54]`** "Such is Our bounty, inexhaustible."
  * **Gloss:** The bounty (fadl) closes the register of the gift — the inexhaustible side of 38:39's "without account."
* **`[38:55]`–`[38:56]`** "All This. But the transgressors will have a miserable return." / "Hell; in which they will roast; what a miserable abode!"
  * **Gloss:** The pivot on "All This": the return-phrase of 38:25/49 inverted ("a miserable return"), then the roasting furnace and its "miserable abode" — the twin verdict-formula of 38:49.
* **`[38:57]`–`[38:58]`** "All this. Let them taste it—boiling and bitter cold." / "And similar torments of diverse kinds."
  * **Gloss:** The twin drinks hammim/ghassaq as this edition renders them — "boiling and bitter cold" (cf. `[78:25]` "boiling water, and freezing hail"); then "similar torments of diverse kinds" — the register widened, matching kinds (cf. 38:8 "tasted").
* **`[38:59]`** ""This is a crowd rushing headlong with you." There is no welcome for them. They will be scorched by the Fire."
  * **Gloss:** The misleaders' arrival: a headlong crowd, greeted with the refusal of welcome — hospitality inverted at the gate.
* **`[38:60]`–`[38:61]`** "They will say, "But it is you! There is no welcome for you! It is you who brought it upon us! What a miserable end!"" / "They will say, "Our Lord, whoever brought this upon us, give him double torment in the Fire.""
  * **Gloss:** The followers return the unwelcome — blame reciprocal ("it is you who brought it upon us") — and then the double-torment demand, the same Day-request as `[7:38]` ("inflict on them a double punishment in the Fire").
* **`[38:62]`–`[38:63]`** "And they will say, "What is it with us that we do not see men we used to count among the wicked?" / "Did we take them for mockery, or have our eyes swerved from them?"
  * **Gloss:** The mockers' poem: the absent men they "used to count among the wicked" — two explanations in one question; cf. `[23:110]` "you made them a target of ridicule." (The quotation opens at 38:62 and is never closed — extraction artifact.)
* **`[38:64]`** "This is certainly true—the feuding of the people of the Fire."
  * **Gloss:** The narrator's verdict line: the whole exchange is the internal quarrel of the Fire's people — and, as an agent observation, the surah's three disputes (the disputants 38:21, the Fire-folk 38:64, the Highest Assembly 38:69) are now decided like cases.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`afflicted` (38:41)**: masssa — contact-affliction.
  * **Rendering:** "Satan has afflicted me with hardship and pain."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[21:83]` "Great harm has afflicted me" — the same verb in the same du'a; the pair nuṣb/'adhāb is this edition's "hardship and pain."
* **`ability and vision` (38:45)**: ulul-aydi wal-absar — hands and eyes.
  * **Rendering:** "endowed with ability and vision" — the phrase is unique in the corpus (rg-verified).
  * **Elsewhere:** the ayd root already carries David at `[38:17]` "the resourceful" — in-surah: the judges are men of strength; the patriarchs, of strength and sight.
* **`the Home` (38:46)**: al-dar — the abode.
  * **Rendering:** "the remembrance of the Home."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:94]` "If the Final Home with Allah is yours alone"; `[29:64]` "the Home of the Hereafter is the Life" — the capitalized "Home" for the abode is this edition's consistent choice (observable term).
* **`place of return` (38:25, 49; inverted at 38:55–56)**: the surah's verdict formula.
  * **Rendering:** "a good place of return" (David 38:25, the devout 38:49) vs. "a miserable return" (the transgressors 38:55) with "what a miserable abode!" (38:56).
  * **Elsewhere:** the chain is in-surah — the two "returns" bracket the ruku, resolving the nearness-formulas of 38:25/40 ("nearness to Us, and a beautiful resort").

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the Garden/Fire census is the early-Meccan two-classes grammar, with the Day of Account (38:53) as its hinge.
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the background of Job's oath (a vow to strike his wife — hence the bundle) is classical, not textual: the corpus states only "do not break your oath" (Source: classical tafsir, Ibn Kathir / at-Tabari on 38:44 — verify). The mocked "men" of 38:62–63 are unnamed; classically the poor/lowly believers (Source: classical reading — verify). Ezekiel/Elisha identities follow the transmitted prophetic lists (Source: classical identifications — verify).
