# Saad: a letter, an oath, and the arrogance that wonders (38:1–14)
**UID:** 202610141932
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 38:1–14 (Ruku 1 of 5 in Saad)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[38:1]` – `[38:14]` (14 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah swears by the "renowned Quran" (1) and in the same breath exhibits a people structurally unable to receive it — "steeped in arrogance and defiance" (2), marveling that a warner came "from among them" (4–5), organized by notables into a "planned" refusal (6–8) — and answers with an ownership reductio (9–10) and the roll-call of the confederates whose armies were defeated (11–14).
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 5; the surah's courtroom opening. Its "wonder" (38:4) and "something strange" (38:5) set the refusal-key that ruku 5's "message of great importance / From which you are turning away" (38:67–68) will re-state, and its letter+oath form belongs to the muqatta'at family (cf. `[50:1–2]`, whose marveling line is verbatim-identical).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[38:1]`** "Saad. By the renowned Quran."
  * **Gloss:** The letter ص stands untranslated, joined in this edition's numbering to the oath on the Book — the kalam anchor of the surah: what is sworn by is revelation itself.
* **`[38:2]`** "Those who disbelieve are steeped in arrogance and defiance."
  * **Gloss:** The oath's first exhibit — a posture ("steeped in," not merely "in") that cannot bow to a Book; the corpus word is "defiance," not "dissent" (task correction in the index).
* **`[38:3]`** "How many generations have We destroyed before them? They cried out when it was too late to escape."
  * **Gloss:** History as precedent: the cry arrives after the window — the ruku's "too late" grammar that `[38:15]` will compress ("from which there is no recovery").
* **`[38:4]`** "And they marveled that a warner has come to them from among them. The disbelievers said, "This is a lying magician.""
  * **Gloss:** The wonder-line, verbatim-twin of `[50:2]`; the two stock Meccan slurs — lying and sorcery — land in one sentence.
* **`[38:5]`** "Did he turn all the gods into one God? This is something strange."
  * **Gloss:** The substance of the offense: tawhid itself is "strange" (cf. `[50:2]` "This is something strange"); the pagans' own words are preserved as quotation.
* **`[38:6]`** The notables among them announced: "Go on, and hold fast to your gods. This is something planned."
  * **Gloss:** The mala' (notables) turn astonishment into policy — the command is persistence in the gods, and the frame is makr, "something planned." (The quotation opens here and closes only at `[38:8]` — extraction typography.)
* **`[38:7]`** "We never heard of this in the former faith. This is nothing but a fabrication."
  * **Gloss:** The appeal to ancestral religion plus the novelty-charge — the notables' speech continuing: the message is un-heard-of and therefore invented.
* **`[38:8]`** Was the message sent down to him, out of all of us?" In fact, they are doubtful of My warning. In fact, they have not yet tasted My punishment."
  * **Gloss:** The candidacy objection ("out of all of us" — the surah's own "among us" phrasing the task looked for at 38:4) answered twice: the real disease is doubt (shakk), and the verdict deferred is a taste of punishment the surah will narrate repeatedly.
* **`[38:9]`** "Or do they possess the treasuries of the mercy of your Lord—the Majestic, the Giver?"
  * **Gloss:** First ownership question: if revelation is a distribution problem to be managed, the managers must own the treasury; the names "the Majestic, the Giver" pre-echo `[38:35]` "You are the Giver."
* **`[38:10]`** "Or do they possess the sovereignty of the heavens and the earth and what is between them? Then let them ascend the ropes."
  * **Gloss:** Second ownership question, met with a taunt-idiom ("ascend the ropes," ḥabāl — classically the sky-ways of ascent or false means of reach; Source: classical tafsir — verify): deciding prophecy would require a ladder they do not have.
* **`[38:11]`** "An army of confederates will be defeated there."
  * **Gloss:** The promise-oracle — same defeat-grammar as `[54:45]` "The multitude will be defeated, and they will turn their backs"; the Trench believers will recognize it (cf. `[33:22]`).
* **`[38:12]`** "Before them the people of Noah denied the truth; as did Aad, and Pharaoh of the Stakes."
  * **Gloss:** The roll-call opens — "denied the truth" renders the same lie-root (k-dh-b) as 38:4's "lying magician": the confederates are their descendants in unbelief. "Pharaoh of the Stakes" recurs verbatim at `[89:10]`.
* **`[38:13]`** "And Thamood, and the people of Lot, and the dwellers of the Woods—these were the confederates."
  * **Gloss:** The list completes (the Woods also at `[50:14]`, `[15:78]`, `[26:176]`) — "the confederates" is now defined: every listed rejecter.
* **`[38:14]`** "None of them but denied the messengers, so My retribution was deserved."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict clause — pattern, not whim: the same idiom at `[50:14]` renders "so My threat came true." Denial-of-messengers is the single sufficient cause.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Saad` (the letter ص)**: the un-translated name of the surah.
  * **Rendering:** transliterated, untranslated, bound inside ayah 1's oath-line ("Saad. By the renowned Quran.").
  * **Elsewhere:** `[50:1]` "Qaf. By the Glorious Quran."; `[68:1]` "Noon. By the pen, and by what they inscribe." — the letter+oath form named in the muqatta'at fleeting note; 38:1 is its second confirmed member after 50:1 in this corpus.
* **`renowned` (38:1)**: the honorific on the Quran in the oath.
  * **Rendering:** "the renowned Quran" — this word occurs once in the corpus (rg-verified: only `[38:1]`), a translator's choice for the Quran's dignity here.
  * **Elsewhere:** the cognate oath-adjectives are "the Glorious Quran" (`[50:1]`) and the pen-sworn "what they inscribe" (`[68:1]`).
* **`arrogance and defiance` (38:2)**: the refusal-posture.
  * **Rendering:** "steeped in arrogance and defiance."
  * **Elsewhere:** "defiance" is this edition's stable rendering of the same stubbornness in `[6:49]`, `[17:60]`, `[23:75]`, `[67:21]`; the archetype surfaces inside the surah at `[38:74]` "He was too proud."
* **`confederates` (aḥzāb, 38:11, 13)**: the league of the listed rejecters.
  * **Rendering:** "confederates" — also the English chapter-name of surah 33.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[40:5]` "as did the confederates after them. Every community plotted against their messenger" (the "planned" makr of 38:6 generalized), `[40:30]` "the day of the confederates," `[33:20]`, `[33:22]` — Medina's own Battle of the Confederates, the living referent of 38:11's promise.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Meccan (Source: classical lists — verify); the wonder-at-a-human-warner and the notables' plot match the Quraysh leadership objection transmitted for the early public-preaching period (Source: classical period reports, e.g., as transmitted in Maududi-type surah introductions — verify in print).
* **Edition notes:** letter and oath form one numbered ayah (`[38:1]`), while the surah-head basmala stays unnumbered (see Fleeting note); the notables' quotation opens at `[38:6]` and closes only mid-`[38:8]` — class-2 extraction typography, not a textual claim.
