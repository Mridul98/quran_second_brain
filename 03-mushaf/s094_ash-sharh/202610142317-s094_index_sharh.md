## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Meccan — agreed in the classical type-lists; it is the second half of the 93–94 consolation diptych addressed to the Prophet under Meccan strain. *Source: classical type-lists — verify.* Revelation order: **transmitted nuzul tables — verify** (corpus states no nuzul; unnumbered Basmala heads the surah). Classical pairing with 93 is strong enough to treat the two as one argument: 93 denies abandonment, 94 inventories soothing.
* **Size:** 8 ayahs, 1 ruku (ruku_map global 536 — verified). Eight verses; the shortest of the six here with 97.
* **Title:** ash-Sharh — Itani's header "THE SOOTHING" tracks `[94:1]` "Did We not soothe your heart?" (*sharh* = expansion/soothing of the breast-heart; rg-cluster: `[6:125]` "He spreads open his heart to Islam", `[20:25]` "put my heart at peace for me", `[39:22]` "whose heart Allah has opened to Islam").
* **Central theme:** Three favors recounted under one "Did We not?" — heart soothed, burden lifted, reputation raised — crown the past; an identical double declaration ("With hardship comes ease." ×2, 94:5–6) crowns the future; and two imperative lines turn the conserved energy outward: "When your work is done, turn to devotion. And to your Lord turn for everything" (94:7–8). The surah converts relief into orientation: you were lifted, so now lean — entirely, and only, toward your Lord.
* **Reading hint:** count the divine verbs: We soothed (94:1), We lift (94:2), We raised (94:4) — with the weight itself stated only descriptively ("Which weighed down your back?" 94:3) — then the single-direction close: "to your Lord… for everything." Ease here is not comfort; it is capacity. (*Source: structural observation on corpus renderings.*)

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Favor inventory (94:1–4):** one interrogation frame over three favors and one descriptor — soothe your heart (94:1), lift your burden (94:2), which weighed down your back (94:3, the descriptor measuring what was lifted), raise your reputation (94:4); "weighed down" (*aqhada*) is corpus-unique (rg-verified).
* **Doubled ease (94:5–6):** two verbatim-identical verses — "With hardship comes ease." — the rhetorical doubling (cf. the doubled interrogation `[82:17–18]` "But what will convey to you… Then again…" — rg-verified): certainty asserted twice.
* **Two imperatives (94:7–8):** "When your work is done, turn to devotion" (*fa-idha faraghta fansib* — motion into worship on completion) and "And to your Lord turn for everything" (*wa-ila rabbika farghab* — single-direction longing). The diptych's ethics half (93:9–11) becomes here the devotion half.
* **Tense-ring of consolation:** 94:1–4 looks back ("Did We not"), 94:5–6 declares the law ("comes"), 94:7–8 commands the turn ("turn") — past favor grounds future trust grounds present motion. (*Source: structural observation on corpus renderings.*)
* **Twin structure with 93:** 93 = "Did He not find you" ×3 under one interrogation → three social duties; 94 = "Did We not" ×4 under one interrogation → two devotional duties. Same genre (divine first-person recounting of past favor to a distressed prophet), same "your Lord" closeness formula (93:3/5/8 ↔ 94:8), both in one breath before the ease promise. (*Source: structural observation — agent inference — verify classical pairing reports.*)

## 5b. Batch-Return Verification Flags (this index)
* **Hint renderings corrected:** "raised your breast" → "Did We not **soothe your heart**?" (94:1); "your burden that broke your back" → "And **lift from you your burden**. / **Which weighed down your back**?" (94:2–3 — "broke" absent; *aqhada* rendered "weighed down", corpus-unique); "raised your renown" → "And raised for you your **reputation**?" (94:4); "with the hardship IS ease" → "With hardship comes ease." (94:5–6, bare, no copula, no article).
* **"To your Lord is the goal" — REJECTED as a 94 quotation:** 94:8 reads "And to your Lord turn for everything." The *goal/return* phrasings belong to neighbors: `[96:8]` "But to your Lord is the return" and `[53:42]` "And that to your Lord is the finality" (rg-verified) — cite those as the cluster, not as 94's text.
* **"You are not over them a judge?" — REJECTED: no such verse in s094** (all 8 ayahs catalogued above; the corpus phrase nearest is `[88:22]` "You have no control over them" — different surah, different word). Nothing in 94 to cite.
* **"Reputation" located:** 94:4's word pairs with Moses' petition `[26:84]` "And give me a reputation of truth among the others" and `[19:50]` "a noble reputation of truth" (rg-verified) — the raised *dhikr* is the answered-prophet motif. Itani renders the same root "message" at `[21:10]` ("a Book, containing your message") — term drift, rg-verified.
* **94:3 is a clause, not a fourth favor:** "Which weighed down your back?" continues 94:2's burden — the divine verbs are exactly three (soothed/lift/raised); count them before preaching the inventory.
