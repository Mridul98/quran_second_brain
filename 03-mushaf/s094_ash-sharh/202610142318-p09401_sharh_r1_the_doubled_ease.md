# The Doubled Ease: three favors, one direction (94:1–8)
**UID:** 202610142318
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 94:1–8 (Ruku 1 of 1 in ash-Sharh)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[94:1]` – `[94:8]` (8 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** God testifies to His own prior care of the distressed prophet — heart soothed, crushing burden lifted, name raised — guarantees the future with the same sentence said twice ("With hardship comes ease." 94:5–6), and then issues the two-line constitution of the relieved life: when you finish one work, move to devotion; and direct every longing "to your Lord" alone.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1; second leaf of the 93–94 diptych. 93 proved no-abandonment from three past reversals; 94 proves it from three past elevations — and the ease-promise of 94:5–6 is the general law that 92:7/92:10 had shown working in both directions.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[94:1]`** "Did We not soothe your heart?"
  * **Gloss:** Opens with the divine first-person plural and a negated question — expecting "yes" as memory. *Sharh* = expansion/soothing; the same word-family gives `[6:125]` "Whomever Allah desires to guide, He spreads open his heart to Islam" and Moses' prayer `[20:25]` "My Lord, put my heart at peace for me" (rg-verified) — 94:1 reads as a prayer already answered.
* **`[94:2]`–`[94:3]`** "And lift from you your burden. / Which weighed down your back?"
  * **Gloss:** The second favor with its own descriptor: a *wizr* (burden) so heavy it *aqhada* — "weighed down" is corpus-unique here (rg-verified). The prophets' felt load (message, rejection, responsibility) named physically: back-language as real as grief. Moses asks exactly this relief at `[20:26]` "And ease my task for me" (rg-verified) — here it is granted before asking.
* **`[94:4]`** "And raised for you your reputation?"
  * **Gloss:** Third favor: *dhikr* raised. The petition-twin is Moses' in the Quranic prayer-list: `[26:84]` "And give me a reputation of truth among the others" (rg-verified), granted to Abraham's kin at `[19:50]` "a noble reputation of truth" — the humiliated prophet of Meccan 93:3 ("did not abandon you") owns an elevated name.
* **`[94:5]`–`[94:6]`** "With hardship comes ease. / With hardship comes ease."
  * **Gloss:** Two verbatim-identical verses — the doubling is the rhetoric of certainty (compare the doubled interrogation at `[82:17–18]` "But what will convey to you what the Day of Justice is? / Then again, what will convey to you…" — rg-verified). The classical maxim "one hardship cannot overcome two eases" is drawn from this pairing by transmitted exegesis (Source: transmitted reports in the tafsir apparatus — verify primary before quoting; the Arabic definiteness arguments behind it are not checkable from this English corpus).
* **`[94:7]`** "When your work is done, turn to devotion."
  * **Gloss:** First imperative: *fa-idha faraghta fansib* — when you are free, labor: Itani's "turn to devotion" takes *nasib* toward worship (cf. `[50:40]` "at the end of devotions", `[22:67]` "acts of devotion" — rg-verified for the word-family). The rhythm: relief is not rest; finished work is followed by devotional work.
* **`[94:8]`** "And to your Lord turn for everything."
  * **Gloss:** Second imperative: *wa-ila rabbika farghab* — desire/lean toward your Lord, full stop, for everything — the surah's tawhid of longing: one address for all outcomes. Together with `[92:20]` ("Only seeking the acceptance of his Lord") this is the single-direction grammar the k-tawhid note lacks (rg-verified: note cites no [92–94]).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sharh (soothe/expand)`**: the surah's namesake.
  * **Rendering:** "soothe your heart" (94:1); header "THE SOOTHING".
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:125]` "spreads open his heart to Islam"; `[20:25]` "put my heart at peace"; `[39:22]` "whose heart Allah has opened to Islam" (rg-verified) — expansion is the physiology of guidance; constriction is its opposite (6:125's second half: "narrow, constricted").
* **`wizr (burden)`**: lifted from the prophet, carried by no one else.
  * **Elsewhere in this surah:** "Which weighed down your back?" — the descriptor makes the lifting measurable (rg-unique phrase).
* **`yusr/ʿusr (ease/hardship)`**: the doubled law (94:5–6).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[92:7]`/`[92:10]` (eased toward ease / toward difficulty); `[2:185]` "Allah desires ease for you, and does not desire hardship for you"; `[87:8]` "We will ease you into the Easy Way"; `[18:16]` "will set your affair towards ease"; `[20:26]` "ease my task" (all rg-verified) — the cluster spans six sites, no knowledge note yet owns it (reported gap).
* **`dhikr (reputation)`**: raised for the prophet.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[26:84]`, `[19:50]` (rg-verified); `[21:10]` renders the same root as "message" — "a Book, containing your message" — term drift worth noting (rg-verified).
* **`raghba (turn/desire for everything)`**: 94:8's closing verb.
  * **Rendering:** "And to your Lord turn for everything" — bare single-direction.
  * **Elsewhere (kin destinations):** `[96:8]` "But to your Lord is the return"; `[53:42]` "to your Lord is the finality" (rg-verified) — the goal-grammar of the Meccan short surahs.
