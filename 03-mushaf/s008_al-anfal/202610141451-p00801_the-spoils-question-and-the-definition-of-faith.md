# The spoils question answered by a definition of faith (8:1–10)
**UID:** 202610141451
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 8:1–10 (Ruku 1 of 10 in al-Anfal)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[8:1]` – `[8:10]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah opens on a loot dispute and answers it the way the whole surah will keep answering — by redistribution of *trust*: ownership goes to Allah and the Messenger (1), four commands reframe the question (1), and a five-trait definition of "true believers" is installed (2–4), immediately tested against the memory of Badr, where believers disliked going out, wanted the easy caravan, cried for help, and were told twice that victory is only from Allah (5–10).
* **Placement:** Opening ruku; it plants the surah's ring verse — "These are the true believers" (`[8:4]`) — which returns verbatim as the surah's final verdict (`[8:74]`).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[8:1]`** "They ask you about the bounties. Say, “The bounties are for Allah and the Messenger.” So be mindful of Allah, and settle your differences, and obey Allah and His Messenger, if you are believers."
  * **Gloss:** A legal question (who owns the spoils) is answered with an ownership claim, then converted into three duties — taqwa, reconciliation, obedience — made conditional on the very faith the disputants claim.
* **`[8:2]`** "The believers are those whose hearts tremble when Allah is mentioned, and when His revelations are recited to them, they strengthen them in faith, and upon their Lord they rely."
  * **Gloss:** The definition begins inward: startled hearts, revelation as faith-increase, exclusive reliance.
* **`[8:3]`** "Those who perform the prayer; and from Our provisions to them, they spend."
  * **Gloss:** The inward states become two auditable acts: prayer, and spending from what is explicitly labeled *Our* provision.
* **`[8:4]`** "These are the true believers. They have high standing with their Lord, and forgiveness, and a generous provision."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict and the triple wage: rank (unique phrase in the corpus here), forgiveness, and a "generous provision" that answers the provision just spent.
* **`[8:5]`** "Even as your Lord brought you out of your home with the truth, some believers were reluctant."
  * **Gloss:** The definition is now dated: the deployment from Medina for Badr, fought by some only under protest.
* **`[8:6]`** "Arguing with you about the truth after it was made clear, as if they were being driven to death as they looked on."
  * **Gloss:** Disputation after evidence — the fear rendered as spectators of their own death.
* **`[8:7]`** "Allah has promised you one of the two groups—that it would be yours—but you wanted the unarmed group to be yours. Allah intends to prove the truth with His words, and to uproot the disbelievers."
  * **Gloss:** You wanted the caravan; the plan was the armed party — Allah's purpose (truth made true, falsehood nullified) overrides preference.
* **`[8:8]`** "In order to confirm the truth and nullify falsehood, even though the guilty dislike it."
  * **Gloss:** The purpose clause repeats — the battle as litigation between true and false, against criminal preference.
* **`[8:9]`** "When you appealed to your Lord for help, He answered you, “I am reinforcing you with one thousand angels in succession.”"
  * **Gloss:** The cry is answered; the reinforcement announced — angels arriving one after another.
* **`[8:10]`** "Allah only made it a message of hope, and to set your hearts at rest. Victory comes only from Allah. Allah is Mighty and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The means are demoted to morale; the victory is re-attributed to Allah alone — the ruku's closing clause of trust.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`anfal` ("the bounties"):** the surah's title-word; the disputed spoils of Badr.
  * **Rendering:** "the bounties" at `[8:1]` only; the English word "bounties" is single-site in the corpus (rg-verified). At `[8:41]` the corpus uses "spoils" for the gain itself — whether Itani pairs two Arabic families here (anfal vs. ghanima) is a lexical claim: *Source: agent inference — verify against Arabic.*
  * **Elsewhere:** the English kin-word "bounty" appears at `[9:75]` ("If He gives us of His bounty") — the broken-pledge verse cited in the nifaq note; treat as English-word pairing, not root identity (verify).
* **`tawakkul` ("upon their Lord they rely"):** reliance as a defined trait (8:2).
  * **Rendering:** "they rely"; the surah later re-prices it twice: "whoever puts his trust in Allah" (`[8:49]`) and "put your trust in Allah" (`[8:61]`).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[14:12]` "upon Allah the reliant should rely"; `[5:23]` "put your trust in Allah, if you are believers" — the same conditional grammar as `[8:1]`'s "if you are believers."
* **Prayer-and-spending pair (8:3):** the two acts that make the trembling heart legible.
  * **Rendering:** "from Our provisions to them, they spend."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[32:16]` "from Our provisions to them, they give" — near-verbatim twin; `[2:3]` "perform the prayers, and give from what We have provided"; `[35:29]` spenders "expect a trade that will not fail" — the pair is the book's signature behavior, and 8:4's "generous provision" answers 8:3's spending (cf. `[22:50]` "forgiveness and a generous provision", verbatim twin of 8:4's wage).
* **"angels ... in succession" (8:9):**
  * **Rendering:** "one thousand angels in succession."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:124]` "three thousand angels, sent down" and `[3:125]` "five thousand angels, well trained" — the Uhud promise, different numbers and wording (classical readings link 3:124–125 to Badr's memory or a pre-Uhud promise — *Source: disagreement in classical reports — verify*).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the surah's material is the Badr aftermath (2 AH per the transmitted sira). The corpus itself never names Badr (rg: only `[3:123]` does) — dating is external to the English text (*Source: classical consensus — verify*).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** the question about "the bounties" is classically reported as the dispute over Badr's spoils after the battle (*Source: transmitted reports collected by classical commentators, e.g., Ibn Kathir on 8:1 — verify*). No occasion is stated in the corpus (observable fact).
