# Good in hearts, alliance law, and the true believers restated (8:70–75)
**UID:** 202610141460
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 8:70–75 (Ruku 10 of 10 in al-Anfal)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[8:70]` – `[8:75]` (6 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The surah's last movement opens captive hearts to grace — "If Allah finds any good in your hearts, He will give you better than what was taken from you" (70), the exact counterfactual of 8:23 run in the merciful direction — then draws the community's membership map: full alliance for those who believed, emigrated, struggled, and sheltered; a suspended clause for believers who did not emigrate; help owed except against treaty-partners (72); a warning that disbelievers' mutual alliance left unchecked means turmoil in the land (73); and the verdict ring — "these are the true believers" (74) — closing with latecomers included and blood-kin restored to their place in Allah's Book (75).
* **Placement:** Final ruku; 8:74 repeats 8:4's definition verbatim after 70 verses of proving it, completing the surah's ring composition.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[8:70]`** "O prophet! Say to those you hold prisoners, “If Allah finds any good in your hearts, He will give you better than what was taken from you, and He will forgive you. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.”"
  * **Gloss:** The captive amnesty: the same divine search for "any good" (8:23) now offered to the enemy — with compensation better than the ransom taken.
* **`[8:71]`** "But if they intend to betray you, they have already betrayed Allah, and He has overpowered them. Allah is Knowing and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** Symmetry to 70: betrayal against the community is already a betrayal against Allah whose outcome He has decided.
* **`[8:72]`** — four clause-chunks:
  * "Those who believed, and emigrated, and struggled in Allah’s cause with their possessions and their persons, and those who provided shelter and support—these are allies of one another."
  * "As for those who believed, but did not emigrate, you owe them no protection, until they have emigrated."
  * "But if they ask you for help in religion, you must come to their aid, except against a people with whom you have a treaty."
  * "Allah is Seeing of what you do."
  * **Gloss:** The wilaya statute: faith-plus-exodus-plus-striving and shelter-minus-exodus graded in alliance; the treaty clause (mithaq-family word in English here) fences even aid obligations.
* **`[8:73]`** "As for those who disbelieve, they are allies of one another. Unless you do this, there will be turmoil in the land, and much corruption."
  * **Gloss:** The mirror-law: unaligned unbelief is itself an alliance; the boundary not drawn becomes "turmoil" (fitna-word again rendered "turmoil"; see Ruku 3's rendering family).
* **`[8:74]`** "Those who believed, and emigrated, and struggled for Allah’s cause, and those who gave shelter and support—these are the true believers. They will have forgiveness, and a bountiful provision."
  * **Gloss:** The ring verse: 8:2–4's definition re-issued at the surah's close with the wage restated — "bountiful provision" answering 8:4's "generous provision."
* **`[8:75]`** "As for those who believed afterwards, and emigrated and struggled with you—these belong with you. But family members are nearer to one another in the Book of Allah. Allah is Cognizant of everything."
  * **Gloss:** Latecomers folded into the community, and inheritance returned to kin — the final clause re-locates "nearness" in Allah's Book, not in contract-brotherhood.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"emigrated" (72, 74, 75):** the third trait of the ring definition.
  * **Rendering:** "believed, and emigrated, and struggled" — a fixed triple in this surah's closing.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:218]` "Those who believed, and those who migrated and fought for the sake of Allah" — same triple, drift "emigrated/migrated, struggled/fought" (observable); `[4:89]` "unless they emigrate in the way of Allah... do not take from among them allies or supporters" — the alliance-emigration nexus outside this surah (verified).
* **"allies of one another" (72–73):**
  * **Rendering:** wilaya as belonging.
  * **Elsewhere:** the exact mirror `[45:19]` "The wrongdoers are allies of one another, while Allah is the Protector of the righteous" (verified); the prohibition-side `[9:23]` "Do not ally yourselves with your parents and your siblings..." and `[3:52]` "Who are my allies towards Allah?" — the corpus's wilaya grammar (all lines verified).
* **"shelter and support" (72, 74):**
  * **Rendering:** the Medinan Ansar-function.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[59:8]`–`[59:9]` "the poor refugees who were driven out of their homes... Those who... had settled in the homeland... love those who emigrated to them" — the same pair of roles named (verified); within surah 8:26's divine shelter ("He sheltered you") and `[32:19]` "Gardens of Shelter" as the reward-form (the root-identity reading shelter/reward is lexical — agent inference — verify).
* **"provision" ladder (3, 4, 74):**
  * **Rendering:** spent from "Our provisions" (8:3) → wage is "a generous provision" (8:4) → "a bountiful provision" (8:74).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[22:50]` "for them is forgiveness and a generous provision" — verbatim twin of 8:4's wage; `[22:58]` emigrants killed or dying get "fine provisions" — the provision-of-hijrah idiom (verified lines; drift generous/bountiful/fine observable).

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the alliance statute assumes the pre-conquest situation where emigration defined membership — classically read as relaxed after "there is no emigration after the conquest" (*Source: transmitted reports — verify*).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 8:70's amnesty offer to prisoners is classically attached to a Badr captive's story (*Source: transmitted reports, e.g., classical asbab material on 8:70 — verify*); 8:75's kin-nearness is classically read as abrogating the muwakhah (brotherhood) inheritance contracts (*Source: classical reports — verify*). The corpus states neither (observable).
