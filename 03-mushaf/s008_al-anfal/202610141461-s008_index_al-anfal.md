**UID:** 202610141461

**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Orientation
* **Revelation:** Medinan — classical consensus counts the whole surah post-Hijrah. The transmitted classical lists place it at revelation order 88 (Source: classical ordering lists — agent labeling — verify against a printed 'ulum al-Quran edition). Note the tension: the surah's material is Badr-era (2 AH, among the earliest Medinan events), yet the lists seat it after al-Baqarah (87) — the enumeration tracks transmission/collection sequence, not strict chronology. *Source: agent inference — verify.*
* **Size:** 75 ayahs, 10 ruku (global ruku 146–155; Hanafi/Idad-e-Quran partition per `data/ruku_map.json`).
* **Badr is never named:** `rg` shows the corpus names Badr only at `[3:123]` ("Allah had given you victory at Badr, when you were weak"). In this surah the day is titled "the Day of Distinction, the day when the two armies met" (`[8:41]`) — the battle functions as a structure of evidence, not a proper name (observable corpus fact).
* **Central theme:** A loot question — "They ask you about the bounties" (`[8:1]`) — is answered by redistribution into a doctrine of trust: 8:2–4 defines the believer as one whose heart trembles at Allah's mention, grows at recitation, relies on the Lord, prays, and spends from divine provision. The surah then replays Badr (5–19), legislates march ethics and cohesion (15–16, 45–46), faces the hypocrite question (21–24, 49), wraps the community in covenant language — hearing, obeying, trusts, treaties (20–28, 56–58, 72) — and closes by repeating the definition verbatim: "these are the true believers" (`[8:74]` ↔ `[8:4]`; the phrase occurs in the corpus only at these two sites).

## 3. Structural Arc
* **Question → definition (1–4):** the bounty dispute (8:1) is answered not with shares yet but with character: trembling, recitation, reliance, prayer, spending (8:2–3), sealed with "These are the true believers" (8:4) — the ring verse that closes at 8:74.
* **Badr re-staged (5–19):** reluctant deployment (5–6), the wrong preference for the caravan (7–8), the cried help and the thousand angels (9–10), drowsiness, rain, angelic command (11–12), no-retreat law (15–16), and the agency claim — "It was not you who killed... it was Allah who launched" (17).
* **Covenant address (20–29):** six "O you who believe!" calls mark the surah's pivot (8:15, 20, 24, 27, 29, 45 — observable count via rg): obey and hear (20–23), respond to what revives (24), fear collective discord (25), remember Meccan weakness (26), keep the trusts (27–28), and the promise that taqwa buys a criterion (29).
* **The other camp and the law of the day (30–44):** the counter-plot of the disbelievers (30), the recitation rejected as "myths of the ancients" (31–32), the two stayings of punishment (33), the custodianship quarrel over the Sacred Mosque (34–35), enemy wealth-spending answered by Allah's claim on spoils — the fifth, on the Day of Distinction (36–41), then the optical and logistical re-staging of the two armies (42–44).
* **Cohesion under pressure (45–58):** stand firm plus much remembrance (45–46); the boastful march and the patron who defects on sight (47–48); the hypocrite-and-sickness taunt (49); deathbed and Pharaoh as the permanent case-law of broken blessing (50–54); the worst of creatures and the treaty-breaker code (55–58).
* **From battle to polity (59–75):** deterrence funded by Allah (60), the peace-inclination and exclusive trust (61–64); the ratio, the prisoners, and the permission to eat the spoils (65–69); the alliance law of emigration and shelter, ending on kin, knowledge, and the ring: "these are the true believers" (70–75).
