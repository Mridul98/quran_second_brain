# Stand firm, remember much, and do not dispute (8:45–48)
**UID:** 202610141456
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 8:45–48 (Ruku 6 of 10 in al-Anfal)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[8:45]` – `[8:48]` (4 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The shortest ruku in the surah is its stamina chapter: meet a force with firmness and much remembrance (45), obey and refuse internal dispute or the courage drains away (46) — and do not be the army that marched out boastfully, whose allied voice ("I am at your side") evaporates the moment the armies can see each other (47–48).
* **Placement:** The sixth and last "O you who believe!" call (45) closes the internal-cohesion sequence begun at 8:20; 47–48 supply its counter-image, the boastful march and the patron who defects on sight.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[8:45]`** "O you who believe! When you meet a force, stand firm, and remember Allah much, so that you may prevail."
  * **Gloss:** The two weapons before the sword: footing and remembrance — steadiness defined as a practice, victory ("prevail") as its stated aim.
* **`[8:46]`** "And obey Allah and His Messenger, and do not dispute, lest you falter and lose your courage. And be steadfast. Allah is with the steadfast."
  * **Gloss:** A chain with one weak link: dispute → falter → lost courage; the promise "Allah is with the steadfast" is the corpus's fixed formula (2:153, 2:249 verbatim).
* **`[8:47]`** "And do not be like those who left their homes boastfully, showing off before the people, and barring others from the path of Allah. Allah comprehends what they do."
  * **Gloss:** The counter-army portrait: exit for show, entrance into the path's waylaying; "comprehends" — no deed in the crowd escapes the encircling knowledge.
* **`[8:48]`** "Satan made their deeds appear good to them, and said, “You cannot be defeated by any people today, and I am at your side.” But when the two armies came in sight of one another, he turned on his heels, and said, “I am innocent of you; I see what you do not see; I fear Allah; Allah is severe in punishment.”"
  * **Gloss:** The patronage confession scene: proximity claimed at the march, disavowal at first sight — reliance on an unseen ally is unmasked the moment seeing begins (cf. 8:49's trust counter-law, next verse).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **"stand firm" / "be steadfast" (45–46):**
  * **Rendering:** "stand firm... be steadfast. Allah is with the steadfast."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:153]` "Seek help through patience and prayers. Allah is with the steadfast" — verbatim promise twin; `[2:249]` "Allah is with the steadfast" at the river-test; `[3:200]` "Be patient, and advocate patience, and be united" — steadfastness as the surah's cohesion verb (the word "steadfast" appears on three lines of s008: 8:46, 8:65, 8:66 — rg).
* **"remember Allah much" (45):**
  * **Rendering:** "remember Allah much."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[33:41]` "remember Allah with frequent remembrance" — the near-verbatim command; `[22:40]` — mosques "where the name of Allah is mentioned much" — the same adverb defending the very Houses the 8:34 custodianship quarrel is about (verified pairs).
* **"boastfully, showing off" (47):**
  * **Rendering:** "boastfully, showing off before the people."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:142]` "stand lazily, showing off in front of people" — riya's ritual face; `[2:264]` "spends his wealth to be seen by the people" — the charity face of the same disease (English-word pairs verified; root-identity claims = inference).
* **"do not dispute" (46):**
  * **Rendering:** "do not dispute, lest you falter and lose your courage."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[3:105]` "do not be like those who separated and disputed after the clear proofs came" — twin warning; `[4:59]` supplies the procedure: "if you dispute over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger"; within surah, 8:1's "settle your differences" and 8:43's "disputed in the matter" bracket this command.

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Satan's defection formula:** `[59:16]` — "he says, “I am innocent of you; I fear Allah, the Lord of the Worlds.”" — 8:48 is the longer, battlefield version of the same disavowal; `[6:43]`/`[27:24]`/`[29:38]` carry verbatim "Satan made their deeds appear good to them" — the motif across the corpus (all verified lines).
* **Two exits from one town:** `[8:5]` — "your Lord brought you out of your home... some believers were reluctant" ↔ 8:47 — "those who left their homes boastfully": the surah stages Badr as a contest of departures, one reluctant-but-obedient, one proud-and-barred (within-surah pair, observable).
* **Trust vs. patronage:** `[8:49]` — "But whoever puts his trust in Allah—Allah is Mighty and Wise" answers 8:48's "I am at your side" in the very next verse — the ruku boundary falls exactly on the thesis' hinge.
* **Courage economics:** `[8:43]` "you would have lost heart" (Ruku 5) ↔ 8:46 "lose your courage" — same failure word, now policed by a command; `[8:63]` (Ruku 8) prices hearts as Allah's asset.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan; the cohesion commands read onto the Badr day, but the corpus never names it here (observable; cf. index note on Badr's absence).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 8:47–48 are classically described of the Quraysh army's march and its ally's counsel at Badr (*Source: classical transmitted reports, e.g., Ibn Kathir on 8:47–48 — verify*); the English text supports no occasion beyond the description itself.
