# The ratio of the steadfast and the prisoners' dilemma (8:65–69)
**UID:** 202610141459
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 8:65–69 (Ruku 9 of 10 in al-Anfal)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[8:65]` – `[8:69]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Steadfastness is priced as arithmetic and then discounted for human weakness — twenty beat two hundred, a hundred beat a thousand, until Allah "has now lightened your burden" and re-rates the exchange at one-to-two (65–66); and the ruku's second half answers the question the ratio raises: what to do with the men you did not kill (67–69) — a rebuke for desiring the world's materials, a rescue by the predetermined decree, and finally the permission to eat the spoils, lawful and wholesome.
* **Placement:** The second and third "O prophet!" calls; the prisoners' scene closes the spoils arc begun at 8:1 and prepares the amnesty of prisoners' hearts in 8:70.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[8:65]`** "O prophet! Rouse the believers to battle. If there are twenty steadfast among you, they will defeat two hundred; and if there are a hundred of you, they will defeat a thousand of those who disbelieve; because they are a people who do not understand."
  * **Gloss:** Morale as mathematics: a one-to-ten exchange rate, grounded in the enemy's ignorance (not Muslim muscle).
* **`[8:66]`** "Allah has now lightened your burden, knowing that there is weakness in you. If there are a hundred steadfast among you, they will defeat two hundred; and if there are a thousand of you, they will defeat two thousand by Allah’s leave. Allah is with the steadfast."
  * **Gloss:** The rate halves to one-to-two — "by Allah’s leave" replaces arithmetic as the operative term; divine realism about weakness is called lightening, not demotion.
* **`[8:67]`** "It is not for a prophet to take prisoners before he has subdued the land. You desire the materials of this world, but Allah desires the Hereafter. Allah is Strong and Wise."
  * **Gloss:** The ransom-of-Badr rebuke (classical reading): capturing for profit before the war's purpose is settled inverts the Prophet's desire for the Hereafter.
* **`[8:68]`** "Were it not for a predetermined decree from Allah, an awful punishment would have afflicted you for what you have taken."
  * **Gloss:** The third "predetermined" line in the surah (8:42, 44, 68 — rg): punishment was pre-empted, not owed.
* **`[8:69]`** "So consume what you have gained, legitimate and wholesome; and remain conscious of Allah. Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** What was rebuked is permitted — the spoils eaten under two adjectives and the taqwa clause, and the surah's first forgiveness-pair closes the arc.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **Steadfastness, priced twice (65–66):**
  * **Rendering:** "twenty steadfast... a hundred steadfast"; "Allah is with the steadfast."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:249]` — "How many a small group has defeated a large group by Allah’s will. Allah is with the steadfast" — the ratio's precedent at Saul's river-test; `[2:153]` — the same promise formula (all verbatim pairs verified).
* **"prisoners" (67, 70 next ruku):**
  * **Rendering:** "take prisoners... he has subdued the land."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[47:4]` — "when you have routed them, bind them firmly. Then, either release them by grace, or by ransom" — the corpus's other prisoner-law verse, which 8:67–68 classically presuppose (*Source: classical linkage of 47:4 to 8:67 — verify*); `[33:26]` — "Some of them you killed, and others you took captive" (verified).
* **"the materials of this world" (67):**
  * **Rendering:** "You desire the materials of this world, but Allah desires the Hereafter."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[4:94]` — "aspiring for the goods of this world" — the same desire-phrase (ʿarad, per the pairing's lexical claim — agent inference — verify), used there for premature judgment of a professing believer; the drift "materials/goods" is observable.
* **"legitimate and wholesome" (69):**
  * **Rendering:** "consume what you have gained, legitimate and wholesome."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:88]` — "eat of the lawful and good things Allah has provided for you; and be conscious of Allah" — near-verbatim twin including the taqwa clause; `[2:168]` "lawful and good" — the pair's canonical form (rendering drift "wholesome/good" observable; same-claim on halalan tayyiban = lexical inference — verify).

## 4. Cross-References (The Web)
* **Ratio precedent:** `[2:249]` — small groups defeating large "by Allah’s will" — 8:65–66 is that principle converted into statute and then re-stated; 3:123 "victory at Badr, when you were weak" is the historical witness (verified).
* **Decree motif:** within surah 8:42/44 → 8:68 — "predetermined" binds the battle's optics to the prisoners' amnesty: punishment precluded before it was owed.
* **Prisoner arc to next ruku:** 8:69's "Forgiving and Merciful" pre-runs `[8:70]`'s offer to the captives ("He will forgive you... Allah is Forgiving and Merciful") — the doublet of mercy-names opens the amnesty paragraph (observable).
* **Spoils ring:** 8:67–69 ↔ `[8:1]` and `[8:41]` — the bounties question, its fifth-law, and now its consumption-permission: one three-stop legal arc.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan, Badr aftermath; classical reports transmit that 8:66 abrogated/modified the exchange rate of 8:65 (the "lightening"), a standard instance in abrogation literature (*Source: transmitted reports via Ibn Abbas and classical naskh lists — verify*).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** 8:67–69 are classically tied to the consultation over the Badr prisoners (the positions transmitted from Abu Bakr and Umar, and the report that a divine address settled it) (*Source: transmitted reports collected by classical commentators — verify*); the corpus itself names no actors or occasion (observable).
