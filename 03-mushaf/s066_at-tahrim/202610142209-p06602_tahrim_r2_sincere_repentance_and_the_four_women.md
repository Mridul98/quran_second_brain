# Sincere repentance, the strive-command, and the four-women paradigm (66:8–12)
**UID:** 202610142209
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 66:8–12 (Ruku 2 of 2 in at-Tahrim)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[66:8]` – `[66:12]` (5 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The road back is opened wider than the fault: repentance commanded with the corpus's only "sincere" qualifier, gardens and an unfailing divine promise to the Prophet and his believers, and a light whose owners will beg for it to be completed (66:8); the communal command to strive against disbelievers and hypocrites follows (66:9) — and then Allah Himself stages the surah's final argument: four women in two example-pairs, the wives of Noah and Lot, inside righteous houses, who betrayed and availed nothing (66:10); the wife of Pharaoh, inside the worst house on earth, who asked for a house with Allah (66:11); and Maryam bint Imran — guarded, Spirit-breathed, believing His Words and His Books (66:12). Kinship to power saves no one; faith inside powerlessness saves everyone; and a surah that began with the Prophet's wives ends on a woman defined by nothing but belief and guarding.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 2; the closing paradigm. Ruku 1 corrected one household and warned all households (66:6); ruku 2 gives the return (66:8), the outward charge (66:9), and the verdict-graph that redefines the family of God by deed, not blood (66:10–12).

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[66:8]`** "O you who believe! Repent to Allah with sincere repentance. Perhaps your Lord will remit your sins, and admit you into gardens beneath which rivers flow, on the Day when Allah will not disappoint the Prophet and those who believed with him. Their light streaming before them, and to their right, they will say, “Our Lord, complete our light for us, and forgive us; You are capable of all things.”"
  * **Gloss:** "Sincere repentance" (tawbatan nasuha) — the phrase is corpus-unique (rg-verified); the adjacent sincerity-family is the nifaq foil: `[9:119]` "be with the sincere," `[57:19]` "these are the sincere and the witnesses." The promise carries divine "Perhaps" — the Lord's own grammar of hope — anchored in a Day-picture where Allah does not disappoint the Prophet and those who believed with him: the ruku-1 alliance-list (Allah, Gabriel, the believers, the angels) cashed out as eventual vindication. The light-bearers, already saved, still petition: "complete our light for us" — guidance is a flame carried, not a possession held (cf. k-guidance's "road, not an abstraction"). The verse-chain of this juz closes: Light sent down (64:8) → darkness into light (65:11) → light streaming, prayed to be completed (66:8).
* **`[66:9]`** "O prophet! Strive hard against the disbelievers and the hypocrites, and be stern with them. Their abode is Hell. What a miserable destination!"
  * **Gloss:** Near-verbatim twin of `[9:73]` — "Strive against the disbelievers and the hypocrites, and be stern with them. Their abode is Hell—what a miserable destination!" (drift: "Strive hard" vs "Strive"; sentence-clip vs em-dash). The believer-versus-hypocrite pair is the nifaq dossier's standing frame; the inner threat of ruku 1 (banding together, 66:4) is here absorbed into the official enemy-list — the hypocrite is the conspiracy formalized. Cf. `[25:52]` for the other mode: "strive against them with it, a mighty struggle" — the Quran as the striving instrument.
* **`[66:10]`** "Allah illustrates an example of those who disbelieve: the wife of Noah and the wife of Lot. They were under two of Our righteous servants, but they betrayed them. They availed them nothing against Allah, and it was said, “Enter the Fire with those who are entering.”"
  * **Gloss:** The disbelief-mathal, two women in one line. "Under" (tahta) — positioned inside prophet-houses; the sin is "they betrayed them" (khiyanah — the trust-betrayal verb of `[8:27]` "Do not betray Allah and the Messenger, nor betray your trusts"). The verdict clause "availed them nothing against Allah" is the family-privilege formula of `[3:10]` ("neither their wealth nor their children will avail them anything against Allah"). And the sentence is a command of co-entry: "Enter the Fire with those who are entering" — no special departure, only shared destination. Note the narrative split: the corpus preserves these women's fates without their identities (`[7:83]` Lot's wife "lagged behind"; `[11:81]` "She will be struck by what will strike them"; `[29:33]` "who will remain behind"; `[26:171]` "an old woman among those who tarried"; and Noah's lost son with the line that redefines family — `[11:46]` "he is not of your family") — while at-Tahrim names the relationship and omits the scene. *Source: structural observation — agent inference, verify.*
* **`[66:11]`** "And Allah illustrates an example of those who believe: the wife of Pharaoh, when she said, “My Lord, build for me, with you, a house in Paradise, and save me from Pharaoh and his works, and save me from the wrongdoing people.”"
  * **Gloss:** The belief-mathal: the only other "wife of Pharaoh" locus is `[28:9]` — the woman who saved the infant Moses ("Do not kill him; perhaps he will be useful to us") — here she speaks her own rescue: three petitions, build / save / save. A "house" (bayt) asked of Allah answers the whole triad's house-obsession: 64 warned the household, 65 legislated its terms and residences, 66 now puts on a tyrant's wife's lips the only house-request that survives — "with you," in Paradise. The double "save me" separates the man from his works and from his people: disentanglement, not just escape. *Source: classical reading of the house-request as counter-architecture — agent inference, verify.*
* **`[66:12]`** "And Mary, the daughter of Imran, who guarded her womb, and so We breathed into her of Our Spirit; and she believed in the truth of her Lord’s Words and His Books, and was one of the devout."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's final woman, the only one named and given a patronymic — "daughter of Imran," answering `[3:35]`'s "The wife of Imran" who vowed her womb-child, and `[3:36]`'s naming of Mary, and `[3:42]`'s angelic address: "Allah has chosen you... chosen you over all the women of the world." Four clauses seal her: guarded ("her womb" — the same act `[21:91]` renders "guarded her virginity," and the guard-family of `[33:35]` renders "chastity" — three nouns, one root, rg-verified); breathed into ("We breathed into her of Our Spirit," near-verbatim twin of `[21:91]`'s "We breathed into her of Our spirit," cf. `[19:17]` "We sent to her Our spirit") — the conception node of the Isa dossier, whose "Word from Him" is `[3:45]`; believed — "in the truth of her Lord’s Words and His Books" (faith defined as assent to speech: cf. `[6:115]` "The Word of your Lord has been completed, in truth and justice"; `[10:82]` "Allah upholds the truth with His words"; and 5:75's appellation for her, "a woman of truth"); devout — the q-n-t word of 66:5's ladder, rendered "obedient" there and "the devout" here: the exchange-list's abstract ladder terminates in a woman no one married (rendering drift rg-observable). *Source: translator's term choice; structural observation — agent inference, verify.*

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`tawba nasuh` (sincere repentance):** the return made undivided.
  * **Rendering:** "Repent to Allah with sincere repentance" (66:8) — corpus-unique phrase; classical lexicography reads nasuha as pure/counseled/firm (*Source: classical lexicons — verify*).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:222]` "Allah loves the repentant, and He loves those who keep clean" — the twin locus, in the very ruku that legislates purity; the sincerity root n-s-h: `[9:119]` "be with the sincere"; `[57:19]` "these are the sincere and the witnesses"; `[26:101]` "no sincere friend" — the nifaq contrast. See [menstruation, oaths and waiting periods](../s002_al-baqarah/202610140938-p00228_menstruation_oaths_and_waiting_periods.md).
* **`nur` (light, running):** 66:8 "Their light streaming before them, and to their right... complete our light for us."
  * **Rendering:** near-verbatim twin: `[57:12]` "their light radiating ahead of them, and to their right" — same scene, two verbs ("radiating"/"streaming").
  * **Elsewhere:** the juz-chain 64:8 "the Light which We sent down" → 65:11 "from darkness into light" → 66:8 light carried and completed; cf. `[24:35]` "Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth."
* **`jahada/khanu` (strive / betray):** the verse's two enemy-figures and the paradigm's one sin-verb.
  * **Rendering:** "Strive hard against the disbelievers and the hypocrites" (66:9 = `[9:73]` twin); "they betrayed them" (66:10) — khiyanah, the trust-verb of `[8:27]`; cf. `[25:29]` "Satan has always been a betrayer of man."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:52]` — striving "with it" (the Book) as the mightier mode; k-nifaq's frame (betrayal from within) is this verse's exact archetype. Forward link.
* **`Ruh/kalimat` (Spirit / Words):** the conception and the creed of Maryam.
  * **Rendering:** "We breathed into her of Our Spirit" (66:12; capital-S) ≈ `[21:91]` (lower-case "spirit"); `[19:17]` "We sent to her Our spirit"; "her Lord’s Words and His Books" — plural assent.
  * **Elsewhere:** the Isa-dossier Spirit family: `[2:87]`/`[2:253]` "the Holy Spirit" (different phrase entirely); `[5:110]` "I supported you with the Holy Spirit"; `[16:102]` "The Holy Spirit has brought it down"; and Isa as "a Word from Him" (`[3:45]`). 66:12 is the mother-node of that dossier — k-isa link below.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (*Source: classical type-lists — verify*). No asbab attaches to the paradigm; the examples are deliberately timeless — the only unnamed women of ruku 1 (66:3–5) are answered by the named and the title-named women of ruku 2. *Source: agent inference — verify.*
* **Classical identifications (recorded, not asserted):** the two conspiring wives of ruku 1 are classically Aisha and Hafsa; Pharaoh's wife is transmitted as Āsiya; Noah's and Lot's wives carry transmitted names (Wāgha/Wāliʿa-type forms) found in no Quranic text — *Source: classical reports — verify.* The claim that Maryam is the only woman named in the Quran fits this corpus (rg finds no other female personal name in s001–s114 heads/verses) — *Source: classical consensus + corpus observation — verify.*
