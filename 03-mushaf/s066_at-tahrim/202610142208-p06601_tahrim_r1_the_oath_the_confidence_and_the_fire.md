# The prohibition reproved, the confidence disclosed, and the family-Fire (66:1–7)
**UID:** 202610142208
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 66:1–7 (Ruku 1 of 2 in at-Tahrim)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[66:1]` – `[66:7]` (7 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Revelation enters the Prophet's own house: a self-imposed prohibition is questioned ("Why do you prohibit what Allah has permitted for you, seeking to please your wives?"), oaths are dissolved by decree, and a private confidence — told, disclosed, and known to Allah first — is transcribed with tact (66:1–3); two conspiring addressees, unnamed in the text, face repentance or a counter-alliance of Allah, Gabriel, the righteous believers and the angels, and an exchange of "wives better than you" (66:4–5); the lesson then pivots to every household: protect yourselves and your families from the Fire whose fuel is people and stones, guarded by angels who never disobey — for on that Day excuses close (66:6–7).
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 2; the prophet-household half and the terminus of the 64–66 household arc: 64 called wives and children trial, 65 legislated their endings, 66 shows revelation correcting the best of households from inside.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[66:1]`** "O prophet! Why do you prohibit what Allah has permitted for you, seeking to please your wives? Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
  * **Gloss:** The rebuke is a question, and the motive is named — the economy of pleasing. The verb-pair prohibit/permitted recurs exactly once more in the corpus, as communal law: `[5:87]` "Do not prohibit the good things Allah has permitted for you." The reproof carries its own acquittal in the closing names, Forgiving and Merciful. Edition note: "O prophet!" is lower-case here and at 66:9, while 65:1 and `[9:73]` have "O Prophet!" — a corpus-observable rendering drift. *Source: translator's term choice (observable in corpus).*
* **`[66:2]`** "Allah has decreed for you the dissolution of your oaths. Allah is your Master. He is the All-Knowing, the Most Wise."
  * **Gloss:** "Decreed" (farada) — the legislation-verb applied to oath-release. The classical fiqh reading takes this as the kaffarah (expiation) duty, whose spelled-out form is `[5:89]` ("The atonement for it is by feeding ten needy people..."); the translation's word is "dissolution." *Source: classical tafsir — verify.* "Allah is your Master" is the verbatim twin of `[3:150]`; the names All-Knowing, Most Wise bracket a domestic ruling the way they bracket cosmic ones.
* **`[66:3]`** "The Prophet told something in confidence to one of his wives. But when she disclosed it, and Allah made it known to him; he communicated part of it, and he avoided another part. Then, when he informed her of it, she said, “Who informed you of this?” He said, “The All-Knowing, the All-Informed, informed me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The narrative core, stripped of names and of content: "in confidence" is corpus-unique (rg-verified). Notice the proportions: Allah tells him everything; he conveys "part" and avoids "another part" — tact is permitted inside the transcript. Her question and his answer stage the epistemology of revelation: information routes through "The All-Knowing, the All-Informed."
* **`[66:4]`** "If you repent to Allah, then your hearts have listened. But if you band together against him, then Allah is his Ally, as is Gabriel, and the righteous believers. In addition, the angels will assist him."
  * **Gloss:** The dual address appears — "if you band together" — two voices implied, never named. "Then your hearts have listened" is an opaque clause (classical readings: their hearts leaned/hearkened to the disclosure — *Source: agent inference — verify*). The counter-alliance is stacked four deep: Allah, Gabriel, the righteous believers, the angels. Gabriel as the Prophet's Ally is the exact inversion of `[2:97]` — "Whoever is hostile to Gabriel—it is he who revealed it to your heart by Allah’s leave" (the other two Gabriel-loci are `[2:98]`; rg-verified).
* **`[66:5]`** "Perhaps, if he divorces you, his Lord will give him in exchange wives better than you: submissive, believing, obedient, penitent, devout, fasting—previously married, or virgins."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's one divorce-word — the same talaq vocabulary as 65:1, now aimed at the prophet-household. The six-virtue ladder echoes the paired list of `[33:35]` ("Muslim men and Muslim women, believing men and believing women, obedient... fasting...") and the choice already given at `[33:28–29]` (world and finery vs. Allah, His Messenger, and the Home of the Hereafter). The ladder's q-n-t word, rendered "obedient" here, returns rendered "devout" at 66:12 — the surah's abstract ladder finds its concrete instance in its final woman. *Source: translator's term choice (observable in corpus); structural observation — agent inference, verify.*
* **`[66:6]`** "O you who believe! Protect yourselves and your families from a Fire, whose fuel is people and stones. Over it are angels, fierce and powerful. They never disobey Allah in anything He commands them, and they carry out whatever they are commanded."
  * **Gloss:** The pivot to every household: the imperative is the waqā-verb of taqwa — "Protect yourselves and your families" — the surah's answer to 64:14's "Among your wives and your children are enemies to you, so beware of them." The Fire clause is verbatim-twin of `[2:24]` ("whose fuel is people and stones"; fuel-language also at `[21:98]`, `[3:10]`). The angelic guard is a counter-image of the malfunctioning household just transcribed: angels never disobey "in anything He commands them" — the Prophet's house is corrected precisely because it is not exempt.
* **`[66:7]`** "O you who disbelieved! Make no excuses today. You are being repaid for what you used to do."
  * **Gloss:** A flash-forward to the Day — the surah speaks to disbelievers in the middle of an address to believers, as if the fire's occupants were already present in the room. The excuse-closing formula pairs with `[30:57]` ("the sinners' excuses will not benefit them") and `[40:52]` ("their excuses will not profit the wrongdoers") — and ruku 1 began with a man's motive (pleasing) and ends with the end of motives: repayment for deeds.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`tahrim` (prohibit):** the surah's name-word.
  * **Rendering:** "prohibit... permitted" (66:1); the same pair as command at `[5:87]`. Passive forms elsewhere: `[40:66]` "I was prohibited from worshiping"; `[16:118]` (Jewish dietary prohibitions); `[58:8]` (secret conspiring); `[60:9]` (alliance prohibitions).
  * **Elsewhere:** the permitting-side verb halala renders "made lawful" (`[5:5]` "Today all good things are made lawful for you") — the hint's "made lawful" belongs to that family, not to 66:1's text. *Source: translator's term choice (observable in corpus); rg-verified.*
* **`yamin` (oaths):** the oath-cluster.
  * **Rendering:** 66:2 "the dissolution of your oaths" — the word "expiation" does not appear in the translation here.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[5:89]` — the atonement graded list; `[2:224–225]` — oaths not as hindrance to virtue; unintentional vs. binding; `[16:92]` — "Nor use your oaths as means of deception." Four legal loci; at-Tahrim's is the only one addressed through a prophet's own case. *rg-verified.*
* **`mawla/wali` (Master, Ally):** the protection vocabulary.
  * **Rendering:** "Allah is your Master" (66:2, = `[3:150]` verbatim); "Allah is his Ally" (66:4); drift to "Protector" at `[8:40]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** the alliance-list — Gabriel (`[2:97–98]`), "the righteous believers," "the angels will assist him" — society and heaven marshaled on the Prophet's side against two household voices.
* **`waqā` (protect):** the household imperative.
  * **Rendering:** "Protect yourselves and your families" (66:6); the same root renders "beware of Allah" (65:10), "be pious before Allah" (65:1), "fears Allah" (65:2, 4, 5), "be conscious of Allah" (64:16) — one waqā root, five English renderings across the household triad. *Source: translator's term choices; rg-verified.* (64:14's "beware of them" is a different root, hadhara — thematic echo, not lexical twin.)
  * **Elsewhere:** `[2:24]` "beware the Fire whose fuel is people and stones" — the identical object-clause, addressed in Baqarah to all people, here to believers with families attached.

## 6. Context Notes
* **Revelation context:** Medinan (*Source: classical type-lists — verify*).
* **Asbab / narrative context:** two competing occasion-reports are transmitted: (1) the honey-drink hadith — the Prophet abstained from honey at a wife's house, the wives "banded together," and 66:1 was revealed (via Aisha; Bukhari, Tafsir al-Quran); (2) reports tying the prohibition to an abstention connected with Maria the Copt. The text opts out of the dispute: no name, no object, no date. The classical identification of the two addressees as Aisha and Hafsa rides on report, not on wording. *Source: classical asbab reports — verify.*
* **Reading caution:** 66:3 "one of his wives" (singular) ≠ 66:4's dual address (two) — the text's arithmetic is part of its deliberate anonymity. *Source: observable in corpus.*
