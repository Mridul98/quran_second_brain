# Three Reversals, Three Responses: the orphan, the seeker, the blessing (93:1–11)
**UID:** 202610142316
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 93:1–11 (Ruku 1 of 1 in adh-Duha)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-15

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[93:1]` – `[93:11]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Answered to an unspoken charge of divine abandonment, the surah swears by morning light and settling night, denies that the Prophet's Lord left him or forgot him, promises giving-to-satisfaction and a better Hereafter — then litigates the past: three times He FOUND you lacking and REVERSED it (orphaned→sheltered, wandering→guided, in-need→enriched) — and the verdict is an ethic: as you were treated, so treat the orphan, the seeker; and the blessing: speak it out.
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 1; first half of the 93–94 consolation diptych, five verses after 92's "he will be satisfied" (92:21) — the same satisfaction-word is now promised to the surah's hearer. The triad-of-reversal → triad-of-response structure is the surah's whole argument: mercy remembered becomes mercy distributed.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[93:1]`–`[93:2]`** "By the morning light. / And the night as it settles."
  * **Gloss:** A two-state oath: brightness after darkness, rest after motion — the day-cycle itself testifies to alternation, not abandonment. Dawn-oath family (rg-verified): `[81:18]` "by the morn as it breathes", `[74:34]` "the morning as it lights up", `[92:2]` "the day as it reveals", and 91:3–4's day-reveal/night-cover pair.
* **`[93:3]`** "Your Lord did not abandon you, nor did He forget."
  * **Gloss:** The denial of the charge — two verbs: abandonment (*hajar*) and what Itani renders "forget." The address is intimate: "your Lord" — the same formula as 92:20's "his Lord." (Corpus-unique "abandon you" collocation — rg-verified; 93:3 does not name the taunt, only refutes it.)
* **`[93:4]`** "The Hereafter is better for you than the First."
  * **Gloss:** Value-reorientation for the present humiliation: the two abodes compared *for you*. Exact conceptual twin `[87:16–17]` "you prefer the present life. Though the Hereafter is better, and more lasting" (rg-verified). "the First" = the first abode, this life — bare adjective-as-noun, Itani's consistent choice (cf. 92:13 "the Last and the First").
* **`[93:5]`** "And your Lord will give you, and you will be satisfied."
  * **Gloss:** The open-ended promise: giving, to satisfaction. The *raḍiya* verb closes 92 (92:21, the giver) and here closes the first movement (the Prophet) — satisfaction as divine gift, with `[20:130]` the third site (rg-verified).
* **`[93:6]`** "Did He not find you orphaned, and sheltered you?"
  * **Gloss:** Exhibit one: found (*wajada*) — the pre-existing lack met by pre-existing care. "orphaned" — the biographical fact also underwrites 93:9's duty; the orphan-cluster: 107:2 "It is he who mistreats the orphan" (verbal twin, rg-verified), 4:5, 2:220 (protection law).
* **`[93:7]`** "And found you wandering, and guided you?"
  * **Gloss:** Exhibit two: *ḍāllan* — Itani "wandering" (not "astray"); guidance (*hadā*) as the answering verb — the personal hidāya case for k-guidance's transit strand (2:257 darkness→light; rg: note cites no [93]).
* **`[93:8]`** "And found you in need, and enriched you?"
  * **Gloss:** Exhibit three: *ʿāʼilān* — "in need" (not "poor"); answered by enrichment (*aghkā*). The third lack is economic, which is why the third command (93:11) is public speech about blessing, and why 92's wealth-triad stands immediately before this diptych.

* **`[93:9]`** "Therefore, do not mistreat the orphan."
  * **Gloss:** Response one: the identical English phrase-pair of `[107:2]` "It is he who mistreats the orphan" (rg-verified) — the surah writes its own counter to al-Kathir's denial-test: there, orphan-mistreatment PROVES denial of the religion; here, it is forbidden by name. (Whether Itani uses one English verb for two different Arabic roots — *qahira* in 93:9, *ḍaḍḍ* in 107:2 per classical reading — is agent inference — verify.)
* **`[93:10]`** "Nor rebuff the seeker."
  * **Gloss:** Response two: *lā tanhar as-sā'il* — "rebuff" (*nahar*: scold away); "the seeker" is Itani's rendering where `[70:25]` renders the same root "the beggar" (rg-verified drift). Guidance received → the questioner not turned away.
* **`[93:11]`** "But proclaim the blessings of your Lord."
  * **Gloss:** Response three: *limma anʿama* — *haddith bi-niʿmati*: narrate/proclaim it. The only "blessings of your Lord" collocation in the corpus (rg-verified; kin: "blessings of Allah" `[29:67]`, `[11:73]`, `[7:96]`). Enrichment received → testimony spoken. (*Source: reading of Itani's renderings; the three-way mapping to 93:6–8 is classical and observable in the reversal structure — structural observation on corpus.*)

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`raḍiya (be satisfied)`**: the promise-verb of 93:5.
  * **Rendering:** "and you will be satisfied" — same as 92:21 "And he will be satisfied."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[20:130]` "…that you may be satisfied" (rg-verified) — satisfaction is the *telos* of glorification; a satisfaction-cluster (92/93/20) no knowledge note owns — gap reported.
* **`wajada (find)`**: the exhibit-verb of 93:6–8 — God FINDS the lack, then REVERSES it.
  * **Rendering:** "Did He not find you orphaned…?" ×1 opening the anaphora; "And found you wandering/in need" ×2 carrying it.
  * **Elsewhere:** the "Did He not find you" formula is corpus-unique (rg-verified); its interrogation cousin "Did We not" runs 94:1–4 and the blessing-anaphora of `[90:8]`, `[77:16/20/25]`, `[78:6]` (rg-verified).
* **`aḍ-ḍāll / hidāya (wandering/guided)`**: 93:7.
  * **Rendering:** "wandering… guided" — motion verbs for the second lack: lost on a road, then shown one.
  * **Elsewhere:** k-guidance's road-grammar (`1:6`, `6:153`, `17:9`); 93:7 is guidance as biography, the note's personal case it lacks (rg-verified: no [93] in k-guidance).
* **`sāʼil (seeker)`**: 93:10.
  * **Rendering:** "the seeker" here; "the beggar" at `[70:25]` "For the beggar and the deprived" (rg-verified) — same root, two Englishers; 93:10 is broader than alms: anyone who asks.
* **`niʿma (blessing)`**: 93:11.
  * **Elsewhere:** k-shukr's whole ledger — 14:7 gratitude-increase decree, 16:83 "They recognize Allah's blessing, but then deny it," 27:40 "to test me, whether I am grateful or ungrateful" (rg-verified on note); 93:11 adds the *speech* command — proclaim what you recognize — note lacks [93]: append candidate.
