# Abraham's enduring word, the great man of the two cities, and the decorations of this life (43:26–35)
**UID:** 202610142024
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 43:26–35 (Ruku 3 of 7 in az-Zukhruf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[43:26]` – `[43:35]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** Against the inherited fathers'-course of the last ruku, Abraham is set up as the father who *breaks* inheritance — his disavowal made "an enduring word in his progeny" — and the same rejection instinct re-enacts itself in the present: "This is sorcery," and the counter-demand that the Quran have gone to "a man of importance from the two cities," answered with the silver-roofed thought-experiment that names the surah: decorations are nothing but the stuff of this life.
* **Placement:** Ruku 3 of 7; the surah's first prophetic exemplar (Abraham, 26–28) brackets its second (Jesus, 57–65), and the "single community" clause (33) states the divine economics that the wealth-rejection of 23 and the gold-demands of 53 will illustrate.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[43:26]`** "When Abraham said to his father and his people, “I am innocent of what you worship."
  * **Gloss:** The disavowal speech; quotation opens here and closes at 43:27 (class-2 typography, quote as-is); near-twin `[60:4]` "We are quit of you, and what you worship apart from Allah."
* **`[43:27]`** "Except for He who created me, for He will guide me.”"
  * **Gloss:** The exception clause: creation is the one worship-claim that survives the purge, and guidance is attributed to the Creator alone — cf. 43:10's pathways "that you may be guided."
* **`[43:28]`** "And he made it an enduring word in his progeny, so that they may return."
  * **Gloss:** "the enduring word" (*kalima baqiya*) is corpus-unique in this form (rg-verified); the tawhid formula becomes a legacy — the deliberate counter-legacy to "We found our parents on a course" (22).
* **`[43:29]`** "I gave these and their forefathers some enjoyment, until the truth and a manifest messenger came to them."
  * **Gloss:** The respite-motif: long enjoyment is not approval; cf. `[6:44]` — when they forgot the Reminder, "We opened for them the doors of everything," until sudden seizure.
* **`[43:30]`** "But when the truth came to them, they said, “This is sorcery, and we refuse to believe in it.”"
  * **Gloss:** The mockery-motif (7) in its present tense; the slur-formula cluster: `[10:2]` "This is a manifest sorcerer," `[40:24]` "A lying sorcerer," `[51:39]`, `[51:52]`.
* **`[43:31]`** "They also said, “If only this Quran was sent down to a man of importance from the two cities.”"
  * **Gloss:** The rank-demand: revelation should follow social hierarchy; "the two cities" (*al-Qariytayn*) is corpus-unique (rg-verified). *Source: classical tafsir reads the two cities as Makkah and Ta'if, the great men proposed as al-Walid b. al-Mughira and Urwa b. Mas'ud al-Thaqafi — verify in print; no names are in the corpus text.*
* **`[43:32]`** "Is it they who allocate the mercy of your Lord? It is We who have allocated their livelihood in this life, and We elevated some of them in rank above others, that some of them would take others in service. But your Lord’s mercy is better than what they amass."
  * **Gloss:** The allocation-answer: rank-distribution belongs to God and serves mundane interdependence ("that some of them would take others in service"); His *mercy* is a different economy than their amassed wealth.
* **`[43:33]`** "Were it not that humanity would become a single community, We would have provided those who disbelieve in the Most Gracious with roofs of silver to their houses, and stairways by which they ascend."
  * **Gloss:** The thought-experiment: unbelief could be refuted by luxury, but then humanity would coalesce around appetite; cf. `[10:19]` "Mankind was a single community; then they differed" — the same *ummah-wahida* logic inverted.
* **`[43:34]`** "And doors to their houses, and furnishings on which they recline."
  * **Gloss:** The silver list continues — doors, reclining-furnishings (the *suruf* of luxury); the same "reclining on furnishings" imagery is promised to the believers' Garden elsewhere (cf. `[18:31]` "Reclining on comfortable furnishings").
* **`[43:35]`** "And decorations. Yet all that is nothing but the stuff of this life. Yet the Hereafter, with your Lord, is for the righteous."
  * **Gloss:** *Zukhruf* — the surah's name-anchor, corpus-unique as a noun here (rg-verified); the value-verdict: stuff of this life vs. the Hereafter reserved for the righteous (which 43:72 will deliver).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`bara'a/innocent of`**: Abraham's disavowal verb.
  * **Rendering:** "I am innocent of what you worship" (43:26).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[60:4]` — the same speech cast as the excellent example for the community ("We are quit of you… Enmity and hatred has surfaced between us and you, forever").
* **`kalima baqiya` (the enduring word)**: legacy as confession, not bloodline.
  * **Rendering:** "he made it an enduring word in his progeny, so that they may return" (43:28).
  * **Elsewhere:** corpus-unique in this form (rg "enduring word": s043 only); its function is measured against the failed inheritance of 43:22–23.
* **`sahr` (sorcery)**: the rejection's favorite diagnosis.
  * **Rendering:** "This is sorcery, and we refuse to believe in it" (43:30).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[10:2]`, `[40:24]`, `[51:39]`, `[51:52]`; and 43:49's address "O sorcerer" will put the same word in Pharaoh's court — this surah keeps the slur in family rotation.
* **`zukhruf` (decorations)**: the gold and finery of worldly life.
  * **Rendering:** "And decorations." as the silver-list's capstone and value-verdict (43:35).
  * **Elsewhere:** noun unique in this corpus (rg-verified); cognate imagery — demanded as proof at `[43:53]` ("bracelets of gold"), gifted in the Garden at `[22:23]` and `[18:31]` — the surah's own gold-irony.
