# The laughing court: "O sorcerer, pray for us," Pharaoh's Egypt-claim, and the drowning of them all (43:46–56)
**UID:** 202610142026
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 43:46–56 (Ruku 5 of 7 in az-Zukhruf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[43:46]` – `[43:56]` (11 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The Moses–Pharaoh dossier is told as the mockery-motif's case-study: signs met with laughter, plagues met with a bargain ("O sorcerer, pray to your Lord for us… and then we will be guided"), the bargain broken at the lifting — and then Pharaoh's sermon to his people, the surah's set-piece of anti-tawhid theatre ("do I not own the Kingdom of Egypt, and these rivers flow beneath me? Do you not see?"), answered by drowning and a precedent made permanent.
* **Placement:** Ruku 5 of 7, the structural center of the surah (its 11 ayahs bracket exactly the midpoint, between the Abraham-ruku pair and the Jesus-ruku pair); it re-runs Ghafir's Pharaoh dossier from the mocker's angle, and its gold-and-reclining imagery (53) has been pre-scored by 43:33–35's silver list.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[43:46]`** "We sent Moses with Our revelations to Pharaoh and his dignitaries. He said, “I am the Messenger of the Lord of the Worlds.”"
  * **Gloss:** The mission filed: revelations (plural, cf. 47–48), addressed to the court as a class ("his dignitaries" — Ghafir's triad names them at `[40:24]`); the creed-title "Lord of the Worlds" spoken into a claimed-king's hall.
* **`[43:47]`** "But when he showed them Our signs, they started laughing at them."
  * **Gloss:** Ledger: the task's "the day We will seize them all together (43:47?)" is ✗ — this is the laughter verse, the motif of 43:7 performed live.
* **`[43:48]`** "Each sign We showed them was more marvelous than its counterpart. And We afflicted them with the plagues, so that they may repent."
  * **Gloss:** Escalating sign-set plus the *plagues* (singular *rajs*-type word here — cf. `[7:134]` "Whenever a plague befell them…"); purpose-clause: "so that they may repent."
* **`[43:49]`** "They said, “O sorcerer, pray to your Lord for us, according to His pledge to you, and then we will be guided.”"
  * **Gloss:** The bargain under torment — verbatim-twin of `[7:134]` ("O Moses, pray to your Lord for us, according to the covenant He made with you. If you lift the plague from us, we will believe you"); they retain the slur (cf. 30, 40:24, 51:39) even while requesting his prayer.
* **`[43:50]`** "But when We lifted the torment from them, they immediately broke their promise."
  * **Gloss:** The pledge-break, verbatim-twin `[7:135]` ("…they broke their promise") — covenant-language in action (cf. `[9:77]` for hypocrisy as the penalty of broken promise).
* **`[43:51]`** "Pharaoh proclaimed among his people, saying, “O my people, do I not own the Kingdom of Egypt, and these rivers flow beneath me? Do you not see?"
  * **Gloss:** The self-claim ✓ — the surah's anti-tawhid theatre: kingship, river-ownership, and an appeal to sight ("Do you not see?"); the garden-formula "beneath which rivers flow" (cf. `[16:31]`, `[7:43]`, `[39:20]`) put in a tyrant's mouth — the inverted claim-stuffing; quotation opens here, runs to 43:53 (class-2 typography, quote as-is).
* **`[43:52]`** "Am I not better than this miserable wretch, who can barely express himself?"
  * **Gloss:** Contempt as theology: status by speech — answering 43:18's scorn-grammar (the "unable to help in a fight" line); dramatic irony: the "wretch's" own petition at `[28:34]` asks for Aaron's eloquence — Moses's speech-trouble is corpus-text, Pharaoh's reading of it is contempt.
* **`[43:53]`** "Why are bracelets of gold not dropped on him, or they angels came with him in procession?”"
  * **Gloss:** The evidence-demand — treasure and angels (verbatim pattern of `[11:12]` "If only a treasure was sent down to him, or an angel came with him"); note the gold: what Pharaoh mocks with, 43:33–35 has already called "nothing but the stuff of this life," and 43:71 will serve on the believers' trays. [sic "they angels" — book typography, quote as-is]
* **`[43:54]`** "Thus he fooled his people, and they obeyed him. They were wicked people."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict on the persuasion: *khārafa* — he "fooled" (made them empty-headed); obedience to a flattered claim is named wickedness, echoing 43:23's followership-refrain.
* **`[43:55]`** "And when they provoked Our wrath, We took retribution from them, and We drowned them all."
  * **Gloss:** The seizure the task looked for at 47 is here in its true form: drowned "them all"; near-twin `[7:136]` ("So We took vengeance on them, and drowned them in the sea").
* **`[43:56]`** "Thus We made them a precedent and an example for the others."
  * **Gloss:** The dossier's closing stamp: precedent (*sunnah*-type) and example (*mathal*) — cf. `[25:37]` ("We drowned them, and made them a lesson for mankind") and the ancients' precedent of 43:8; the "example" word carries straight into the next ruku's Jesus dispute (57, 59).

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`sahir` (O sorcerer)**: the slur retained inside the plea.
  * **Rendering:** "O sorcerer, pray to your Lord for us" (43:49) — no name; the title is the insult.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:134]` (the same plea addressed "O Moses"), `[40:24]` "A lying sorcerer," `[51:39]`, `[51:52]`; the demand-cluster "treasure/angels" pairs with `[11:12]`.
* **`mulk` (kingdom claimed)**: Pharaoh's self-property.
  * **Rendering:** "do I not own the Kingdom of Egypt, and these rivers flow beneath me?" (43:51).
  * **Elsewhere:** the surah answers at `[43:85]` "blessed is He Who has sovereignty over the heavens and the earth"; inverted rivers — cf. `[16:31]`, `[7:43]`, `[39:20]` (gardens beneath which rivers flow, given, not owned).
* **`wa'd/pledge and promise-break`**: the plague-bargain's covenant shape.
  * **Rendering:** "according to His pledge to you… they immediately broke their promise" (49–50).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:134–135]` the verbatim twin pair; `[9:77]` — breaking promise as the seed of hypocrisy.
* **`ibrah/precedent and example`**: history as exhibit.
  * **Rendering:** "a precedent and an example for the others" (43:56).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[25:37]` (lesson for mankind); "precedent of the ancients" at `[18:55]`, `[35:43]`, `[33:62]`; the same *mathal* word pivots the Jesus-ruku (43:57, 59).
