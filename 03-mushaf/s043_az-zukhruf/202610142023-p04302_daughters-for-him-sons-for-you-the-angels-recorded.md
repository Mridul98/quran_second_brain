# Daughters for Him, sons for you: the angels dressed female, the willed-excuse, and the fathers' course (43:16–25)
**UID:** 202610142023
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 43:16–25 (Ruku 2 of 7 in az-Zukhruf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[43:16]` – `[43:25]` (10 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The ruku prosecutes one slander in three layers — the attribution of daughters to God while sons are kept for themselves (16–19), the fatalistic alibi "Had the Most Gracious willed" (20–21), and the inheritance alibi "We found our parents on a course" (22–24) — and closes with the verdict that every town's wealthy have said exactly this, and were dealt with accordingly.
* **Placement:** Ruku 2 of 7; the immediate consequence of 43:15's "part of Him" verdict. The recorded-and-questioned clause (19) plants the surveillance motif that ripens at `[43:80]`; the fathers'-course formula (22–24) becomes the surah's specimen of the mockery-motif (7) at the social level.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[43:16]`** "Or has He chosen for Himself daughters from what He creates, and favored you with sons?"
  * **Gloss:** The taunt turned back on its owners: their own preference-structure makes the claim absurd; cf. `[53:21]` "Are you to have the males, and He the females?" and `[37:149]`.
* **`[43:17]`** "Yet when one of them is given news of what he attributes to the Most Gracious, his face darkens, and he suppresses grief."
  * **Gloss:** A daughter's birth still darkens the face of the man who calls angels Her daughters — the hypocrisy of the slander exposed by its own reflex; the divine name here is "the Most Gracious" (this edition's consistent *al-Rahman* rendering in s043).
* **`[43:18]`** “Someone brought up to be beautiful, and unable to help in a fight?”
  * **Gloss:** Their contempt spoken in their own idiom (a bare quotation with no speech tag — book typography, quote as-is); it rhymes — deliberately, the ruku-reader will see — with Pharaoh's contempt for Moses at `[43:52]`: two lines of the same scorn-grammar, one against daughters, one against a prophet.
* **`[43:19]`** "And they appoint the angels, who are servants to the Most Gracious, as females. Have they witnessed their creation? Their claim will be recorded, and they will be questioned."
  * **Gloss:** The charge named: *servants* made female and divine; the courtroom aside — recorded and questioned — pre-announces `[43:80]` ("Our messengers are by them, writing down") and pairs with `[53:27]` "Those who do not believe in the Hereafter give the angels the names of females."
* **`[43:20]`** "And they say, “Had the Most Gracious willed, we would not have worshiped them.” But they have no knowledge of that; they are merely guessing."
  * **Gloss:** The fatalism alibi — providence pleaded as permission; verbatim-twin `[6:148]` ("Had Allah willed, we would not have practiced idolatry…") and near-twin `[41:14]`; "merely guessing" answers with the same phrase-end as 6:148 ("You follow nothing but conjecture").
* **`[43:21]`** "Or have We given them a book prior to this one, to which they adhere?"
  * **Gloss:** The evidence-demand: no prior scripture supports it — answered structurally at `[43:45]`, where every prior messenger is cross-questioned on exactly this point.
* **`[43:22]`** "But they say, “We found our parents on a course, and we are guided in their footsteps.”"
  * **Gloss:** The second alibi: religion as inheritance; cf. `[7:28]` "We found our parents doing this" — the same defense cited in al-A'raf's indecency indictment.
* **`[43:23]`** "Likewise, We sent no warner before you to any town, but the wealthy among them said, “We found our parents on a course, and we are following in their footsteps.”"
  * **Gloss:** The sociology of rejection: the refrain is universal and class-marked — "the wealthy among them" is corpus-unique phrasing (rg-verified: s043 only).
* **`[43:24]`** "He would say, “Even if I bring you better guidance than what you found your parents following?” They would say, “We reject what you are sent with.”"
  * **Gloss:** The warner's reductio — and their answer is verbatim the same line as `[41:14]` "we reject what you are sent with": the repudiation formula of every mission.
* **`[43:25]`** "So We wreaked vengeance upon them. Behold, then, what was the fate of those who deny."
  * **Gloss:** The precedent closes the case; the behold-the-fate formula matches `[16:36]` "see what the fate of the deniers was" — and prepares Pharaoh's own fate at `[43:55]`.

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`banat` (daughters attributed)**: the slander's pivot — daughters to God, sons to yourselves.
  * **Rendering:** "Or has He chosen for Himself daughters… and favored you with sons?" (43:16).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[53:21–22]` ("What a bizarre distribution"), `[37:149–152]` ("Are the daughters for your Lord…? 'Allah has begotten.' They are indeed lying") — the same prosecution in the two sibling surahs.
* **`‘ibad` (the angels as servants)**: the title that makes the daughters-claim incoherent.
  * **Rendering:** "the angels, who are servants to the Most Gracious" (43:19).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[53:27]` — the female-naming diagnosis; within s043, 43:15 already fixed them as "one of His servants."
* **`inna Allah shā'a`-type fatalism (the willed-excuse)**: providence invoked to license idolatry.
  * **Rendering:** "Had the Most Gracious willed, we would not have worshiped them" (43:20).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[6:148]` verbatim pattern; `[41:14]` "Had our Lord willed, He would have sent down angels" — the same alibi at the moment of rejecting messengers.
* **`sabil/athar` (the fathers' course)**: religion as footstep-inheritance.
  * **Rendering:** "We found our parents on a course, and we are guided in their footsteps" (43:22; repeated verbatim by the wealthy, 43:23).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[7:28]` — the same "we found our parents" defense under indictment.
