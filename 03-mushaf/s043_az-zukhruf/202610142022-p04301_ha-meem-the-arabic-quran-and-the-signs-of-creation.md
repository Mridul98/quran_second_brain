# Ha, Meem: the Arabic Quran from the Source Book, and the creation-court's verdict (43:1–15)
**UID:** 202610142022
**Type:** Passage / Ruku Note
**Passage:** 43:1–15 (Ruku 1 of 7 in az-Zukhruf)
**Last Studied:** 2026-10-14

## 1. Unit & Thesis
* **Range:** `[43:1]` – `[43:15]` (15 ayahs)
* **Thesis:** The letters hand over a Book sworn by its own clarity — Arabic, intelligible, kept sublime in the Source Book — and when the mockers' precedent of rejected messengers is recalled, the ruku turns the accusation around by putting creation itself on the stand: the pagans' own answers (who made the heavens, the earth, the rain, the mounts?) convict them of ingratitude, ending "Yet they turn one of His servants into a part of Him. Man is clearly ungrateful."
* **Placement:** Ruku 1 of 7; the surah's creedal and epistemic frame. Its "No messenger came… but they ridiculed him" (7) is the mockery-motif the whole surah demonstrates (23–25, 30, 47, 58) and dismisses (83, 89); its creation-court (9–14) is answered at the close by the ring-question of `[43:87]`.

## 2. Verse Arc (corpus-anchored)
* **`[43:1]`** "Ha, Meem."
  * **Gloss:** Muqatta'at letters, transliterated and untranslated, numbered as ayah 1 — head of the Ha-Meem family (40–46, all seven openings rg-verified; see Fleeting link).
* **`[43:2]`** "By the Book that makes things clear."
  * **Gloss:** The oath-form Book-claim the letters front; the same root phrase runs in the openings of `[15:1]` ("a Quran that makes things clear") and `[27:1]`, while the Ha-Meem neighbor swears by its variant, `[44:2]` "By the Enlightening Scripture."
* **`[43:3]`** "We made it an Arabic Quran, so that you may understand."
  * **Gloss:** The intelligibility-claim: language chosen for comprehension. Ledger: the task's pairing is exact — cf. `[20:113]` "Thus We have revealed it an Arabic Quran…" and `[26:195]` "In a clear Arabic tongue."; and `[12:2]` repeats this verse's tail verbatim ("so that you may understand").
* **`[43:4]`** "And it is with Us, in the Source Book, sublime and wise."
  * **Gloss:** The same Book is simultaneously local (Arabic, addressed) and transcendent ("with Us, in the Source Book"); cf. `[13:39]` "With Him is the source of the Scripture" — this edition's only other near-phrase of *umm al-kitab*.
* **`[43:5]`** "Shall We hold back the Reminder from you, since you are a transgressing people?"
  * **Gloss:** Incredulous question as rebuke: the reminder (dhikr) was not withheld — they are the problem; "Reminder" recurs inside the surah at `[43:36]` ("Whoever shuns the remembrance of the Most Gracious").
* **`[43:6]`** "How many a prophet did We send to the ancients?"
  * **Gloss:** Ledger check ✓: the task's "And how many prophets did We send to the ancients" is this verse verbatim.
* **`[43:7]`** "No messenger came to them, but they ridiculed him."
  * **Gloss:** The surah's governing motif stated as divine custom; near-twin `[51:52]` "Likewise, no messenger came to those before them, but they said, 'A sorcerer or a madman.'" — where 43:30 and 43:49 will supply this surah's version of the slur.
* **`[43:8]`** "We destroyed people more powerful than they, and so the example of the ancients has passed away."
  * **Gloss:** Mockery has a body count; the destroyed ancients stand as precedent (the *mathal/precedent* vocabulary tightens at `[43:56]`–`[43:59]`).
* **`[43:9]`** "And if you asked them, “Who created the heavens and the earth?” They would say, “The Mighty, the Knower created them.”"
  * **Gloss:** The court convenes: their own creed answers the question; cf. `[31:25]` (same question, answer "Allah") — here the names "the Mighty, the Knower" anticipate the divine signature of `[43:84]`.
* **`[43:10]`** "He who made the earth a habitat for you, and traced pathways for you on it, that you may be guided."
  * **Gloss:** Creation as guidance-infrastructure; "made the earth a habitat for you" is the same phrasing as `[2:22]`, and pathways recur at `[16:69]` ("go along the pathways of your Lord").
* **`[43:11]`** "He who sends down water from the sky in due proportion; and so We revive thereby a dead land. Thus you will be brought out."
  * **Gloss:** Rain-to-life becomes the proof of resurrection ("Thus you will be brought out"); "in due proportion" is corpus-unique wording (rg-verified: s043 only).
* **`[43:12]`** "He Who created all the pairs; and provided you with ships, and animals on which you ride."
  * **Gloss:** Pairs, ships, riding-animals: the transport-catalogue; cf. `[36:71–72]` ("livestock that they own… Some they ride, and some they eat") and `[40:80]` ("on them, and on the ships, you are transported").
* **`[43:13]`** "That you may mount their backs, and remember the favor of your Lord as you sit firmly upon them, and say, “Glory be to Him Who placed these at our service; surely we could not have done it by ourselves."
  * **Gloss:** The liturgy built into technology: remembering the favor (*ta'kib al-ni'ma*) with a set words; the quotation opens here and closes at 43:14 (book typography, quote as-is — see Fleeting artifacts note).
* **`[43:14]`** "And surely, to our Lord we will return.”"
  * **Gloss:** The dua's second half binds gratitude to return; cf. `[20:55]` "into it We will return you" and the calamity-formula `[2:156]`.
* **`[43:15]`** "Yet they turn one of His servants into a part of Him. Man is clearly ungrateful."
  * **Gloss:** The verdict of the court: the angels — *His servants* (19) — are claimed as His "part," i.e., daughters; ingratitude (the opposite of 13's remembered favor) named. *Source for referent: classical tafsir reads 43:15 as the angel-daughters claim — verify in print.*

## 3. Key Terms & Roots
* **`Ha, Meem`**: transliterated letter-names, non-semantic.
  * **Rendering:** "Ha, Meem." exactly, as ayah 1; family signature of surahs 40–46 (rg-verified at 40:1, 41:1, 42:1, 43:1, 44:1, 45:1, 46:1).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[44:2]` — the next surah swears by "the Enlightening Scripture" where this one swears by "the Book that makes things clear" (43:2): one family, two oaths.
* **`al-Kitab al-Mubin` (the Book that makes things clear)**: clarity as the Book's self-description.
  * **Rendering:** "the Book that makes things clear" (43:2); variant phrase at `[15:1]`, `[27:1]`.
  * **Elsewhere:** `[41:3]` "A Scripture whose Verses are detailed, a Quran in Arabic" — clarity and Arabness paired outside this surah too.
* **`Quran ‘arabiyy` (an Arabic Quran)**: 43:3; the language-of-revelation claim.
  * **Rendering:** "We made it an Arabic Quran, so that you may understand."
  * **Elsewhere:** `[12:2]`, `[20:113]`, `[26:195]`, `[41:3]`, `[41:44]` (the hypothetical foreign-language Book they would have demanded clarified) — the full rg-verified cluster the task asked to check.
* **`umm al-kitab` (the Source Book)**: the transcendent register of the Book.
  * **Rendering:** "in the Source Book, sublime and wise" (43:4).
  * **Elsewhere:** `[13:39]` "With Him is the source of the Scripture" — this edition's only other occurrence of the phrase-image (rg-verified).
