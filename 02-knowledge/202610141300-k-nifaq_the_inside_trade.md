# Nifaq: the inside trade — belief as a transaction
**UID:** 202610141300
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines (all rg-verified against `corpus/`). Classical frame: the definition of *nifaq* as outward profession of belief covering inward concealment the identification of `[2:8]`–`[2:20]` as the Medinan hypocrite-dossier, and the reading of *marad* in `[2:10]` as doubt/weakness in religion, are standard in Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi on 2:8–20; the lexical note on the root *n-f-q* (a hole/tunnel — an entry made unseen) is classical lexicography — verify wording in a printed edition before quoting it. The "counterfeit of covenant / bad exchange" synthesis and all cross-surah pairing in §Concept are agent inference — verify.

## Concept
The Quran's word for fraudulent belief is built almost entirely out of commerce. The hypocrite (*munafiq*) is not portrayed as someone who lacks belief-language, but as someone who **books the transaction in the mouth and settles it somewhere else** — and the text keeps his ledger open until the Day when "no ransom will be accepted" (`[57:15]`).

1. **The longest portrait never uses the label.** `[2:8]`–`[2:20]` is thirteen verses of diagnosis with the noun "hypocrite" absent — `rg -i "hypocrit" corpus/s002_al-baqarah.md` returns 0 hits (observable corpus fact). It opens on the confession formula itself: "Among the people are those who say, 'We believe in Allah and in the Last Day,' but they are not believers" `[2:8]`. The *name*-cluster is elsewhere: 6 sites in an-Nisa, 5 in at-Tawbah, 6 in al-Ahzab, and a whole surah, al-Munafiqun (63). Practical effect: the type is defined by conduct, so it can be met in passages that never name it — `[2:76]` ("when they come across those who believe, they say, 'We believe'") uses the *identical* clause as `[2:14]`, and `[2:264]` profiles the man who "spends his wealth to be seen by the people, and does not believe in Allah and the Last Day."
2. **The mechanism is a mouth–heart split.** `[3:167]` "They say with their mouths what is not in their hearts; but Allah knows what they hide" and `[48:11]` "They say with their tongues what is not in their hearts" — one formula, two renderings (observable). `[63:1]` closes the gap with a divine counter-witness: "Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are liars." `[4:142]` gives the ritual signature: "when they stand for prayer, they stand lazily, showing off in front of people, and remembering Allah only a little."
3. **The master metaphor is a trade that does not clear.** `[2:16]` "Those are they who have bartered error for guidance; but their trade does not profit them, and they are not guided." The same idiom runs through the book: `[2:86]` "bought the present life for the Hereafter" (addressed to the `[2:85]` people who "believe in part of the Scripture, and disbelieve in part" — so the idiom is shared across partial belief, not native to 2:8–20); `[2:174]`–`[2:175]` "exchange it for a small price" / "It is they who exchange guidance for error, and forgiveness for punishment"; `[9:9]` "They traded away Allah's revelations for a cheap price, so they barred others from His path." (9:9's referent is the treaty-breakers of 9:7–9, not the labeled hypocrites — the idiom, not the category, is the point) The honest transactions are stated in the same vocabulary: `[9:111]` "Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and their properties in exchange for Paradise... So rejoice in making such an exchange—that is the supreme triumph"; `[61:10]` "Shall I inform you of a trade that will save you from a painful torment?" — answered by `[61:11]` "That you believe in Allah and His Messenger, and strive in the cause of Allah with your possessions and yourselves."; `[35:29]` the praying, spending ones "expect a trade that will not fail." *Agent inference — verify:* nifaq is therefore not the absence of the market but a losing position in it — unbelief that still expects a payout.
4. **It is covenant-counterfeiting, and the counterfeiting device is the oath.** `[9:75]`–`[9:77]`: they pledged "If He gives us of His bounty, we will donate and be among the upright"; when the bounty came "they became stingy with it" — "So He penalized them with hypocrisy in their hearts, until the Day they face Him—because they broke their promise to Allah, and because they used to lie." Note the grammar: *nifaq here is a verdict handed down on a broken promise, not a hidden identity discovered* (observable in the line; the theological weight is inference). `[63:2]` "They treat their oaths as a cover, and so they repel others from Allah’s path." The sincere contrast is the pledge at Hudaybiyyah: `[48:10]` "Those who pledge allegiance to you are pledging allegiance to Allah. The hand of Allah is over their hands. Whoever breaks his pledge breaks it to his own loss."
5. **The portrait decays through the senses: sickness → silence → darkness.** `[2:10]` "In their hearts is sickness, and Allah has increased their sickness"; `[2:18]` "Deaf, dumb, blind. They will not return."; `[2:17]` the fire lit and confiscated, "left them in darkness, unable to see"; `[2:20]` the lightning, "almost snatches their sight away. Whenever it illuminates for them, they walk in it; but when it grows dark over them, they stand still" — opportunism rendered as optics. Downstream echoes: `[47:20]` "those in whose hearts is sickness looking at you with the look of someone fainting at death," `[47:23]` "He made them deaf, and blinded their sight"; `[33:60]` "the hypocrites, and those with sickness in their hearts, and the rumormongers in the City"; `[74:31]` lines up "those in whose hearts is sickness and the unbelievers." And `[2:17]`'s light is billed at the end: `[57:13]` "Wait for us; let us absorb some of your light," answered by a wall, while `[57:19]` gives the believers "their reward and their light."
6. **The verdict, and the exit.** The punishment is pronounced *inside* the claim of faith: `[2:10]` "They will have a painful punishment because of their denial"; `[4:138]` "Inform the hypocrites that they will have a painful punishment" — an order to *announce* it. The rank is unique: `[4:145]` "The hypocrites will be in the lowest level of the Fire" — "lowest level" is the corpus's only site for this phrase (verified single hit). They are grouped with open unbelief — `[9:68]` "Allah has promised the hypocrite men and hypocrite women, and the disbelievers, the Fire of Hell"; `[48:6]` and `[33:73]` pair them with idolaters as the condemned classes — while `[9:101]` keeps them unfindable by the community: "They have become adamant in hypocrisy. You do not know them, but We know them." *Verify before repeating as exclusivity:* "painful punishment" is also pronounced on scripture-concealers `[2:174]` and on those who "love to be praised for what they have not done" `[3:188]`; what is distinctive to nifaq is the rank (4:145), the announcement (4:138), and the epistemic asymmetry (9:101). The category is not a caste: `[4:146]` "Except those who repent, and reform, and hold fast to Allah, and dedicate their religion to Allah alone. These are with the believers," and `[33:24]` leaves divine discretion open — "punish the hypocrites, if He wills, or pardon them."

## Evidence in the Text
* `[2:8]` "Among the people are those who say, 'We believe in Allah and in the Last Day,' but they are not believers." — the definition: confession narrated, then denied by the narrator.
* `[2:9]` "They seek to deceive Allah and those who believe, but they deceive none but themselves, though they are not aware." — deception re-routed onto the deceiver; the trade's first loss is ignorance of the loss.
* `[2:10]` "In their hearts is sickness, and Allah has increased their sickness. They will have a painful punishment because of their denial." — diagnosis (*marad*) plus the sentence; sickness compounds when indulged.
* `[2:14]` "And when they come across those who believe, they say, 'We believe'; but when they are alone with their devils, they say, 'We are with you; we were only ridiculing.'" — the two price-quotes; `[2:15]` "It is Allah who ridicules them, and leaves them bewildered in their transgression." — the market's counter-move.
* `[2:16]` "Those are they who have bartered error for guidance; but their trade does not profit them, and they are not guided." — the anchor verse for this note's title: the vocabulary is *tijarah*, and the headline is a failed P&L.
* `[2:17]`–`[2:20]` — the two parables: light withdrawn "and left them in darkness, unable to see" `[2:17]`, "Deaf, dumb, blind. They will not return" `[2:18]`, and the storm where "the lightning almost snatches their sight away" `[2:20]` — sensory capacity as the asset actually being traded.
* `[3:167]` "On that day they were closer to infidelity than they were to faith. They say with their mouths what is not in their hearts; but Allah knows what they hide." — the mouth/heart split made explicit, and dated to a battlefield moment (Uhud), i.e. when the deal looks risky.
* `[4:142]`–`[4:145]` "they stand lazily, showing off in front of people, and remembering Allah only a little... Wavering in between, neither with these, nor with those... The hypocrites will be in the lowest level of the Fire" — worship as a position held in two markets, settled in only one.
* `[4:138]` "Inform the hypocrites that they will have a painful punishment." — the only announcement of this kind addressed *to the Prophet about a group inside the ummah* (observable; "only" — verify against 2:174, 3:188).
* `[9:75]`–`[9:77]` "they became stingy with it... So He penalized them with hypocrisy in their hearts... because they broke their promise to Allah, and because they used to lie." — nifaq as the penalty for covenant default, with money as the test case.
* `[9:101]` "They have become adamant in hypocrisy. You do not know them, but We know them." — the category is deliberately unidentifiable from inside the community.
* `[48:11]`–`[48:15]` "Our belongings and our families have preoccupied us... They say with their tongues what is not in their hearts... Those who lagged behind will say when you depart to collect the gains, 'Let us follow you.'" — the same mouth/heart formula applied to the desert-Arabs, with *gains* (`[48:15]`) as the motive; `[48:10]` is the counter-pledge, and `[48:16]`–`[48:17]` attach "a painful punishment" to turning away.
* `[63:1]`–`[63:3]` "We bear witness that you are Allah’s Messenger... Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are liars. They treat their oaths as a cover... they believed, and then disbelieved; so their hearts were sealed." — testimony and oath as forged instruments; `[63:7]` "Do not spend anything on those who side with Allah’s Messenger, unless they have dispersed" — the counterparty trying to defund the community, and `[63:9]`–`[63:10]` ending the surah on spending, before death says "if only You would delay me for a short while."
* `[57:13]`–`[57:15]` "let us absorb some of your light... you cheated your souls, and waited, and doubted, and became deluded by wishful thinking... today no ransom will be accepted from you" — the trade's settlement: light borrowed too late, and no buy-back.
* `[47:20]`–`[47:24]` "those in whose hearts is sickness looking at you with the look of someone fainting at death... being true to Allah would have been better for them... Will they not ponder the Quran? Or are there locks upon their hearts?" — 2:10's sickness re-dramatized at the moment a decisive chapter arrives.
* Negative control (checked, does **not** belong to the trade motif): `[2:153]` "O you who believe! Seek help through patience and prayers. Allah is with the steadfast." — patience-and-prayer, no exchange language; the trade anchor in this ruku-region is `[2:16]`, and `[2:86]` belongs to the `[2:85]` part-believers.

## Practical Reading
Read the *ledger*, not the label. When a passage shows belief-language, ask four questions: **Where is the speech directed (people, or Allah)? What does it cost the speaker? What happens when the price moves (fear, spoils, defeat)? Is there an exit offered?** Nifaq always answers: speech aimed at people, cost avoided, position flipped under risk, exit still open (4:146; 33:24).
* Where to look next: **9** (the dossier at its most legislative — 9:64–68 mockery and the shared "one another" of `[9:67]`, 9:75–77 the broken pledge, 9:84 the refused funeral prayer, 9:101 "you do not know them"), **48** (the lagging bedouin and the pledge that counterfeits nothing — 48:10–17), **63** (the named surah: testimony, oaths, and a budget war, 63:1–8), **74** (only via the sickness phrase `[74:31]` — a good test that the motif travels without its noun), plus **33**, **57**, **59:11**, **47**.
* Searches to run: `rg -i "hypocrit|munafiq" corpus/ 03-mushaf/` (name-sites); `rg -i "hearts is sickness|sickness in their hearts" corpus/` (symptom-sites, catches unnamed cases); `rg -i "say with their (mouths|tongues)|lips|lip service" corpus/`; `rg -i "barter|trade|bought|purchased|exchange|sold himself|ransom|gains" corpus/` (the ledger vocabulary — read it as one field); `rg -i "painful punishment|severe torment" corpus/` (verdict-wording, and watch which rendering Itani uses where).
* Reframing effect: `[9:111]`, `[61:10]` and `[35:29]` stop being generic exhortations and become the *offer sheet* the hypocrite declines; `[2:264]` (the smooth rock) and `[63:10]` (the postponed charity) become the same transaction priced two ways.
