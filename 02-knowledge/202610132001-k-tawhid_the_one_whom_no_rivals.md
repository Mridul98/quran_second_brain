# Tawhid: one God — and the end of "partners you cannot see"
**UID:** 202610132001
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines; the shrine/rivals framing reflects classical tafsir consensus on 2:22 (associating partners after listing favors) and Ibn Kathir's treatment of 112; agent synthesis of structure — verify.

## Concept
Tawhid in the Quran is not a philosophical proposition but a double exclusion: **no god but He** (negation of rival objects) and **no Lord but He** (negation of hidden benefactors). It opens at the level of daily habit — the Fatihah's "It is You we worship" — then attacks its economic and cosmic roots: the creator-inventory ("who created you and those before you" 2:21; "He who made the earth a habitat for you, and the sky a structure, and sends water down from the sky" 2:22) immediately precedes the command "Therefore, do not assign rivals to Allah while you know." (2:22), even the four-line surah al-Ikhlas reduces God to irreducible claims: “He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Absolute. He begets not, nor was He begotten. And there is nothing comparable to Him.” (112:1–4, verbatim).

## Evidence in the Text
* `[1:5]` "It is You we worship, and upon You we call for help." — the daily oath of exclusivity.
* `[2:21–22]` — the creation inventory (habitat, sky-structure, water, fruit) ends: "do not assign rivals to Allah while you know." Tawhid is built from known benefits, not abstract oneness.
* `[2:163]` "Your God is one God. There is no god but He, the Benevolent, the Compassionate." — the clause with its mercy-names attached.
* `[7:172]` — the pre-birth testimony: "Am I not your Lord?" — tawhid as memory, not argument.
* `[59:22]` "He is Allah... the Knower of secrets and declarations..." — the oneness list closes with sovereignty over the unseen.
* `[112:1–4]` — the counter-claim to lineage theology whole: “Allah, the Absolute” (al-ɢamad — this edition’s unique rendering) followed by the double negation of begetting and the final “And there is nothing comparable to Him.”
* `[23:84–89]` — the triple interrogation: "To whom does the earth belong...?" — "To Allah." — "Will you not reflect?" repeated through heaven, Throne, dominion, protection: the pagans’ own admissions convict their partners. This is the book’s courtroom method for tawhid.
* `[23:91]`, `[23:116]` — "Allah has never begotten a son, nor is there any god besides Him. Otherwise, each god would have taken away what it has created, and some of them would have gained supremacy over others." — the same passage closes: "There is no god except He, the Lord of the Noble Throne." — the competing-gods reductio, sealed by doxology.
* `[114:1–3]` — "Say, “I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind. The King of mankind. The God of mankind." — the creed compressed into a three-name ladder on the lips of every reciter; the Quran’s last words before its final surah’s object (Rabb→Malik→Ilāh: nurture, sovereignty, divinity).

## Practical Reading
Tawhid is testable in the text by one question: **where does the passage assign to God an act humans were secretly crediting to another** (rain, wealth, children, fate, intercession)? That substitution is the Quran's real definition of shirk. Read 2:22, 6:100–102 (partners made from jinn), 39:3 (intercessors as friends) and the whole of 112 as the same argument from four angles.
