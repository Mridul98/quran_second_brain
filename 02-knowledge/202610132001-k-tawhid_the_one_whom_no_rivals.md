# Tawhid: one God — and the end of "partners you cannot see"
**UID:** 202610132001
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines; the shrine/rivals framing reflects classical tafsir consensus on 2:22 (associating partners after listing favors) and Ibn Kathir's treatment of 112; agent synthesis of structure — verify.

## Concept
Tawhid in the Quran is not a philosophical proposition but a double exclusion: **no god but He** (negation of rival objects) and **no Lord but He** (negation of hidden benefactors). It opens at the level of daily habit — the Fatihah's "It is You we worship" — then attacks its economic and cosmic roots: weather-ownership ("Who created the heavens and earth and sends water?" — 2:21–22 immediately precedes the command "do not set up rivals for Allah while you know"), even the one-word surah al-Ikhlas reduces God to irreducible attributes: One, Refuge-begetter, unbegotten, unmatched.

## Evidence in the Text
* `[1:5]` "It is You we worship, and upon You we call for help." — the daily oath of exclusivity.
* `[2:21–22]` — the creation inventory (habitat, sky-structure, water, fruit) ends: "do not assign rivals to Allah while you know." Tawhid is built from known benefits, not abstract oneness.
* `[2:163]` "Your God is one God. There is no god but He, the Benevolent, the Compassionate." — the clause with its mercy-names attached.
* `[7:172]` — the pre-birth testimony: "Am I not your Lord?" — tawhid as memory, not argument.
* `[59:22]` "He is Allah... the Knower of secrets and declarations..." — the oneness list closes with sovereignty over the unseen.
* `[112:1–4]` — "He is Allah, the One..." — the counter-claim to lineage theology: unbegotten and unpartnered.
* `[23:84–89]` — the triple interrogation: "To whom does the earth belong...?" — "To Allah." — "Will you not reflect?" repeated through heaven, Throne, dominion, protection: the pagans’ own admissions convict their partners. This is the book’s courtroom method for tawhid.
* `[23:91]`, `[23:116]` — "Allah has never begotten a son, nor is there any god besides Him. Otherwise, each god would have taken away what he created..." with the close "There is no god except He, the Lord of the Noble Throne." — the competing-gods reductio, sealed by doxology.

## Practical Reading
Tawhid is testable in the text by one question: **where does the passage assign to God an act humans were secretly crediting to another** (rain, wealth, children, fate, intercession)? That substitution is the Quran's real definition of shirk. Read 2:22, 6:100–102 (partners made from jinn), 39:3 (intercessors as friends) and the whole of 112 as the same argument from four angles.
