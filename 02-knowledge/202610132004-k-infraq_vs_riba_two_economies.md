# Two economies: infaq (circulating wealth) vs.riba (hoarding it)
**UID:** 202610132004
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines; the riba-destruction / charity-growth contrast is 2:276 verbatim; classical tafsir consensus treats 2:278–279 as the decisive prohibition (Ibn Kathir); the "two economies" synthesis is agent-structured — verify.

## Concept
The Quran reads money as theology made visible. Its counter-thesis to hoarding is a **divine business model**: wealth must flow toward God's named claimants or it dead-ends. The verbs are *infāq* ("spend in Allah's way," repeated as the community's signature act) and its metaphor engine: offering Allah a "generous loan" that He will "multiply it for him manifold" (2:245, 57:18, 64:17). Against it stands *ribā* — guaranteed self-multiplication of capital — declared war against God and His Messenger (2:279), physically impossible ("will not rise, except as someone driven mad by Satan’s touch" (2:275)), and economically inverted: "Allah condemns usury, and He blesses charities" (2:276), "usury... will not multiply with Allah" (30:39). Charity is audited by intent at the edges: 2:267 forbids giving "the inferior things" that "you yourselves would not accept... except with eyes closed" (2:267/2:268 family) makes spending the threshold of righteousness ("You will not attain virtuous conduct until you give of what you cherish"), and 63:10 makes it the last request of the dying (63:10 — "give from what We have provided for you, before death approaches one of you").

* `[2:261–262]` — the grain/seven-ears parable + "Do not nullify your charitable deeds with reminders and hurtful words." (2:264)
* `[2:267]` — give from the good you earn and from what We produced for you.
* `[2:274–276]` — night-and-day spending; the usury-declaration; the inversion verse.
* `[2:279]` — "if you do not... then take notice of a war by Allah and His Messenger... you shall have your principal."
* `[3:92]` — righteousness gated on giving what you cherish.
* `[30:39]` — usury does not multiply with Allah; zakat does.
* `[57:18]`, `[64:17]` — the loan-multiply-forgive formula.
* `[9:103]` — zakat as purification and sanctification taken by the Messenger.
* `[63:10]` — the deathbed petition to give — charity as time's deadline.
* `[76:7–9]` — the feeding triad at its purest: "they feed, for the love of Him, the poor, and the orphan, and the captive" answering themselves: "We only feed you for the sake of Allah. We want from you neither compensation, nor gratitude." — infaq with the social-return wire cut; the deliberate inverse of every riba economy.
* `[26:181–183]` + `[17:35]` — the measure-and-scale flank: Shu'ayb's commerce sermon "Give full measure, and do not cheat..." and "give full measure... weigh with accurate scales. That is fair, and the best determination" — economic justice as the ledger-side of the same circuit (refrain sites: 11:85, 7:85, 83:1–3 — verify renderings before quoting).
* `[111:2]` — “His wealth did not avail him, nor did what he acquired.” — the accumulation’s verdict in six words, paired with Qarun ([28:78]) as the book’s two named-case studies of wealth that failed at the audit.


## Practical Reading
Any passage about wealth is a test between the two economies: **does this money move toward named others, or does it compound toward self?** Track the verb: infaq-verbs with God as counterparty appear most densely in 2:195–274, 3:76–92, 57:18–21, 64:16–17, 103; riba passages cluster at 2:275–279, 3:130 ("do not feed on usury, compounded over and over"), 4:161, and 30:39. The economics chapter of the book is one argument with two balance sheets.
