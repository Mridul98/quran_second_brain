# Iblis/Shaytan: the declared enemy and the cure of refuge
**UID:** 202610150800
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines; the "open enemy" self-disclosure reading and the four-strategy catalog are agent synthesis — verify against primary tafsir.

## Concept
The Quran gives the adversarial force a dossier more complete than any other character — because the enemy introduces himself. Three movements: **(1) the declaration of war** — after the luring, Iblis states his strategy verbatim: ambush "on Your straight path," assault "from before them, and from behind them, and from their right, and from their left" (7:16–17), glamorize the world (15:39), by God's majesty "I will seduce them all. Except for Your loyal servants among them." (38:82–83) (38:82–83). The exception clause is the tell: the enemy's own admission concedes the refuge works. **(2) the limits of his power** — the book never makes him a dualist threat: "Over My servants you have no authority, except for the sinners who follow you" (15:42); "He has no authority over those who believe and trust in their Lord. His authority is only over those who follow him, and those who associate others with Him" (16:99–100) — authority (sulṭān) flows from the follower's own association, not from satanic force; the whisperer's entire arsenal is speech ("He promises them, and He raises their expectations, but Satan promises them nothing but delusions," (4:120) 4:120). **(3) the protocol** — the book names its own defense: seek refuge. "When a suggestion from Satan assails you, take refuge with Allah. He is Hearing and Knowing" (7:200); "When you read the Quran, seek refuge with Allah from Satan the outcast" (16:98 — the recitation protocol, printed on this very translation's frontispiece); "When a temptation from the Devil provokes you, seek refuge in Allah; He is the Hearer, the Knower" (41:36). The righteous difference: "when an impulse from Satan strikes them, they remind themselves, and immediately see clearly" (7:201). The closing two surahs generalize this into daily recitation: refuge from "the sneaky whisperer. Who whispers into the hearts of people. From among jinn and among people." (114:4–6).

## Evidence in the Text
* `[7:16–17]` — the strategy speech: straight-path ambush, four-directional approach, the minority forecast "you will not find most of them appreciative" (7:17).
* `[15:39]`, `[38:82–83]` — the two oath-framed declarations ("since You have lured me…" / "By Your majesty…") with the built-in exception.
* `[15:42]`, `[16:99–100]` — authority-by-permission: none over the believers, only over followers/associators.
* `[4:119–120]` — the promise-economy: mislead, entice, alter God's creation — "Satan promises them nothing but delusions."
* `[16:98]` — the refuge-before-recitation command (this edition quotes 16:98–100 on its own title page).
* `[7:200–201]`, `[41:36]` — the refuge protocol for the prompted heart; the God-wary "see clearly."
* `[20:117]` — the domestic frame on Adam: "this is an enemy to you and to your wife" — enmity announced, not hidden.
* `[2:168–169]` — "do not follow the footsteps of Satan. He is to you an open enemy. He commands you to do evil and vice, and to say about Allah what you do not know" — the footstep idiom + the false-speech definition.
* `[17:53]` — the social vector: "Satan sows discord among them" — whispering as speech-weapon between people.
* `[6:112]` — the universalized pattern: "an enemy—human and jinn devils— inspiring one another with fancy words in order to deceive" (6:112) — the whisperer dossier's human half, ratified at 114:6 "from among jinn and among people."
* `[114:4–6]` — the sneaky whisperer (al-wās-wās al-khannās): approach and withdrawal compressed into one adjective; the refuge surah as the protocol's liturgical form.

## Practical Reading
Whenever a passage mentions Satan, check which quadrant it occupies: strategy-declaration (7/15/38), power-limitation (15:42/16:99), promise-economy (4:120), or protocol (7:200/16:98/41:36/114). The dossier's argument in one line: the enemy is real but *bounded*, his only tool is persuasion, and the book hands the reader a two-move counter — remember, and take refuge. Searchable: `rg -i "satan|devil|whisper|refuge" 03-mushaf/`.
