# Sabr and taqwa: the two stamina verbs of the covenant
**UID:** 202610132003
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines; pairing of sabr with prayer is explicit at 2:45/2:153; the "tested by subtraction" reading reflects classical tafsir on 2:155 (Ibn Kathir); synthesis agent-structured — verify.

## Concept
The Quran never promises an untested life; it promises **tested-with-meaning**. Tests arrive by subtraction (2:155 — fear, hunger, loss of wealth/lives/fruits) and the covenant people's answer is two words: **sabr** (endurance that stays on the road under pressure) and **taqwa** (the watchfulness that keeps the road visible). They are operational, not emotional: sabr is explicitly *sought alongside prayer* ("Seek help through patience and prayers," 2:153, "it is difficult, except for the devout," 2:45), made communal ("Be patient, and advocate patience, and be united, and revere Allah, so that you may thrive," 3:200), and priced at the end ("Except those who believe, and do good works, and encourage truth, and recommend patience," 103:3 — the shortest surah’s entire exit clause). The reward grammar is disproportionate — "The steadfast will be paid their wages in full, without reckoning" (39:10) — the only account in the book with no ledger.

## Evidence in the Text
* `[2:45]` "And seek help through patience and prayer. But it is difficult, except for the devout."
* `[2:153]` — command form; Allah is "with the steadfast."
* `[2:155–157]` — the subtraction test and the returning-phrase response (2:156 "To Allah we belong...").
* `[13:22]` — “And those who patiently seek the presence of their Lord, and pray regularly, and spend…” — sabr folded into worship and charity in one clause.
* `[13:24]` — “Peace be upon you, because you endured patiently. How excellent is the Final Home.” — the greeting IS the reward of sabr.
* `[12:90]` — Yusuf’s test-case verdict: “He who practices piety and patience—Allah never fails to reward the righteous.”
* `[3:146]` — a prophet’s godly fighters who "did not waver for what afflicted them in the cause of Allah, nor did they weaken, nor did they give in" — sabr as battle-stamina, closing on the clause "Allah loves those who endure."
* `[3:200]` — patience as collective, competitive endurance.
* `[39:10]` — "For those who do good in this world, is goodness. And Allah’s earth is vast. The steadfast will be paid their wages in full, without reckoning." — the no-ledger clause.

* `[8:46]` — "be steadfast. Allah is with the steadfast" — linked to cohesion ("do not dispute, lest you falter").
* `[103:3]` — the rescue clause from loss: faith + works + mutual encouragement to truth + mutual recommendation of patience.

## Practical Reading
When a passage pairs a hardship with a command, check whether *sabr* names the **stamina** and *taqwa* names the **steering**; most verses that feel "repetitive about patience" are actually different quadrants: sabr under loss (2:155), sabr in combat (8:46), sabr as worship-partner (2:153), sabr as social contract (103:3). The map is four searches.
