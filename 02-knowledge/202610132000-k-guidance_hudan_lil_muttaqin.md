# Guidance (hudan) is a road with conditions — not a possession
**UID:** 202610132000
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines; classical distinction rahman/rahim and the "Fatihah answered by Baqarah" reading per consensus of tafsir-type analysis (Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi on 1:5–7 / 2:2–5); the "road not possession" synthesis is agent inference — verify.

## Concept
The Quran treats guidance (*huda/hidayah*) as its central verb, not a static label. Five properties recur: (1) guidance is **addressed, not owned** — the Book is "a guide" and the guided are *carried upon* guidance (2:2, 2:5); (2) it is **conditional on stance**, not lineage — the five traits of 2:3–4 are the stated terms; (3) it is a **road (sirat) with travelers on it** — the Fatihah defines the path socially (1:6–7); (4) it **can be forfeited** — 2:6–7's sealed hearts, 28:56's reminder that guidance is God's prerogative, not the preacher's power; (5) it is **answered by seeking** — the book opens with a petition and immediately answers it.

## Evidence in the Text
* `[1:6–7]` "Guide us to the straight path. The path of those You have blessed..." — petition + social definition of the path.
* `[2:2–5]` — the Book "a guide for the righteous," the five traits, "These are upon guidance from their Lord."
* `[2:6–7]` — the seal: guidance withheld after persistent rejection.
* `[6:153]` "This is My path, straight, so follow it. And do not follow the other paths..." — one road, many diversions.
* `[3:101]` "Whoever cleaves to Allah has been guided to a straight path." — guidance as holding on.
* `[16:9]` "It is for Allah to point out the paths, but some of them are flawed..." — sovereignty + real divergence.
* `[28:56]` "You cannot guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills..."
* `[42:52]` — the Book made "a light, with which We guide whomever We will of Our servants."
* `[31:3]` "A guide and a mercy for the righteous." — *hudan* paired with *rahmah*.
* `[17:9]` "This Quran guides to what is most upright..." — guidance by content, not coercion.
* `[2:257]` — "Allah is the Protecting Friend of those who believe; He brings them out of all darkness and into light" — guidance as transit, the surah’s own darkness→light verb.
* `[14:5]`, `[57:9]` — the same transit given to Moses and to the Messenger "to bring you out of darkness into the light"; with `[1:6]` this forms a guidance-family rg can pull in one query.
* `[64:11]` — "No disaster occurs except by Allah’s leave. Whoever believes in Allah, He guides his heart." — guidance as the interior stabilization under calamity, the tightest iman↔hidaya equation in the book.

## Practical Reading
At every mention of guidance ask: who is addressed, what condition attaches, what forfeits it, what road-image appears. Read the Fatihah as the question and al-Baqarah 1–7 as the first answer; most downstream "guidance" claims are variations on that pair.
