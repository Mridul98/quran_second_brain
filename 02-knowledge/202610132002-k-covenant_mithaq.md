# Covenant (mithaq): made once, broken often, renewed by grace
**UID:** 202610132002
**Type:** Permanent / Domain Knowledge
**Source:** observable corpus facts in the cited `[s:a]` lines; the three-layer covenant reading (primordial / national / personal) is standard in classical tafsir (Tabari and Ibn Kathir on 7:172 and 2:40); synthesis agent-structured — verify.

## Concept
The Quran's moral engine is a contract, not a vibe. It posits three nested covenants: **primordial** (7:172 — every human soul testified "Am I not your Lord?" before creation), **national/historical** (2:40, 2:83 — with Israel: worship none but Allah, be good to parents/kin/orphans, speak kindly to people, establish prayer and charity; 5:7 — with the believers: the covenant "by which He urged you"), and **personal** (16:91 — every oath-backed promise is "Allah as surety"). The book's recurring tragedy is breach: 2:27 "violate Allah's covenant after its confirmation," 2:100 "whenever they make a covenant, some of them toss it aside." The counterweight is not punishment but renewal: 3:76 "whoever fulfills his commitments and maintains piety— Allah loves the pious," and 48:10 making the pledge to the Messenger a pledge on God's own hand.

## Evidence in the Text
* `[7:172]` — the pre-creation summons and testimony: covenant as memory.
* `[13:19–26]` — the covenant-keepers’ “good final abode”; and `[13:25]` “As for those who violate the promise to Allah, after pledging to keep it, and sever what Allah has commanded to be joined…” — a near-verbatim twin of `[2:27]`, showing the covenant frame extends beyond Israel’s story.
* `[2:40]` "Remember My blessings... and fulfill your pledge to Me, and I will fulfill My pledge to you..." — reciprocal, conditional.
* `[2:83]` — the Israel covenant clause list in one sentence (worship, kin, orphans, speech, prayer, zakat).
* `[5:7]` — the blessing-covenant "by which He urged you" addressed to the new community.
* `[16:91]` — "Fulfill Allah's covenant when you make a covenant, and do not break oaths..." — covenant scaled to daily promises.
* `[2:27]`, `[2:100]` — breach named as a pattern, with the curse motif (2:159–161 for concealment).
* `[3:76]`, `[48:10]` — fulfillment as the beloved state; pledging as touching God's hand.

## Practical Reading
In every legal passage ask: which covenant clause is being enforced, renewed, or cited as broken? That question converts the "repetition" complaint about the Quran into its real structure — the same contract being renegotiated with each group across centuries. Watch for it hardest in 2:40–141, 5, 7:148–172, 33:7–8, 48:10–25.
